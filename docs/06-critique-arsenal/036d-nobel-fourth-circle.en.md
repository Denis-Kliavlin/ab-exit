## 0. Why a fourth circle

The first three circles covered behavioural economics, market design and institutions through economists. Two gaps remained: (1) institutionalists and contract theorists explaining *why* rules matter more than people; (2) physicists of complex systems explaining *what happens* to a system of millions of agents when one parameter changes. If economists (mechanism) and physicists (phase transitions) say the same thing in different words — AB-EXIT is not an "opinion" but a property of systems.

## 1. Institutions and contracts

**Douglass North (1993, institutional economics).** Growth depends on the "rules of the game", not on resources or virtue. The current mobilisation machine is an institution with gigantic transaction costs of political participation (campaigns, propaganda, fear). AB-EXIT replaces an inefficient institution with an efficient one: the costs of participation become explicit for the first time (price = dividend) and fall to zero for those who do not need to participate. North would add: institutions change when relative prices shift enough for the old order to become costlier than the new — the protocol changes exactly the relative price of a vote.

**Bengt Holmström (2016, contract theory).** Politics is a classic principal–agent failure: the agent (politician) acts in his own interest, the principal (voter) can neither observe effort nor measure output. Holmström would rate AB-EXIT as a pay-for-performance contract: the sole metric — median earnings — is observable, verifiable, incompressible; the dividend rises — the agent works, falls — failure. His own caveat (the multitasking problem): the agent optimises the measured to the detriment of the unmeasured — a mayor may chase the median while wrecking the environment. The protocol's answer: the median is not the mayor's KPI but a thermostat; policy itself is set by the voting core, for whom the environment is part of the territory's capitalisation (§13.9).

**James Buchanan (1986, public choice) — supplement to §13.** §13 covers the Calculus of Consent. The dialogue adds the constitutional-economics thesis: the system is saved not by education but by a hard change of rules under which selfishness works for creation. AB-EXIT is the ideal "Buchanan contract": the politician's survival is tied to the size of the citizen's dividend. In the dialogue Buchanan is named one of the three laureates who came closest to the protocol's core (with Roth and Thaler).

**Alvin Roth (2012) — supplement to 036b.** In 036b Roth is about repugnant markets. His second instrument is clearinghouses for markets where prices do not work: school placement, kidney exchange. AB-EXIT in this optic is a "vote-matching market": through the dividend, those who want to participate are separated from those who create noise, without direct purchase and sale and without selling to oligarchs — the asset returns to the state's "till".

## 2. Physics of complex systems

**Giorgio Parisi (2021, complex systems).** The electorate is a spin glass: millions of "moments" with high entropy, frustrated interactions, many local minima (party camps) from which the system cannot escape. Introducing the dividend is a phase transition: removing part of the moments (those who took the money) sharply reduces disorder, and the remaining ones align into a single structure.

**Ilya Prigogine (1977, dissipative structures).** Current politics is a structure far from equilibrium that requires constant energy injection: billions on advertising, fear, mobilisation — otherwise it disintegrates. AB-EXIT is an entropy dump: the system self-organises into a more efficient state without external pumping. The bifurcation point the architect speaks of (040d.3) is literally a Prigogine term.

**Philip Anderson (1977, "More is different").** The quality of governance is an emergent property; it cannot be derived from the properties of one voter. Now the atoms are disoriented by noise; the dividend "magnetises" them: a stable structure emerges from the chaos of atoms. Consequence for critics: arguing about "the rationality of the individual voter" is pointless — the mechanism works at the level of the ensemble.

## 4. Weak point of the answer

Physical analogies are heuristics, not proof: a spin glass does not vote for a populist out of resentment. Their value lies in predicting the *shape* of the transition (sharp, irreversible, with hysteresis), not its result. The only laureate whose position obliges action is Duflo: a pilot in one city with a control group. Until there is a pilot, the whole fourth circle is a plausibility argument. 🟡
