# 40bl. Elinor Ostrom: Ten Theses — Elections as a Commons by the Eight Principles, Crowding Out Citizenship, "No Panaceas" and Rules-in-Use

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites
**File:** 06_040bl · v1 · 28 October 2026
**Source:** the architect's question of 28 October 2026 ("what would Elinor Ostrom say about AB-EXIT"). Elinor Ostrom: *Governing the Commons* (1990, the eight design principles), "A Behavioral Approach to the Rational Choice Theory of Collective Action" (APSA presidential address, 1998), "Crowding Out Citizenship" (2000), "Going Beyond Panaceas" (PNAS, 2007), the IAD framework (rules-in-form and rules-in-use), the work on polycentricity and co-production. Before this chapter Ostrom had a paragraph in [036](036-nobel-laureates.md) ("probably the strongest academic ally") and the ladder of sanctions in [048k §4b](../08-implementation/048k-charter-inventory.md).

---

*How to read. Ostrom is the one laureate with a ready checklist for an institution: the eight principles for governing a commons. The chapter runs the protocol through all eight and through ten of her theses by one yardstick — is the thesis true today, and what does the protocol do with it — and then examines three of her blows: the first (crowding out) is removed by her own condition, the second ("no panaceas") by the protocol's design, the third (rules-in-use) remains an honest work front.*

## 1. Ten theses

| No. | Thesis (source) | What is true today | What the protocol does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The tragedy of the commons is solved not only by the market or Leviathan — there is a third way: the users themselves (1990) | Elections are a commons ([036](036-nobel-laureates.md)): every sold or herded vote pollutes the common signal, and nobody pays for it personally | The signal is given neither to the market (the vote is not sold) nor to the state (the government does not count for the people): the user decides whether to contribute his unit, and is paid for it himself |
| 2 | Clearly defined boundaries: who is a user and who is not (principle 1) | A register with no owner, a million surplus entries ([057h §2.7](../09-other-countries/057h-serbia-2026.md)) | The boundaries are set by the choice itself: three states for everyone, the sum balances (048m art. 10); an entry with an owner ([059h](../10-usa/059h-voter-roll.md)) |
| 3 | Congruence between rules and local conditions (principle 2) | Uniform national rules ignoring incomes and cycles | D is a percentage of the local median (art. 2); each level pays from its own median and budget (048k, decision 10); the cycle is the jurisdiction's own |
| 4 | Collective-choice arrangements: those affected by the rules can modify them (principle 3) | Election rules are changed by those elected under them | The charter is adopted and changed only by referendum, elected bodies do not touch it (art. 14); p once a cycle (art. 3) |
| 5 | Monitoring by the users themselves or by those accountable to them (principle 4) | A commission appointed by the government counts; the observer sees a box (the OSCE on Serbia, 057h §1) | A public counter every day (art. 9); a parallel count by supporters and tokens ([048i](../08-implementation/048i-three-channels-parallel-count.md)); the one who chose nothing is the monitor of his own entry (decision 12) |
| 6 | Graduated sanctions: mild the first time, harsher on repetition (principle 5) | Either nothing or a criminal case | The ladder of [048k §4b](../08-implementation/048k-charter-inventory.md): from refund to the dissolution of a commission; the entry and the polling station are punished, not the person |
| 7 | Cheap and local conflict resolution (principle 6) | Election litigation takes years, after the result | A court within days, a suit by anyone affected, open data as evidence, an automatic recount by a rule written in advance ([048j](../08-implementation/048j-courts-and-local-tuning.md), [048g §3c](../08-implementation/048g-rollback.md)) |
| 8 | Minimal recognition of the right to organise: external authorities must not undermine it (principle 7) | A bottom-up referendum is impossible in most countries ([006b §3c](../02-history/006b-predecessors-and-gaps.md)) | Route B: a private referendum, a petition, an initiative ([035](../07-manifesto/035-route-b.md), [048n](../08-implementation/048n-underdog-questions.md)) — the right to organise without the government but through the state |
| 9 | Nested enterprises and polycentricity (principle 8; "no panaceas", 2007) | One reform for the whole country from above | Courtyard → city → country ([048l](../08-implementation/048l-courtyard-pilot.md)); each level with its own p; the protocol is one rule, not a programme, everything else is local |
| 10 | Second-generation rational choice: people are conditional co-operators and willing punishers; communication raises co-operation (1998) | A full hall cannot tell a co-operator from a free rider | The one who stays is a conditional co-operator who has paid; he is also the willing punisher ([40bi §5](040bi-q-demography-blocks-change.md); 048k §4b, the "zero step"); the kitchen conversation "where is my share" is her cheap talk ([056f §13i](../09-other-countries/056f-russia-september-2026.md)) |

## 2. The eight principles as a checklist

| Principle | The present system | The protocol |
|---|---|---|
| 1. boundaries | a register with no owner | three states, the sum balances |
| 2. fit to place | uniform rules, alien sums | local median, local p, local cycle |
| 3. collective choice | changed by those elected under the same rules | referendum only |
| 4. monitoring by users | the government's commission | the counter, the parallel count, the monitor of one's own entry |
| 5. graduated sanctions | nothing or a criminal case | the ladder of 048k §4b |
| 6. cheap conflict resolution | years, after the result | days, on open data |
| 7. the right to organise | a bottom-up referendum closed | route B |
| 8. nesting | one reform from above | courtyard → city → country |

On all eight the protocol is closer to Ostrom's principles than the present system, and this is a checkable claim, not a slogan: each row points to an article of the charter or a chapter.

## 3. The first blow: crowding out citizenship

Ostrom (2000): an external monetary incentive imposed on a self-governing system destroys intrinsic motivation — Titmuss's donors, the day-care fine. This blow is examined under Sandel in [040as §3](040as-walzer-sandel.md): the price stands on the exit, not on the act; the protocol pays not the donors but those who do not want to be donors, and the blood becomes cleaner; to stay when one could take is the most explicit civic act.

Ostrom would add to Sandel what he lacks — a condition. In her data, crowding out happens when the incentive is *imposed from outside* on a self-governing community: government irrigation in Nepal against farmer-managed irrigation. Here the community adopts the rule itself by referendum (principle 3) and changes it itself (art. 14); that is exactly her condition under which an incentive does not crowd out but reinforces co-operation. And the architect's move from [040bk §2](040bk-north.md): there is nothing to crowd out — there is no norm of duty, there is a practice of bringing-in, and the protocol buys it out. The honest residue: the core that votes by habit rather than by bringing-in — a measurement of 048l §4.

## 4. The second blow: "no panaceas"

Ostrom did not believe in one blueprint for all: an institution must be diagnosed in place, and blueprints transplanted from one context to another fail. The protocol looks like a blueprint. The answer: it is one rule, not a system; it does not change the voting system (048k, decision 9), the levels each pay from their own (decision 10), courts and tuning are local ([048j](../08-implementation/048j-courts-and-local-tuning.md)). And it has what she demanded of any institution — a small-scale test before the large one: a courtyard of 500–1,000 people (048l), not a country. The only thing the protocol carries between contexts unchanged is the formula D = p × M and everyone's right to choose; that is not a blueprint but a unit of measurement.

## 5. The third blow: rules-in-form and rules-in-use

The IAD framework distinguishes rules-in-form from rules-in-use: the charter is rules on paper; what will work are commissions, courts and the habits of the polling station. The answer is partial and honest: the three counting channels (048i), the ladder of sanctions (048k §4b), the two watchmen (048g §3c) are designed for rules-in-use, but until the pilot they are a design. This is the main item on her work front, and the repository keeps it where she would — in the pilot's measurements (048l §4), not in the text of the charter.

## 6. What Ostrom gives the protocol

A language in which the protocol is neither "bribery" nor "a reform from above" but the self-governance of a commons, the electoral signal. A checklist of eight principles on which the protocol passes better than the present system on all eight (§2). The condition under which money does not destroy motivation — the rule adopted by those it concerns; the protocol is built exactly so. And the demand for a small pilot before a large one, which the repository met before she would have made it.

## 7. Summary

| Thesis | What is true | Protocol | What remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| a third way between market and Leviathan | yes | the user contributes his unit and is paid himself | — |
| the eight principles | yes | better than the present system on all eight | — |
| conditional co-operators and punishers | yes | the one who stays has paid and keeps watch | — |
| crowding out citizenship | yes, for an incentive imposed from outside | the rule adopted by the community; the price on the exit, not the act | the core by habit — the pilot |
| no panaceas | yes | one rule, everything else local; courtyard before country | — |
| rules-in-use | yes | channels, ladder, watchmen — a design | a pilot measurement |

## Weak point

Ostrom is paraphrased from the main works without quotations; the "eight principles" are given in her 1990 wording, the later refinements (2010) are not examined. Conformity on the eight principles is a comparison of designs, not a measurement; principles 4–6 (monitoring, sanctions, conflicts) exist on paper until the pilot, and that is her own objection (§5). Her field cases are not retold here, so as not to substitute irrigation for elections.
