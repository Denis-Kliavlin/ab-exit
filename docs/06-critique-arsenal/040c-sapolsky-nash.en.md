## 0. Two opposite defences of one mechanism

Sapolsky defends AB-EXIT from the position of biological mercy, Nash from the position of mathematical cynicism. Both arrive at the same point: the protocol does not require people to be better than they are. The section is needed because it gives an **armour-piercing answer to moralists** ("you are buying the votes of the poor") and formalises the thermostat as a credible threat with precise timing.

## 1. Sapolsky: "Determined"

The book's thesis: there is no free will. Every decision is the result of hormones, neurotransmitters, glucose levels, childhood experience, evolution. Looking at AB-EXIT, Sapolsky would experience dissonance: he would demolish the philosophy of the "free contract" and acknowledge the system as an ideal biological interface.

**1.1. Demolishing the illusion of a "free sale of the vote".** "There is no choice here. The poor person takes the money not as a rational investor but because the amygdala is screaming about a lack of resources now; the prefrontal cortex that should think about the country in four years is switched off by chronic stress and allostatic load." And the 20 % who forgo the money for x3 are not "better people" but individuals lucky to be born with different neurochemistry and low baseline stress that lets them delay reward (delay discounting).

**1.2. The end of moralising.** And yet — a huge step forward compared with classical democracy, which "reads morals to biological machines": scolds for apathy, demands awareness, appeals to duty. The apathy of the "sleepers" is learned helplessness (011b): for years the brain received the signal that actions do not change the environment. AB-EXIT stops lecturing and changes the stimulus: instant reward in exchange for legalising the neurobiological status of non-participation.

**1.3. The thermostat as a Skinner box at the scale of the state.** People return to the polls not from an awakening of civic consciousness but because an external irritant (a falling dividend) exceeded the pain threshold and triggered a stress response. AB-EXIT lays the shortest, safest path for that response: instead of a cobblestone (energy-costly, dangerous) — a "return the vote" button (safe, promising a change of conditions).

**1.4. Humanism without free will.** The book's message is mercy: if people are not to blame for what they are, fix the environment rather than hate them. Reconstructed verdict: "AB-EXIT is the first post-determinist political system. You acknowledged that the ordinary person cannot govern a complex state because their biology is tuned for survival here and now. Instead of blame — compensation for biological limits; governance — to those whose cortex can withstand forgoing immediate gain. A society that works with human nature rather than against it."

**1.5. The answer to moralists (ready formula).** To "you are buying the votes of the poor, it is immoral" — by Sapolsky's logic: *"It is immoral to demand that a person who cannot pay the rent think about macroeconomics ten years ahead. We do not deprive him of choice — for the first time we create an incentive that converts his daily stress into guaranteed capital, leaving governance to those who have the resource for it."*

**The architect's softening** (from the "underdog" dialogue): the point is not that the needy are "stupid" or "harmful" — poverty narrows the planning horizon (scarcity mindset). Investor mode thinks in 5–10 years; survival mode in 24 hours and votes for anyone who freezes tariffs now, even if it bankrupts the city in a year. The old paradigm solved this cruelly — with a census. AB-EXIT — with an exchange: "you need to feed your family, not study a 147-page budget — here is your share". And if the money is desperately needed but the person refuses anyway, their motivation is so conscious that they have earned the amplified vote. The exception for the "accidentally impoverished" is built into the choice itself.

## 2. Nash: a payoff matrix instead of morals

**2.1. Equilibrium.** In classical democracy there is no equilibrium: power always wants to steal more, the people always want to overthrow it — perpetual imbalance. In AB-EXIT: with a high dividend it is unprofitable for the voter to rebel (the "take the money" strategy yields maximum utility); it is unprofitable for the authorities to cut the dividend or steal, because that destroys the equilibrium and leads to dismissal. Nobody wants to change strategy — the system freezes at the point of maximum efficiency (see §15.5 in 006, three players at the optimum).

**2.2. From a zero-sum to a non-zero-sum game.** $10 million stolen by a mayor is minus $10 million for society: zero sum. AB-EXIT legalises the manager's super-income and ties it to the crowd's dividend: for the mayor to earn a million he must generate a billion for society. Predator and prey no longer need to trust each other — only the distribution algorithm.

**2.3. The cold rationality of the "apathetic".** A voter taking the dividend is crystal-rational: in a system where his vote weighs 1/1,000,000, a free trip to the polls is an irrational waste of time. AB-EXIT is the first to set an honest price on this micro-asset; selling a share of influence useless to you for guaranteed cash is the optimal utility-maximising strategy. (Poverty's pragmatism — see 040d.4: the poor are not stupid, they play the game imposed on them and squeeze the most out of it.)

**2.4. The thermostat as a credible threat.** Protests and revolutions are an *incredible* threat: people are afraid of the batons, and power knows it. The multiplier for those who refuse makes the threat of dismissal mathematically inevitable: if the dividend falls, the utility of the remainder drops below the utility of a change of power; the voter need not risk his life — press "refuse" and get the amplified weight. Power, knowing the inevitability, works itself to the bone to prevent the fall.

**2.5. Timing of the threat: paid once per cycle, not monthly.** Models drift by inertia into UBI logic; the architect corrects: the payment comes only at election time. That makes the "wallet memory" explosive. Monthly money is spread across the budget and quietly eaten by inflation — the psyche forgives small losses. A single large payment once per cycle is an Event: the family planned repairs, a loan, a car around it. The person remembers last cycle's sum to the cent; opens the interface expecting at least as much, and the system says "the economy sagged, this cycle it is less". The shock of loss flares **in the same second and the same interface** where the political decision is taken: the instrument of punishment is one click from the pain indicator. Managers cannot "show good statistics on TV" — by election day a tangible bag of money whose size the voter remembers must be on the table.

**2.6. Nash's verdict (reconstruction).** "You need not raise good citizens or find honest politicians — honesty and altruism are too unstable as variables. AB-EXIT builds the state on the most reliable constant — human greed — balanced by a strict matrix of incentives."

## 3. Weak point of the answer

Both defenders assume the link "bad governance → falling median → falling D" fires within one cycle. For a municipality with a data lag of a year to eighteen months (§3) the punishment arrives a cycle late; Nash would call this a "noisy" threat. The protocol's answer — the public counter (§4) shows the median continuously, and anger accumulates until election day — is empirically untested. 🟡
