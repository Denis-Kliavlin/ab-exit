*How to read. Buchanan is the one laureate who asked not "which decision is right" but "by what rules is it taken and who pays for it". He has four things that stand behind the protocol more exactly than anyone's: the veil of uncertainty, the generality principle, the constitutional constraint on Leviathan, and consent as exchange. And one question he would ask first: who pays. The chapter checks ten of his theses by one yardstick and examines three blows.*

## 1. Ten theses

| No. | Thesis (source) | What is true today | What the protocol does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Politics without romance: politicians and officials are maximisers like everyone; there is no "public interest", there is exchange (1979/2003) | Every reform asks the government to be better | The protocol does not ask: it changes the calculation ([036d §1](036d-nobel-fourth-circle.md): the politician's survival is tied to the size of the citizen's dividend) |
| 2 | Two costs of collective decision — decision-making and external; turnout is not a good in itself (1962) | Maximising turnout is declared a good | [013 §13.3](../03-theory/013-game-theory.md): decision costs lower (a hall with a stake), external costs do not rise (nobody is removed by force) |
| 3 | Constitutional choice differs from in-period choice: rules are chosen behind a veil of uncertainty, not knowing one's future position (1985) | Election rules are written by those who know their position | The referendum on the protocol is behind the veil: nobody knows whether he will be a taker or a stayer next cycle. This is "states, not types" in Buchanan's language |
| 4 | The generality principle: a rule applies to all alike, without targeted benefits (1998) | Targeted payments to pensioners and public-sector employees are the norm ([057h §2.6](../09-other-countries/057h-serbia-2026.md)) | D is the same for every holder of the right (048m art. 4) — his principle of generality in pure form; Vučić's package is his own counter-example |
| 5 | Leviathan: the state is a revenue maximiser and must be constrained constitutionally, by base and rate (1980) | No constraints | D is a constitutional line that elected bodies do not touch (art. 14); the treasury is bound by half the population ([040v §6](040v-przeworski.md)) |
| 6 | Democracy in deficit: the short horizon of voter and politician produces debt; a constitutional norm is needed (1977) | Yes | The short horizon is taken out of the hall ([016](../04-electoral-dynamics/016-age-economics.md), [040bk §2.5](040bk-north.md)); the current budget pays, not debt (art. 8; 048k, decision 13: no money — it accumulates, it is not borrowed) |
| 7 | Fiscal illusion: the voter does not see the price of public goods (1967) | The budget is an abstraction | "Where is my share" makes the budget a number on the card ([056f §13i](../09-other-countries/056f-russia-september-2026.md)); D is tied to the median everyone sees (040v §6) |
| 8 | Rent-seeking: competition for political rents burns resources (Tullock 1967, Buchanan 1980) | Campaigns are a race for the apathetic with airtime and packages | The payment is not a rent: by rule, to all, without a contest; the race for the apathetic loses its point, because they are not in the hall (057h §2.4) |
| 9 | The theory of clubs: optimal group size and self-selection by willingness to pay (1965) | The hall is not a club, people are brought to it | The protocol's hall is a club with an entry price everyone pays by refusing D ([036e §6](036e-nobel-fifth-circle.md), Mirrlees) |
| 10 | Politics as exchange, legitimacy from consent; the ideal is Wicksellian unanimity (1986) | Consent once in a lifetime, to a constitution | Consent every cycle: everyone consents to the rule anew by choosing under it (art. 5); unanimity is approached because nobody loses ([040bj §3](040bj-machiavelli.md)) |

## 2. The first blow: who pays

Buchanan's main question to everything — external costs on those who do not consent. D is paid from the budget, that is from the taxes of the productive: the stayer pays for the leaver. The repository's answer — [1g](../01-introduction/001g-objections-index.md) "A payment from taxes — the producers' money handed to parasites": the apathetic vote is already bought from the same pocket — with benefits, indexations, promises, without ceiling or receipt; the protocol puts a ceiling (D = p × M) and a receipt on that purchase, and the producer gets weight ×2 ([040aj §7](040aj-dalton.md)). Buchanan would accept this as the replacement of a hidden transfer by an explicit and bounded one — it is his own programme in *Public Finance* — and would ask about Wicksell: is the payment tied to the payer's consent? The answer: p is adopted by a referendum in which the payer takes part, and changes only that way (art. 3); under the protocol the stayers are those to whom the vote is worth more than the payment, and among them payers above the median weigh more than today. The Wicksellian connection — the payment and the consent to it in one procedure — is closer here than in any budget adopted by a parliament.

## 3. The second blow: unanimity against simple majority

A constitutional rule, by Buchanan, is adopted by a qualified majority; [040v §6](040v-przeworski.md) says a simple one suffices for the protocol. The answer: his demand for unanimity is not a procedure but a property of a rule under which nobody loses; the protocol approaches unanimity in substance (the taker gets money, the stayer gets weight, the politician a hall without bought votes) and in procedure — everyone confirms consent every cycle by a personal choice (art. 5), and whoever disagrees with the rule can take the money and not take part in what he disagrees with. This is closer to Wicksell than any constitution adopted once by a generation that is no longer there.

## 4. The third blow: Leviathan will capture the instrument

The government may wish to raise p to push its opponents out of the hall. The answer: p only by referendum and once a cycle (art. 3); a rise pushes out first the apathetic and the habitual, not opponents with a stake — the most determined remain ([40bi §5](040bi-q-demography-blocks-change.md)), and that is the worst thing a government could do to itself. The architect's move on overstating ([057g §1b](../09-other-countries/057g-ireland.md)) works the same way: overstating is cheap, lowering is a kamikaze ([059l §8b](../10-usa/059l-five-thousand-rehearsal.md)). And the reverse — lowering p to bring the apathetic back — needs a referendum in which half the country votes against its own money ([048g](../08-implementation/048g-rollback.md)).

## 5. What Buchanan gives the protocol

The veil of uncertainty as the exact name for "states, not types": the rule is just because it is adopted by those who do not know who they will be. The generality principle as the legal name for "the same to everyone" and as the boundary between the protocol and targeted grace. A language in which the protocol is not a social payment but a constitutional constraint on Leviathan: the one budget line the elected can neither repeal nor redirect. And the question "who pays", to which the repository has an answer with a ceiling and a receipt.

## Weak point

Buchanan is paraphrased from the main works without quotations. The answer to "who pays" is a comparison with the present system, not a proof of neutrality: the budget share of the payment (1e: 3–4.5 % of the fund at half taking) is a real cost borne by taxpayers; the protocol makes it explicit, not zero. Unanimity is approached, not reached: one who is against the rule and does not want the money keeps his vote, but inside a rule he opposes — Buchanan's external cost, reduced, not removed.
