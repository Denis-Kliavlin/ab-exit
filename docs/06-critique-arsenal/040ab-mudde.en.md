*How to read. Mudde's is the most used definition of populism, and that makes him the most dangerous author for the protocol: by his three markers a critic will test not the government but us. The first argument here is not a reconstruction but an audit: where the repository sounds like a populist and how it differs in mechanism.*

## 1. Populism is a "thin ideology": the pure people against the corrupt elite

**The argument.** Populism is not a programme but a thin ideology that divides society into two homogeneous and antagonistic groups, "the pure people" and "the corrupt elite", and holds that politics should express the general will of the people. Three markers: a moral dichotomy, a homogeneous people with one will, anti-pluralism ("we are the people").

**The strike.** The repository says "the government steals the citizen's money", "legalised bribery", "I return, I do not hand out" — this is exactly Mudde's frame, and a critic will quote it.

**The answer by mechanism — zero markers of three.**

| Mudde's marker | Populism | The protocol |
|---|---|---|
| moral dichotomy | the people pure, the elite corrupt | the people are broken into four states, and the apathetic with the core are no better than the government — they are its condition (1f); the elite is not corrupt: "there is no enemy, there is a creature and a lever" (1f §2b), the government is a predictable response to the composition |
| homogeneous people, one will | "the people want one thing" | the protocol pays those who disagree and amplifies those who stay — a design for a heterogeneous people; it neither assumes nor seeks one will |
| anti-pluralism | "we are the people", the rest are not | "those who stay" are re-elected every cycle (048m Art. 5 part 2); the taker is a citizen with the same rights (Art. 16) and a vote in the referendum on the rule (Art. 5 part 5) |

**The answer by register — an audit.** A Mudde critic quotes words, not mechanism. A search of the repository for "steals", "bribery", "stolen", "enemy" yields three groups.

| Place | Phrase | What it is in fact |
|---|---|---|
| 029 §29.11 | "the government steals the citizen's money and forbids him property" — the architect's thesis, recorded as a thesis | an economic statement: the citizen's resource is used for free and without his consent; next to it the mechanism (two monopolies, the table of coincidences) |
| 040y item 3 | "state funding of parties is legalised bribery" | legally exact: payment from the budget for the vote, permitted by a law written by the recipients (029 §29.11, row 10) |
| 048n, 040y | "I return, I do not hand out" — the source of the payment | audit and return of what was appropriated without payment; the word "stolen" without a court has been removed from 040y by this examination |
| 1b, the slogan | "Complicated where they steal; honest where it is simple" | about the complexity of rules, not about people |
| 1f, index | "they will call her an enemy" — the core's fear | and right beside it "the protocol names no enemies" (the head of `llms-full`) — the anti-populist formula stated explicitly |

The descriptive clusters — 056f (Russia), 018 (three epochs), 053 (Moldova) — are about regimes and history: there "steals" is a statement of fact, not a frame.

**Where the real difference is — and it must be known in one sentence.** The populist and the protocol part not in the diagnosis but in what they do with the thief: **the populist jails, the protocol presents a bill.** With the populist, after victory the elite is punished and replaced with his own — and Mudde showed the replacement behaves the same. With the protocol the government stays in place and starts paying for what it took for free: the brought-in vote costs the payment, the free guarding of the apathetic ends (029 §29.11). The test for any reader: **punishment or price.** Every phrase in the table passes it — none says "jail", all say "pay". That is the difference from populism a critic cannot close with a quotation: a populist's quotation about theft ends in a court, the protocol's ends in a counter.

**What was done after the audit.** A line in 001d (frequent misreadings): "steals" in the repository is an economic term, not a moral verdict. A line in 1g: "this is populism". In 040y "return of what was stolen" replaced by "return of what was appropriated without payment". The architect's quotations are untouched: they are marked as theses, and each has the mechanism beside it.

## 2. Populism is anti-pluralist: "we are the people"

**The argument.** The most dangerous marker: the populist recognises no legitimate opposition, because the opposition is not the people.

**The strike.** "Those who stay at the polls" are the new "real people", and the takers are not the people.

**The answer.** See the table of markers: the composition is re-elected every cycle, the taker keeps his rights and his vote in the referendum on the rule. Anti-pluralism needs a permanent "we"; under the protocol "we" is the result of one election, and at the next it is different. More: the protocol is the only offer in which the dissenter gets not a label but money.

## 3. Populism is a corrective and a threat at once

**The argument.** Populism brings back onto the agenda those who were excluded (the corrective) and destroys the institutions that protected them (the threat); the duality cannot be removed.

**The strike.** The protocol is the same duality: it includes the excluded and breaks elections.

**The answer.** The corrective — yes: the excluded, the invisible (040z item 5), get a price. The threat is absent by construction: the protocol touches no institution (040x item 2), does not annul elections (001d, the misreading about "against all"), is introduced and repealed only by referendum (048m Art. 15). Mudde's duality rests on the populist taking power; the protocol takes no power — it is a rule, not a party.

## 4. Populism is demand, not supply

**The argument.** Populism arises where the mainstream stopped answering the questions of part of the voters; it is a symptom, not a cause.

**The strike.** The protocol too feeds on the demand for "against all".

**The answer.** The protocol is the only offer that satisfies the demand for "against all" and extinguishes it: "against all" gets the payment and stops being a vote for the populist (1d, the misreading about "against all"; 040z item 3). Populism lives on an unexpressed "no"; the protocol gives it a price and an exit — and the populist loses the voter who fed him with free protest.

## 5. The answer to populism is not a ban but a better offer

**The argument.** Mudde is against cordons and bans: they confirm the populist's thesis "we are excluded". Populists are beaten by answering their question better than they do.

**The strike.** None — this is the argument closest to the repository.

**The answer.** The protocol is the answer to the populist's question — "why are we not heard" — without the populist: they are heard by money and the counter, not from a platform. Mudde demands a better offer; the protocol is an offer that does not ban the populist but leaves him without a free voter.

| anti-pluralism | — | "we" is re-elected every cycle; the dissenter gets money, not a label | — |
| corrective and threat | the corrective — yes | no threat: no institution touched, referendum only | — |
| demand, not supply | yes | the protocol extinguishes the demand for "against all" with a price | — |

## Weak point

The register audit closes the mechanism but not the quotation: a critic who needs three words will take "the government steals" from 029 and will not read the table of coincidences beside it. The only defence is the line in 001d and the test "punishment or price", and it works only for a reader who reaches 001d. And there remains the honest question Mudde would ask last: an offer that "leaves the populist without a voter" itself competes for the same voter — how then is it not populism by function? The repository's answer: it asks for no vote and takes no power; but the functional resemblance in whom it is addressed to is acknowledged.
