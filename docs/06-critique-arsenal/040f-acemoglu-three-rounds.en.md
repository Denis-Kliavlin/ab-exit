## 0. Why they are the chief critics

Utilitarians and cyberneticists see a beautiful model in AB-EXIT. Acemoglu and Robinson look only at the nature of power, and their apparatus (inclusive/extractive institutions, the narrow corridor, the Red Queen, de jure vs de facto) is the heaviest that can be brought against the protocol. The section walks through three iterations of their critique; the result is not a "rout" but two arguments the repository lacked: **comparison with the real corridor rather than the textbook** and **the structural cause of corruption: lying as a survival tax under populism**.

## 1. Round one: a bridge to technocratic oligarchy

1. *Voluntary dismantling of inclusive institutions.* The rich and the fanatics will remain at the polls; having received x3, the new elite immediately builds an extractive system — rewrites taxes, monopolises markets, redistributes from dividend-takers to vote-keepers. Power always works for those who hold it.
2. *The thermostat's switch.* "Your thermostat assumes the elite will play by the smart contract's rules. Institutions are not laws of physics. Seeing the dividend fall and the mass ready to return, the elite will vote to switch it off: a returned vote will cost $10,000, or an educational census will be introduced, or the constitution rewritten." Michels' iron law of oligarchy.
3. *Death of the Red Queen.* Freedom is not a state but a run: society must run flat out to keep the Leviathan in the corridor. "You pay society to stop running. When the sleepers want to wake, the keys will long since have been changed."

Verdict: "a brilliant engineering utopia that ignores political economy: power cannot be pawned and redeemed — the first reform of a minority with a multiplier will be the destruction of the return mechanism itself".

**The answer to items 2 and 3 is already in the repository**: cancellation only by nationwide referendum with automatic summons of all (§42, 040b.6); the corridor — see §4 below. But item 1 required development.

## 2. Round two: attacks around the mechanism

Having conceded that the referendum cannot be cancelled, the institutionalist changes target:

- *Data manipulation.* "The elite will make the statistics office paint the median." — The dividend is paid not from painted figures but from real treasury money: it is either on the accounts or it is not; if the elite steals, the till empties, there is nothing to pay with — the pain signal gets through guaranteed. (Plus independence of the data source — §2.)
- *Indirect confiscation.* "They will impose 50 % VAT; the dividend returns to the treasury through the shop." — Here the thermostat works at full force: the dividend's purchasing power collapses, the voter is furious, nobody removed the right of return — next cycle he refuses the "funny money" and takes the ballot.
- *The dirty switch.* "Politics rests on a monopoly of violence. The elite will not cancel the referendum legally — it will declare martial law, cut the internet, seize the servers, keep the awakened from the polls." — AB-EXIT does not abolish courts and the constitution; using the army to cancel the system is a coup, against which no system protects, including current democracy. Moreover, a coup against a *payment* is the only coup where the army must shoot at its own dividend (056e, Z2).
- *Atomisation.* "The dividend turns the citizen into a lone consumer; those returning under the thermostat are an unorganised angry crowd without parties or leaders that will lose to an organised elite or elect a dictator promising to restore the dividend." — The dividend is taken for one cycle, not forever; the protocol acts at the level of elections and does not abolish local self-government, where "social muscles" train. Honest remainder: the organisational deficit of the returners is a real risk of the transition cycle (🟡).
- *Skill atrophy.* "Democracy is a skill like cycling; a generation that took the cheque for fifteen years will in a crisis vote for whoever shouts loudest — incompetence returns tenfold." — The same answer, plus: half that generation does not vote now either (040b.5); a skill that does not exist cannot atrophy.

## 3. Methodological turn: the nirvana fallacy

To the architect's question "does AB-EXIT improve or worsen *current* democracy?" the academic critique crumbles: it compares the protocol with an ideal spherical democracy that does not exist. Acemoglu writes as if the masses now control the elite; but the masses now watch TikTok while the elite paints percentages through state employees and television. Compared with reality, AB-EXIT heals three festering wounds:

1. **Administrative resource.** The elite herds teachers and doctors under threat of dismissal; the person has no choice. Under AB-EXIT the state employee has a financial shield: to make him cast a free ballot with a "take the money" button on the table, the elite needs direct terror — and that is dictatorship, which does not live long. The administrative resource becomes unprofitable.
2. **Cheap populism.** A promise of "greatness" costs nothing and is believed. Now the populist faces not another politician but a calculator: abstract "someday" against money now. The feeding base leaves.
3. **Free apathy.** 50 % do not vote — their silence is free for politicians. AB-EXIT imposes a tax on silence: want to govern without mass interference — pay the dividend; to pay — grow the economy. The elite's greed is tied to the sleepers' welfare.

A pitch formula born in this round: *AB-EXIT set the voter's greed against his own stupidity* — and greed beats stupidity (a bird in the hand against a crane in the sky). You cannot tell an investor "my project will make people smarter"; you can say "my project monetises incompetence, sets mercantilism against it, and algorithmically purges power of populists".

## 4. The corridor: not a dirt track but rails

"Where is the narrow corridor now? In most countries the state fell out of it long ago" — by propaganda, bribery of state employees and media monopoly the walls have been pushed out to a stadium where the elite plays and the crowd watches from the stands. Society cannot outrun the Leviathan — it is tired and wants to survive.

Under AB-EXIT the corridor becomes **narrower** — walls of mud turn into mathematics:

- *The right wall (against ochlocracy and populism):* the politician shouts "enemies all around" — the pragmatist answers "keep talking, I'm taking the dividend". The audience has legally left the hall.
- *The left wall (against dictatorship and corruption):* a technocrat left with a smart electorate steals — the treasury empties, the dividend falls, the sleepers get an electric shock and return with amplified weight.

This is **automation of the Red Queen**: society no longer has to run as a whole — the engaged minority runs, and the sleeping majority is protected by a watchdog built into the budget's smart contract. The architect's correction against the simplification "people are lazy": the point is not laziness but **bandwidth**. A surgeon, an engineer, a mother of many may be geniuses in their work — they do not have twenty hours a week for politics. The engaged minority are those with time, education, a cushion or civic passion. The protocol does not discard voters — it rationally allocates their energy.

## 5. Round three: "Saudi Arabia without oil"

The coldest version of the critique: "You are not saving inclusive democracy. You have built the most perfect model of an extractive state that benefits the majority: an algorithmic rentier state whose resource is not oil but managerial efficiency; legalised clientelism; efficient authoritarianism with a human face, where minority shareholders receive dividends but do not vote." Plus de jure/de facto ("an algorithm cannot hold a gun; the central bank will turn on the printing press, the dividend will be paid in scraps") and *the destruction of pluralism* ("you filtered out the poor, the marginal, the fanatics — you got a sterile ward of managers thinking in growth charts; how will it decide ecology if ecology requires lower profit now?").

Their own verdict for the real world: "for societies stuck in the post-Soviet matrix, where democracy is de facto dead, AB-EXIT turns kleptocracies into transparent technocracies — a brilliant transitional stage that launches growth where institutions have failed". For a venture pitch this position is stronger than stories about democracy.

## 6. The key answer: why would the winners steal

The architect's common-sense question: "What stops the cool managers who removed the bureaucracy from just governing well and living happily ever after? Are they all crooks? Turn on the printing press to get inflation?"

The academic axiom "all power corrupts" presumes that whoever grabs power immediately starts stealing unless slapped on the wrist daily. But under AB-EXIT:

- *Honesty is the most profitable business.* Today a politician steals because (a) his horizon is short and (b) he must feed an army of loyal officials and enforcers to hold power. Under AB-EXIT the crowd's loyalty is already paid by the dividend, no army of propagandists and commissions is needed; the horizon extends from four years to decades; the elite's assets are inside the country: to steal a million from the budget and crash your own business's capitalisation by a billion is not "corruption by power" but clinical stupidity — and the protocol filtered out the stupid at the entrance.
- *The printing press.* Inflation wrecks their own businesses, devalues their own income, and the thermostat fires anyway — shop prices hit the mass instantly. This is a pilot unscrewing the engine's nuts in flight to sell them as scrap.
- *Lee Kuan Yew.* Not everyone in power wants to be a petty crook; many want to be in the textbooks. Absolute de facto power in Singapore was used to burn out corruption and turn a village into a financial centre. The protocol creates the conditions for a collective Lee Kuan Yew: with noise and the need to lie to the mass removed, a manager can carry out unpopular but necessary reforms, knowing the crowd will not carry him out on pitchforks — the crowd gets its share of growth.

**And the main thing — the structural cause.** "They presume that anyone who reaches power will steal and print money — precisely because any smart politician is *forced* to seek the crowd's love and compete with populists. After AB-EXIT does he need to?" A brilliant honest technocrat in the current system must get 51 %; half of those want hand-outs and believe simple solutions; next to him a populist promises to "cut prices by decree". Let the technocrat tell the truth ("raise the pension age, cut the apparatus by 30 %, in five years we'll live better") — he loses in the first round. The system **forces** the smart to become a liar: first deceive the mass, then steal from the budget to feed loyal state employees and media. *Corruption and lying are a survival tax under populism.* Remove the need to fight for the votes of an incompetent majority — the need for corruption and populism disappears by itself. Western political science spent centuries writing laws forbidding politicians to steal — treating the cough while ignoring the pneumonia. Cf. §15 (honest politicians) — the same thought from the other side.

## 7. What Acemoglu leaves open (honest remainder)

- **Pluralism.** A sterile ward of managers is a real price. The answer via "the territory's capitalisation includes ecology" (040c) is plausible, not proven. 🟡
- **Technocratic capture of the code.** Who guarantees the developers do not embed hidden coefficients? The formula D = M × 1.5 × K leaves no room for magic (a fifth-grader divides one by the other), but theft hides *before* the formula (understating the numerator via a "coefficient X" for infrastructure) and *after* (dead souls in the denominator; a "salami" attack on rounding). The only protection is open source and reproducibility: any student runs public data through the code and gets the figure that arrived to his grandmother. A requirement on the protocol, not on faith. See §2, §4.
- **The commitment problem** ("elites never surrender power voluntarily") — closed by zugzwang: nobody asks them (047, 056d.2).
