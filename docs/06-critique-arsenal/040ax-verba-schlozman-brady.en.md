*How to read. The civic voluntarism model explains participation by three things: resources (time, money, skills), engagement (interest, a sense of efficacy), recruitment (who asked). The chapter shows that the protocol is the first to take the load off skills — the one question it asks everyone needs no skill — and that the "chorus" of organisations with no poor in it the protocol does not fix and does not promise to.*

## 0. What they claim

Three factors of participation: resources — time, money and civic skills (the ability to speak, write and run a meeting, acquired at work, in church and in organisations); psychological engagement — interest and a belief in one's own efficacy; recruitment — a request to take part from an acquaintance or an organisation. Participation rises with socio-economic status in every channel, but unequally: voting is the most equal channel, contributions the most unequal. The church is the great equaliser of skills for the poor. *The Unheavenly Chorus* of 2012 adds the universe of organisations: among thirty-five thousand interest groups there is not one representing the poor as the poor; the class skew is stable across decades and is smoothed neither by the internet nor by rising education.

## 1. Resources decide: whoever is poorer in time and money takes part less — a paid exit will deepen the skew

**Argument.** The payment is a resource that the person poor in time and money will take more often; voting, the most equal channel of participation, will become as unequal as contributions.

**Answer.** Their own scale: voting is the most equal channel because it is the cheapest in resources. The protocol does not make it dearer — it places beside it a second channel of the same price: taking. In time and money both are free; only the stake tells them apart (1g "A tax on the poor", 19b.1). The skew they measured is a skew by resources in halls where participation costs resources: a meeting, a letter, a contribution. The protocol's one question costs none. And the hall is counted in three movements (040aw §2): those leave whose vote today belongs to whoever brought the package, and the burned return, whom their surveys do not contain.

**Residual.** None.

## 2. Civic skills: participation needs an ability given by work, church and organisations — those who stay will be the skilled

**Argument.** The main predictor is not income but skills: to speak, write, run a meeting, understand where a question is decided. To decide "take or stay" is to assess what the government is worth and what my vote is worth, and that is a civic skill; whoever lacks it will take by default, will not acquire the skill, and the circle closes.

**What the repository already answers.**

1. *What must be assessed is not the government but one's own stake.* The question by which selection is measured is "will your life change with the outcome" ([040p §1](040p-how-to-refute.md)): not a judgement about programmes and budgets but knowledge about oneself — a mortgage, children, a business, a job. Everyone has such knowledge; no meeting skill is needed for it.
2. *Income, intelligence and information are three different axes* ([019b §19b.1](../04-electoral-dynamics/019b-poor-voter-price.md)): the poor man counting every penny knows the mechanism better than a sociologist. Verba glues skill to resources; the repository forbids gluing them.
3. *The production chain — the architect's move.* A working person is part of a chain and has at least a remote idea of where goods and services come from; this practical skill protects against promises of "money from nowhere" and is spread more widely than any of Verba's civic skills ([040bd §3](040bd-q-citizens-are-rational.md)).
4. *The honest limit* ([001b §12](../01-introduction/001b-essence.md)): the protocol does not make voters wiser — refusing money proves motivation, not competence. It selects not the skilled but those with a stake.

**Proof by contradiction** (at the architect's suggestion: "what happens if we disagree — let us prove it from the opposite"). Accept Verba's argument in full: the choice "take or stay" requires a civic skill, the unskilled take by default, the hall shifts toward the skilled.

- *Today the unskilled vote even worse.* If a binary question is beyond them, a choice among several candidates and programmes is even more so: it is a much harder task. So today the unskilled person's vote is a cue from a campaign, a boss or the feed — by the accounting standard a fictitious entry at full value. Removing such votes from the total improves the quality of the decision — the conclusion "the protocol will worsen representation" contradicts the assumption itself.
- *The unskilled person gains under the protocol.* Today he has neither influence (a vote by cue) nor money; under the protocol he has the payment, the right to vote stays forever and he can return at any election (048m arts. 5, 16). Compared with what exists, not with an ideal, he is better off.
- *The circle is not closed by the protocol.* Verba himself measured that the skill skew is stable for decades: non-participants do not acquire skills today either. And by his own model the first factor of participation is resources, money included; the protocol gives the unskilled exactly this resource — by Verba's logic that widens, not narrows, his future participation.

**The fork.** Either the argument is false, and choosing needs only knowledge of one's stake — then selection runs by stake, as designed. Or the argument is true — then the protocol removes cue votes from the total, gives the unskilled money without loss of rights and opens the circle rather than closing it. There is no third case: the skills argument cannot be an objection to the protocol in any case — it is either false or confirms the protocol.

**Residual.** The skills of those who stay are a pilot measurement, the same 048l row as income; a skill shift, if it occurs, is not an objection but a consequence assessed above.

## 3. Recruitment: people take part when asked; the payment removes those whom nobody can ask

**Argument.** Half of participation is a response to a request from an acquaintance, a church, a union; whoever has no network is not asked; the protocol pays exactly them to leave.

**Answer.** Today recruitment is the main line of the turnout machine: bringing-in, phone banks, the package (040al, 057d §3b). The one "asked" most often is the apathetic, and he is asked with the campaign's money. The protocol keeps recruitment by substance and removes recruitment by price: the person whom nobody can call is "called" for the first time by the budget itself, and called honestly — take or stay. And those who stay recruit themselves: 30 % of the nonvoters at $100 each (040ap §4), one or two touches (040aq §4). The largest request for participation in history is a line on the ballot.

**Residual.** None.

## 4. Organisations without the poor: the chorus of interest groups sings without them; the protocol does not fix the organisational world

**Argument.** Not one of thirty-five thousand organisations represents the poor as the poor; exit from the booth does not create one for them.

**Answer.** True and not disputed: the protocol does not touch the world of organisations and does not promise to (040aw §1, the lobbying residual). What it changes is that world's price. Organisations are strong while they buy turnout and votes; turnout can no longer be bought. And the poor man gets the one representation he has in no organisation — a share of the median to his account, without dues and without a meeting (048m art. 4). That is not a voice in the chorus; it is the chorus's first payment to him.

**Residual.** Yes, theirs: the world of organisations without the poor remains. The protocol does not fix it and does not promise to.

## 5. The persistence of the skew: decades, the internet and education shifted nothing — nor will the protocol

**Argument.** The class skew survived every participation reform; one more will not shift it.

**Answer.** Every reform they measured lowered the price of participation: registration, the internet, education. The protocol is the first to raise the price of non-participation for the government: the budget pays for every leaver, and the share of takers is a quotation that cannot be drawn (013g §3b). The participation skew survived a lower price for citizens; it has not been tested against a higher price for the government.

**Residual.** None.

## 6. What they give the protocol

Three variables for the pilot — time, money, skills — and the question along which of them those who stay are shifted (048l §4). A scale of channel equality on which voting is the most equal, so that the choice "take or stay" is the most equal choice available. And *The Unheavenly Chorus* as an honest map of what the protocol does not fix.
