## 32.1. The current splits of the tech elite

Besides the division into B1 and B2 (see §31), the tech elite has one more important structural characteristic: **they are now politically fragmented and have no common unifying project**.

The current picture of splits:

- **Musk vs Altman** — a public feud over OpenAI and xAI
- **Andreessen vs Hoffman** — Andreessen backed Trump in 2024, Hoffman funded the Democrats
- **Thiel vs Khosla** — different political camps
- **Peter Thiel vs Sam Altman** — different positions on AI regulation
- **Musk vs Satya Nadella** (Microsoft) — competition for the AI narrative
- **Armstrong (Coinbase) vs Warren** — different approaches to financial systems

## 32.2. The structural weakness of fragmentation

The tech elite has **no common political project** under which they could unite. The old elite has Davos, Bilderberg, the CFR, Brookings — institutional platforms. The tech elite has practically none of this.

**1. AB-EXIT is non-partisan by nature.** Structural mechanism design, not an ideological programme. Andreessen (right), Hoffman (Democrat), Khosla (centrist), Altman (liberal technocrat) can support it **without an obligation to accept anyone else's ideology**.

**2. AB-EXIT protects their common interests.** All suffer from populist volatility. One crisis in the USA/Europe devalues their assets by tens of billions. AB-EXIT gives a common solution.

**3. AB-EXIT is in their own cultural paradigm.** Mechanism design, game theory, structural redesign — the native language of all of them. This is not "politics" — it is "an engineering task of system design", which they understand better than political scientists.

**4. AB-EXIT gives everyone a concrete role.** Andreessen can write essays on Substack. Hoffman can organise meetings with politicians. Khosla can fund a pilot referendum. Cuban can run a media campaign. Armstrong can work with the crypto community. This is **a division of labour** under a single project, not a fight for the leader's chair.

**5. AB-EXIT gives them a collective legacy.** Each is already a billionaire. What do they need next? Not more money — **a legacy**. AB-EXIT as "the mechanism we carried through together in 2026–2028 that fixed democracy" is a historical legacy of the order of the invention of the telephone, the internet, container shipping. The glory is distributed among them, not concentrated on one.

## 32.4. Launch strategy — not from above but through a coordination moment

**Do not try to organise the tech elite from above.** Launch the project so that they themselves see the possibility of coordination.

The dynamics:

1. The first anchor supporter (Andreessen or Khosla) starts a public discussion through Substack or a podcast
2. The second (Hoffman) responds, sees that Andreessen supports it, understands the project is non-partisan
3. The third (Altman or Armstrong) joins, sees there are already two supporters
4. **The coordination moment** — everyone sees that the others support it, they join en masse
5. A movement arises that is already hard to stop

This is the classic dynamic of **network effects** — every subsequent joining raises the value of participation for the rest.

## 32.5. An additional effect — legitimisation for class A

When the tech elite (B1) unanimously supports the reform, the academics of class A **can no longer keep calling it "marginal"**. AB-EXIT gains a legitimacy that spreads back to class A.

This is **a double effect** — mobilising B1 simultaneously weakens A's resistance. One right move gives two results.
