# 40bu. Talcott Parsons: Ten Theses — Power and Money as Two Media of Exchange, the Rate between Them, De-differentiation, the Legitimacy of the Departed, and Function L without a Ritual

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites
**File:** 06_040bu · v1 · 7 November 2026
**Source:** the architect's question of 7 November 2026 (analysis of a Gemini chat: "what would Parsons say"). Talcott Parsons: *The Structure of Social Action* (1937), *The Social System* (1951), "On the Concept of Political Power" (1963; power as a generalised symbolic medium of exchange, analogous to money), the AGIL scheme (adaptation, goal attainment, integration, latency — pattern maintenance), *The System of Modern Societies* (1971; differentiation of subsystems as the measure of evolution). Quotations are paraphrased from these works.

---

*How to read. Parsons is the only one in the series who said it outright: power is a currency, with its own "bank", "deposits" and "inflation". He is therefore the hardest to accuse of not understanding the protocol: he was the first to describe the vote as a deposit. The chapter tests ten of his theses and three blows: "you merge two currencies — a relapse into feudalism", "those who took the money will not recognise the government", "you removed the ritual — and function L with it".*

## 1. Ten theses

| No. | Parsons's thesis | What is true today | What the protocol does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Power is a generalised symbolic medium of exchange, like money (1963): the vote is a deposit, the politician a banker, the law a credit | Yes, but nobody sees the rate between deposit and credit; "inflation of power" (promises without cover) is not measured | For the first time a rate between the two currencies is declared, the same for everyone and only with the state ([048m art. 3](../08-implementation/048m-charter.md)); a promise gets a price to be compared with ([1i: D as the numeraire of politics](../01-introduction/001i-consequences.md)) |
| 2 | Inflation of power: when credits (promises) exceed deposits (real support) — a run on the bank | Turnout and real support diverge, and by how much is unknown ([040bq thesis 10](040bq-lenin.md)) | The three states under art. 10 show the divergence as a number; the share who took is a measurement of the "run" before it reaches the street |
| 3 | Society evolves by differentiation: economy, polity, law, culture — distinct subsystems with distinct currencies | True as history; but the subsystems are already mixed covertly: the vote is bought with benefits, the seat with money ([023f](../05-empirical-base/023f-money-at-elections.md)) | The rule does not mix the currencies; it places the single open sluice between them — one-way, towards the state, with a ceiling; the hidden sluices (busing, packages, carousels) lose their material ([030b](030b-ten-tricks.md)) |
| 4 | Function A (adaptation): the system must obtain resources | The budget is obtained from those who rarely vote and spent on those who bring voters in | The one budget line the elected do not touch goes to the citizen (art. 14; [040bm §5](040bm-buchanan.md)) |
| 5 | Function G (goal attainment): politics gathers and allocates power to goals | Goals are set by whoever gathered the brought-in | Goals are set by a hall that has counted ([040bo §3](040bo-montaigne.md)); power turns to the centre, not to force (1i) |
| 6 | Function I (integration): the losers submit because they took part | Half take no part and submit to nothing by consent — only by habit ([040aa](040aa-runciman.md)) | The taker submits to a rule he adopted by referendum and to a choice he made himself; the departed has a price, a date of return and a reason to return (§3) |
| 7 | Function L (latency): pattern maintenance — values, ritual, the "sacred" | The ritual stands, the congregation is gone: 50–70 % do not come ([1i: zombie democracy](../01-introduction/001i-consequences.md)) | Refusing money for the vote is a sacrifice with a sum and a date; the value of the vote returns not by sermon but by opportunity cost (§4) |
| 8 | Roles and expectations: the system rests on everyone knowing what is expected of him | The voter is expected to come, the politician to promise; neither is checked | The roles are rewritten: the voter is the one who compared two numbers; the politician is the one who persuades his own not to take ([1i: campaigning changes genre](../01-introduction/001i-consequences.md)) |
| 9 | Equilibrium: the system tends to homeostasis through feedback | There is one feedback — every four years — and it is damped by busing | Feedback is continuous and numerical: the median, the share who took, the price; "the swings once a cycle vanish with the replacement of the hall" ([059o](../10-usa/059o-ship-turning-every-700-days.md)) |
| 10 | De-differentiation is regression: when the currencies merge, society slides back to the chief who is at once rich, strong and holy | His main blow, §2 | The rate is one-way; nobody can buy a vote except by handing it to the state for D; the currencies do not merge — a price appears between them where there was none |

## 2. The first blow: "you set a rate of 1 vote = D, so you merge politics with the economy — de-differentiation, a relapse into a feudalism where everything is for sale"

Parsons's strongest argument, and it is systemic, not moral. The answer is a fork ([proof from the contrary](040bj-machiavelli.md)). Accept the argument whole: any open rate between vote and money is a merger of currencies and a regression. Then the present system is already a regression, and a deep one: the vote is bought with a benefit, an indexation, a package before the election, a bus; the seat is bought with money ($32 million against Massie, Musk's half a billion — [023f §4b](../05-empirical-base/023f-money-at-elections.md)); the rate exists, hidden, targeted and without a ceiling. Either the argument is false and an open rate is not a merger; or it is true — and then the protocol does not create a regression but brings the existing one out of the shadow, sets a ceiling and makes the rate the same for all. The second is differentiation in Parsons's own sense: the boundary between the subsystems does not vanish; for the first time it becomes visible and guarded. The exchange is one-way: a citizen may hand his vote to the state for D for one cycle; nobody — no party, corporation or neighbour — can buy a vote, because a vote for which money was taken is cancelled in the register (art. 10). The chief who is "at once rich and strong" is exactly whom the rule deprives of his instrument: his strength rested on the hidden rate.

## 3. The second blow: "those who took the money will not recognise a government they did not choose; you split the country into shareholders and the departed, and the function of integration collapses"

The argument about the legitimacy of the losers: they submit because they took part. The answer. First, the rule is adopted by a referendum of all ([048m art. 14](../08-implementation/048m-charter.md)): the taker submits not to a government he did not choose but to a rule he chose — and for this cycle he himself chose money over the vote. That is a fuller participation than today's: he made a choice for which he received a sum, rather than being silently absent. Second, "I did not choose this" is said today by half the country — the half that did not come and received nothing; the split into shareholders and the departed exists before the rule, for free and with no date of return. The rule gives the departed a price, a date (the next election) and a reason to return — a strengthened vote if he dislikes the government; the register records it as the rows "the burned get a reason to return" and "the rule enforces itself: the takers lose money and the stayers lose their share if it is broken" ([1i](../01-introduction/001i-consequences.md)). Third, the government knows the departed will return, and that knowledge is part of integration: it is addressed not to force but to the centre (1i). What remains of the blow: the share of takers who return "out of resentment" is unknown — the same one number. The objection is entered in [1g](../01-introduction/001g-objections-index.md).

## 4. The third blow: "you hang a price tag on the sacred; without the ritual, function L — pattern maintenance — dies, and in a crisis nobody will defend such a state"

The answer is short, because the fact is known: the ritual stands and the congregation is gone. 50–70 % do not come; the "sacredness" of the vote is already worth zero to them, and half a century of sermons has not changed that ([040aa](040aa-runciman.md); [1i: zombie democracy](../01-introduction/001i-consequences.md)). The rule does not remove the sacred — it gives it back a price: whoever refused D for the vote made a sacrifice with a sum and a date, and his vote is again worth exactly what he gave up. That is function L read as accounting: the pattern is maintained not by words but by opportunity cost. On "nobody to defend": those defend who have something to defend and who count — the hall with a stake; [040bs §7](040bs-paine.md) and [040ag](040ag-tilly.md).

## 5. What Parsons gives the protocol

A language in which the vote is a deposit and the promise a credit, and the inflation of power is a measurable quantity for which there was no instrument before the protocol. The AGIL scheme as a checklist: the rule closes all four functions without a new apparatus. And the most exact of the systemic blows — de-differentiation — whose answer shows that the rule does not merge the currencies but for the first time draws a guarded boundary between them.

## 6. Summary

| Thesis | What is true | Protocol | What remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| power is a currency | yes | the rate is declared, one-way | — |
| inflation of power | yes | measured by the share who took | — |
| differentiation | yes, as history | an open sluice instead of hidden ones | — |
| A, G | yes | a line for the citizen; goals set by a hall that counted | — |
| I (integration) | yes | the rule adopted by all; the departed chose himself, returns by date | the share returning "out of resentment" is unknown |
| L (latency) | a ritual without a congregation | a sacrifice with a sum and a date | — |
| roles | yes | rewritten | — |
| homeostasis | yes | feedback is continuous | — |
| de-differentiation | the main blow | not a merger but a boundary | — |

## Weak point

Parsons is paraphrased; his theory of power as a medium of exchange (1963) is far subtler than "the vote is a deposit", and in it power does not convert into money directly — the chapter takes his model as a language, not as a proof. The AGIL scheme is a classification, not a mechanism; "closes all four functions" is the author's claim, not Parsons's. The blow about the legitimacy of the departed remains partly open: the rule gives the departed a date and a reason to return, but does not guarantee that he returns.
