*How to read. Bannon is not a political scientist but a technologist: what interests him is not whether an idea is true but what it does to the hall. So this chapter differs from the rest of the arsenal: the five blows are not objections but moves, and the last of them is not an objection at all but a takeover. Bannon is the only figure in the arsenal who can take the protocol and use it, and the chapter is written with that in mind.*

## 1. "The elites are paying the people to shut up"

**The blow.** Bannon's whole populism is the mobilisation of "the forgotten man" to the polls against Davos. The protocol pays the same man for not coming. To Bannon this is the demobilisation of the base with its own money, and he will call it Davos's most refined scheme: before, they did not listen to you; now they will pay you not to speak.

**Answer.** Bannon's "forgotten man" today is the brought-in voter (1f §5): he does not decide, he serves as turnout for a campaign, and Bannon's campaign brought him in like any other (030b). The protocol gives him, for the first time, a choice that counts rather than a bus: to take or to stay is his decision, secret (048m art. 9.3), and both answers are counted (040ag item 2). And the base will say itself what it thinks of "the working-class movement": if it takes, the movement rested on the working class not needing a vote but needing someone to speak for it. The "you are being bought" register is handled in 040ab (the test "punishment or price"): they are bought today, with packages of benefits, without ceiling or receipt; the protocol sets a ceiling and a receipt.

**Residual.** The register is more dangerous than the logic: "you are being bought" is clearer to the brought-in than "you were given a choice". The defence is 1d and 1g, and it works only for the one who reads to the end.

## 2. "That is arithmetic, and we win by noise"

**The blow.** Bannon wins by volume: the zone is flooded, and the hall judges by what is louder, not by what is true. The protocol is a number, and a number does not shout.

**Answer.** This is not a blow but an admission, and it coincides with ours: the number D cannot be flooded, it is one and in plain view, and it will be asked of the boss at the meeting (059g §4). Noise wins where the hall judges by the speaker's face; under the protocol the middle judges, and it judges by substance (1f §6b). Bannon will see that his main weapon does not work against a number — and that is the one thing he will genuinely see first, because he is a technologist.

**Residual.** None — but see item 5: a technologist whose weapon does not work against an idea takes the idea.

## 3. "The producers are us, not your burned"

**The blow.** The real producer is the worker from Ohio in a red cap, the farmer, the trucker. "The burned" of 1f are coastal professionals whom Davos has already paid. The protocol gives weight to the wrong people.

**Answer.** 1f §3 counts by product, not by cap, and the Kansas farmer going broke on diesel (059i) is a burned one in the process of burning: he voted, saw the result and is leaving. The protocol does not ask who is for whom; it asks who will take the money. If Bannon's base are producers, they will not take it and will get weight ×2; if they take it, Bannon learns who his base was. Either way the protocol makes for him the measurement he never commissioned (1f §6b, why the burned were never studied).

**Residual.** None.

## 4. "You strengthen the system that must be torn down"

**The blow.** A Leninist needs a collapse, and the protocol makes the state more efficient and more stable (040ah §4, 040v §6). An efficient Davos state is worse than an inefficient one: it is harder to bring down.

**Answer.** The protocol tears down exactly what Bannon wanted torn down — the unelected apparatus — but not with a bulldozer: by depriving the apparatus of its buyer, bought turnout (040ag item 5; 040y — state funding of parties first under the knife). The difference is that after the demolition a working state remains, not rubble on which the Leninist builds his own. Bannon spent nine years demolishing the apparatus through Trump and got an apparatus that works for Trump; the protocol removes the apparatus's customer rather than changing it.

**Residual.** For one who needs the collapse itself this is no answer; but Bannon's "collapse" is a slogan, not a goal: his hall lives in this state and wants it to pay.

## 5. The takeover: he can steal it

**The move.** The most interesting thing is not an objection. The idea "50 % of AI equity to citizens" is the same gesture as D: take from concentration, give to everyone, a number per person. Bannon is a technologist, not an ideologue, and if he sees that the number works on his hall he will rename it "the patriot dividend" and float it as a trial balloon through a jester (059g §4) — he has the jester, the hall and a daily broadcast for it.

**Answer.** That is the mechanism, not a threat: the protocol does not care who introduces it (059g §4, the architect's refinement), and no force has a good move (1c). Having floated the number, Bannon cannot recall it: his hall will ask "is it true we are owed it?", and from there everything described for the jester works — the repository as a source outside the jester, the underdog who takes the content, the bosses in the matrix. And "a wing's face" is fuel, not risk (040aj §7, the architect's correction): the protocol uses the face while it exists and washes it off when it gets in the way. Bannon floating the protocol will do for the movement what he did for Trump in 2016 — and for the first time the result will not belong to him.

**Residual.** Bannon proved that a hall can be flooded; the protocol can prove the opposite only by referendum.
