## 1. Thomas Schelling (2005) — the threshold beyond which the system does not return

**Fourth in harm — Schelling, and the strongest of those left, because it is about dynamics, not shares (08.10.2026).** A system with positive feedback has two stable states and an unstable one between them. The repository holds that the apathetic leave first and the avalanche runs towards "the producers decide". But "I don't care" comes in two kinds — the apathetic man's indifference and the weariness of the moderate who has already lost to the machine and no longer believes. If the weary take the payment first rather than the indifferent, those who remain are the core plus the fringes, and the avalanche runs towards "the irreconcilable decide"; the state we need may turn out to be a saddle between two stable ones. Schelling would not say "it will not work" — he would say "you do not know on which side the threshold lies, and it can be learned only in motion". What stands against it: the weary moderate is the burned man (1f §6), and he has two reasons to return, not to leave — his vote weighs more for the first time, and for leaving he is paid as much as the apathetic man, which is little for him and much for the apathetic; selection is by whom the payment is worth more than the vote, not by who is weary, and weariness does not make the payment worth more. The fringes do not grow — they always vote; an avalanche towards the fringes needs the moderates to leave, and a moderate gains by staying when his weight grows: the feedback works for us — the nearer the threshold, the dearer leaving is to one who cares. And the counter is visible before the election: the motion is seen before it has become an avalanche, and the moderate sees that he is awaited, not that he is alone. The residual is Schelling's own: the side of the threshold cannot be derived from a poll. Hence the requirement already passed to 048l: a small percentage in the first round and observation of who leaves first; if the indifferent leave — the threshold is on our side; if the weary — the percentage is too large for the moderate, and that is tuned, not refuted. *The architect's correction, simpler and stronger than the analysis above because it measures from the present system, not from an ideal:* "if the moderates leave first — why would he leave? The apathetic have already left and do not vote now — what is there to prove? The moderates did not leave even for free, without the amplified vote. Weary of what? Of working much and earning little — otherwise they are the apathetic; and those who are weary need the protocol most of all". Schelling describes an avalanche from a state where everyone is inside — and we are not there: two-thirds at home, the apathetic already outside, only uncounted and unpaid, with the moderate's vote weighing little. The moderate did not leave for free with a light vote — why would he leave with a heavy one, for the same payment that is large to the apathetic and small to him? The "weary" are those with a stake in taxes, given for the first time a way to present it. The only motion the rule adds is inward: the return of the burned; the exit has already happened without the protocol, the protocol counts and pays it. There is no saddle between two states, because one of them is today's. The residual shrinks to one thing: which of today's voters take the payment — and by the same logic the answer is those brought in, who have no stake but a boss — not a risk but the aim. What to watch in the pilot is not "which side of the threshold" but "which of the present voters take", with the expectation — the brought-in.

The closest of the absent. His model of residential sorting (1971): each tolerates neighbours up to his own threshold, and once a small share leaves, leaving becomes an avalanche — the city divides without anyone wanting it. The protocol is the same mechanics with the opposite sign: each who leaves increases the weight of those who stay, each who returns raises the price of the next exit for the authorities, and the feedback has a threshold (1f §7; 013f, the second theorem). Schelling would be the first to say that the threshold cannot be computed from a poll — it is visible only in motion, on the counter — and that a system with a threshold has two stable states, not one. His second thing is commitment as strength: the one who has burned his bridges is stronger than the one who can retreat. Choice B is final within one election (048m Art. 4) — a burned bridge in his sense: the voter who took the payment cannot be "pressed" on polling day, and the machine has nobody to come to. *He would object:* the threshold cuts both ways — if the first to leave are not the apathetic but the moderates, the avalanche goes the wrong way; hence his demand for a pilot with a small percentage, to see on which side the threshold lies.

## 2. William Nordhaus (2018) — the political business cycle

**Sixth — Nordhaus, examined (08.10.2026).** His real objection is deeper than "short memory": the cycle exists because the voter rewards the pre-election heating, and under the protocol those with a stake remain — and the stake is exactly what is heated: the contract, the indexation, the subsidy. You did not remove the cycle — you kept at the polls those for whom it is staged. The answer in three steps. The heating is a payment not to all and not by rule: targeted, one-off, a month before polling day; the protocol places beside it a payment to all, by law and without a date, and the heating loses not its existence but its exclusivity — the authorities are no longer the only ones paying before an election, and "care" stops differing from the rule. The cycle feeds on the reckoning coming after the election, unseen; the counter and the budget line make the price visible before — a voter with a short memory but a long bill before his eyes, which Nordhaus's model did not have. And those staying with a stake in the budget are balanced by those staying with a stake in taxes — the same counterweight as with Tirole. The residual: heating before the referendum on the rule itself — a one-off payment "so that it need not be by law"; that is the fork of 056f §13n: to pay is to confirm that the vote is worth money. Nordhaus's cycle for the first time works against the one who starts it. *The architect's answer, shorter and stronger than the three steps above (08.10.2026):* "and why would they want the heating at their own expense — they pay the taxes? And why would they want a reckoning after the election — they are for logic and stability, they never need a crisis and a reckoning". That strikes the condition of the model's existence, not its consequences: the political business cycle exists only where the rewarded voter and the taxpayer who settles the bill are different people; the heating pays for the one who receives and does not pay. Under the protocol those who pay remain at the polls, and the heating is for them a bill to themselves with a delay; they need neither heating nor crisis — they need stability, because they will be the ones paying. The cycle is not weakened — it loses the voter for whom it was staged.

The prize was for climate, but the junction with the protocol is his paper of 1975, "The Political Business Cycle": a government heats the economy before an election and pays after it, because the voter remembers the last year. Ten thousand roubles to pensioners before the Duma election of 2021, a thirteenth pension before the Hungarian election of 2022, grants to rural pensioners (1f §2, item 5) — the Nordhaus cycle in pure form, which the repository described without naming it. The protocol does two things to this cycle: it strips the pre-election payment of its monopoly — it becomes lawful, permanent and equal, and the authorities can no longer sell as care what the rule gives to all (056f §13n); and it moves the payment from the eve of the election to a rule that does not depend on the date. *He would object:* a voter with a short horizon remains one under the protocol — the payment does not lengthen memory. The answer: the protocol does not promise to lengthen it — it takes short memory out of the deciding vote for money, it does not re-educate.

## 3. Gunnar Myrdal (1974) — circular cumulative causation

**Seventh — Myrdal, the shortest (08.10.2026).** "I do not believe in a market incentive" — but his circle is opened not by the incentive but by a change in who decides; the incentive is only the door through which the composition changes, and that is exactly his "push": external to the circle, internal to the system. His "soft state", not enforcing its own laws, is the apparatus of §88.9; the protocol is the one law the apparatus cannot fail to enforce, because non-enforcement shows in the precinct balance (048m Art. 10). Myrdal the planner would object; Myrdal the diagnostician would recognise his circle.

His mechanism is the "vicious circle": poverty creates the conditions that reproduce poverty, and without an external push the circle does not open. The closed cycle of 1f §5 — the vote, income and inheritance in one generation of the core, with no point opened by voting because those who vote are inside — is Myrdal's cumulative causation in politics. He would recognise it at once and ask where the push is. The protocol's answer: the push is not external but internal — the payment changes the point at which a person decides to take part, and the circle opens on the side where it held, where the producers did not vote. His "soft state" — an apparatus that does not enforce its own laws — is §88.9 thirty years later. *He would object:* he favoured planning from above and would not believe that the circle opens by a market incentive; the same dispute as with Goldscheid (040o): take the diagnosis, not the prescription.

## 4. Paul Samuelson (1970) — revealed preference and the free rider

**Second in harm — Samuelson, and the harm is concrete (08.10.2026).** The free rider not at the referendum (that is examined in 048n) but in its campaign: once the apathetic leave, every challenger gains at once, and by his theorem nobody pays first — each waits for another to pay for the signatures and the ads. That explains question 3 of 048n ("why did nobody do it before") more exactly than "nobody thought of it": it does not pay to be first. *The architect's answer:* "the burned will pay for everything: they already pay the most into the budget, and to pay for being given an amplified vote they are very willing". That removes the free rider with his own instrument: a public good goes unpaid when the benefit is spread equally — but here the burned man has a private benefit, the weight of his own vote for ever; he pays for his own asset, as a port builds a lighthouse for its own ships. Three things make him the best payer: he already pays the most — those who give 70 % of the labour (1f §3) give most of the taxes, and the budget of the payment is theirs anyway; the return is plainer than any donation to a candidate — a candidate's donor buys a probability for one term, a referendum's donor buys the weight of his own vote for every term, and at 28–35 thousand euros a round (048n, question 6) that is the price of one dinner for people with six-figure taxes; and the group is small and gathered — a concentrated benefit among few gets paid for, a diffuse one among all does not (Olson). The boundary: willingness to pay is a thesis until the first donor, but it is tested by one letter, not by a pilot. And the scale, in the architect's words: "compared with the taxes their populists have passed, this will be a trifling sum" — the burned man pays not for a good but for the right to stop paying so much.

Two junctions, both terms the repository already speaks in without naming the author. "Revealed preference" (1938): preferences are judged not by words but by choice; the protocol is revealed preference in pure form, choice A or B instead of a survey (one of the architect's settled positions: selection by revealed preference, not by declaration). "The Pure Theory of Public Expenditure" (1954): nobody pays voluntarily for a public good because everyone uses it — the free rider. The referendum on the rule is his case (048n, "The free rider at the referendum"): the rule will be adopted without you. The repository's answer — no quorum, and people come to votes with a personal gain — Samuelson would accept as evidence, not as theory: his theorem says that without compulsion the good is underfunded, not that nobody comes. *He would object:* the payment itself is a public good for the machine — when the apathetic leave, every challenger gains, and none wants to pay for the referendum campaign alone. That is question 6 in 048n.

## 5. Jean Tirole (2014) — pandering to the voter

**Third in harm — Tirole (08.10.2026).** The objection is pointed and strikes our own argument "the protocol fixes the principal": a stake is not information. Those remain to whom the outcome is worth more than the payment, but "worth more" is about stake, not knowledge: the union voter — up to the contract, the budget employee — up to the indexation, the subsidy recipient — up to the next subsidy. Tirole would say: you removed those to whom pandering was useless and kept those to whom pandering pays — pandering does not vanish, it concentrates, and a politician answerable to fewer people with clearer bills may be worse than one answerable to many with blurred ones. The answer is by comparison with the present system, as §5d of 040m requires. Today the politician panders to two addressees: those brought in (by a stake up to the contract) and the core (by familiarity); the burned are silent, and their stake — tax, rent, the city's debt — takes no part in the pandering at all: concentration is already at its maximum — those with a bill in the budget decide, with two-thirds at home. Under the protocol there are three addressees, and the third is new: the returned burned man with a stake of the opposite sign; pandering to recipients becomes visibly dearer, because the payment stands in the budget as a line beside the contract (040o). Concentration does not grow — for the first time it gets a counterweight. The residual is honest: the proportion of "stake in the budget" against "stake in taxes" among those who stay is a pre-regime number; the sign of the shift is clear — today there is no counterweight, under the rule one appears.

The prize was for regulation, but the junction is Maskin and Tirole's "The Politician and the Judge" (2004): electoral accountability makes a politician pander to the voter's opinion even when he knows the voter is wrong; an unelected judge is free of this but also unanswerable. Their conclusion: accountability is useful where the voter is informed and harmful where he is not. The protocol answers not by choosing between elected and appointed but by changing whom one panders to: when the apathetic man takes the payment, those left to pander to are those with a stake who look beyond the eve. Tirole would be the first to notice that the protocol regulates the principal, not the agent: every reform fixes the politician, the protocol fixes the one he answers to. *He would object:* a stake is not information; the union voter's stake is high and his horizon runs to the contract (040m §5i). True, and already recorded: selection by stake, not by knowledge.

## 6. James Mirrlees (1996) — screening by a choice that cannot be faked

**Fifth in harm — Mirrlees, and he is closed by the charter (08.10.2026).** His condition: self-selection holds only on irreversibility — "took the payment and came anyway" must be impossible, not forbidden. Checked against 048m: this is already built as accounting, not as prohibition. Article 5 part 3: the vote of one who chose B is extinguished, a ballot on his record is a double spend, and the precinct commission answers for it, not the citizen. Article 10 part 2: ballots at a precinct cannot exceed the register entries less those who chose B; part 3: the precinct balance — entries = votes + payments + those who chose neither, and a precinct without balance is a precinct without a result; parts 4–5: two channels, open with a token and secret on paper, with a discrepancy threshold written in advance and a repeat vote if exceeded. So "took and came" is not punished — it does not add up: the extra ballot shows in the balance as extra, and nobody gains from it, because a precinct with an extra ballot loses its result. Mirrlees would accept this as a mechanism, not a promise. The honest residual is one, and it is about time: choice B is made in the window before polling day, and the extinguished entry must reach the precinct before a ballot is issued; where the roll is not updated, the double spend is found afterwards at reconciliation, and the sanction — no result for the precinct — falls on all its voters, not on one. That restrains the commission (losing a precinct does it no good), but it is restraint after the fact; enough for a first pilot, and for a city a question of how fast a B entry reaches the paper roll — a setting (the annex), not a rule.

Optimal taxation under asymmetric information: the state does not see ability, but it can offer a menu from which people choose and thereby reveal themselves. The protocol is a menu of two lines, A or B, and the choice reveals what no register shows: how much a person values his vote. That is Mirrlees self-selection, and it is the only way to separate the one who does not care from the one who does — without interrogation and without an official. He would say the design is right and ask his own question: at what price does the menu separate, and at what price not. That is the question of the percentage (048k, which percentage and its cost), and its answer is a pilot with different sums (048l). *He would object:* self-selection works only if the choice cannot be reversed — otherwise everyone takes B and then "changes his mind". That is Article 4: the choice is final within one election.

## 7. Robert Lucas (1995) — the critique that strikes our forecasts

**One at a time, from the most harmful — Lucas first (08.10.2026).** What he strikes in ours: the share of takers (every reference number comes from a world where taking is shameful or impossible; under the rule the norm shifts in the first cycle, either way); the return of the burned (Scotland and Brexit are stake without payment, and under the rule the machine makes a second move — pays the apathetic man more than the budget so that he does not take and comes, the fork of 1c); the behaviour of those who stay (the new deciding minority re-optimises and grows a rent of its own); any "+N points"; and the first cycle ≠ the second — the share is not a constant, everyone already knows what happened. What stands: the arithmetic of share (an identity, not a forecast), the charter, the counter (his own remedy — observation under the new regime) and the forks of 1c entire, because they are built the Lucas way — from incentives under the rule, not from old turnouts. What he strikes in the opponents — symmetrically: "the radicals remain", "the apathetic do not mobilise", "turnout falls to 15 %" — all computed from the world without the payment.

*The architect's answer — with Lucas's own remedy.* "The protocol invented nothing: money relations are thousands of years old, elections thousands of years old; one need only lay one over the other; there are hundreds of analogues alike in parts; one may not know how a complex mechanism works yet know how each of its elements works. Nobody has seen the Earth's core — it is proved by indirect evidence and mathematics." That is Lucas's prescription from the same paper: build not on historical correlations but on deep parameters — preferences that do not change when the rule changes. The protocol's elements are such parameters, each with thousands of years of observation under every regime: a person takes money for not doing what he does not value (compensation, buyouts, the Alaska dividend); a stake raises turnout (the four cases above); an irreversible choice reveals the true price (deposits, collateral, Mirrlees's menu); a visible counter coordinates those who have not conspired (the exchange, Aumann); a budget obligation is kept within solvency (pensions, bonds); the one who pays watches what he pays for (the taxpayer, the shareholder, 040o). None is invented and none changes because they were put together; chapter 006 lists the same elements one by one over two and a half thousand years. What remains to Lucas is one thing, and an honest one: interaction. Six stable elements together may produce behaviour none shows alone — a norm "taking is normal" that shifts the share. That is not a refutation but a residual with a gauge: the pilot is the seismic wave, the counter the seismograph; two rounds, and one sees whether there is interaction and of what sign. Added to 048l: measure the change between rounds, not the level.

The most useful opponent. The Lucas critique (1976): the consequences of a new policy cannot be predicted from data gathered under the old one, because people change behaviour when the rule changes. That is a direct objection to everything the repository computes from old turnouts: the share who take the payment, the return of the burned, the transfer of votes — all estimated in a world without the payment. The honest answer: the repository knows this and therefore labels every number (040m §5d) and demands a pilot, not a model. But Lucas would give a second point, in our favour: the critique applies to the opponents too — "under the protocol the radicals remain" is also computed from the old world, where the moderate had no reason to return. His rule cuts both sides equally, and the only way out is observation under the new rule. *He would object:* nothing beyond this; he would simply believe no percentage, including ours — and be right.

## 8. Card, Angrist, Imbens (2021) — how to measure the pilot

**Eighth — Card, Angrist, Imbens: not an objection but a specification (08.10.2026).** A discontinuity at the referendum threshold is the best design but needs many referendums, and the first pilot has none. Difference in differences — two courtyards, with and without the payment, turnout and composition before and after — is already in 048l. What they would add: randomise the percentage between courtyards (one and two), not only "with/without", to see a slope rather than a point; and measure after a round, not at once — because of Lucas. Passed to 048l.

The prize was for natural experiments: how to reach a causal conclusion where a laboratory experiment is impossible. For the protocol they are neither allies nor critics but the manual for 048l. A discontinuity at the referendum threshold (one city adopted at 51 %, the neighbour rejected at 49 % — two nearly identical jurisdictions, one with the rule); difference in differences (turnout and composition before and after, in the adopting and the non-adopting); an instrument (the random date of the first cycle). Without this the pilot yields a number any opponent will call a coincidence, and he will be right. Angrist would ask one thing: where is the control group. If the courtyard pilot (048l) is run in two courtyards, one without the payment, there is an answer; if in one — there is not.

## 9. Mokyr, Aghion, Howitt (2025) — creative destruction and those who block it

**Ninth — Mokyr, Aghion, Howitt (08.10.2026).** The honest objection: creative destruction in the economy is paid for by growth everyone sees; in politics "growth" is better government, seen years later, while the Luddites lose their trade tomorrow — an asymmetry in time, the same as Dalio's. The answer: the protocol has what the machine tool does not — a dividend on polling day, seen by all and at once; the payment is growth presented in advance. And it is the only case in which the trade being destroyed is paid for by those it exploited.

This year's prize — for growth through creative destruction and for what blocks it: incumbents who profit from nothing changing, and institutions that let them. The Luddites of politics (039 §88.9) are their case word for word: the trade of bringing in the apathetic, which disappears, and the apparatus that will do the sums first. Aghion and Howitt showed that growth needs institutions under which the loser from novelty cannot forbid it; the protocol is such an institution for politics: the incumbent cannot forbid the referendum without saying aloud why (the fork, 1c). Mokyr would add culture: novelty is accepted where it is not held to threaten order — and that is the question of the first jurisdictions (040m §5d, "the sick first"). *They would object:* creative destruction in the economy is paid for by growth, and what pays for it in politics is a thesis (019), not a theorem.

## 10. Claudia Goldin (2023) — two-thirds of the core

**Tenth — Goldin (08.10.2026).** Her real thought is finer than "a filter by sex": the aunt of the core is not apathetic, she always votes; she will take the payment not from indifference but because for her it is money she does not have — so the filter by horizon is for her a filter by poverty. Hence the requirement for data by sex and income together, passed to 048l. The answer on the merits: if the aunt takes it, she receives for her vote for the first time what her boss used to receive for it; if she does not, her vote weighs more than yesterday. For her the protocol wins both ways, and she is the only group of whom that can be said without reservation.

**Goldin — examined more deeply at the architect's request (08.10.2026).** She has not one junction with the protocol but six, and one overturns an expectation.

1. *The aunt of the core is her heroine at the end of a career.* The cohort that entered work in the seventies and settled in the school, the clinic, the registry. Goldin explained it by "greedy jobs" (*Career and Family*, 2021): the highly paid positions demand unpredictable hours, couples specialise — he in the greedy job, she in the predictable one — and a budget job is the most predictable of all: fixed hours, no rush jobs, low pay. Seventy percent women in the civil service (039 §88.9) is not "the apathetic on low pay" but a bargain: pay in exchange for predictability, which only the state offers. The vote is a rent the authorities take off that bargain from above: they pay with predictability for the work and take the turnout for nothing. That is more exact than "the clerk as parasite", and kinder to the aunt. *The architect's correction (08.10.2026):* "loyalty is built into this job — otherwise they do not hire; loyalty is checked at the interview, and the aunts hire their own kind". Three consequences. The first, with the architect's refinement: "weakness and dependence on the wage — that is the incentive; turnout is only the consequence". Not the loyal are hired but the dependent — those with nowhere to go: no second trade, a mortgage, children, a town with one employer; the dependent are governable without orders, and turnout is produced by dependence itself, like a shadow — it need not be demanded, the selection was made at entry, years before. They hire their own kind not from solidarity but because a dependent boss chooses a dependent subordinate — one who is not dangerous. That is Hirschman in pure form (036c): where there is no exit, loyalty remains, and it costs the employer nothing; and it is Robinson and Verdier (039 §88.9) with a mechanism. The apparatus reproduces itself by hiring, not only by voting: the closed cycle of 1f §5 gains a fourth point — personnel, the clerk chooses the next clerk in her own image; and it is not a Russian peculiarity — Chicago has the document, the Shakman decree (1972–2014), forty-two years of federal court supervision of City Hall hiring, imposed because the city hired by political loyalty and lifted when the court found the city substantially reformed. And what the protocol does with it — the mechanism for the efficiency thesis (019) that was missing: choice B is not published by name and the secret channel is open to everyone, so the protocol does not remove dependence — the wage stays as it was — but gives the dependent woman an exit she need not declare: take the payment in secret and keep the job, the first exit of her life for which nobody is fired; dependence remains, but stops converting into turnout, because the shadow is no longer visible; the criterion "one of ours" loses its price, and the boss for the first time has a reason to hire for the work — not because he became better, but because loyalty no longer converts. The boundary: in the first cycle nothing changes — the hired are hired; a change in the hiring criterion is the work of a generation of staff, a thesis about consequences, not a rule.
2. *The filter by horizon keeps more women, not fewer.* The literature on the motherhood penalty she began shows that women invest more in children and grandchildren — in time, money and choice of job. The horizon "I think of my grandson on the service register" (1f §7) is statistically more often female; the "wise elder" the protocol keeps is more often a grandmother than a grandfather. Her own frame predicts the opposite of what was feared: the filter by horizon is pro-female. *The architect's correction (08.10.2026), closing the first and second junctions into one mechanism — the state as husband:* "the authorities exploit the heightened conservatism of older women without a husband, replacing the husband and providing subsistence in exchange for modest help, but above all for loyalty; that is exactly why these posts run without KPIs and on low pay". Demography hands the authorities a widow: the gap in life expectancy in Russia is about ten years, and among women over seventy most are widows 🟡; the husband who was both provider and the one decisions were made with disappears — and his place is taken by the only one who offers the same: subsistence and a place. The authorities do not win her loyalty, they inherit it. The bargain is not work for money but subsistence for loyalty: modest help is what she gives formally, loyalty is what is bought; hence a KPI on these posts is impossible in principle — it would measure the work, and the product is not the work, and a KPI once introduced would show the post is not needed; and the pay is low not for lack of budget but because there is one buyer — a monopsony over the dependent: nowhere to go, the price does not rise. The heightened conservatism is not of age but of situation: for one whose husband has been replaced, "let nothing change" means "let this one not vanish too". The protocol strikes exactly here, without taking the "husband" away: for the first time it pays her for her vote, not for her loyalty, and pays in secret — the state-husband will not learn that she took it and cannot punish. It is the one tie in her life the rule can cut without wrecking the life itself (1f §2, items 3 and 5). *The architect's second correction (08.10.2026):* "women invest more in children — true, but they are many times more fearful, and any horizon collapses under fear; men are more fearless, and that gives them a longer horizon; women have a very long horizon when there is a reliable husband — but precisely those the authorities dislike, and they destroy families". Right in two points of three. Fear collapses the horizon — data: women on average are markedly more risk-averse (Croson and Gneezy, 2009; SOEP), and scarcity and threat shorten anyone's horizon (Mullainathan and Shafir, *Scarcity*); hence the formula that reconciles this with Goldin: horizon = capacity to invest × security — investment in grandchildren is a long aim, and fear makes one pursue it with short moves, "let this one not vanish too". "Men are more fearless — hence a longer horizon" — a correction: fearlessness gives not a horizon but the capacity to act on one, and by itself gives the opposite too — impulsiveness, the third state of 011b, which the protocol removes alongside apathy; more exactly: a man's horizon depends less on security, a woman's more, so a reliable husband lengthens her horizon more than anything lengthens his. "The authorities destroy families" — as intention unprovable, as benefit and connivance true and with mechanisms: a family with a reliable husband is the only one in which a woman has a long horizon without the state, that is, the only one that does not need the state-husband; the authorities need not destroy it deliberately — it is enough not to hinder what destroys it, because the result pays: mobilisation, prison, alcohol mortality, an alimony regime that makes divorce pay better than marriage (019f, 027 — "the loop"); each explicable alone, together they produce the widow and the single woman who depend on the state. Intention is a thesis; benefit is a fact; paying her in secret and past the "husband" is the rule.
3. *Time poverty among producing women of 30–45.* A vote costs an evening, and a working mother has fewest of them; she is burned not by disappointment but by lack of time (1f §6). The payment for the first time pays her for what exists anyway — non-participation; if she takes it the protocol loses nothing: she has revealed that the election is not worth her evening and been paid for it, and whoever wants her back should make the election worth an evening. Nordhaus's bill, presented through time.
4. *A flat payment under a pay gap.* D is the same for all, women's incomes are below the median — relative to her income the payment is larger; the protocol redistributes slightly towards women without naming them: the only payment in the budget tied neither to seniority, nor to rank, nor to the greediness of a job.
5. *Her real objection — coercion of the dependent.* The most coercible is the dependent employee: a boss can demand that she not take the payment and come. Choice B is not published by name (048m Art. 9 part 3), and the boss cannot check it; but the open channel with a token (Art. 10 part 4) creates an object that can be demanded as proof of turnout — a gap, and a gendered one. The charter's answer: the secret paper channel is open to everyone and voting through it is a right; demanding a token is coercion under Art. 11 (048h). But the token is a new object that did not exist yesterday. Goldin would require the pilot to count not only by sex and income but by dependence on an employer: the budget employee against the self-employed. Passed to 048l.
6. *What the payment does not do.* Her "quiet revolution" came from a change in expectations — education, contraception — not from money. The payment changes no expectations: the apathetic woman stays apathetic. True, and the protocol does not promise it — it does not change the woman, it counts her, and for the first time gives the aunt a choice she has not had in forty years.

Her subject is women in the economy: careers, pay, the "quiet revolution". One junction with the protocol, but a direct one: the core electorate is two-thirds a woman of 55–75 on a budget wage or a pension (1f §2), and the registry the authorities keep for turnout is 60–70 % female (039 §88.9). Goldin would ask whether the protocol's filter falls more heavily on women — and the answer must be given before she does: the filter is by stake and horizon, not by sex; the aunt with a grandson on the service register stays (1f §7); and that the budget pays her twice — a wage and turnout — is Goldin's own theme of invisible unpaid work, carried over to the vote. *She would object:* there are no data on who takes the payment by sex, and the pilot must count it separately. True; added to the requirements for the pilot.

## 11. In one line — three

- **Robert Aumann (2005), correlated equilibrium.** A public signal everyone sees and can tune to yields an equilibrium unreachable without communication between players. The counter before the election is such a signal: the burned see that they are enough, without conspiring.
- **Paul Milgrom (2020), influence costs.** In an organisation resources go not to the work but to influencing the deciders; the machine, GOTV and consultants carry the same influence costs, and $15–16 billion a cycle (039 §88.9) is their size.
- **Oliver Williamson (2009), the hostage as guarantee.** A deal holds when each side has something to lose; the payment that cannot be cut (048m Art. 8 part 5) is a hostage the authorities give the citizen, and Dalio (037 §86.10) called it exactly that.

## 13. Weak point

The fifth circle is a reconstruction from their work; none of the ten wrote about the protocol, and the closest — Schelling, Nordhaus, Samuelson, Myrdal — cannot reply. Three requirements from the circle pass into the list for the pilot (048l): a small percentage and observation of which side the threshold lies on (Schelling); a control group (Angrist); counting by sex (Goldin). And one rule — Lucas's — stands over the whole repository: no number computed from the world without the payment is a number about the world with it.

*The circle's result (08.10.2026).* Ten objections derived from the laureates' theories are closed logically — each removed by an argument or turned into a measurement. An argument against a theory is tested by logic, not by its author's consent: 2×2=4 convinces Archimedes though he died long ago. What remains is measurement only, and it is in 048l. Whoever wants an outside check — a cold model in the role of a scholar (040m §5g), or a letter to the living asking whether the reading was right.
