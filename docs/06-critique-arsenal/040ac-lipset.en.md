*How to read. Lipset is the most direct opponent of the protocol among all recorded: he argued that low turnout of the uninformed is good for stability, and rested on Weimar. The protocol does the same — it removes the uninformed from the decision — but for money and visibly. One question to Lipset: did the fuse he described ever work?*

## 1. Apathy as a stabiliser

**The argument.** In *Political Man* (ch. 6) Lipset rests on Weimar: turnout rose from 75 % in 1928 to 88 % in March 1933, the increase came from those who had not voted before, and they went to the NSDAP. Conclusion: the non-voting uninformed voter is not a defect but a fuse; high turnout in a crisis is the sign that the fuse has blown. The consequence for us: a system that *touches* the apathetic removes the fuse.

**The strike.** The protocol pays the apathetic — it addresses him, gives him for the first time a reason to think about the election; you are waking the one whom for half a century you successfully did not wake. Worse: a sum desirable to 40–50 % of the population is the loudest campaign in history, addressed exactly to those Lipset asked to leave alone.

**The answer.** Lipset saw the same figure as the repository — the apathetic with a short horizon — but described half of its behaviour. The apathetic does not "sleep": he is *brought*. The Weimar rise in turnout was not an awakening but a delivery: fear, the street, the employer, a promise. Lipset called it an awakening because in 1960 he did not distinguish the one who came by himself from the one who was brought; for the repository these are two states (1f §2, §5). It follows that his fuse never worked: the apathetic did not protect the system, he lay in reserve, and the reserve was taken by whoever came for it first with enough fear. Weimar was not the failure of a fuse but the first large withdrawal of the reserve.

The protocol is the only device that closes this reserve. The one who took the payment does not vote at this election (048m Art. 5 parts 2–3), he cannot be brought, and he makes the choice in advance, at home, without the street. Lipset wanted the apathetic not to vote; the protocol gives the same — with a lock Lipset did not have, and at a price the apathetic set himself. To wake and to bring are different verbs: the protocol wakes for one decision (to take or not) and thereby makes the second (to bring) impossible.

**The critic's continuation and the architect's move.** The critic will go on: under the protocol an extremist candidate will tell the apathetic "do not take the payment, come for me" — and with enough fear the price will not hold, as in 1933. The architect's answer: "with enough fear — that is already pure violence, and there nothing from politics works; the bounty trap again. And if the fear is not strong — they will take the money. Money is always more reliable when one is afraid." This closes the residual entirely. Fear at which a price does not hold is not campaigning but coercion; against coercion no electoral mechanism works, the present one included (048m Art. 11 — coercion is criminal), and to demand it of the protocol is to compare with an ideal instead of with the present. And in the range where fear is still politics, money is more reliable than any promise: to the frightened a bird in the hand is worth more, and the more frightening, the more it is worth. The protocol does not "fail to hold under strong fear" — it works across the whole range where anything works at all, and works better as fear grows, until fear stops being politics. It is the same threshold as Krastev's (040w item 3): hysteria is for sale, violence is outside the field. And the one who came for the extremist is visible under the protocol on election day by the precinct balance (Art. 10), not in hindsight by turnout, as with Lipset.

**Residual.** None.

## 2. Working-class authoritarianism

**The argument** (ch. 4). From the surveys of the 1950s: low income, short schooling and isolation from complex information produce intolerance of ambiguity, a pull towards simple answers and a strong hand; extremism of left and right feeds on one stratum. The correlation has held — from Weimar to 2016.

**The strike.** The protocol selects by horizon, and Lipset's short horizon is the same stratum: the poor, the little-educated. They are exactly the ones who will take the payment; so this is "aristocracy" (040x item 1) and it does not cure — the authoritarian voter leaves with the money rather than being corrected, and will come back the same when he is paid for turnout with fear.

**The answer.** A coincidence of diagnosis: Lipset described the short horizon as the source of authoritarianism sixty years before the repository, and that confirms that the horizon filter cuts exactly what he called dangerous. The protocol does not promise to correct a person (040u item 3 — it produces no enlightenment); it removes the vote from the decision at a price the person set himself, and Lipset wanted exactly that — for free and without a lock (item 1). The main point: Lipset's authoritarian voter *votes* today — he is brought, because he is free; the protocol for the first time makes him expensive for the one who brings him and cheap for himself. "Will come back when paid with fear" — item 1, fear-as-politics versus fear-as-violence, closed.

**Residual.** Ours, not Lipset's: if the payment is taken out of poverty rather than horizon — the income-selection threshold (040p §1), which only the pilot will measure. Lipset's stratum is poor *and* short of horizon at once; only a question in the pilot can separate the two reasons for exit (048l: by income and by dependence on the employer).

## 3. Modernisation: income, education, urbanisation → democracy

**The argument** ("Some Social Requisites of Democracy", 1959). The richer the country, the more likely a stable democracy; the base of democracy is the middle class, literacy, cities.

**The strike.** The protocol will not work in poor jurisdictions — there is no base there.

**The answer.** Przeworski refined Lipset (040v item 5): income does not create democracy but keeps it — and after 2016 it did not keep it with full wallets. "The sick first" (040m §5d) is not because the base is there but because it is cheaper to test and there is less to lose. And the protocol does not need Lipset's base: it needs not a middle class as a share of the population but the 40–50 % for whom the sum is desirable — a condition on the payment, not on the country.

## 4. Cross-pressures as the source of moderation

**The argument.** A person belonging to several groups with different interests votes more moderately; the isolated, with one belonging, vote radically. Moderation is the product of multiple ties.

**The strike.** The protocol amplifies the isolated: the connected leave, the homogeneous stay.

**The answer.** The reverse. The isolated apathetic with one tie — the budget, the boss, the benefit — takes the payment first: he has nothing to lose elsewhere (036e, Goldin — dependence as the incentive; 040s item 4 — bonding against bridging). The one who stays has many ties, because he has something to lose in several places at once — and that is exactly Lipset's cross-pressured voter. The protocol does not select the homogeneous, it selects the multiply connected; Lipset looked for moderation in the structure of ties — the protocol finds it by price.

## 5. Legitimacy and effectiveness — two axes

**The argument.** A system lives while at least one is high; the crisis comes when both fall. Legitimacy is the belief that the institutions are appropriate; effectiveness, that they deliver results.

**The strike.** The protocol strikes legitimacy: "votes were bought", the belief in appropriateness falls.

**The answer.** The protocol gives legitimacy what Lipset lacked — a number: the share who decided rather than the share who came (040t item 5), and everyone sees it on the counter. And effectiveness — through a long horizon in government (040w item 5). Lipset measured both axes indirectly, by surveys; the counter measures the first directly, and "votes were bought" becomes checkable: there are no bought votes, there is paid non-participation, and it is counted separately (048m Art. 10).

## Weak point

A reconstruction from a 1960 book; Lipset did not write on payment for non-turnout. The distinction "came by himself / was brought" is ours, Lipset does not have it, and a critic may ask whether it is measurable: the repository answers with the precinct balance (048m Art. 10), but that is the charter, not data on Weimar. And the boundary "fear as politics / fear as violence" is a threshold without a number: where it runs at a particular election the protocol does not predict and need not.
