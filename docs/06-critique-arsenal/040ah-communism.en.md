# 40ah. Communism and the Protocol: How Support Is Won — a Hundred Years Without Logic or Morality Against a Rule With a Number

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites
**File:** 06_040ah · v1 · 12 October 2026
**Source:** the architect's question of 12 October 2026: "communism is a far more complex, illogical idea, inconsistent with morality, yet supported by the masses; it raged across the planet for almost a hundred years and survives in parts; let us compare it with the protocol by ideology and support". The architect's three corrections to the first answer are recorded as moves: a party with a lifespan, meaning for the soul, the carrier.

---

*How to read. The comparison is honest only if one first says what communism won with — because it did not win with logic, and that is the main lesson for a rule that rests on logic. The question is not "which is better" but "through which channels does mass support come, and which of them does the protocol have".*

## 1. The channels of support

| Channel | Communism | The protocol |
|---|---|---|
| slogan | simple despite a complex theory: "land to the peasants, factories to the workers, peace to the peoples"; nobody read *Capital* | simpler still — a number: "$840 into your account, or a ×3 vote"; the theory (the repository) need not be read, the price is understood without it |
| enemy | yes, and moral: the bourgeois, the kulak, the imperialist; energy from hatred (040ab, Mudde) | no enemy by construction: the protocol is a cure for enmity, it changes who decides and pays the dissenters |
| promise | a bright future, unverifiable in one's lifetime; verification deferred for generations | a future with a price in the present: every election the counter shows whether it works (040w item 5) |
| personal interest | immediate and material: redistribution — land, a flat, a post; the majority had something to take from the minority | immediate and material: money personally, from the budget, not from a neighbour; nobody loses anything |
| carrier | a party of professional revolutionaries (Lenin), then an apparatus that outlived the idea by half a century | now — "the burned", who pay themselves (059g); after the announcement — a single-issue party with a lifespan (§3) |
| way of taking power | coup and civil war (Russia), the Red Army (Eastern Europe), guerrilla war (China, Vietnam, Cuba); no country became communist by a free vote | referendum only; cannot be imposed by force by definition (048m Art. 15) |
| result at free elections | the Constituent Assembly of 1917 — Bolsheviks 24 %; Czechoslovakia 1946 — 38 %, the record; Italy and France — 20–30 %; a third at the peak | referendum forecast 55–60 % (059g); measurement — the pilot |
| what held it | a monopoly on information, coercion and a "core" that was brought: the party meeting, the quota, fear | self-enforcement (040v §6): breaking the rule gathers everyone against the breaker; nobody is brought — the counter |
| how it ended | when the promise stopped matching the refrigerator and that could no longer be hidden; it died as a zombie for decades | repealed by the same referendum; a zombie is impossible — exit raises the share and brings people back (040aa item 5) |
| morality | inconsistent with ordinary morality but consistent with the morality of the aggrieved: envy as justice, "take and share out" | consistent with one thing: price, not punishment (040ab); takes nothing away |
| meaning | belonging, an enemy, a song, martyrs, a "we" — Arendt (040r): the mass seeks something to belong to | efficacy: "my vote counts", "I build a country for my children", "I can go into government honestly" — §4 |

## 2. Four conclusions

**Mass support needs neither logic nor morality** — communism proved it. It needs a simple slogan, a personal material interest, a carrier and a moment of state weakness. The protocol's first two are stronger: the promise is not "later and to all" but "now and to you", and without taking from anyone. The third exists (§3). The fourth is not needed: the protocol needs no collapse — it needs a referendum.

**Communism raged for a hundred years not because it was supported but because it was imposed.** At a free vote it nowhere took a majority — a third at the peak, and that is the same third as "the core": those brought by the party meeting. A hundred years are a hundred years of coercion and an information monopoly, not a hundred years of consent. The protocol is its opposite in method: it cannot take power by force and cannot hold by coercion; it spreads as an accounting practice — from city to city, by data, like double-entry bookkeeping, not like a faith. That is slower, and therein lies its protection: it has no apparatus to outlive the idea.

**The most unexpected coincidence.** Communism promised "to take from the minority and give to the majority" — and the majority went. The protocol promises "to return to the majority what was taken from it for free" (029 §29.11) — and takes nothing away. In the structure of interest it is the same move — material, immediate, for the majority — but without a victim, without an enemy and without a party-apparatus. If a move with a victim held for a hundred years, a move without one can hold longer: it has nobody to fear.

**Where communism had what the protocol lacks** — countries with a collapsed state, where taking power was cheap. The protocol is designed for countries with a working budget and roll; where there are none, it is not introduced (040w item 4).

## 3. The carrier: a party with a lifespan — the architect's move

The first answer: "the third channel does not exist as a party, but exists as 'the burned'". The architect: "there is no party *now*; after a bright announcement one may well appear, though it is not needed". It will certainly appear: any loud cause in a democracy gives birth to a single-issue party within a year — Five Star from Grillo's blog, the Brexit Party in six weeks, pirate parties after one court case. The question is not "whether" but what its function is and when it should disappear.

*The function — to speed up the parties' fork* (059g): while both old parties are "officially against", a single-issue party takes "the burned" and shows the old ones the price of silence at the first local elections; the Brexit Party within half a year forced the Conservatives to adopt its single point — and vanished. The best outcome for a protocol party: its success is its own dissolution, once the old parties cross the fork. *What it must not do — take power:* the protocol names no enemy; a party winning seats "in the protocol's name" turns the rule into a faction, and Mudde gets his argument "the protocol is them" (040ab). The right form before the referendum is a ballot-measure committee, like People Not Politicians: with accounts, a deadline and dissolution after the vote; a party only as a battering ram, if the old ones hold the front longer than one cycle. *Why it is not needed afterwards:* an adopted protocol itself changes the menu's customer (040ae item 5), the old parties converge on "those who stay", and the single-issue party has nothing left to offer — its point has become common. Communism's party outlived the idea by half a century; the protocol's party must die before the rule — the first party in history for which self-dissolution will be a victory.

## 4. Meaning: what the protocol gives the soul — the architect's move

The first answer said: "the protocol gives money and a share of the vote — and nothing for the soul". The architect: "you forgot again. For the soul — the ×3 vote, and you change laws and build a better country for your children with a chance of being heard, and you sacrifice money and time; you can even go honestly into an honest and effective government". The answer's error was to confuse "without faith, enemy and songs" with "nothing for the soul". Communism's meaning is collective and *against* someone; the protocol's is personal and *for* someone, and it is not weaker but more honest:

- "my vote counts" — after twenty years when it did not; Pateman's school for those who stay (040ad item 1), efficacy backed by a number, not a slogan (040ad item 3);
- "I change laws and build a country for my children" — with a real chance of being heard, because my kind became the median (059g §5); the long horizon pays off for the first time not only in money;
- sacrifice of money and time — voluntary and without sacrificing *anyone*; communism demanded sacrifice too, but someone else's;
- "to go honestly into an honest and effective government" — because a government elected by those who stay is for the first time one it is not shameful to join; under communism a career in power was the price of conscience, here it is its application;
- and for "the taker" — the freedom not to pretend: not to go by quota and not to say one believes.

Communism gave meaning through belonging and hatred; the protocol through efficacy and children. The second sings no songs, but demands no martyrs either.

## Summary

| Question | Communism | The protocol |
|---|---|---|
| what it won with | slogan, enemy, redistribution, party, force | a number, nobody is an enemy, return without taking, "the burned", referendum |
| support at a free vote | a third at the peak | forecast 55–60 % |
| duration | a hundred years of coercion | while the counter adds up; repealed honestly |
| meaning | belonging and an enemy | efficacy and children |
| what they share | a material, immediate move for the majority | the same move — without a victim |

## Weak point

A comparison by channels of support, not by the content of ideas; the shares at free elections are historical, the protocol's forecast is without a pilot. "Spreads as an accounting practice" is a thesis: no city has yet adopted it, and the speed of spread "from city to city by data" is unknown. And Krastev (040w item 5) would leave a question: meaning through efficacy works for those who stay; to "the taker" the protocol offers the freedom not to pretend — and that is less than communism gave its brought-in, even if by a lie.
