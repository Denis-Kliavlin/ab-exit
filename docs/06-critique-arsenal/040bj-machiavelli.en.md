# 40bj. Machiavelli: Ten Theses from *The Prince* and the *Discourses* — the People Are Honest on a Particular Question, Discord Must Be Channelled, and New Orders Are Killed by Their Losers

*How to read. Machiavelli is not a twentieth-century political scientist, and the frame of the "Political Scientists' Questions" series (40bc–40bi) does not apply to him: he wrote five hundred years before turnout statistics and rational choice theory. But he is the one classic who puts the protocol's three questions directly: what the people want, where to put discord, and why new orders perish. The chapter checks ten of his theses by one yardstick: is the thesis true today, and what does the protocol do with it — confirm it, carry it out, or describe another subject.*

## 1. Ten theses

| No. | Thesis (source) | What is true today | What the protocol does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The great wish to oppress, the people wish only not to be oppressed; so the people's desire is the more honest (*Discourses* I.5) | The people do not demand power, they demand to be left alone | An exact portrait of the one who takes the payment: he does not want to rule, he wants to be left in peace — and for the first time he is paid for it. Matches the repository's rule: "not to wrong" unconditionally, "to support" only after convincing those who pay ([029](029-hypocrisy.md)) |
| 2 | The quarrels of plebs and nobles made Rome free; good laws were born of tumult (I.4) | Conflict cannot be removed; the question is the channel | The protocol does not quench conflict, it gives it an entry: one act of "taking" is a demand, a count and the weight of those who stay at once ([40bg §4c](040bg-q-change-needs-organization.md)). Tumult without the square |
| 3 | A lawful channel for accusations saves a republic from conspiracies and calumny; where grievance has no outlet it goes by crooked paths (I.7–8) | In an autocracy grievance goes into the feed and into emigration | A lawful exit without risk: the question "where are my 30,000" in the kitchen ([056f §13i](../09-other-countries/056f-russia-september-2026.md)). A tribunate for everyone — without a tribune |
| 4 | The people err in generalities and judge rightly in particulars; the multitude is wiser and more constant than a prince (I.47, I.58) | Promises in general are bought; a concrete bill is not | The protocol asks one particular question with a price: money or the vote. That is the very setting in which, by Machiavelli, the people do not err ([40bd](040bd-q-citizens-are-rational.md)) |
| 5 | Where there are "gentlemen" living on rent without labour no republic can be built; it needs equality (I.55) | Rentier states ([057d](../09-other-countries/057d-venezuela.md)) | The protocol makes the vote dear with one number, without waiting for equality; the rent for the first time serves those whose vote is dear (057d §3b) |
| 6 | A republic lives while it returns to its beginnings — ritornare al segno (III.1) | Institutions wear out ([013g](../03-theory/013g-decay-of-democracy.md)) | The return is built in: the choice is made anew every cycle (048m art. 5), the percentage only by referendum and no more than once a cycle (art. 3). A thermostat, not a one-off reform |
| 7 | Men forgive the death of a father sooner than the loss of property; above all a prince must not touch what belongs to others (*The Prince* XVII) | Money for the family is closer than any slogan | The payment is unseizable (art. 8) and tied to the median; to repeal the rule is to take from half the population what is theirs. No politician will do that by name ([059l §8b](../10-usa/059l-five-thousand-rehearsal.md)) |
| 8 | Nothing is harder than new orders: the enemies are all who do well under the old, the defenders are lukewarm; the unarmed prophet perishes (VI) | The main objection to any reform | Proof by contradiction — §2 |
| 9 | A people used to living under a prince can hardly keep its freedom (I.16–17) | The habit thesis ([040ba §3](040ba-turnout-theory.md)) | The protocol needs no habit of freedom: one priced choice is grasped at once ([040at §4](040at-achen-bartels.md)). It does not ask the people to be republicans — only to count |
| 10 | Fortune rules where no dikes have been built; dikes are built in calm times (XXV) | Reforms are made in crisis and lost | The protocol is the dike: a rule adopted by referendum that elected bodies cannot repeal (art. 14). Built with one number, not a revolution ([006b](../02-history/006b-predecessors-and-gaps.md): assembly, not invention) |

## 2. Thesis 8 — proof by contradiction

Accept the argument in full: a new order is impossible without force; its enemies are all who do well under the old, its defenders are lukewarm because their gain is not obvious; the unarmed prophet perishes.

- *Then the protocol must have force.* It does, and it is not arms: money paid by a rule, not by a man. Machiavelli demands force because otherwise there is nothing to hold supporters with and nothing to answer enemies with; the payment does both without a single soldier.
- *Then the protocol must have enemies — all who do well under the old order.* But the protocol has no losers: the one who takes gets money, the one who stays gets double weight, the politician gets a hall without bought votes and not one kamikaze who will tell a family by name "I am against the three thousand" ([040aw](040aw-gilens-page.md), [059l §8b](../10-usa/059l-five-thousand-rehearsal.md)). No one robbed — no conspiracy.
- *Then the defenders must be lukewarm.* But the one who stays has paid for his vote out of his own pocket — the most determined voter in history ([40bi §5](040bi-q-demography-blocks-change.md)). Machiavelli explains lukewarmness by the gain from the new order not being visible; here it is visible on the card and on the ballot.

**The fork.** Either the thesis is false, and new orders are introduced without force — then the protocol is introduced like any other. Or the thesis is true — and then the protocol is the one new order that satisfies it: with force without arms, without losers, and with defenders who have paid. There is no third case.

## 3. Where Machiavelli seems to strike the protocol — and why it is another plane

*Discourses* I.37: quarrels over property ruined Rome, while quarrels over honours strengthened it. The agrarian law took land from some and divided it among others: every such law had a loser, robbed by name, and he went into conspiracy. Is the payment a quarrel over property?

**The architect's move.** "The payment does not take, it gives on top — there is no property here. The protocol returns to the poor their own taxes. And it lawfully and honestly removes the apathetic. This is again next to Machiavelli, but another plane."

Check by his own yardstick: the dangerous quarrel over property is the one with someone robbed. Under the protocol there is none. The money comes from the budget, which already today buys the apathetic vote with benefits, indexations and promises ([040aj §7](040aj-dalton.md)), not from a neighbour's pocket, and it is paid to everyone equally by rule, not to supporters. No loser by name — no conspiracy. Machiavelli is not wrong here: he describes another subject.

**The architect's move on the plane: price, not discord.** "The protocol's action is closer to economics and to the equilibrium free price of a good, which balances everyone and does not let the seller war and conflict with the buyer. They are forced somehow to bargain and agree, and they have a mechanism."

Machiavelli knew two ways to settle a dispute over distribution: force (the agrarian law, conspiracy) and an institution for discord (the tribunate). The third — price — did not exist for politics in his time: the market as an equalising mechanism was described by Smith two hundred and fifty years later. The protocol puts a price on the vote, and from then on the sides do not fight but bargain: the government sees the share of takers as a quotation ([013 §13.10](../03-theory/013-game-theory.md)), the voter compares D every cycle with what he is promised, and the sum is visible to all — unlike the market price of a vote in Moldova, paid secretly by a sanctioned bank ([019d](../04-electoral-dynamics/019d-market-price-of-vote.md)). Where there is a price, the market does not shoot: the seller can neither force the buyer nor rob him — only offer better. That is what Machiavelli sought in the tribunate: a channel for discord. Price gives the same channel without tribunes and without tumult.

## 4. What Machiavelli gives the protocol

Three things today's political scientists lack. He thinks the people neither rational nor stupid — he thinks them honest on a particular question, and that is the protocol's exact frame. He knows that conflict must be channelled, not quenched: *Discourses* I.4 is the best justification of the hall's three movements in five centuries. And he is one of the few who says plainly: the trouble with any reform is that it has losers. The protocol answers that not with words but with its design.

## Weak point

Machiavelli's chapters are paraphrased, not quoted; numbering follows the standard division. The one substantive point: the balance of the price — what share takes at what p, and whether it calms the dispute over distribution as a market price does — is a pilot measurement ([048l §4](../08-implementation/048l-courtyard-pilot.md)). The analogy with an equilibrium price is the architect's move, not a proven model; the formal statement is in [013f](../03-theory/013f-formal-statement.md).
