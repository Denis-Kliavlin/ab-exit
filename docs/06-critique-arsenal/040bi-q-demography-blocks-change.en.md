# 40bi. Political Scientists' Question 7: "Demography Blocks Change"

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites
**File:** 06_040bi · v1 · 25 October 2026

**Source:** the architect's analysis of E. Shulman's episode "Youth Is Silence" (24 October 2026), theses 11 and 20. The architect's moves are verbatim. This chapter closes the analysis of the episode: twenty theses in seven questions.

*How to read. The seventh premise: change comes with generations, and an ageing society blocks it; the young go to rallies, the old go to elections, and whoever comes wins. The protocol disputes that age is a bloc: the old divide by motive, not by years, and the count changes without rallies.*

**The series frame — the architect's move.** Political science wants the same thing the protocol delivers: a change of power by ordinary procedure, without bloodshed, through a law that cannot be repealed. It understands that the apathetic and the impulsive must be removed from the decision, but it knows no honest franchise qualification. And within the magnitudes and parameters that qualifications have had for two thousand years, it is right: every known qualification took the vote away from outside and hit the weak, and non-participation was a loss of the vote, that is, an illness ([006](../02-history/006-2500-years.md)). The architect: "AB-EXIT somehow went around all of this. I am in shock myself." The protocol is not a subtype of the known qualifications but another dimension: a qualification each person passes himself, in secret, by refusing money, and a balance in which nobody loses — the one who leaves gets the payment and the right to return, the one who stays gets double weight.

## 1. Who says so

- **E. Shulman** (2026, thesis 11). Each cycle new voters arrive with no political memory — the "Bolotnaya case" and the Bolotnikov uprising are equally distant history to them; at school they were told "the people decide," they go to decide and are surprised. But there are very few young people, society is ageing, the backbone of the system is women over 50 in electoral commissions, schools, hospitals and housing services; so youthful surprise will not become protest.
- **E. Shulman** (2026, thesis 20). Youth is not one body. The global paradox: the young go to rallies, the old go to elections, and whoever comes wins. Young men and young women are diverging; the poor are moving right — the more aggrieved, the more right-wing. An autocracy needs a passive population of any age; now it needs infantry and drone operators, the indoctrination goes badly — an autocracy has no project, it fears enthusiasts and does not know what to do with civic energy.

## 2. The architect's move: the old are for stability, and money is more reliable than promises

"'You cannot outvote the old' stops being true. The old are for stability and reliability, and money is more reliable than promises and forecasts — and they will remove themselves." The certainty effect (Kahneman and Tversky): a guaranteed outcome weighs more than a probable one, the more so the shorter the horizon. The promise "we will raise pensions" is a forecast; a payment to the card is a guarantee. The old divide by motive, not by age: those voting for stability will take, those voting from conviction will stay and weigh double; "will remove themselves" is their own choice with money and the right to return (048m arts. 5, 16), not exclusion.

## 3. The architect's move: the community vote is not a personal choice

"Rural and churchgoing people vote out of duty — to the community. But it is not their choice, and they will ask their elders how to get the money." And the generalisation: "The protocol breaks any deals — of a community, a union or any other. Taking the money is lawful and simple, and you personally do not have to ask anyone's permission." The choice can be found out only by forcing people to vote, but "it is already irreversible once the money is taken, and it is morally understandable" ([040al §4](040al-rove.md), [015c §7b](../03-theory/015c-sign-change.md)).

## 4. What follows for "the young to rallies, the old to elections"

The young stay away not out of laziness: they know the old cannot be outvoted — the mechanism of the burned, who stay away while the count is foregone ([059m](../10-usa/059m-chicago-2027-wilson.md): "the old decide everything from above"). Under the protocol the count stops being foregone: the old who vote for stability take the money; the community vote falls apart into persons; the young who stay weigh double. The balance of who wins changes without rallies. The backbone of the system — public-sector women over 50 through whom ritual turnout is produced — can also secretly take the payment, and there is nobody left to drag to the polls. A new voter with no memory of Bolotnaya needs none: an eighteen-year-old grasps "money if I do not go, or my vote weighs more" at once ([040at §4](040at-achen-bartels.md)).

**In numbers** ([016 §25.5](../04-electoral-dynamics/016-age-economics.md)): the share of the hall under 35 — from 21 % to 38 %, over 65 — from 30 % to 12 %; today one and a half old per young voter, under the protocol three young per old one. The architect: "the old who leave with the money give way to the young."

## 5. The architect's move: the aggrieved voter with a stake is the most determined voter in history

Shulman: the more aggrieved, the more right-wing. Today grievance converts only into a vote for the extremes — free mobilisation on which populists live ([40bf](040bf-q-populism-weaker-than-fascism.md)). The protocol gives grievance a second, priced move: the aggrieved person brought by the feed takes the money; the aggrieved person with a stake stays.

**The architect's move.** "And what matters here is that he is angry with all his money. And you have no chance of simply deceiving him. Politics has never known such determined people." The aggrieved person who stays refused the payment in order to vote: his anger is paid for out of his own pocket. He cannot be taken by the feed — the feed brings those who left with the money; he cannot be bought with a promise — he has already refused money; and he has the largest investment in the result, so he will be the first to notice a forgery (40bg §4b). Politics has always dealt with the aggrieved, but never with the aggrieved who paid for the right to vote.

## 6. An autocracy that fears enthusiasts

The protocol needs no enthusiasm: everyone carries it out with one choice, without inspiration and without risk. Energy that an autocracy can only suppress cannot be suppressed, because there is none — there is a lawful payment. And the one who stays is not an enthusiast but, by the architect's move, a determined voter with a stake: he too needs no inspiring, he has already paid.

## 7. What follows

| Premise | What is true | Answer |

| the community and rural vote is reliable | today, yes | the led vote falls apart: a secret choice, personal money, no permission needed (the architect's move) |
| the young to rallies, the old to elections | yes | the count stops being foregone, the young who stay weigh double — without rallies |
| the more aggrieved, the more right-wing | yes, today | the aggrieved from the feed take the money; the aggrieved with a stake is angry with all his money — the most determined voter in history (the architect's move) |
| an autocracy fears enthusiasts | yes | the protocol needs no enthusiasm |

## Weak point

Shulman's quotations follow automatic subtitles. The shares of the old voting for stability and from conviction, and the share of the aggrieved with a stake among those who stay, are a pilot measurement (048l §4).
