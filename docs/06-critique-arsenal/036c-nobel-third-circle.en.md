## 0. The centre of the circle — a non-Nobel: Albert Hirschman, "Exit, Voice, and Loyalty" (1970)

One must begin with an honest paradox: the closest relative of AB-EXIT in the whole history of thought **did not receive** the Nobel — the non-award to Hirschman is counted among the prize's famous injustices. His triad: a participant in any organisation (a firm, a party, a state) has two answers to deterioration — **Exit** (leave) and **Voice** (the voice of protest), linked by **Loyalty** (loyalty restrains exit, giving voice time).

The protocol bears a Hirschmanian name not by accident — but it does not illustrate the triad; it **solves its central problem**. For Hirschman exit and voice are substitutes that undermine each other: easy exit bleeds voice (the most sensitive leave first; the organisation loses the signal and rots further — his classic example of the Nigerian railways); locked exit makes voice powerless (nowhere to go — the shout can be ignored). Politics is the worst case: exit from citizenship is almost impossible, so voice is ignored for decades, so citizens invented **a third answer absent from the triad: silent non-participation** — not exit (he stayed) and not voice (he is silent), a pure loss of signal.

AB-EXIT rebuilds the triad: (1) it turns the third answer into **a formalised, paid, informative exit** — the departure is for the first time visible and measured; (2) it breaks the substitution — compensated exit does not bleed voice but **strengthens** it (the weight of those remaining ×1/(1−exit)): the more leave, the louder those who remain — exit and voice are for the first time complementary; (3) it inverts loyalty: for Hirschman loyalty is what *hinders* leaving; here staying is an active costly choice (refusing money), so loyalty is for the first time **measurable and paid in weight**. Where Hirschman stopped: he described the triad and its tragedy — a mechanism making exit and voice allies he did not propose. AB-EXIT is "Exit, Voice, and Loyalty" brought to a construction: exit paid in favour of the one leaving, voice weighted, loyalty monetised in influence.

## 1. Milton Friedman (1976) — money for exit from the state system

Two Friedmanite nodes are direct ancestors. **School vouchers**: do not repair the state school from inside — give the family money and the right of exit; competition for those leaving repairs the system better than reforms. AB-EXIT is a voucher applied to the last state monopoly: the electoral one. Button B is a voucher for exit from participation; politicians, like Friedman's schools, for the first time compete for **the right to be chosen by those who could leave**. **The negative income tax**: instead of an army of bureaucrats deciding who is entitled — a direct payment by formula; the NIT is the direct ancestor of "a dividend by formula without discretion" (038: no operator — nothing to capture). Where he stopped: Friedman applied "money + exit" to schools, pensions, currencies — he did not reach the ballot; probably because he considered the political market incorrigible in principle. AB-EXIT is the last chapter of "Free to Choose" that he did not write.

## 2. Ronald Coase (1991) — property rights in the vote

The Coase theorem: externalities are cured not by a regulator but by **a clear definition of property rights** + low transaction costs — after that the parties bargain their way to efficiency themselves. Apply it to the vote: today the property right in the vote is **blurred** — everyone monetises it (parties through state funding per vote, platforms through advertising, political technologists through campaign budgets) except the nominal owner; the classic Coasean tragedy of an asset without an owner. AB-EXIT is a Coasean reform: for the first time it fixes full property right in the vote to the citizen (the right to use it with amplification — or to lease it to the state for a rent) and reduces the transaction costs of disposing of it to two buttons. Further, by Coase: an efficient allocation of influence arises by itself, without a regulator. Where he stopped: Coase examined lighthouses, radio frequencies and cattle in someone else's field; the most massive asset without an owner — 8 billion votes — remained unexamined.

## 3. Kydland and Prescott (2004) — rules rather than discretion

Their Nobel work is **time inconsistency**: a government promising today rationally revises the promise tomorrow (circumstances!) — so promises are not believed in advance, and all equilibria spoil. Their cure — **rules rather than discretion**: tie the authorities' hands in advance, by a mechanism that cannot be replayed by situation. From this work grew the independence of central banks — the greatest quiet institutional reform of the late 20th century: inflation was beaten not by a wise minister but by **discretion taken from the minister**. AB-EXIT is built entirely to this blueprint: a formula instead of a decision (median × 1.5 %), a referendum lock instead of parliamentary amendment, an autonomous trust instead of a treasury transfer — **the electoral analogue of an independent central bank**. The answer to "the government will cheat" is not "let's elect the honest" but the Kydland–Prescott one: "eliminate the point at which one can cheat". Where they stopped: applied it to monetary policy; electoral policy remained in the world of discretion — where promises to the voter are revised every cycle exactly by their model.

## 4. In a short line — three more junctions

**Vernon Smith (2002), experimental economics:** mechanisms are tested in the laboratory before the field — AB-EXIT can be run in laboratory markets (student groups, real small money, the A/B choice) **before** the pilot: cheap preliminary calibration of exit rates and framings (converges with Grok's KPIs and Kahneman's caveat). **Angus Deaton (2015):** deaths of despair (with Case) — the empirics of the death of the "vanishing men" of §27: people the economy has written off and politics monetises as rage; Deaton's statistics are the evidence base that the price of their "free" non-participation has already been paid in lives. **Diamond–Dybvig (2022):** a self-fulfilling bank run = the mirror of the spiral of silence (056c): the loyalist majority is a "run in reverse", held by the belief that everyone believes; public statistics of choice B are a mechanism of honest discovery that stops the self-fulfilling equilibrium.
