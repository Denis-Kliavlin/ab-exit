# 40be. Political Scientists' Question 3: "The Answer to the Crisis Is to Include Everyone and Make Participation Cheaper"

*How to read. The third premise: since the old representative institutions are not coping, they should be opened wider — direct democracy, crowdsourced lawmaking, participation that is simpler and cheaper. Last time that is how it was solved: the crisis of the 19th–20th centuries was closed with universal suffrage. The protocol disputes that the recipe will work a second time.*

**The series frame — the architect's move.** Political science wants the same thing the protocol delivers: a change of power by ordinary procedure, without bloodshed, through a law that cannot be repealed. It understands that the apathetic and the impulsive must be removed from the decision, but it knows no honest franchise qualification. And within the magnitudes and parameters that qualifications have had for two thousand years, it is right: every known qualification — property, education, a test — took the vote away from outside and hit the weak, and non-participation was a loss of the vote, that is, an illness ([006](../02-history/006-2500-years.md)). The architect: "AB-EXIT somehow went around all of this. I am in shock myself." The protocol is not a subtype of the known qualifications but another dimension: a qualification each person passes himself, in secret, by refusing money, and a balance in which nobody loses — the one who leaves gets the payment and the right to return, the one who stays gets double weight. Political science does not see this balance because it counts votes one to one and never puts a price on exit.

## 1. Who says so

- **E. Shulman** (2026, theses 2, 3, 5). The crisis of institutions in democracies is a new wave of democratisation, as in the 19th–20th centuries, when peasants became townspeople and demanded participation. The old equilibrium — "everyone votes, tens of thousands of educated people decide" — has run its course. The way out is probably more direct democracy and crowdsourcing in lawmaking.
- **H. Landemore** (040au): open democracy, lot and mini-publics instead of elections.
- **F. F. Piven and R. Cloward** (040az): remove barriers, make participation cheaper.
- **C. Pateman** (040ad): participatory democracy.

## 2. The architect's move: both parts of the old problem are closed

"The old equilibrium rested on two parts: everyone votes, and a few decide. The protocol solves exactly this problem: no longer does everyone vote, and they choose the clever not by birth but by programme. Both problems are closed."

Last time those included were those who themselves demanded participation: the literate worker wanted to vote. Today the majority does not want to take part until things get really bad (040bc), and wants influence without cost, as with a like. So "include everyone even more widely" does not fit this wave. The protocol gives a new equilibrium of two parts:

- instead of "everyone votes" — **everyone chooses**: each decides whether to take or to stay, nobody is excluded;
- instead of "a few decide, by origin and through the elites' filter" — **those with a stake decide**: selection not by education and not by a test (040d), but by what a person is himself willing to give up for the vote.

Self-selection by stake replaces selection from above — the editors, experts and party bureaucracies that decided who would advance. That answers Shulman's own observation that people no longer want to delegate decisions to the elite.

## 3. The architect's move: the vote must not become a like

"Shulman wants a like and a vote to be identical in cost and result."

She does not say so herself and adds caveats: a like becomes politics only when it turns into a mandate; popularity is not influence; she does not trust Russian electronic voting. But her way out — direct democracy and crowdsourcing — leads to a vote at the price and speed of a like. Carried to the end: a vote at the price of a like is decided by the impulsive; it is inflated by bots, as likes are; policy follows today's rage. By her own definition of populism (a leader addresses the masses directly, over the heads of institutions), this is populism turned into procedure, and Gramsci's "time of monsters" does not end but becomes permanent.

**Do people go to direct votes?** No. Italy, the June 2025 referendums — turnout about 30 %, the quorum not reached. Average turnout at parliamentary elections worldwide — from 77 % in 1985 to 65 % by 2020. It is not that people are not allowed to decide directly: they are allowed and they do not come, because a ballot shows no return, the vote dissolves among the brought-in, and staying away is free and invisible.

**The protocol takes a like's return, but not its price.**

| | Like | Vote today | Protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| is the return visible | at once | no | at once: money or double weight, the share of takers by district |
| is the result personal | yes | no, it dissolves | yes |
| what it costs | nothing | coming, with no return | refusing the payment |
| who decides | the impulsive | everyone mixed with the brought-in | those to whom the vote is worth more than money |

The cost of refusal works as a filter on the impulsive — the protocol's recorded aim: remove the apathetic and the impulsive. A democracy of likes gives return at the cost of quality; the protocol gives return at the cost of money, and it is the cost that makes the vote heavy.

**The architect's move: why a referendum, but one.** "The protocol is altogether a basic rule, almost constitutional, and that is exactly why a referendum." Direct voting is needed not on every question at the price of a like, but once — for a rule of constitutional rank that determines who decides everything else (048m art. 14: it can be changed only by referendum). Modern political science offers direct democracy as a permanent procedure; the protocol uses it once, for the foundation, and from then on the hall cleaned by that foundation decides.

## 4. What follows

| Premise | What is true | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| the crisis is a new wave of democratisation | yes | the old equilibrium is closed on both sides: not everyone votes, the choice is by programme, not by birth (the architect's move) |
| the answer is to include everyone more widely | no | last time those who wanted were included; today the majority does not want — what is needed is a choice for everyone and the decision with those who have a stake |
| make participation cheaper, direct democracy | no | a vote at the price of a like is populism as procedure; the protocol takes a like's return without its price; direct voting once, for a near-constitutional rule (the architect's moves) |

**Boundary.** The media and expert platforms of the old equilibrium are untouched by the protocol; it changes the composition of the hall.
