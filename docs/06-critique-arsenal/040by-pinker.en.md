# 40by. Steven Pinker: Nine Theses — the Tragic Vision, Democracy as Dismissal without Blood, the "Mythology Zone", Common Knowledge, Taboo Trade-offs; and Why Elections Did Not Make His List of Institutions of Reason

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites
**File:** 06_040by · v1 · 11 November 2026
**Source:** the architect's request of 9 November 2026 ("examine Steven Pinker, if he has not been done"). Steven Pinker: *The Blank Slate* (2002), *The Better Angels of Our Nature* (2011), *Enlightenment Now* (2018), *Rationality* (2021), the book on common knowledge (2025). He did not write about paid non-participation; everything below given as "he would say" is built from his books; quotations are paraphrased.

---

*How to read. Pinker is not a critic of elections but a defender of progress and reason. The chapter takes nine of his theses and shows that seven work for the protocol while two — "democracy is valuable as dismissal without blood" and "the system works anyway" — deliver the strongest blows against it. The chapter's main finding: Pinker himself writes that what makes us rational is not persuasion but institutions with rules — courts, peer review, prediction markets — and elections are not on his list. The protocol makes elections such an institution. The architect's move on "gentle commerce" is recorded verbatim in thesis 2.*

## 1. Nine theses

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | "Gentle commerce": positive-sum exchange makes the other more valuable alive than dead | True for one who calculates. **The architect's move:** "'More valuable' is a calculation, and on meeting a stranger the apathetic and the meek have only fear. Those who did not take the money have the resolve, the courage and the calculation that they can be more valuable alive under the right trade. For the apathetic and the meek this channel is closed for good." The fear of the meek under dependence is rational, not cowardice ([1h](../01-introduction/001h-glossary.md)); he is sold a wall and a tariff, not an exchange. Pinker himself (after Fiske) calls the market model of relationships the latest and least natural: it requires counting | The question about the stranger — trade, tariff, migrant — is decided by a hall that sees the chain ([040bd §3](040bd-q-citizens-are-rational.md)); "for good" is about the state, not the person: one leaves the state by refusing D ([1i](../01-introduction/001i-consequences.md)) |
| 3 | The tragic vision (after Sowell): people cannot be remade, institutions are built for people as they are; Madison: "if men were angels, no government would be necessary" | Applied to the ruler (checks and balances) but not to the voter: he is still expected to come free of charge and work it out | If voters were angels, no price would be necessary. | 5 | Two zones of belief: the near "reality" (you pay for a mistake: petrol, the bank balance) and the far "mythology" (politics: one may believe anything, it costs nothing); the remedy is a bet, "a bet is a tax on bullshit" | The free vote lives in the mythology zone: this is expressive voting ([1f §4](../01-introduction/001f-core-electorate.md)). ## 2. The first blow: "the hall need not be wise; it needs to be universal, so that the ruler fears everyone and the loser submits"

Accept it whole. Then the only job of elections is to dismiss the bad, and the voter's quality does not matter. But the source Pinker relies on shows that rulers are dismissed for what they do not control: six months of prices, a drought, a thousand-dollar truck ([059o §4b](../10-usa/059o-ship-turning-every-700-days.md)). So the fuse blows at random, and a rational ruler answers with a short horizon and handouts before the election — a turn every 700 days ([059o](../10-usa/059o-ship-turning-every-700-days.md)). The fork: either the accuracy of dismissal matters — and then a hall is needed that tells a tariff from a barrel, and its horizon of at least one cycle is proved by construction ([040at §2](040at-achen-bartels.md), the architect's move); or it does not matter — and then a lottery and term limits would do, which Pinker does not propose.

The universality of the right the protocol does not touch: the right stays with everyone, and everyone chooses anew each cycle ([048m art. 5](../08-implementation/048m-charter.md)). The loser's submission rests not on universality but on trust in the count — and here the protocol gives what Popper lacks: a count verified against payments, with a reserve for stuffing of ~3 % instead of 40–50 % ([048f](../08-implementation/048f-double-entry-elections.md)). The freedom to complain stays and gets a number: the share of takers by district is a complaint that cannot go unheard ([1i](../01-introduction/001i-consequences.md)). Will the taker not recognise the government? The rule was adopted by referendum of all, and he chose himself ([1g](../01-introduction/001g-objections-index.md)).

## 3. The second blow: "the system works, the graphs go up; radical reform is a risk, and your discontent is progressophobia"

The comparison must be with what exists, not with an ideal ([040m](040m-cold-reader-audit.md)). Pinker himself describes the backsliding after 2016 and proposes against it to defend institutions and wait for generational turnover (thesis 8) — the wait did not come true. The price of the present order has been counted: a turn every 700 days, the government's horizon as compound interest ([059o §6](../10-usa/059o-ship-turning-every-700-days.md)), coalitions without a contract ([059p](../10-usa/059p-third-force-demand.md)).

A contradiction within Pinker. In politics he is a tragedian (thesis 3): people cannot be remade. In remedies he is a utopian: teach statistics, "factfulness", critical thinking. In *Rationality* he himself admits that such teaching transfers poorly, and gives another answer: what makes us rational is institutions with rules in which a mistake costs money or reputation — courts, peer review, fact-checking, prediction markets. Elections are not on the list, and by his own criterion they cannot be: the free vote is the one institution in which nobody pays for a mistake. The protocol adds nothing to his criterion, it applies it: the rule makes a bet the condition of the vote. By the Enlightenment criteria Pinker lists himself the rule passes: measurable (the share of takers), reversible (referendum, p changes), built on an incentive rather than on correcting people.

## 4. The third blow: "money for the vote is a market price in the domain of equality; people will be sick. Expanding the franchise was moral progress, and you narrow it"

Disgust Pinker himself treats as a fact of psychology, not an argument: by his own account of Tetlock, the taboo trade is made anyway, only blindly and worse. Here the trade is already under way — the vote is brought in, bought at full price and not recorded ([040br](040br-ten-propaganda-devices.md)); the protocol provides the entry. His own three reservations work for the rule. The taboo as a sign of loyalty: one who takes D shows that the vote was not sacred to him — this is exactly the revelation the taboo hid. Refusing to count gives worse decisions: that is precisely what happens when the price of a vote is known to the buyer and unknown to the hall. The taboo is lifted by renaming: D is a return of one's own taxes, a dividend, as in Alaska, where there is no disgust; so the rule is named ([1b](../01-introduction/001b-essence.md)). Roth gives the same construction from the design side: disgust is evoked by buying the direction of the vote, paying for exit is a different transaction ([036b §5](036b-nobel-second-circle.md)).

The circle does not narrow. Expanding the franchise was about who *may*; "may" stays with everyone, every cycle, and no threshold — sex, property, literacy — is introduced ([1g](../01-introduction/001g-objections-index.md): "bribery", "vote suppression"). What changes is not the circle but the price, and everyone pays the same price. The answer to this blow is the assistant's; there is no architect's move on it.

## 5. What Pinker gives the protocol

- **A name for the main effect.** The vote moves from the "mythology zone" into the "reality zone". His own formula of the bet as a tax on bullshit he did not apply to elections — the rule does.
- **A criterion.** An institution of reason is a rule under which a mistake costs. By this criterion elections with a free vote are not an institution of reason, and with a price they become one.
- **The theory of common knowledge** for a ready construction: the choice private, the sum public. A poll is shared knowledge, the counter is common knowledge; the autocrat loses the option of banning the square, because the square has become the sum.
- **The tragic vision** as the strongest allied argument against "first educate the voter".
- **Confirmation of the move on commerce.** The market model of relationships is the latest and requires counting; so "gentle commerce" pacifies only one who calculates.

## Weak point

Pinker's theses are given from memory, without checking against the texts; his empirics on the fall of violence are disputed (Taleb, on the fat tails of wars), but the chapter does not rest on them. The vote's move into the "reality zone" works only if D is felt by the one who stays — the same yardstick "desirable for 40–50 %" ([1d](../01-introduction/001d-exact-answers.md)). The rightward shift of the young in 2024–2026 is given from memory of press reports, without references. The third blow is answered by the assistant, not the architect. 🟡
