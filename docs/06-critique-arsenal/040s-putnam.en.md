# 40s. Robert Putnam: *Bowling Alone* — Five Arguments on the Decay of Social Capital and What a Price for the Vote Answers to Them

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites
**File:** 06_040s · v1 · 11 October 2026
**Source:** the architect's assignment of 11 October 2026: "each of the five — five arguments from his book, separately". The first is Putnam: *Making Democracy Work* (1993), *Bowling Alone* (2000), *The Upswing* (2020). A reconstruction from the books; where he would object, the objection is recorded before the answer; the architect's correction to the first argument is recorded as a correction.

---

*How to read. Putnam is the most measured of the critics: forty years of series on membership, turnout, trust and meetings. His subject is what the repository calls the atomised mass (040r) and the core (1f), only with numbers. That is why he is "the most harmful" of the five: his data on the decay are right, and the only question is what the protocol does with that decay — entrenches it, or counts and reverses it.*

## 1. Social capital has decayed — and it is measured

**The argument.** From the 1960s to the 1990s membership in civic associations, unions, parent-teacher associations, church attendance and bowling leagues fell by 25–50 %; turnout by a quarter, attendance at meetings by a third; letters to politicians, petitions, campaign work — all down. The American bowls alone. It is Arendt's atomised mass with forty years of numbers.

**The strike.** To pay for non-participation is to legitimise the decay he measured: a paid exit becomes the norm.

**The answer.** The exit has already happened — for free and invisibly, long before any payment; the protocol does not create it, it counts it for the first time and presents the price to those who use it (029 §29.11). The door is open at every election (048m Art. 5 part 2).

**The architect's correction, and it changes the result.** "The ×2 vote creates precisely the wish to return." Putnam measured *why* they left: a vote is one in a hundred million, it decides nothing, and those stopped coming who had no habit. His remedies — education, urban design, technology — do not touch the cause: the vote decided nothing and still decides nothing. The protocol touches exactly it: the amplified vote (through share, no multiplier — 1d, statements 11–12) is the only means proposed in thirty years that changes the *weight* of participation rather than pleading for participation. The burned return not because they were educated but because for the first time there is a reason (1f §7; the four cases of return — Scotland, Brexit, the US 2020, Hungary 2026, 037 §86.10). The protocol is not only a counter of decay: it has an active part against decay, and it strikes Putnam's cause more exactly than his own remedies.

## 2. The causes: generations, television, suburbs, two jobs

**The argument.** Half the fall is generational replacement: the "civic generation" (born before 1930), which went everywhere by habit, was replaced by those with no habit; a quarter is television; the rest is suburban sprawl and the time squeeze of two-career families.

**What it means for the repository.** The core of 60+ in 1f §2 is *Putnam's last civic generation*. It comes not from a stake but from habit and duty (up to 80 % of pensioners "from a sense of duty", 056f §13s). Putnam explains why the core is old: not because the old are loyal, but because the habit dies with its carriers and nobody inherits it.

**The strike.** The protocol will hasten the habit's death: it offers money to the last who still come by habit.

**The answer.** Habit without a stake is exactly what the machine rides on: a vote from duty does not judge, it confirms (1f §4c). The protocol separates those with habit plus stake (they stay) from those with habit alone (they take). Putnam would mourn the habit; but a habit bought with ten thousand before the Duma has already been bought — the protocol only makes the buyer the budget, equal for all.

## 3. Social capital makes government work

**The argument** (*Making Democracy Work*). The Italian regions: the civic north with its choral societies and cooperatives and the clientelist south with its patrons — and civicness predicts the quality of institutions centuries ahead.

**The strike.** The protocol creates no civicness, it substitutes money for it — the result will be the south, where the vote is bought by patrons anyway.

**The answer.** In the south the vote is already bought — by the patron, by name, for loyalty, with the answer on the ballot. The protocol replaces the patron's payment with the budget's: equal, unaddressed, from nobody in particular (048m Art. 1 part 4). That is not clientelism but its abolition: the patron loses the only thing he sold (048n, the Chicago frame: "pay everyone or no one"). Putnam's south is the repository's Chicago and Russia.

**Residual.** Whether this creates a north over time is a thesis about consequences (019), not a theorem.

## 4. Bonding and bridging capital

**The argument.** Bonding — closed groups that unite their own and wall off outsiders: the union, the sect, the machine; bridging — ties between strangers. Democracy needs the second; the first can harm it.

**The strike.** The protocol takes groups apart (040r) — and so the bridging capital along with the bonding.

**The answer.** The brought-in voter is the product of bonding capital in pure form, and the protocol takes it apart deliberately (040r §2: each decides alone). And the counter is a bridging fact: one number for all groups at once, seen by one's own and by strangers (Aumann, 036e §11). Putnam will object that a number is not a relationship. True; but a relationship between strangers begins with a shared fact, and before the counter they had no shared fact.

## 5. His remedies — and "less money in politics"

**The argument.** Putnam's agenda for 2010: civic education, the workplace, urban design, technology for connection — and less money in politics: he showed that television advertising displaced door-knocking, money displaced participation.

**The strike — the strongest.** The protocol is "more money in politics", directly against his programme.

**The answer.** He measured money going *into the machine* — advertising, consultants — and it is that money that displaces participation. The protocol moves money from the machine to the voter and makes it equal; it takes the raw material from advertising rather than adding money to it (039 §88.9: influence costs of 15–16 billion a cycle). By his own data money is bad by its recipient — the protocol changes the recipient.

**Residual.** In *The Upswing* (2020) — the "I–we–I" curve: a century from the 1890s to the 1960s the "we" rises, then falls. He would class the protocol among the "I" instruments, and that is honest: by its input it is individual — each decides alone. Its "we" is one thing — the counter, and it promises nothing collective beyond that; but it is a "we" that needs no meeting, and so is available to a society that, by his own measure, has stopped meeting.

## Summary

| Putnam's argument | What is true | Answer | What remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| the decay is measured | yes, forty years of data | the protocol counts the exit and presents the price; the amplified vote is an active part against decay, striking the cause (the architect's correction) | it does not cure the decay entire |
| habit dies with the generation | yes; the core is the last civic generation | habit without stake is the machine's raw material; the protocol separates habit with stake from habit alone | — |
| civicness makes government | yes, for centuries | in the south the vote is already bought by the patron; the protocol abolishes the patron | the north is a thesis (019) |
| bonding against bridging | yes | the machine is bonding, the protocol takes it apart; the counter is a shared fact for strangers | a number is not a relationship |
| less money in politics | yes, money displaces participation | money is bad by its recipient; the protocol changes the recipient | by its input the protocol is an "I" instrument |

## Weak point

A reconstruction from the books; Putnam did not write on payment for non-turnout. His strongest point is the "I" character of the protocol: none of the five answers makes the protocol collective action in Putnam's sense, and the repository does not claim it. And his data are American; the transfer to the Russian core is made through 1f §2 and §13s, not by him.
