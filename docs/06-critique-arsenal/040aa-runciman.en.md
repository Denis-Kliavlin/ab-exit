# 40aa. David Runciman: *How Democracy Ends* — Five Arguments on Fatigue, Technology and Zombie Democracy

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites
**File:** 06_040aa · v1 · 12 October 2026
**Source:** the architect's assignment of 11 October 2026: "each — five arguments from his book, separately". The ninth is Runciman: *How Democracy Ends* (2018), *The Confidence Trap* (2013). A reconstruction from the texts; the architect's move on the fifth argument is recorded as a move.

*How to read. Runciman is about how democracies die today: not by tanks but by fatigue with institutions intact. For the protocol this is a test of diagnosis: does it treat the disease they die of, or another one.*

## 1. Democracy dies not by coup but by midlife fatigue

**The argument.** Tanks are the twentieth century. Today the institutions stand, elections are held, and less and less happens inside them; a "midlife crisis" — democracy too old for revolution and too young to admit the end.

**The strike.** The protocol is a cure for a disease that does not exist: nobody is abolishing elections.

**The answer.** Runciman's fatigue is the exit of the burned (1f §6): the institution is intact, the composition has left — for free and unseen. The protocol treats not the form but the composition, and of all proposals it is the only one addressed to those who tired, not to those who stayed; Runciman described a disease that has no name in his book — the repository has one.

## 2. Three threats: coup, catastrophe, technology

**The argument.** Of the three the real one is the third: platforms know the voter better than he knows himself, and elections become manageable without violence, through targeting and the feed.

**The strike.** The protocol does not protect against targeting.

**The answer.** Targeting works on short attention — on the layer and the brought-in (040v item 1; 040x item 3, audience democracy); its target is the one who decides last, by image. The protocol does not protect against the technology — it removes its target: it takes out of the hall, for a payment, those the feed is designed for, and keeps those who come with memory (040x item 5). Targeting will remain; it will stop paying off.

## 3. Brennan's epistocracy is a temptation, not a way out

**The argument.** Runciman examines "the rule of the knowing" (Brennan, *Against Democracy*, 2016) separately and rejects it: the voter's exam is written by whoever is in power, and an exam always selects those who resemble the examiner.

**The strike — the most dangerous.** The protocol is easily read as epistocracy through money: "the rule of those who do not need the payment".

**The answer.** Epistocracy selects by knowledge that the government checks; the protocol by horizon that the voter checks himself, by his own choice, without exam or examiner (040u item 3, Dahl — the protocol neither produces nor requires enlightenment). The difference is who holds the filter: with Brennan the state, with the protocol each person, and the choice repeats every election (048m Art. 5 part 2). **Residual:** a critic will call it "the epistocracy of the wallet", and the only answer is the income-selection threshold (040p §1), which the pilot will measure; until the pilot this argument of Runciman's stands.

## 4. Democracy promises dignity and results — and has long delivered only the first

**The argument.** The vote as recognition that "you count" still works; the results — security, growth, order — are absent, and the gap grows.

**The strike.** The protocol trades in dignity: it offers to sell recognition for money.

**The answer.** Today non-turnout gets neither dignity nor a result — it is not counted (040t item 5). The protocol for the first time gives both a price: the taker gets a result — money, now, personally; the one who stays gets dignity with a visible share. Not trade in dignity but the end of a situation in which two-thirds received nothing and were called "those who did not come".

## 5. Democracies do not know how to end — and so live on dead for a long time

**The argument.** There is no mechanism for honestly admitting "it does not work"; democracy cannot declare itself failed and lives on as a zombie — procedures without content.

**The strike.** The protocol is one more procedure for the zombie.

**The answer — the architect's move.** "Under the protocol this is simply impossible: the more have left, the higher the price of the vote, and the more have returned." Runciman's zombie lives because exit has no feedback: those who left are not counted, those who stay get nothing for staying, and the system does not notice it has emptied. The protocol makes exit into feedback: every taker of the payment raises the share of every remaining vote (1d, statement 12), and the further the exit goes, the more it is worth returning — and they return (040s, the architect's move: the amplified vote creates the wish to come back). It is a thermostat with negative feedback (001b): emptying itself raises the price of entry. Democracy under the protocol cannot die quietly by construction — it can only change in composition, and the counter shows that in advance: the share who took, the share who stayed, whether the burned came back. And if it does not work, it is repealed by the same referendum (048m Art. 15): Runciman wanted democracy to be able to end honestly; the protocol can both end and not die.

## Summary

| Runciman's argument | What is true | Answer | What remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| fatigue with intact institutions | yes | it is the exit of the burned; the protocol treats composition | — |
| technology | yes | targeting's target is removed for a payment | targeting remains |
| epistocracy | yes, a temptation | the voter holds the filter, not the state; no exam | "epistocracy of the wallet" — until the pilot |
| dignity without results | yes | a price for both; non-turnout got nothing | — |
| zombie democracy | yes | exit = feedback: the more left, the higher the share and the more returned (the architect's move) | — |

## Weak point

A reconstruction; Runciman is about rich stable democracies, where by the architect's decision the protocol is not first (048l), and his diagnosis there is more exact than our forecast. The third argument stands until the pilot. And the architect's move on the impossibility of a zombie is a construction, not a measurement: that exit raises the share is arithmetic, that a rising share brings people back is the thesis of 040s, and it is tested only by the pilot's second cycle: did those who left in the first come back.
