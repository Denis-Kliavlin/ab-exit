## 1. Robespierre: the guillotine for the architects

The only one in the gallery who would write no essay — he would declare AB-EXIT the greatest counter-revolutionary conspiracy. His politics rests on one word: Virtue — readiness to sacrifice the personal for the common; egoism for him is not a given but a crime.

**Three blows from the Jacobin Club rostrum.**
1. *Prostitution of the general will.* By Rousseau sovereignty is inalienable; the vote is not a share but a sacrament. A "zero exit barrier" = legalised political prostitution.
2. *Aristocracy of wealth.* The most insidious census ever devised: "who refuses the money? the rich man, for whom it is dust; who takes it? the father of hungry children. You say 'free choice' — need forces him. The dictatorship of the golden calf under the mask of charity."
3. *Death of the citizen.* One who does not take part in governance is a slave; "if the citizen sleeps while he is paid, he is no longer a citizen — cattle in a stall waiting for its portion of hay".

**Verdict in the Convention:** "The only thermostat of freedom is terror against the enemies of Virtue. I demand a decree for the arrest of the architects of AB-EXIT."

**What this gives the protocol.** Robespierre is the most honest critic because he names the price: AB-EXIT is entirely devoid of moral pathos, it wants things simply to work. Robespierre needs the Kingdom of Justice, even at the cost of half the heads. Whenever the critique of the protocol sounds like "you are buying the votes of the poor", check whether it is Robespierre's position — and recall how it ended. The answer to blow 2 is not rhetoric but reversibility and the structure of wealth (040b.5): 85 % of refusers are self-made, not heirs; and the "accidentally impoverished" who refuses the money gets the amplified vote — the exception is built into the choice (040c.1.5).

## 2. Beccaria: inevitability over cruelty

The father of criminology ("On Crimes and Punishments", 1764) looks at the protocol as an **instrument for preventing state crimes** — pure utilitarianism of justice that does not avenge but makes crime unprofitable.

**Inevitability.** His main postulate: what deters is not the cruelty of punishment but its certainty. Why do politicians steal? Because in classical democracy the punishment (loss of power at the next election) is vague, deferred and circumvented by propaganda. The thermostat is "a masterpiece of institutional justice: punishment is instant and inevitable; the ruler knows that for a crime against the economy retribution comes mathematically; fear of algorithmic inevitability paralyses the very thought of corruption".

**Prevention instead of the scaffold.** "Better to prevent than to punish": democracy before AB-EXIT lets the populist come, wreck the country and only then tries to punish — too expensive. Filtering the apathetic majority is the ideal preventive measure: "it is pointless to punish demagogues for deceiving the crowd — such is their nature; you took the crowd out from under the blow".

**The algebra of pleasure and pain.** "Laws are the conditions on which isolated people united into society. You proposed a crystal-clear social contract: to those whose happiness is peace and comfort — a dividend; to those whose happiness is governing the polis — an amplified vote. You coerce no one; you weigh egoisms on the finest scales." Verdict: "You built the overseer into the architecture of incentives and turned justice from an act of violence into an act of book-keeping — the highest point in the development of Law."

## 3. Bentham: nonsense upon stilts and the panopticon

The founder of utilitarianism would recognise the author as "his most brilliant pupil".

**The end of the cult of voting.** Natural rights are "nonsense upon stilts"; the vote has no mystical value, it is a tool. "If the utility to Vasily of the dividend exceeds the tiny utility of a paper in the urn for a candidate he does not know — forcing him to vote is a crime against his happiness."

**Felicific calculus.** Bentham spent his life seeking an algorithm for the balance of pleasure and pain from a law; AB-EXIT is that algorithm in code: the apathetic majority gets money and is rid of a boring duty; the active minority loses the dividend but gets triple power and the pleasure of realised ambition. Total utility rises because each gets what he values more.

**The political panopticon.** In his prison the warder sees all the prisoners. In the AB-EXIT state **the economy watches the politicians round the clock**: steal a dollar — the dividend falls — pain in the wallet — dismissal. The politician is locked in a transparent cell before the whole society; his wish to keep power makes him work for the common good. A preface to the whitepaper: "The democracy of the past tried to govern through duty and guilt, forcing the ignorant to take decisions they suffered from. The authors dropped poetry and took up the mathematics of governance: the state is not a temple but a machine for producing utility."
