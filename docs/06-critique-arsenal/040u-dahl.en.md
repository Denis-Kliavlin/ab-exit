# 40u. Robert Dahl: Five Criteria of Democracy as an Examination for a Price for the Vote

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites
**File:** 06_040u · v1 · 11 October 2026
**Source:** the architect's assignment of 11 October 2026: "each — five arguments from his book, separately". The third is Dahl: *Polyarchy* (1971), *Democracy and Its Critics* (1989), *On Democracy* (1998). Dahl has no remedy — he has an examination: five criteria any process claiming to be democratic must meet. The protocol is run through all five; where it does not pass, that is recorded.

---

*How to read. Dahl is the most formal of the five: his criteria are not evaluative but testing, and that is why his examination of the protocol is the cleanest. The question to each criterion is one: does the protocol meet it worse than ordinary elections, the same, or more exactly.*

## 1. Effective participation

**The criterion.** Before a decision is taken, all members must have equal and effective opportunities to make their views known about what the decision should be.

**The strike.** The one who took the payment makes no view known — his opportunity has been bought.

**The answer.** The opportunity remains his entire: he chose not to use it, and the choice is repeated at every election (048m Art. 5 part 2). Dahl distinguished the *opportunity* from its *use*; the criterion requires the former. Today two-thirds have the opportunity and do not use it — for free and invisibly; the protocol does not change that, it changes the price of non-use. And the participation of those who stay becomes more effective in the literal sense: the share of each ballot cast grows (1d, statement 12). **Met more exactly than by ordinary elections.**

## 2. Voting equality

**The criterion.** When the decision is reached, every vote counts, and counts as equal.

**The strike.** If one vote "weighs more", the criterion is violated.

**The answer.** One ballot is one vote (048m Art. 1, Art. 5 part 4); nobody has a multiplier or coefficient (1d, statement 11). The amplification is through share, equally for all who vote, whoever they vote for. Dahl would say this is his criterion met more exactly than usual: equality among those who cast, not the fiction of equality between the one who cast and the one who kept silent, whom nobody counts. **Met more exactly.**

## 3. Enlightened understanding

**The criterion.** Each member must have equal and effective opportunities to learn about the relevant alternatives and their likely consequences.

**The strike — Dahl's strongest.** The protocol encourages the one who does not understand to go on not understanding — for money; instead of enlightening, it pays for unenlightenment.

**The answer.** The criterion is about the opportunity to learn, and it is taken from nobody: the one who took the payment may read, argue and know as much as yesterday. But the protocol adds what Dahl lacks — the counter and the price: a fact understood without enlightenment, because the sense of reality is strongest in what is personal and now (040q, Schumpeter). Enlightenment needs time and a teacher; a price needs neither. **The honest residual:** the protocol does not enlighten and does not promise to; it stops counting an unenlightened vote as an enlightened one, and that is not the fulfilment of the criterion but the refusal to counterfeit it. **Neither worse nor better — with the addition of a fact in place of enlightenment.**

## 4. Control of the agenda

**The criterion.** The people must have the exclusive opportunity to decide which matters are placed on the agenda and how.

**The strike.** None. The protocol is introduced and repealed only by referendum (048m Art. 15); the percentage is set by referendum (Art. 3); neither mayor, nor council, nor parliament introduces or amends the rule. It is the only element of the electoral design over which the citizens' control of the agenda is complete — whereas electoral law is usually written by those elected under it (029 §29.11, row 10: a judge in his own cause). **Met more exactly, and this is the strongest coincidence.**

## 5. Inclusion

**The criterion.** All adult permanent residents must have the full rights of citizens implied by the first four criteria.

**The strike.** Does the payment not exclude someone — the poor, the dependent, those who were "bought out"?

**The answer.** The right to the payment belongs to everyone who holds the right to vote, automatically, without applications or appearance in person; exclusions only by referendum (048m Art. 4). Inclusion under the protocol is wider than usual: the one who does not vote is included too — for the first time he is counted and paid rather than listed as absent. **Met more exactly.**

## Summary

| Dahl's criterion | Ordinary elections | The protocol | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| effective participation | the opportunity exists for all, unused by two-thirds for free | the same opportunity; non-use is counted and priced; the share of participants grows | more exactly |
| voting equality | one ballot one vote among those who came | the same, no multipliers; equality among those who cast, not a fiction of equality with the silent | more exactly |
| enlightened understanding | the opportunity to learn exists; an unenlightened vote counts as enlightened | the same opportunity; a fact added (the counter, the price); the unenlightened vote is not counterfeited | neither worse nor better |
| control of the agenda | electoral law written by the elected | the rule and the percentage only by referendum | more exactly |
| inclusion | the one who came is included | everyone with the right to vote is included, and the non-voter counts | more exactly |

**Result by Dahl:** four criteria of five the protocol meets more exactly than ordinary elections; the third — neither worse nor better, with the addition of a fact in place of enlightenment.

## Weak point

Dahl wrote his criteria for a *decision process*, not for a turnout design; the transfer to the protocol is ours, and it needs one caveat Dahl would make himself: the criteria are an ideal no polyarchy fully meets, and "more exactly" here means "closer to the ideal than the present", not "the ideal attained". And the third criterion remains the place where the protocol promises nothing: it produces no enlightenment, it only stops pretending that there is any.
