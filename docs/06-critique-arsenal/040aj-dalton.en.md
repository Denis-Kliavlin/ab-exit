*How to read. Dalton is the only author in the arsenal who measured not "the voter in general" but the very type the repository calls "the burned" (1f §6, §6b): educated, informed, without a party, sceptical of parties as representatives. He came right up to the class and did not name it — because in his grid turnout is not an axis but an outcome. This chapter is about where he stopped and what his instrument gives the protocol.*

## 0. Dalton's grid and its translation into the repository's language

Dalton builds the electorate on two axes: *cognitive mobilisation* (education plus access to information — the ability to make sense of politics without a party's cue) and *partisan attachment*. Four cells result:

| Dalton's cell | Mobilisation | Party | In the repository |
|---|---|---|---|
| apolitical independents | low | none | "the apathetic" (013 §13.2, 1f §4) |
| ritual partisans | low | yes | "the brought-in" and the dependent — "the core" (1f §5) |
| cognitive partisans | high | yes | thinking supporters — the future second side of the contest of ideas (040ah §4) |
| **apartisans** | **high** | **none** | **"the burned" — before exit** |

Dalton's main findings: apartisans grow in all the old democracies along with education; independents in the US already outnumber either party; apartisans *vote almost as often as partisans* but switch between parties on the substance of the issue; dealignment is not the citizen's decline but his coming of age.

Translation: the burned one is an apartisan who has taken one more step. Dalton has no such step, because for him turnout is not a coordinate but what is measured afterwards. An apartisan who has stopped going falls, by behaviour, into the "apolitical" cell while by his head he stays in the upper right. So the class Dalton described best of all disappears in his own work — into the line "did not come".

## 1. Dealignment is already happening by itself — the protocol is not needed

**Argument.** Party ties weaken with education and media; independents are already the majority; the system is rebuilding itself around the thinking voter without any payment. Why pay for what modernisation does for free?

**The blow.** The protocol (001b) starts from the premise that "the core" cannot be outvoted and the thinking leave. If Dalton is right, the thinking do not leave but grow and vote, and the core dissolves by itself as education rises.

**Answer.** Dealignment removes the *attachment* but does not change the *price* of the vote. The apartisan still votes in the same system against the same core, and the only thing independence gives him is the right to waver between two machines that both bring in turnout (030b). His weight at the urn is one vote, like the brought-in voter's; his information converts into nothing but the choice of the lesser evil. Forty years of dealignment (Dalton counts from the 1970s) changed neither the number of the brought-in nor the cost of campaigns — both grew. The protocol finishes what dealignment started: it gives the detached voter not only freedom but weight — a share of ×2 and above (001b).

**Residual.** None: Dalton's argument describes the same group as the protocol and disputes not the group but whether freedom without weight is enough for it. Forty years of data answer: it is not.

## 2. The burned do not exist as a class: the thinking vote

**Argument.** By Dalton's data apartisans turn out almost like partisans. It is the apoliticals who do not — low mobilisation, low interest. So the "producing non-voter" of 1f §3 is a construct: whoever produces and thinks, votes.

**The blow.** Straight at 1f §6: if there are no burned, the protocol has nobody to bring back, and "those who stay" are the same apartisans who vote today.

**Answer.** Three things. *First — the data.* Turnout in Dalton is self-reported in surveys (ANES and its analogues), and self-report overstates turnout by 10–20 points, most of all among the educated — those ashamed to say "I did not go". An apartisan who stopped going is, in a survey, most often "went". *Second — the grid.* Dalton has no cell for "mobilised, stopped going": turnout is not an axis. Such a person does not vanish from the population — he vanishes from the typology, landing by behaviour among the apoliticals. The class is not refuted; it is unmeasured. *Third — the test.* The dispute is settled not by a survey but by the protocol: who returns with a share. If Dalton is right and only the apoliticals left, the return after adoption will be small — there is nobody to return. If the repository is right, those who return are apartisans by Dalton's grid and "did not come" by the behaviour of the last cycles. This is a testable forecast for the pilot (048l), and it is the first answer to the "weak point" of 1f §6b: a class absent from the literature cannot be confirmed by the literature — but it can be by Dalton's instrument.

**Residual.** Until the pilot — a forecast. If the return proves small, Dalton is right, and the repository is bound to record it.

## 3. Cognitive mobilisation grows by itself — thinking voters need not be created

**Argument.** The thinking voter is a product of education and media, not of a rule. Their share grows every decade. The protocol produces not a single apartisan.

**The blow.** At "the protocol creates a demand for knowledge" (040u item 3, 1f §7): does it, if knowledge grows without it?

**Answer.** The protocol does not create them — it removes those who dilute them. In Dalton all four cells vote together, and the two lower ones — the apoliticals and the ritual partisans — outnumber the two upper ones; so campaigns are built for the lower cells (030b: frighten, bring in, buy), and the upper cells get a menu not composed for them (040ae item 5). Under the protocol the lower cells take B — that is their choice (040ag item 2) — and the electorate shrinks to the two upper cells: cognitive partisans against apartisans. Both sides think; comparing programmes against the figure D works both inside a party and between parties (040ai §4, the refinement on the waverer). Dalton described the growth of the numerator; the protocol removes the denominator. The demand for knowledge (040u item 3) appears not because people were taught but because, for the first time, there is nobody left to frighten.

**Residual.** None.

## 4. Apartisans are volatile — without party anchors the electorate is unpredictable

**Argument.** Studies using Dalton's grid (South Korea, Mexico, Brazil) show that apartisans and apoliticals are the main "swing" from election to election. An electorate of apartisans alone — no anchors, sharp reversals; parties cannot plan, governments cannot govern.

**The blow.** At stability after the protocol (048d, 040v §6): an electorate without a core may be an electorate without memory.

**Answer.** Volatility in Dalton is switching *on the substance of the issue*: the apartisan votes for the result, not the colour, and changes his choice when the result changes. That is not wavering but accountability — the thermostat (040aa item 2, 040x item 3): a government re-elected for what it did is predictable in the one sense that matters — it knows what it will be removed for. The ritual partisan, by contrast, is predictable for the party and useless for accountability: his vote does not depend on what was done. And the second half — the architect's move (1f §6b): "they are hard to convince, but once they have changed their mind, convincing them again is hard too — they are consistent and principled". Dalton's volatility is a transition with memory: the apartisan leaves a party once and does not come back at the first slogan; the apolitical who "swings" has no memory, and his swinging is what leaves with B under the protocol.

**Residual.** None.

## 5. Sceptics of parties will take the money in protest — the protocol loses its best

**Argument.** Dalton's apartisan is a sceptic of parties as representatives. He will read the offer "take the money and do not vote" as confirmation: the system admits it does not need my vote. He takes it on principle — and the protocol loses those it was designed for.

**The blow.** The most direct — at 1f §7: what if the burned read the protocol as one more purchase?

**Answer.** The apartisan calculates — that is his defining property in Dalton himself. The share grows with the number of takers (001b, 040ae item 3): the more have taken, the dearer his vote, and he sees this in the first table. Taking B is a decision (040ag item 2), and the very fact that abstention is paid for is the first evidence in his life that the vote is worth something: "the vote is worth nothing today" (039b §4b) is for him a diagnosis, not a sentence. The protocol addresses him by interest, not by duty (1f §6b): not "come" but "your vote will count with a share". A sceptic of parties is not a sceptic of the weight of his own vote, and it is precisely the sceptic of parties who first understands that a share of ×2 is not a party promise but arithmetic in the charter (048m art. 4). Any apartisan who nevertheless takes B thereby reports that he does not need the vote — and that too is an honest answer (040ag item 2).

**Residual.** The first reading — "paid not to vote" (001d) — is the worst for the apartisan precisely because he reads fast; the defence is the reader's layer (1d, 1g), which answers before he closes the page.

## 6. What Dalton gives the protocol — the architect's move

"Dalton came very close and described the burned best of all." Three things nobody else in the arsenal has.

*The portrait is taken.* Educated, informed, without a party, sceptical of parties as representatives, often young, growing in number in all the old democracies — this is 1f §6, written ten years before the repository on sixty years of surveys. The protocol adds one step — exit — and one explanation of the step: the core cannot be outvoted (1f §5).

*The instrument is ready.* Dalton's two axes — cognitive mobilisation and partisan attachment — are taken with a standard questionnaire. The pilot (048l) can measure the four cells before adoption and after: from which cell B was taken, from which cell people returned. This is the only known way to confirm the existence of a class absent from the literature (1f §6b, weak point) — not by literature but by measurement.

*The forecasts diverge.* Dalton and the repository differ on one testable point: whether apartisans vote. If they do — the return is small, and the protocol removes only the lower cells; that is still a result (item 3), but a smaller one. If they do not — the return is large, and 1f §3 is confirmed. The pilot decides, and the repository undertakes to record either outcome.

## Weak point

A reconstruction; Dalton did not write about turnout rules and did not pose the question of exit — the transfer of his apartisans onto "the burned" is ours. The assistant recorded here the residual "it rests on the assumption that part of the apartisans stopped going, and until the pilot it is an assumption" — the architect withdrew it: "Think about why Ayn Rand's *Atlas Shrugged* is so popular. Do the apathetic read a fairy tale about Atlantis and spend mad money on three volumes of a kilo each?" Confirmation of the class need not wait for a survey — it has lain on the shop counter for seventy years. *Atlas Shrugged* (1957) is a novel about the producers leaving a system that lives on them, and the system collapsing; more than ten million copies, in the Library of Congress survey of 1991 the second most influential book after the Bible, a sales spike in 2009 when the country was being rescued at their expense. Twelve hundred pages the apathetic will neither buy nor read; the buyer is the one who recognises himself. The market has sold the burned a mirror for seventy years — that is the measurement, paid for by the measured themselves, and it is larger than any pilot. The residual is rewritten: the class is confirmed; the pilot measures not "whether it exists" but how much of it returns — and Dalton's instrument (§6) is for that. The figures on under-reported abstention in self-reports (10–20 points) come from the ANES literature, not from Dalton's own data.

*Rand and the protocol.* Rand gave the class a fairy tale, not a mechanism: her exit is total — to Galt's Gulch — and without return, and seventy years of readers received not a single rule. The protocol is the same exit, but with a price and with a return, and without the gulch: "Who is John Galt?" becomes "where is the ballot line" (048m art. 15). The size of the unmet demand is the print run.

*The catch — the architect's move.* The assistant wrote that Rand "gathered the American half of the class, with a libertarian leaning", and that the burned of the left "read something else". The architect: "There is a catch here too. The burned are not a party now. The populists pulled people to the sides and simply abandoned the middle." The error is in the word "leaning": the burned do not lie on the party axis, they are the middle both sides left behind, and left behind by the same arithmetic as in 1f §6b: the edges are cheap (there one frightens and brings in), the middle is expensive to persuade, and it was not even abandoned — they simply stopped addressing it. Fiorina showed this in the data: the parties polarised, not the voters; the middle went nowhere, its offer was taken away. The proof is in Rand herself: ten million readers — and the Libertarian Party, which tried for seventy years to collect them, takes one to three percent. The readers did not become a party because they are not a wing but the abandoned middle, which recognised a story of exit, not a label. Hence a correction to §6: Dalton's instrument measures cognitive mobilisation and attachment but not position on the left–right axis, and that is right — the burned have no axis, they have abandonment. The protocol does not invite them to become a party: it removes to B those for whose sake the parties went to the edges, and to win, the parties are left to return to where they have not been for seventy years (040ag item 5, power turns to the centre). And the figures on under-reported abstention in self-reports (10–20 points) come from the ANES literature, not from Dalton's own data.
