# 40z. Pierre Rosanvallon: *Counter-Democracy* — Five Arguments on Distrust as an Institution and on the Invisible People

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites

**Source:** the architect's assignment of 11 October 2026: "each — five arguments from his book, separately". The eighth is Rosanvallon: *Counter-Democracy: Politics in an Age of Distrust* (2006), *Democratic Legitimacy* (2008), *The Parliament of the Invisible* (2014). A reconstruction from the texts.

---

*How to read. Rosanvallon is about democracy not reducing to elections: the people also watch, block and judge, and live on distrust rather than faith. For the protocol this is a test of narrowness: is it too much about one circuit of four.*

## 1. The three circuits of counter-democracy: oversight, prevention, judgement

**The argument.** The people do not only elect — they watch (the press, ratings, investigations), block (protest, the veto of the street) and judge (courts, impeachments). Elections are a quarter of democracy, and the other three quarters grew precisely because the electoral quarter weakened.

**The strike.** The protocol changes only the electoral circuit; it does not touch the other three.

**The answer.** The protocol changes not the circuit but who is in all four. Those who stay at the polls are the same people who read the investigation, go to court and into the street; the apathetic was in none of the four (1f §5; 040s item 2). Rosanvallon described how oversight and judgement compensate for the weakness of elections; the protocol removes the reason for compensation — elections decided by the one who neither watches nor judges.

## 2. Distrust is not a disease but an institution

**The argument.** Counter-democracy is organised distrust; democracy lives on it, not on faith in the elected. To demand "trust in institutions" is to misunderstand what they stand on.

**The strike.** The protocol asks for trust in the secrecy of choice B (036e, Goldin item 6; 048m Art. 9 part 3).

**The answer.** The protocol is an institution of distrust in its pure form: a counter in place of faith, the precinct balance (048m Art. 10 parts 2–5) in place of a promise, double spending on the commission (Art. 5 part 3) in place of an oath. What must be trusted is not the government but arithmetic anyone can check; and the secrecy of B is protected by the same thing as the secrecy of the ballot — if that is not trusted, elections as such are not trusted either (001d, the statement on privacy). Rosanvallon wanted organised distrust; the protocol is its cheapest form: one line that adds up or does not.

## 3. Negative democracy: the people can say "no" but not "yes"

**The argument.** Counter-democracy blocks but does not build; hence impotence — a people that only obstructs does not rule.

**The strike.** The protocol is one more "no": exit.

**The answer.** Exit for the payment is the first "no" that is counted and costs, unlike non-turnout, which is neither counted nor costs (040t item 5). And the amplified vote of those who stay is the "yes" of those who stayed to build: the protocol for the first time separates "no" from "yes" and shows both on the counter (040w item 5, the promise of a future). Rosanvallon's negative democracy is impotent because its "no" is free and invisible; the protocol makes it paid and visible, and thereby finite.

## 4. The legitimacy of proximity, impartiality and reflexivity

**The argument** (*Democratic Legitimacy*). The electoral legitimacy of a one-off majority has worn away; three new ones are needed — independent bodies (impartiality), constitutional courts and public debate (reflexivity), a government that hears everyone (proximity).

**The strike.** The protocol strengthens exactly electoral legitimacy — the one that wore away.

**The answer.** The protocol gives electoral legitimacy what it lacked and what Rosanvallon compensated for from outside — a composition that is visible (040u item 2, equality among those who cast). And his independent bodies are the same question as the fifteenth decision (048k §3): who computes the median; the protocol does not reject impartial bodies, it gives them one checkable function instead of the general role of "guarantor".

## 5. The invisible people

**The argument** (*The Parliament of the Invisible*). People whom nobody represents or hears — not minorities but a majority without a voice; their invisibility is democracy's main deficit.

**The strike.** None — this is the argument closest to the repository of all eight.

**The answer.** The protocol makes the invisible visible not by words or a parliament but by money and the counter: the one who took the payment stops being "non-turnout" and becomes a budget line the government sees next to the contracts (040t item 2); the one who stayed gets a share that is visible. Rosanvallon wanted to give the invisible representation; the protocol gives them what is cheaper and more exact than representation — a price.

## Summary

| Rosanvallon's argument | What is true | Answer | What remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| three circuits | yes | not the circuit changes but who is in all four | — |
| distrust as institution | yes | the protocol is organised distrust: the counter, the precinct balance | the secrecy of B — like the secrecy of the ballot |
| negative democracy | yes | "no" becomes paid and visible; "yes" belongs to those who stay | — |
| legitimacy beyond elections | yes, compensation | a visible composition; impartial bodies — the fifteenth decision | — |
| the invisible people | yes, the closest | the invisible are given a price, not representation | — |

## Weak point

A reconstruction; Rosanvallon is about France and about legitimacy as an idea, and the protocol does not answer an idea: it answers with a composition, and Rosanvallon would say a composition without an idea is not legitimate — those voting with a ×3 share still have to explain why their decision binds those who took the payment. The repository's answer is Art. 5 part 2 (the choice repeats every election) and Art. 16 (rights do not depend on the choice) — but that is an answer by charter, not by legitimacy, and here Rosanvallon remains unpersuaded.
