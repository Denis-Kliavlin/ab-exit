*How to read. Paine asks any strategy one question: do ends match means, and is there a theory of victory. Half of her framework stands behind the protocol (a negative objective is cheaper than a positive one, institutions outlive leaders, a dictator fails for lack of an honest signal); half seems to strike it (rules are written by whoever can enforce them; in war security overrides price; a maritime rule in a continental country). The three blows are removed by the architect's moves, not by the assistant's answers. §7 examines her five pillars of the path to a world war.*

## 1. Ten theses

| No. | Paine's thesis | What is true today | What the protocol does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A maritime power lives by common rules and positive sums (trade); a continental one by spheres of influence and zero sums (territory) | The electoral system is built the continental way: votes are taken, people are brought in, territory is divided into districts and "cores" | The protocol is a maritime rule for the hall: one rule for all, a positive sum (the taker gets money, the stayer weight, nobody loses, [040bj §3](040bj-machiavelli.md)); no seizure, only exchange |
| 2 | A negative objective ("prevent") is cheaper and achievable by one's own means; a positive one ("impose") requires the opponent's consent | Reforms set positive objectives: make the voter conscious, turnout high | The protocol sets a negative one: that the bought and brought-in vote should not decide the outcome. It is achieved without the consent of those who buy, by one right, and it requires re-educating nobody |
| 3 | A dictator fails not from stupidity but from the absence of an honest signal: nobody tells him the truth, so he cannot correct course | The autocrat receives reports, not reality ([040m](040m-cold-reader-audit.md)) | The counter ([048m art. 9](../08-implementation/048m-charter.md)) is the one number that cannot be faked by a report: it is paid out in money and reconciled with payments (art. 10). The signal the dictator is denied by design, the protocol gives to everyone, him included |
| 4 | Institutions matter more than leaders: Japan died of an institutional defect (an army outside civilian control, gekokujō), not of ill will | Elections rest on people: commissions, courts, payroll lists | The charter is fourteen articles changed only by referendum (art. 14); the count is given not to a commission but to the choice and the payment ([048i](../08-implementation/048i-three-channels-parallel-count.md)); the rule does not depend on who holds power |
| 5 | A theory of victory: without a clear path from operational successes to a political settlement, battlefield victories lead to catastrophe | Protest movements win operationally (the street) and lose strategically ([057h](../09-other-countries/057h-serbia-2026.md)) | The protocol has a theory of victory: courtyard → city → country ([048l](../08-implementation/048l-courtyard-pilot.md)), private referendum → underdog → article of law ([048n](../08-implementation/048n-underdog-questions.md), "forbidden, but possible"); each step is a settlement, not a battle |
| 6 | Continental powers treat population as expendable; maritime powers count the price of a life and so build smaller, better armies | Turnout is built as mobilisation: bodies at the ballot boxes, each at a price of zero | The protocol puts a price on everyone: D; the hall becomes "a smaller, better army", where everyone came of his own will and weighs more |
| 7 | Empire does not pay: seizing territory is a negative sum, wealth comes from trade within rules | Seizing the electorate with packages and payroll lists does not pay: the price rises, loyalty does not | An open price with a ceiling is cheaper than covert seizure ([1g](../01-introduction/001g-objections-index.md); [040bm §2](040bm-buchanan.md)); the politician moves from conquest to trade — persuading on the merits (057h §2.1) |
| 8 | An operational solution to a strategic problem fails: the levels must be distinguished | Electoral reforms are operational: ranked choice, districting, turnout | The protocol enters at the strategic level: first it changes the hall's composition and does not touch the procedure, so as to pass as an addition ([048k, decision 9](../08-implementation/048k-charter-inventory.md)); after adoption and the pilot, a hall that already counts improves the count and the procedure itself (048k, second addition to decision 9). The order of levels by Paine: strategy first, operations after |
| 9 | A continental regime needs an external enemy for internal legitimacy | The enemy image is the main device ([040br](040br-ten-propaganda-devices.md), device 2) | "Where is my share" is a question an enemy cannot drown: it is nonpolitical and lies on the card ([056f §13i](../09-other-countries/056f-russia-september-2026.md)) |
| 10 | Dictatorships break on succession: there is no mechanism of peaceful transfer | The autocrat cannot leave, because leaving is defeat | The protocol gives a weak elite a lawful exit: to offer the people a nonpolitical rule is easier than to fight for power ([40bh §5](040bh-q-change-from-above.md); 057h §2.2) |

## 2. The first blow: rules are written by whoever can enforce them

Paine: the maritime order was held by a navy; a rule with no force behind it is paper. Who will force an autocrat to count honestly?

**The architect's move (1 November 2026).** "Against force, only force. But today the autocrat rests on popular support, and from outside it is hard to push him to leave or to push the people to a coup. The protocol weakens the autocrat's support in the masses and gives the elite a way to remove the autocrat nonpolitically. This changes percentages, not a hundred-per-cent option, but even that is better than it was."

The autocrat's strength is not in force but in support: the core plus the brought-in, stretched to a majority (057h §2.4). While that support stands, neither external pressure nor the elite takes the risk: a coup against a man with 78 % turnout looks like a coup against the people. The protocol does not take the force away; it shows what the force rests on: the brought-in take the money and leave by themselves, the share of takers by district is public, and the elite sees for the first time a number it can follow without the risk of standing against the people — and gets a nonpolitical path: to offer a rule, not a coup ([40bh §5](040bh-q-change-from-above.md); 057h §2.2 on Vučić). The protocol changes probabilities, not outcomes: an autocrat with an honest signal about his support leaves earlier and more cheaply than an autocrat with a painted turnout. The residue is the same as with Lenin ([040bq §3](040bq-lenin.md)): force is cancelled only by force; the protocol is a defence not against a coup but against the illusion of support on which both the autocrat and the elite's inaction rest.

## 3. The second blow: in war, security overrides price

Paine: when survival is at stake, the negative objective "survive" subordinates everything, and money will not change a frightened man's vote.

**The architect's move (1 November 2026).** "The autocrat slowly and imperceptibly boils the frog by changing laws, then brings things to a crisis or a war, and then rules the country in a critical situation by managing fear. The protocol changes exactly the slow boiling and makes it visible. And reaching a crisis becomes harder when everyone can see the crisis approaching. And at the moment of war, politics does not work anywhere even now."

The blow lands on a stage the protocol does not claim: in war no electoral system works, and that is a property of war, not a defect of the protocol. The protocol works on the stage before: where the autocrat changes laws one at a time over years, shifts dates, numbers the register, and no single change is worth going into the street (057h §2.5: the blind zone of the short horizon; [013g](../03-theory/013g-decay-of-democracy.md)). Today the boiling is invisible because the only signal, turnout, is painted. Under the protocol every step of the boiling shows in the share of takers by district in the next cycle, and the crisis stops creeping: it is visible two or three cycles before it becomes a war, that is, while it can still be stopped by a referendum rather than by bodies. Serbia 2026 showed the path without the protocol: noticed when things got very bad; the protocol moves the moment of "noticed" years earlier. If the frog is already boiled and the war is on, the protocol shows fear as a number — at 78 % turnout the share of takers would have said how many came from fear — but it does not change the outcome; its work was before.

## 4. The third blow: a maritime rule in a continental country

Paine: a country of continental culture will not accept a positive-sum rule.

**The architect's move (1 November 2026).** "The protocol is not culture at all and is understood by any nation. There is the country's wealth, it comes from taxes and from the ground, and all of it already belongs to the people, and the people take their money by law. Where is the difference here between Japan, the USA or Sweden?"

The division into maritime and continental rests on where a country's wealth comes from: trade or territory. The protocol does not touch that question: it takes wealth that already exists, from taxes and from the ground, and returns a share of it to its owner, the citizen, by law and by his own choice. This operation requires neither a culture of trust, nor a tradition of self-government, nor a maritime horizon; it requires tax records and a card, which exist in Tokyo, Washington and Stockholm alike. The difference between countries shows not in whether the rule is accepted but in what the counter shows: in one country 40 % take, in another 60 %, and that is no longer culture but measurement ([040bp](040bp-gumilev.md)). Where the rule is unfamiliar, it is unfamiliar, not wrong ([040bo](040bo-montaigne.md), thesis 1). And her own example: Japan adopted foreign institutions within one generation once the old ones stopped paying; the protocol is simpler than any of them, because it introduces nothing foreign — one's own treasury, one's own taxes, one's own choice.

## 5. What Paine gives the protocol

The language of strategy: a negative objective instead of a positive one, the strategic level instead of the operational, a theory of victory instead of hope in the street. The main diagnosis of dictatorship — the absence of an honest signal — which the protocol closes with one number; this is stronger than any argument about fairness, because it is said in the language of those who decide. And the five pillars of the path to war (§7), four of which the protocol touches directly.

## 7. Five pillars of the path to war and what the protocol does with them

The interview "Diary of a CEO", 29 October 2026. Paine does not use the word "inevitable": "we're potentially barreling our way into a world war, and it looks like World War II" (04:40; 1:01:00). To the reproach "people say that every year" she answers with the framework of negative objectives: how many times a war was prevented cannot be proved. The path rests on five pillars; all five are defects of signal and exit, not defects of force.

| No. | Pillar (time) | What Paine says | What the protocol does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Dark personalities" running large countries** (04:30; 1:02:30) | "Reckless people running some very large and important countries." Putin "doesn't care how many people he kills." "We human beings have real trouble identifying dark personalities until they become Adolf Hitler" | **The architect's move:** "Responsible and intelligent people see these personalities at once, but their voice drowns among the apathetic without a horizon; dark personalities are visible on a long horizon. The protocol solves both problems: the apathetic leave, and the thinking receive a vote ×2." The problem is not recognition but counting: there were always enough who saw; they were outvoted. The protocol does not teach recognition — it stops drowning those who have recognised. And this is its origin: the rule was conceived in Russia in 2014, when those who saw watched the laws being changed for war and could outvote nothing ([1e](../01-introduction/001e-by-role.md), "Why it was conceived") |
| 2 | **Wars are easy to start and impossible to end: the loser doubles down** (04:40; 1:03:40) | "Whoever's losing doesn't want to lose, so they keep doubling down long after they've lost." Her remedy for the US: "the next Congress — impeach and convict him, and then just say, 'Sweetheart, you're retired'" | **The architect's move:** "Only knowing that the current populist rulers will not take harsh measures against the aggressor." Doubling down is not the loser's psychology but a calculation on the opponent's hall: the aggressor doubles because on the other side a populist elected by the apathetic rules and there will be no harsh measures. The protocol changes the aggressor's calculation through the other hall: a government elected by people with a stake and a long horizon takes the measures a populist postpones, and the aggressor knows it before he doubles. Deterrence moves from the arsenal into the hall's composition. And her impeachment the protocol makes a standing procedure: the hall shows the leader and his elite the number of their support every cycle (§2) |
| 3 | **The order rested on norms, and the norms are gone** (17:00–18:30) | Traffic rules: "there aren't enough policemen ever" if everyone decides to drive as they please. Her example of a norm is literally electoral: "when you lose an election, you congratulate the other side and say, I will do better next time — instead of 'oh no, they cheated'" | The most direct link. The protocol replaces the norm with accounting: the result is reconciled with payments (art. 10), the stuffing reserve falls from 40–50 % to about 3 %, and "they cheated" loses its ground not because the loser is noble but because he has nowhere to stand: every record is confirmed by a receipt. On 29 October 2026 Likud said "stolen by the Arab parties" exactly as she describes; under the protocol the phrase is checkable within a day. A norm that rested on people is replaced by a norm that rests on bookkeeping |
| 4 | **The generation that remembered the war is dead** (1:00:30; 1:02:20) | The Chinese after Mao did not want more wars, "but all those people who did all that fighting are all dead." NATO's Article 5 worked "while the generation who remembered World War II lived; that's not true of who we've got now" | The memory of war the protocol cannot replace and does not try to. It keeps something else in the hall: the long horizon ([016](../04-electoral-dynamics/016-age-economics.md)). Today's vote from fear, the one that drives escalation, is a short-horizon vote; it takes the money. Those who remain are the ones who will live with the consequences for forty years — the only substitute for memory that can be arranged by a rule rather than by loss |
| 5 | **Prevention gets no credit, victory does** (05:00–06:30) | Governments "are primarily about negative objectives: preventing disasters", but "on a normal day it's normal, so you think, well, my government hasn't done anything for me lately. Untrue." A prevented war cannot be proved, so the politician chooses a visible "victory" | **The architect's move:** "Only the apathetic with a short horizon do not value a prevented war, because they cannot count the consequences of war in the long run. Those who count will count and take measures. This is clearly visible in insurance: the poor do not insure life and property, while the intelligent and the middle class do." The numbers (US): life insurance is held by 27 % of households with income under $20,000 and by 85 % with income over $100,000 (Financial Health Network, 2024); property insurance among renters — 25 % at income under $30,000 against 69 % over $75,000; 32 % among the "financially vulnerable" against 71 % among the "financially healthy". The price of the policy does not explain the gap, and that is the architect's correction: the premium is proportional to the insured sum, cheap property is insured cheaply, and the share of income spent on a policy is about the same for the poor and the middle class; were it a matter of price the gap would be small, and it is threefold. So the poor man does not insure his cheap home not because he is poor but because he does not count in the long run. The residue — the "poverty premium" (the same cover costs more in a poor district because of risk zoning) — is tens of per cent, not threefold. A negative objective gets no credit not in general but in a hall without a horizon; a hall that insures its own home will value a prevented war too — it is the same operation: pay now for what will not happen. And D = p × M grows with the median: a government that merely kept the economy from falling shows it to everyone on the card ([040bn](040bn-hayek.md), thesis 7). Prevention gets, for the first time, a voter who counts it |

**What insurers' research says about the logic of the uninsured** (the architect's question: the industry wants to insure everyone and must have commissioned such studies). The stated reason and the revealed one diverge just as in elections ([036e §4](036e-nobel-fifth-circle.md)). The stated reason is "too expensive": the Geneva Association's 2024 survey of 28,000 households in seven advanced economies puts affordability first in every group, especially at low incomes; Swiss Re finds the same in Asia. The revealed reason is horizon, and the classics of insurance economics showed it themselves. Kunreuther (1978): people do not buy flood insurance even when it is subsidised, with premiums below actuarial cost — the price is below fair and there is still no demand. Kunreuther and Pauly (*Insurance and Behavioral Economics*, 2013): people treat a low probability as zero ("it won't happen to me"), buy insurance only after suffering a loss and cancel it after several years without claims, because they regard it as an investment that "did not pay off" rather than as protection; to this are added present bias, status quo bias, search costs and product complexity. The industry's practical conclusion is the protocol's: behaviour is changed not by persuasion but by the frame of the decision — when the premium was renamed a contribution to a mutual support fund, uptake among the poor rose. So "too expensive" is the answer on the questionnaire, "I do not count in the long run" is the behaviour; the threefold gap by income is explained by the second, and a residue of price exists only at the very bottom of the income scale.

**In one sentence.** Dark personalities in power (1) cannot exit the wars they are losing (2); the norms that restrained them died with the generation (3, 4); and those who prevent get nothing for it (5). The protocol strikes four of the five: it gives a signal about the leader's support, an exit to the elite, accounting instead of norms, and credit for prevention; with memory it does not argue — it substitutes the horizon. Her own way out (44:00) — "deploy your diplomats and lawyers to international institutions; sure, the deal isn't perfect, but the wealth will compound if people your age have laws that allow them to build instead of trying to poach everyone's stuff" — is the maritime logic of positive sums, and the protocol is its domestic version: not to rob the hall but to trade with it openly.

## Weak point

Paine did not write about elections or the domestic institutions of democracies; her framework of states and wars is transferred to the hall by analogy. Theses 3, 9, 10 and the whole of §7 are taken from oral lectures and an interview, not from the books, and are paraphrased from subtitles. The instrument's boundary lies in two places, both named by her: in war no electoral system works, the protocol works before war; the memory of war is not replaced by a rule, only the horizon is. The insurance statistics are American; the income gap in other countries has the same sign (emerging markets hold 16 % of world premiums at 40 % of world GDP) but its size is not checked.
