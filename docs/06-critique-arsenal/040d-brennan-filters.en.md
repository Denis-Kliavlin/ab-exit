## 0. The question this section asks

"Is there any way today to effectively switch off people who ended up at the polls by accident — other than non-turnout?" The answer: historically all attempts fall into two classes — artificial friction and direct exclusion. None works without the risk of civil war or accusations of fascism. Below — the catalogue, the diagnosis, and the point where AB-EXIT leaves that axis.

## 1. The catalogue of filters and why each breaks

| Filter | Who | Mechanics | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Epistocracy** | Jason Brennan, "Against Democracy" (2016) | A ballot only for those who pass an economics/politics exam | Whoever writes the test takes the power: the elite filters out the opposition. Direct violation of anti-discrimination constitutions; in the US — the durable association with Jim Crow literacy tests |
| **Tax census / skin in the game** | Heinlein (literary), Taleb (as principle) | Only net contributors to the budget vote | Open division into patricians and plebeians; disenfranchising dependent strata → street riots; the security apparatus refuses to defend the system, because police and soldiers are themselves state employees |
| **Engineering of difficulty** (voter suppression) | Practice, not theory | Voting on a workday, fewer polling stations in poor areas, complex registration | A filter not for intellect but for fanaticism: a six-hour queue weeds out the busy engineer but not the unemployed fanatic or the paid activist |
| **Quadratic voting** | Weyl, Posner; popularised by Buterin | A budget of credits, the n-th vote costs n² | Psychologically unbearable for the mass: nobody sits with a calculator choosing a mayor. It does not remove populists — it changes their tactics |
| **Sortition / deliberation** | Van Reybrouck, "Against Elections" (2016) | Parliament by lot, like a jury; 500 random citizens hear experts | Legitimacy: a random person answers to nobody; the experts are picked by the organiser; scales to a council, not a state |
| **Futarchy** | Hanson | Vote on values, bet on methods | Abstract for the ordinary person (see 040b) |

The common denominator: **all methods are top-down exclusion.** The state tells the citizen: "you are stupid / poor / lazy — we take your vote". That breeds hatred and destroys legitimacy, because the decision about a person's worth is made by someone else.

## 2. Brennan: a correct diagnosis, a stillborn prescription

Brennan honestly did what liberal political science avoids: he divided voters into "hobbits" (who do not care about politics) and "hooligans" (fans of their parties) and said the "vulcans" (the rational) are a minority. The protocol agrees with the diagnosis. The prescription — an exam — relocates the problem: someone above decides who is worthy.

**Knockout by AB-EXIT.** Brennan's task (filtering out the incompetent) is solved without exams and without coercion: a price tag is set. The "hobbits" voluntarily take the money and leave. Those for whom influence is worth more than money voluntarily refuse. No commission, no test, no sorting of people into grades — and the filter is reversible every cycle (§13.9). A person leaves the polling station not humiliated but satisfied, with money on the card. The mechanism reaches the goals of a hard census (those who create value at the helm) through the interface most pleasant to the crowd.

## 3. Not a "buy-out from below" but a phase transition

The first formulation in the dialogue — "AB-EXIT is a bottom-up buyout: the state makes an offer and buys the vote". The architect rejected it: this compares "−1 with +1", when in fact it is a crossing of zero, a bifurcation point.

The purchase-and-sale logic keeps everyone in the old paradigm (seller, buyer, goods) and invites the critics' questions "what if the state goes bankrupt and cannot buy?", "is this not a hidden bribe?". The real nature is a change of aggregate state: water at 0 °C is not "very cold water" but ice with a different lattice. The dividend is not the price of a vote but **activation energy**. The person pressing the button does not sell the vote to the state — the state does not need it, the vote is annulled. It is an act of **separation**: heavy fractions (the apathetic majority) separate from light ones (the motivated core), and the core crystallises a new system of governance. In a transaction both parties stay on the market; here the dividend-taker drops out of the choice of managers (but not out of referendums on the rules — 048g.6b) into rentier status — from an element of governance to an element of the balance sheet. A pitch metaphor: not buying the ore-bearing rock but washing the ore, where the dividend is merely the reagent that makes the rock separate without resistance. (Protocol language: "they sell, we do not buy" — see 033c in 07.)

## 4. The poor are not stupid: the pragmatism of an imposed game

The architect's clarification that strengthens the section: "keep lying to them, promising golden mountains — they are not stupid, they know it too". Voters know perfectly well that politicians lie; they vote for populists not from stupidity but because it is a rational strategy in a broken game. If a person is stuck in loans and the choice is a technocrat who promises nothing versus a populist who promises $5,000 or debt forgiveness, the expected value from the populist is higher: "if he delivers even 10 % of the lies, I'll buy the kids winter boots; I have nothing to lose".

Traditional democracy humiliates people: it makes them play a spectacle of belief in "sacred duty". AB-EXIT is the first political technology that treats the mass voter as a smart, adult, rational agent: "We know you are no fool and believe not a word. Enough of the fake lottery — here is an honest deal." *Precisely because they are not stupid, the mechanism fires instantly:* a politician's lie may not come true; a smart contract executes in a second. The smart person chooses guaranteed cash over a promise from the podium — and power flows to the builders without a shot.

## 5. The taboo: political children

There is a question classical political science sidesteps: we do not let 14-year-olds vote because "the brain is not formed", but we do let a 45-year-old who believes the first swindler he meets, signs a contract unread and cannot say "no" to a man in uniform. Opposition speakers are forced to live in a split: in videos about how the state deceives people they address the people as children to be saved by the hand; in videos about elections — the same people as wise citizen-co-creators. To ask "why are these children admitted to elections" in public is impossible — it wrecks the dogma of "one person, one vote".

AB-EXIT solves the problem of infantilism without IQ tests: a gravitational separator. The "children" choose the immediate reward and leave the room where decisions are made — satisfied. The "adults" (long horizon, critical thinking) look at the cheque and say "my influence is worth more". And if the adults steer so badly that the toys run out (the dividend falls), the children come back and sweep them away. (The Russian context of this observation — 056d.)

## 6. The diagnostic elite

"Gaaze described the problem but gave no solution?" — Yes. Nor did Acemoglu, nor Fukuyama. Modern political science has become an elite diagnostic service: it describes with stunning depth how populism and autocracy kill the political organism, and to "how do we treat it?" replies with incantations — "develop civil society", "educate the voter", "protect the press". That is treating a fracture with a moral plantain leaf. The three radical prescriptions of the last 10–15 years (epistocracy, quadratic voting, sortition) are the only honest attempts, and all three break against human nature because they require the ordinary person to become smarter. AB-EXIT is the only one that does not.

## 7. Weak point of the answer

The catalogue proves that *prohibitive* filters do not work; it does not prove that a *compensatory* filter selects the competent rather than merely the well-off. The answer via wealth structure (85 % of refusers are self-made, 040b.5) is statistical, not mechanical. The only mechanical protection is the thermostat: if the selected govern badly, the selection repeats. 🟡
