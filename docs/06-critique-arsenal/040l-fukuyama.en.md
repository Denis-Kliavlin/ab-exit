## 1. How his position changed

| When | What he said |
|---|---|
| 1989–92 | Liberal democracy is the end point of the history of ideas: it has no rival idea. Not a forecast of universal victory but a claim about the absence of an alternative |
| 2006 | The break with the neoconservatives over Iraq |
| 2014 | Political decay: vetocracy — so many veto points that decisions are not taken; the state is captured by insiders for insiders |
| 2018 | Revolt comes not from poverty but from thymos — the need for recognition of dignity; as a remedy he proposed a shared civic identity and national service |
| 2025–26 | The state lacks capacity: there are too many procedures, the bureaucracy should be trusted more and held to results. He calls himself a social democrat, with qualifications |
| 2026 | A return to Nietzsche's "last man": the sated revolt out of boredom, "for the sake of struggle"; no recipe of his own in the open parts |

## 2. What he would object to the protocol

**Dignity is not for sale.** To pay for non-participation is to tell a person how much his vote is worth. This is Kant's objection, and it is removed with his own weapon in 040j: to forbid a person to dispose of his vote for his own good is paternal despotism; the right is untouched, the price is put on the act.

**Equal recognition.** The core of liberal democracy for him is equality of recognition. A different effective weight of votes he would read as legalised superiority. The answer: the protocol has no norm "a vote counts for three", each ballot counts as one (033c §9b); exit is voluntary and reversible; the option is the same for everyone.

**Revolt cannot be bought.** If people vote for the wrecker out of boredom and a thirst for recognition, money will not lead them away. This objection is analysed separately.

## 3. The sated bored rebel: the analysis and the architect's answer

Fukuyama's figure is a well-off person whom nothing threatens and who votes for the wrecker "to show them". The assistant took it for a place where two rules of the repository diverge: by stake (001b §3) he has none, so he takes the money; by the simulator's formula (055c §6.3b) the payment is small relative to his income, so he stays. From this the assistant concluded that the yardstick "desirable to 40–50 % of the population" does not capture the well-off impulsive voter.

The architect's answer removes the figure entirely:

- **A populist always threatens the well-off** — with higher taxes. If a person earned his money honestly, he is not a fool and he counts that. He has a stake, and it is personal.
- **The truly bored are below one per cent**, and what they do at elections does not affect the outcome.
- **Money against free swagger.** The rebel is bored because he wants to show himself; given a choice between money and a free gesture, most will take the money.
- **He stayed — he has already shown it.** "If he is a real rebel, he goes to vote, and he has already shown the crowd that took the money that he is the fine fellow." The need for recognition is met by the fact itself: he is among those who paid for the vote.

The architect's conclusion: "after AB-EXIT there is no such person at all — the bored rebel".

The assistant objected to the first premise: a right populist does not threaten the well-off but promises them a tax cut. The architect's answer: "true, they *promise*; and what do they actually do?" The deed diverges from the promise. Orbán delivered a flat income tax — and at the same time the highest VAT in the European Union at 27 %, the nationalisation of private pension savings in 2010–11, "crisis" taxes on banks, retail and telecoms, windfall taxes in 2022, and state contracts for his own. Trump cut taxes — and imposed tariffs paid by the same business and the same buyer. The person who earned honestly loses under a populist of any colour: one takes through the rate, the other through a levy, a nationalisation, or a contract that goes to a crony. The well-off always have a stake, and it is against the populist. And second, the main point: "the populist always wins in the present system of voters — otherwise AB-EXIT would not be needed; Orbáns come to power and will go on coming". The rebel out of boredom has nothing to do with it: the populist is brought in not by him but by the free mass, which is exactly what the protocol leads out.

The consequence for the formal statement (013f). No separate "value of the gesture" is needed: the stake is everything for which a person is willing to forgo the payment, be it taxes, influence on the outcome or the wish to be among the voters. The motive does not matter; the willingness to pay does. The divergence "takes by stake, does not take by formula" disappears: income acts through the value of money, and that is the second assumption of 013f.

## 4. Where he describes the world before

**The blind spot: he idealised not the voter but the form.** The architect's question: "was it the idealisation of the voter's choice and rationality that failed Fukuyama, and had he no protection against the apathetic and the populists?" In the main, yes, with a refinement. He did not think the voter rational — the risk is in the title of the book. He took the form itself for the end point: universal equal suffrage is, for him, the recognition of everyone's dignity, and so the composition of the voters is a given that is not discussed. Hence he looked for every breakdown anywhere but in the voter: in 2014 the institutions and the elites were to blame, in 2018 identity, in 2025–26 a weak bureaucracy, in 2026 human nature itself. All his remedies are on the supply side; on the demand side — who votes and at what price — there is nothing. Protection against the free apathetic mass and against whoever mobilises it cheaply is absent from every one of his books. He could not see it: any filter — a property qualification, an exam, epistocracy — breaks equal recognition, the core of his theory, and he rightly refuses filters. The protocol closes this place without touching the core: the right is equal for all, exit is voluntary and reversible, a ballot counts as one; the composition changes not by prohibition but because free abstention and the free vote stop being free. His last cause — human nature — is already a surrender: nature cannot be repaired. The protocol's answer is that what needs repair is not nature but the price.

**His "last man" is our apathetic and our burned.** A person whose vote weighs one millionth and changes nothing is precisely the creature without struggle or meaning. Boredom in Fukuyama is a consequence of a weightless vote, not of satiety (011b, 029.10).

**His "struggle for the sake of struggle" is a free gesture.** Voting out of spite is cheap while the vote decides nothing. After the protocol the gesture acquires a price tag — forgoing the payment — and the vote acquires weight. Whoever stayed has paid and answers for the outcome; this is the sign of the thermostat (015b §6b): a person who refused the money in order to vote against brings the power a real grievance.

**The protocol gives thymos a peaceful outlet.** To the active — weight, that is recognition by deed; being among the voters becomes a distinction. To the one who leaves — for the first time he was asked, and he personally was paid. The national service Fukuyama proposed is the same door for the Hero as in 040k, only through mobilisation.

**He found no cure for vetocracy.** He wants to hold the state to results instead of procedures, but he has no result-number. A payment tied to the median income is such a number: it reaches everyone, does not depend on a poll and does not "recover" from a change in the parliament's owner (029.10).

**The end of history gets its missing part.** By his own theory liberal democracy has no rival, only internal decay. The protocol proposes no rival — it repairs the decay from within, with one norm. This is what his theory requires and what he himself did not supply.

## 5. Verdict (reconstruction)

> "For thirty years I explained that liberal democracy has no rival and that it is being killed by the boredom of its own citizens. You say the boredom comes from a weightless vote, and you offer the vote weight and the gesture a price. If that is so, my last man is not the outcome of history but an unfinished piece of construction. I am not sure. But I have no other cure."

## 6. Weak point

The position of 2026 is assembled from reviews and the open beginnings of the texts; the assistant has not read the full memoir, and if its closed part contains a reform proposal of his own, the comparison will have to be redone. The estimate "the truly bored are below one per cent" is the architect's judgement, not a measurement; there is one way to measure it — to see in a pilot how many of the well-off forgo the payment for the sake of a vote "against". 🟡
