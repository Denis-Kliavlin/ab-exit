# 30b. Ten Tricks of the Campaign Trade: Before the Protocol and Under It

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites
**File:** 06_030b · v1 · 12 October 2026
**Source:** the architect's assignment of 12 October 2026: "find ten winning tricks that campaign technologists like to use at elections, and let us examine them under the protocol — before and after". The tricks are international and Russian, the ones the trade is proud of; each is examined on its own: how it works today, what the rule does to it, what remains. A supplement to 030 (the six mechanisms of the elites): there — who orders, here — what they execute with.

---

*How to read. Campaign technology is the art of working with those who decide without thinking: "the brought-in", "the layer", "the meek". The protocol removes them from the decision for a payment, and the trade loses its material. One question to each trick: whom is it designed for — and is that person in the hall under the protocol.*

## The ten tricks

| № | Trick | How it works today | Under the protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bringing in "the core"** — the employer, the bus, "check in", the quota | the free turnout of the dependent decides the outcome; the main instrument of power in autocracies and of party machines in democracies | **rises from zero to D per vote and becomes visible — but survives under direct supervision:** the secrecy of B holds at home and in the treasury, not at the commission's window — the taker is refused a ballot, and the organiser sees it (Art. 5 part 3; the remark on Art. 5 in 048m); so a dependent voter under direct control can be forced not to take, but the price of such a vote is at least D plus coercion (Art. 11), paid by the organiser, not the budget; a precinct with full turnout and zero B is an anomaly on the balance (040ag item 4). Step by step — §4 |
| 2 | **Drying up** the opponent's turnout — negativity, despondency, "they are all the same", "your vote decides nothing" | cheaper than mobilising one's own: persuade the others not to come | **turns over:** the one who does not come takes the payment and is counted; "your vote decides nothing" is false by arithmetic (1d, statement 12); there is nobody left to dry — those who left are already paid and counted |
| 3 | **Spoilers and doubles** — a namesake, a fake party, a clone candidate | works on the layer and the inattentive: names confused, the others' vote split | **weakens, does not die:** "those who stay" read and recognise the double; but splitting votes between similar candidates is arithmetic, not attentiveness, and a spoiler still works against the thinking. An honest residual — §2 |
| 4 | **Microtargeting and dark ads** — segments by fears, posts the rest do not see | strikes short attention — those who decide last by image (040x item 3) | **loses its target** (040aa item 2): those the feed is designed for are not in the hall; on "the thinking" targeting works only by proof, and proof is not a trick |
| 5 | **Last-week dirt** — no time to rebut | the layer learns from an ad and decides; Platner, Paxton (059g item 3) | **loses its buyer:** a headline without a document is noise to "the thinking"; a document works harder and earlier, because "the remembering" check (040x item 5) |
| 6 | **Pre-election gifts from the budget** — indexation, a one-off payment, a road; the political budget cycle (036e §2, Nordhaus) | works on the dependent and the short horizon: "they gave — you vote" | **expensive and useless:** the dependent took B and the gift will not pay off; "those who stay" count whose money the gift is and remember; the only lawful "gift" is raising p, and that only by referendum once a cycle (Art. 2 part 2) |
| 7 | **Carving the rules** — districts, the polling date (August in Ohio), the threshold, the roll | the cheapest lever: whoever writes the law wins (029 §29.11) | **loses its purpose:** the protocol's rule is by referendum only (Arts. 14–15); districts can be carved, the composition cannot, because the composition selects itself; the roll turns from a weapon into a queue (059h, 057f) |
| 8 | **Stuffing, carousels, dead souls** — the reservoir of non-voters | works where silent records exist, and is irrefutable where there are many | **stuffing on silent records disappears** — the double spend (Art. 5 part 3), the precinct balance (Art. 10), and with it the irrefutability of "we were robbed" (1i); **the carousel** — coercion under supervision — does not disappear but becomes dearer and visible (§4) |
| 9 | **The enemy image and the wedge** — the migrant, "them", the fear of disappearance | mobilises hysteria with a short horizon (040w item 3) | **misses:** hysteria is for sale for the payment, real fear is not; the wedge hits those already at home with the money; those who stay are the ones it is costly to frighten |
| 10 | **Astroturfing and networks** — paid "volunteers", fake movements, bots, a "people's initiative" from headquarters | an imitation of mass for the layer and the press | **loses its audience and its competitor:** "the burned" are a real network that pays itself (059g §4), and a paid one is visible against them; bots take no payment and cast no vote — the counter does not count them |

**Summary.** Of the ten tricks the protocol kills two outright (drying up, stuffing on silent records), devalues six because their target — the layer and the brought-in — leaves the hall, reprices one from zero to D per vote (bringing in and the carousel — §4) and leaves one — spoilers. What remains of the trade is the part that works on the thinking — and that is no longer tricks but arguments (040af item 4).

## 2. Two honest residuals

**Spoilers.** Splitting votes between similar candidates does not depend on the voter's attentiveness, only on arithmetic: two honest candidates of one camp divide "those who stay" as they would have divided the layer. The answer is not in the protocol but in the electoral system — ranked voting, a second round — which the protocol does not touch (040x item 2) and with which it is compatible (040t item 4). Under the protocol the namesake double dies, the spoiler-by-arithmetic lives.

**Trick 6 in a new form.** A candidate promising to raise p is not vote-buying under the charter (048n: the budget pays under a rule adopted by referendum, not the candidate), but it is a promise to "those who stay" at the expense of their own tax, and they will count it: at 30 % taking, raising p gives them a share of ×1.4 instead of ×1.3 and a minus from their pocket. A promise to raise the payment is the first campaign promise with a price printed on the ballot.

## 3. The general principle

Each of the ten tricks is a way of getting a decision from someone who does not decide: bring him, dry him up, frighten him, fool him with a name, show him what others do not see, give him a gift before the vote. All ten share one premise — that the hall holds those who decide without thinking, and many of them. The protocol bans none of the tricks; it pays their target to leave, and the trade is left with instruments and no material. That is "price, not punishment" (040ab) applied to campaign technology: the technologists are not jailed, they stop being paid.

## 4. The carousel step by step, with motives — the architect's correction

The first version of the table said bringing in "dies" because B is taken in secret. The architect: "here you misunderstood the secrecy of the payment: he can no longer vote — he is marked on the list and will not be given a ballot, and that will be visible; the carousel cannot be killed one hundred per cent." Correct: Art. 5 part 3 extinguishes the taker's vote, and with a secret paper ballot only the *issuance* of a ballot can be extinguished — the remark on Art. 5 in 048m records this. Step by step.

**The carousel today.** The organiser — a boss, a foreman, a "network" — gathers the dependent by fear or pay; hands out a pre-filled ballot; the voter at the precinct receives a blank one, drops the pre-filled one, carries the blank out as proof, gets his mark or money. Motives: the organiser gets a guaranteed vote at the price of a day off; the voter keeps his allowance or gets a thousand; the commission does not look. The price of a brought-in vote is near zero, because fear is free.

**The carousel under the protocol, charter as it stands.** The third step breaks for the taker of B: no ballot is issued, and the organiser sees it. The secrecy of B holds at home and in the treasury (Art. 9 part 3), not at the commission's window. By motive:

- *The organiser* learns who took — after the fact, at the precinct; he cannot tell in advance, he can punish afterwards. His demand is "do not take B, come, show the ballot", and for a dependent under direct control the secrecy does not work: the carousel lives for those who can be marched to the window.
- *The voter* chooses between D in his account and fear; before, fear stood against zero, now against D. For him not to take, the threat must cost him more than D, and the organiser pays that from his own pocket or his own risk (Art. 11 — coercion is criminal), not from the budget. The price of a brought-in vote is at least D plus coercion: not "dies" but rises hundreds of times and changes payer.
- *The commission* leaves a trace for the first time: a precinct in a budget-employee district with full turnout and zero B is an anomaly on the public balance (040ag item 4) that everyone sees. The carousel becomes statistically visible even if nobody informs.

**Summary.** The protocol kills stuffing on silent records (the reservoir, the double spend) and the carousel's economics, but not the carousel as coercion under direct supervision — there it reprices and exposes it. The four supports of "the meek" (040af item 5) stand — not everyone can be dismissed, the law is on her side, common knowledge — but the support "in secret" at the commission's window is weaker than recorded there: secrecy from the neighbour and the treasury exists, from the supervisor at the precinct it does not.

**Can secrecy at the precinct be closed — a question for the charter.** The only way is to issue a ballot to everyone and extinguish the taker's vote at the count, not at issuance: the organiser cannot tell, and the carousel pays for votes some of which are empty — which kills it economically. But with a secret paper ballot a cast ballot cannot be extinguished by record without linking the ballot to the person, and that breaks the secrecy of the vote (048h: a receipt is a threat to secrecy). A dead end between two secrecies — of the payment and of the ballot; a candidate for the seventeenth charter decision (048k §3), for the architect to decide.

## Weak point

The ten tricks are the author's selection, not a measurement of frequency; the trade has more, and the list is open. "Dies" and "is devalued" are deductions from the charter, not observations: no election under the protocol has been held, and the first thing a pilot will show is which tricks the campaigns try first and how many of them work on "those who stay". Spoilers, the promise to raise p and the carousel under direct supervision (§4) are honest residuals; the second may turn out to be the main trick of the protocol era, the third the main question for the charter.
