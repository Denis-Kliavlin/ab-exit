## 0. Plouffe's craft

Obama's 2008 campaign manager: the ground game, data, millions of small donors, "the Obama coalition" of the young, Black, Latino and educated — voters with a low propensity to vote, brought in for the first time. In 2012 the data machine "the Cave". Then Uber and the Zuckerberg foundation; in 2024 senior adviser to Harris. The post-mortem: the main mistake was the absence of an open primary after Biden's exit; internal polls never had Harris ahead; "not sure any Democrat could have won given the headwinds"; Democrats need 60 % of moderates in the battlegrounds, and in each of them there are more conservatives than liberals; for 2028 — an outsider.

## 1. The Obama coalition is the turnout of low-propensity voters; you pay my coalition to stay home

**Argument.** The young and the minorities vote only when brought; the protocol pays exactly them for not coming and takes the coalition apart.

**Answer.** Low propensity is the diagnosis. In 2008 they were brought by hope, and in 2010 and 2014 they did not come by themselves, for free, and the coalition vanished in the midterms. They have been taking B for twenty years — only without the money. The protocol pays for what they do anyway, and divides the coalition not by age but by horizon (040al §4): the young with a long horizon stay — the strongest filter group (040ai §4); the young without one take — those Plouffe brought by hope for one time.

**Residual.** None.

## 2. Data: we model everyone's propensity and persuadability; a rule that pays for abstention breaks the model

**Argument.** The whole engineering of 2008–12 was a propensity model; the protocol makes propensity meaningless.

**Answer.** The model measured a rental. Under the protocol propensity becomes a decision with a price, and the data move from "who will come" to "who will stay" — Dalton's instrument (040aj §6) and the pilot's task (048l). Plouffe's craft becomes the protocol's instrument, and the "persuadables" of his model are the moving centre (040ao §1), which takes B.

**Residual.** None.

## 3. In every battleground there are more conservatives than liberals; an electorate of the standing with a position will lean right

**Argument.** Democrats lose structurally.

**Answer.** The partisan balance of takers is unpredictable (040al §4). "Conservative" in self-identification is often the mood of an apathetic without a position (040ao §1, Converse's non-attitudes). The standing centre has a position, but a mixed one — cross-pressured (040ac item 4). And Plouffe himself says Democrats need 60 % of moderates, that is, precisely the standing centre: the protocol gives him an electorate of exactly those people, and then whoever persuades wins.

**Residual.** None.

## 4. The movement and small donors: nobody donates $25 to "take the money" — and three moves by the architect

**Argument.** The 2008 campaign was a movement of hope with millions of small donations; the protocol cannot have a movement — nobody donates for a payout.

**The assistant's answer.** The protocol's donors are the burned with the motive of "a place in history" (059g §4), and that is not $25. The hope of 2008 did not survive one midterm, because hope without weight is a rental; the protocol gives hope weight ×2.

**The architect's move — the takers as donors.** "Purely arithmetically: for the apathetic who want to support the protocol so that it wins, it pays to give $100 to the fund of a politician promoting the referendum and then receive 800 — quite a profitable investment. Those who want the ×2 vote and have money we have already written about." A contribution of 100, a payment of 800 per cycle: the break-even is a 12.5 % probability of the referendum winning. Above one in eight the contribution pays, and D is paid every election, so $100 buys a stream of 800 for life. For the first time the apathetic has a financial, not a moral, reason to give money to a campaign: the Obama coalition donated to hope, here they donate to their own cheque. This is a fourth tier of funding on top of the three of 059g §4 (a place in history, 2–3 thousand "to try", the small donations of the burned) — and the only one in which donor and recipient are the same person. Legal: contributions to a ballot-measure committee, no ceiling (Bellotti), only foreign money prohibited.

**The second move — the scale.** "If 30 % of those who do not vote give $100 each, it will be a very decent sum, and they can always be told: you will get your money back ×7."

| Scale | Non-voters | 30 % at $100 | For comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| US, presidential 2024 | ~88 million of ~245 million eligible | $2.6 billion | the Harris campaign with all allies — ~$1.5 billion, the most expensive in history |
| US, 1 % of non-voters | ~880 thousand | $88 million | more than any state ballot measure ever |
| Missouri (059g) | ~1.7 million of ~4.4 million | $51 million | the Ohio map — $20 million |

"You will get your money back ×7" is Luntz's cheque in its pure form (040an §4): not a word about others but a sum in your own name. The campaign does not pay — the referendum pays, and they fund it themselves, chipping in.

**The third move — a realistic response.** "Even if one per cent respond, which for the apathetic is more realistic than thirty — if the underdog tells them for real that all this can be done, and asks for $20 each to chip in for their own future payments." An entry of $20 instead of $100 gives ×40 per cycle, and the ask changes meaning: not "donate to a politician" but "chip in for yourselves" — a pre-order of one's own payment, like crowdfunding. The condition is "for real": the underdog (059e, 048n) shows not a slogan but the charter, the number and the ballot line (048m art. 15) — the package for the cold reader. At $20 and a 10 % response among US non-voters — $176 million; in Missouri — $3.4 million, enough for signatures and a campaign without a single large donor.

**Residual.** The apathetic's response to a request for a contribution has been measured by nobody; 1 % is an estimate, 10 % an upper bound with a good underdog. A pilot measurement (048l): how many non-voters give $20 towards their own payment.

## 5. "Not sure any Democrat could have won": the candidate and the headwinds decide, not rules

**Argument.** Internal polls never had Harris ahead; the hall was not there.

**Answer.** This is 059i from the losing side — the mirror: the hall is the hall, and a campaign does not beat it, as Plouffe admitted. The only thing that changes "the headwinds" is a rule that changes the hall. His wish for an outsider in 2028 is the underdog of 059e, only without the ballot line that makes the underdog the winner.

**Residual.** None.

## 6. What Plouffe gives the protocol

The best data machine in the country, which under the protocol becomes the instrument for measuring the return (§2). The collapse of the Obama coalition in 2010 — the first mass measurement of "B for free" (§1). The admission "nobody could have won" — from the man who won in 2008 by bringing those whom nobody can bring now (§5). And his small donors — who under the protocol become donors to themselves (§4).
