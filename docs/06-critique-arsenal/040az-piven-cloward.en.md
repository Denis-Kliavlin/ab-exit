# 40az. Piven and Cloward: Why Americans Don't Vote — Five Arguments: Apathy as a Consequence, the Barriers of the 1890s, Demobilisation by Class, the Biggest Barrier in History, Remove Barriers Rather Than Pay

**Chapter:** 06 — The Structural Hypocrisy of Criticism and the Arsenal of the Elites

**Source:** the architect's assignment of 16 October 2026 ("Put them all in the repo"). Frances Fox Piven and Richard Cloward, *Why Americans Don't Vote* (1988), *Why Americans Still Don't Vote: And Why Politicians Want It That Way* (2000). The authors of the motor-voter law of 1993, Schattschneider's pupils (040av) with a history: nonvoting is the product of barriers built in the 1890s, and apathy is a consequence of exclusion, not its cause. Written without a move by the architect.

*How to read. Piven and Cloward showed that the American was first excluded and then called apathetic. The chapter shows that their own history answers their main blow: the elite built the barrier of the 1890s for itself and for free, while the protocol pays the person and counts him; and that their cure — remove the barriers — has been tested for thirty years and did not wake the giant.*

## 0. What they claim

Before 1900 turnout in the United States held at 70–80 %. In the 1890s–1920s both parties — Republicans in the North for fear of the immigrant worker, Democrats in the South for fear of the Black voter — introduced registration in one place during working hours, literacy tests, the poll tax; turnout fell, and the fall was attributed to apathy. Apathy is a consequence of exclusion, not its cause. The cure is to remove the barriers: automatic registration, registration with a driving licence, on election day; the law of 1993 is theirs. The 2000 book admits: the registered have increased, turnout has barely moved, because the parties did not call the new ones.

## 1. Apathy is a consequence of exclusion, not its cause; paying the "apathetic" treats the symptom with money

**Argument.** You pay those whom the system first switched off; the payment entrenches exclusion as a personal choice.

**Answer.** The diagnosis is shared: 011b (learned helplessness as a product of the system), 1f §6b (the burned left knowing the price), 19b.1. The cure differs. Exclusion today is free and nameless: nobody pays for the switched-off and nobody counts him. The protocol makes exclusion paid for by the state and counted (048m arts. 4, 10) — and reversible at every election (art. 5). The choice "take" is not the entrenchment of exclusion but its first price; and it is paid not by the apathetic but by the budget.

**Residual.** None.

## 2. The elite built the barriers of the 1890s for itself; the protocol is the same barrier with a cheque

**Argument.** Registration was invented so that workers would not come; the payment is the same design in honest packaging.

**Answer.** Their own history answers. The barrier of the 1890s was built by the parties, for themselves, and for free: the excluded got nothing, nobody counted his absence, and both parties lived on it for a hundred years. The protocol differs on all three points: it pays the person, not the party; it counts every leaver and presents the share of takers as the government's quotation (013g §3b); and it is removed by referendum, not in committee (art. 14). A barrier works for the one who built it; the protocol works against whoever lives on bought turnout — which is exactly why it has no owner (059l §4).

**Residual.** None.

## 3. Demobilisation by class: the workers and the poor did not come; the protocol demobilises the same people

**Argument.** The barriers of the 1890s hit the bottom of the scale; the payment will hit the same place.

**Answer.** The hall is counted in three movements (040aw §2): those leave whose vote belongs to whoever brought the package; the burned return, whom the barriers of the 1890s never knew; the rich remain a tenth. And selection runs by stake, not by class (1g "A tax on the poor"): the poor man with something to lose stays. Their own key observation — the parties did not call the newly registered because they did not need them. The protocol gives the party a need for the first time: turnout cannot be bought, and those remain who have to be earned.

**Residual.** Yes, shared with 040ax: the class composition of those who stay is a pilot measurement (048l §4).

## 4. The biggest barrier in history — and a paid one

**Argument.** No barrier ever paid people not to come; the protocol is demobilisation with a budget.

**Answer.** A barrier is what stands between a person and the booth. The protocol puts nothing between them: both rights are kept forever and chosen anew at every election (048m art. 16), registration and access do not change. It places a second right alongside. Their scale of barriers is a scale of the price of coming; the protocol leaves the price of coming untouched and sets a price on not coming, paid by the state. And the giant they tried to wake for thirty years by removing barriers — forty million became eighty-eight (040av §2) — wakes from money, not from registration.

**Residual.** None.

## 5. The cure is to remove barriers; the payment is a step the other way

**Argument.** Automatic registration, postal voting, a holiday on election day — that is the direction; the payment is the opposite.

**Answer.** Their cure has been tested: the law of 1993, motor-voter registration, postal and early voting. More registered, almost no more turnout, and they wrote it themselves in 2000: the parties did not call. The protocol cancels none of their steps and adds what their prescription lacks — a reason for the party to call by substance. In form it is the opposite, in aim the same: to make the government need those it excluded. And unlike their reforms it does not need the consent of those who "want it that way" (their own subtitle): the initiative, not the legislature (040aw §3).

**Residual.** None.

## 6. What they give the protocol

A history: exclusion was invented by the parties for themselves and named apathy. Proof that removing barriers does not wake the giant without a reason for the party to call. And the 2000 subtitle — "and why politicians want it that way" — as the shortest formula of the cornerstone (013g §3b).
