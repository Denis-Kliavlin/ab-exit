# 40an. Frank Luntz: "It's What People Hear" — Five Arguments, the Reverse Luntz, an Audit of the Protocol by the Ten Rules of Language

*How to read. Luntz is the one technologist whose craft coincides with the protocol's weakest point, the first reading (001d). So the chapter not only answers his arguments but applies his rules to the protocol's text as a checklist (§6) — and ends with the architect's move that turns Luntz's own rule over: what matters is not what was heard but who heard it (§1, §7).*

## 0. Luntz's craft

Focus groups and words. The main rule is the book's title: it's not what you say, it's what people hear. Ten rules of language: simplicity, brevity, credibility, consistency, novelty, sound, aspiration, visualisation, ask a question, context. Renamings that won debates without a single figure: "estate tax" → "death tax", "global warming" → "climate change", "drilling" → "energy exploration", "health-care reform" → "government takeover". After 2016 he admitted that words had stopped working: polarisation made language tribal.

## 1. "It's not what you say, it's what people hear" — and the reverse Luntz

**Argument.** They will hear "bribery", "selling the vote", "paid to shut up", and in the word EXIT they will hear Brexit and leaving the country. The protocol loses on vocabulary before it reaches the number.

**The blow.** At the first reading (001d) — by measurement, not reasoning: 1d answers with an argument, Luntz tests with a focus group.

**The assistant's answer.** A number is the simplest word, and it has no connotation (his own rule 1). The residual is real: neither the name nor the ballot line has ever been run through a focus group.

**The architect's move — the reverse Luntz.** "All correct — but those who hear it are the ones who have already left, and they do not care at all what it is called; what matters is that they were given money. And here is the interesting part: those who went and thought — 'if everyone goes, maybe I will stay and vote alone… and my vote is 100 %'." The word "bribery" has two audiences, and both work for the protocol. *The first* — those who have left and those who went under pressure: they hear "given money" and take; the dirtier the word, the clearer to them that the money is real — "bribery" is not a promise but a payment. The opponent shouting "you are being bought" advertises B. *The second* — those who went and thought: they hear the same word and calculate — "if everyone sells, who stays? I do. How much does my vote weigh then?" This is the mechanism of 001b (the share grows with the number of takers), triggered not by our explanation but by the other side's shouting. "Everyone sold their vote" means, to the thinking, "my vote got dearer", and the limit of that thought is "I will stay alone, and my vote is 100 %": nobody will stay alone, but the thinking will calculate to the limit, and the limit will hold them. The opponent tells those who stay the one thing they need to know: how many are leaving. Hence the reverse Luntz: it does not matter what they hear — it matters who heard. One word, two listeners, both decide for the protocol; the attacker has no good word, because any word of his sorts the hall our way (1c).

**Residual.** A focus group is needed, but not that one: what must be tested is not "what people hear" but "what those who stay hear" — the takers do not care (§7).

## 2. Aspiration beats arithmetic

**Argument.** People vote for "morning in America", not for a formula; nobody will love D = p × M.

**Answer.** The formula is for the charter; the ballot carries one number, and the slogan has four readings, one of them aspirational: "you are building a country for your children" (040ah §4), "your vote will count — with a share" (1f §6b). The lawyer reads the formula; the voter reads the sum and the line. But Luntz is right that the line "The vote is worth nothing. Today" itself carries no aspiration (§6, rule 7).

**Residual.** A companion line with aspiration — the architect's decision.

## 3. Consistency: you cannot say "worth nothing" and "weighs ×2" at once

**Argument.** A message with two opposite claims dies in a focus group.

**Answer.** The word "today" is the context — his tenth rule. The contradiction is the message: worth nothing today, worth something after. The four readings of 039b §4b do not contradict one another: each reads "today" as its own.

**Residual.** None.

## 4. Fear in one word beats any money

**Argument.** "Death tax" beat the calculations. The opponent will have one word — "vote-selling", "paid silence" — and no number beats one word.

**Answer.** The opponent's word is known — "bribery", and so is the answer — "price" (040ab, the test "punishment or price"). And by §1 the opponent's word works for the protocol: it sorts the hall. Luntz is right in one thing: the side with the better word wins, not the side with the better explanation — so the investment must go into the word.

**Residual.** The choice of slogan among four variants is the architect's open decision, and Luntz makes it urgent.

## 5. Words have stopped working

**Argument.** Since 2016 no word crosses the aisle; the protocol needs a word for both sides, and there is none.

**Answer.** The number crosses — along and across (040al §4), and "today" works in both camps. And that words stopped working is 059i: the hall judges by the face. The protocol changes the hall, after which words work again: Luntz's craft returns exactly when the protocol is adopted.

**Residual.** None.

## 6. Luntz's ten rules applied to the protocol

The architect's assignment: "take his own rules apart in relation to the protocol". What is checked is what the voter will actually see: the name, the ballot line (048m art. 15: the percentage, the formula and the sentence "Both rights — to vote and not to vote — are kept forever; change is possible only by a new referendum"), the number, the slogan "The vote is worth nothing. Today", the internal terms.

| Rule | What is checked | Verdict | What to fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Simplicity | the name AB-EXIT; the art. 15 line; the number | the name — fail: "A/B" says nothing to a voter, EXIT sounds like Brexit; the number — the best word possible; the line is bureaucratic | keep the name off the ballot; rewrite the line: "Both rights stay. Change only by referendum" |
| 2. Brevity | art. 15 requires the percentage, the formula and the sentence | the formula on the ballot — fail: D = p × M is not a word to a voter but an exam | the ballot carries the sum in local currency and one sentence; the formula stays in the charter |
| 3. Credibility | "kept forever" with repeal by simple majority (arts. 14–15) | fail: "forever" is refuted by the charter itself and sounds like a politician's promise | drop "forever": "stay until you yourselves decide otherwise" |
| 4. Consistency | four slogan variants; terms in quotation marks (1h) | the terms pass — 1h is consistency; four slogans — fail | choose one |
| 5. Novelty | nobody has heard "paid not to vote" | passes most strongly in the arsenal; the novelty is the first-reading risk | — |
| 6. Sound | "abexit" in Russian; the slogan | the slogan has rhythm, passes; "abexit" sounds like a medicine | the name — for internal use |
| 7. Aspiration | the slogan is negative | half a fail: "Today" hints, but there is no hope in the line | a companion line: "Your vote. Your share" |
| 8. Visualisation | the mechanism is abstract; "the scale with one beam" is for insiders | fail: not one image for the voter | one picture: two people at two doors — one with a cheque, the other with a heavier ballot; that is "two rights" |
| 9. Ask a question | the slogan is a statement | unused | a question form for the poster: "What is your vote worth?" — the voter answers himself |
| 10. Context | "Today"; the number without comparison | "Today" is context in one word, passes; "p % of the median" without a yardstick — fail | the number always with a yardstick: "a month of the median wage" (the architect's yardstick, 1h) |

Result: four of ten pass — novelty, the slogan's sound, the context "Today" and the number itself. Three fails are fixed by rewriting the art. 15 line — drop the formula and "forever", give the sum with a yardstick; this is a candidate charter decision awaiting the architect (048k §3). Three require his decision too: one slogan, a companion aspirational line, one picture. None of this touches the mechanism: the charter was written for the lawyer, the site for the reader, and the line for the voter has not been written yet.

## 7. Who heard: the architect's formula

"The protocol does not care what those who took will say. It matters what those who stayed will say." This is the chapter's conclusion and the rule for all work with words. The takers took and left: what they heard and what they say does not affect the result — their choice is already counted as a decision (040ag item 2), and they need no word. After the protocol it is those who stay who will speak and decide, and they are the only audience worth choosing words for: the contest of ideas (040ah §4), programmes against the number (040v item 1), Luntz's craft returned to a hall that judges by substance. One focus group à la Luntz is needed, and one only: what those who intend to stay hear in the word "bribery". If they hear "my vote got dearer", the opponent's word is ours.
