*How to read. Gilens and Page measured the cornerstone from the demand side of policy: the median's coefficient is zero. The chapter shows that the protocol does not reduce an influence they measured at zero but creates it for the first time — by the return of those their sample never contained; that money outside the law has no chance against money by law in a weighty sum; that the inertia they measured has no agent, because the protocol has no losers among those who must let it through; and that their own yardstick fits the pilot.*

## 0. What they claim

In a multivariate model on 1,779 policy issues of 1981–2002, the preferences of the economic elite (the 90th income percentile) and of business interest groups have a substantial independent effect on policy; the preferences of the average citizen (the 50th percentile) and of mass-based groups have almost none: the standardised coefficient of the elite ≈ 0.8, of the median ≈ 0.03 (059g). Of four theories, "economic-elite domination" and "biased pluralism" are supported, "majoritarian democracy" is not. Policy changes rarely — status-quo inertia. Gilens 2012: when the poor and the middle diverge from the affluent, there is almost no link to policy; it appears before elections, especially presidential ones. Page and Gilens 2017: the cure is wider participation, institutional reform, limits on money. The 2017 critique: the rich and the middle agree on 89.6 % of issues, and where they diverge, the rich win 53 % to 47 %. The word "oligarchy" is the press's, not theirs; they disowned it.

## 1. The problem is not the booth but money between elections — and the architect's move: money outside the law and money by law

**Argument.** Even if those who stay judge by substance, policy is written by donors and lobbyists between elections; the protocol touches only the booth.

**Answer.** The protocol does not ban money — it devalues it. The main product bought with donor money today is turnout: mobilising the apathetic by advertising, bringing-in and packages, the most expensive line of a campaign. Under the protocol the apathetic have taken the payment and there is nothing to mobilise them with; those who stay are reached by one or two touches (040aq §4) and judge by substance. The marginal value of a donor's dollar falls, and with it the donor's leverage over the politician: what does he need so much money for? This is not a campaign-finance law but a market.

**The architect's move.** "Money outside the law and money by law, and in a weighty sum — here there is no chance." The money that buys turnout today is outside the law or on its edge: the package, the Carnet de la Patria, the envelope, the bus (057d §3b, 030b). The protocol puts money by law on the same table — a share of the median to everyone who did not come (048m arts. 2, 4) — and in that competition the envelope has no chance: nobody sells his vote for a package when the law pays ten times more for an honest leaving, and the bought voter is exactly the one who took and left. Grey money loses not to a ban but to a price (040as §1: the payment as a tax rebate that removes bribery; 059l §4: legality is the first strike). Gilens 2012 himself showed the lever: responsiveness to the middle rises before elections — fear at the booth works; the protocol makes it permanent, because the booth can no longer be bought over.

**Residual.** Lobbying on particular issues below the electorate's radar the protocol does not touch and does not promise to; the answer is indirect — a politician who serves the lobby against those who stay meets an electorate that remembers a cycle (040at §2) and cannot be bought over.

## 2. Those who stay are the affluent: you amplify exactly the bias we measured — and the architect's move: where are the burned

**Argument.** The poor will take, the rich will stay; to the influence of the 90th percentile you add ×2 at the booth.

**The architect's move.** "And where are the burned? They will come back, and in a good percentage." The argument counts the hall in two movements — the poor left, the rich stayed — and there are three. First: the takers leave — the apathetic, the brought-in, the meek, those whose vote today belongs to whoever brought the package. Second: the burned return — those who left knowing the price (1f §6b) and whom Gilens's sample does not contain at all (040at §1): they do not answer the pollster and do not go to the booth while the count is foregone, and they return when their vote decides — Hungary 2026, plus nine points of turnout on a tight count (1f §6b). These are enterprising and well-off people, but not the 90th percentile — the producing middle, dropped by both sides, the elite's lobby and the government on bought turnout. Third: the rich remain what they were — a tenth, and one elite vote weighs the same as the vote of a returning plumber. The result is not "richer" but "producing": among those who stay, the burned and those like them are 60–70 % (059g).

**Their own numbers.** The influence of the median today is 0.03, of the poor where they diverge — zero; a measured zero cannot be reduced. The median's coefficient after the protocol rises not because the poor left but because those returned whose preferences Gilens's model never saw, and on the 10 % of issues where the middle diverges from the affluent and today loses 47:53, they now decide, ×2. The destitute man who takes: his preferences today affect nothing, by their own measurement; the protocol gives him the one policy outcome he gets for certain — D (040au §4). The accent of the chorus is set by the payer (040av §4).

**Residual.** None. The share of the burned who return is a pilot measurement (048l §4, the upper and lower bound rows), but the sign is known: those return whom the model did not count.

## 3. Status-quo inertia: the elite blocks change and will block the protocol — and the architect's move: not one kamikaze

**Argument.** By their data policy changes rarely even with elite support; a reform against the elite will never pass the legislature.

**The architect's move.** "The protocol has no losers: we went through ten politicians — and not one kamikaze." The inertia they measured is nameless: a bill dies in committee, and nobody has to stand up and say "no". A referendum on the rule cannot be blocked that way — it is blocked by name: somebody has to tell a family "I am against three thousand two hundred dollars for you" (059l §8). This was tested on ten real politicians across the spectrum (059l §8b): four for in some form — Trump, Vance, Ramaswamy, Fetterman; four walked away from the question but did not say "no" to the money — Harris, DeSantis, Haley, Newsom; two against the rule but for money by another route — Sanders, Ocasio-Cortez; against — zero. The inertia has no agent: the elite's instrument for blocking is a politician, and there is no politician who loses by supporting the protocol, neither before the referendum nor after (040ak §7). The inertia of Gilens and Page is the inertia of reforms with losers; the protocol has no losers among those who must let it through.

**The channel is tested.** They measured legislative policy; the protocol enters through a referendum (048m art. 14, 048n), and that channel has been tested on exactly the issues where the median diverges from the elite and the legislature blocks: Medicaid expansion by initiative in Idaho, Nebraska and Utah in 2018, Oklahoma and Missouri in 2020, South Dakota in 2022 — against legislatures that had refused for years. The attempts to narrow the channel afterwards — a 60 % threshold in Arkansas 2022, South Dakota 2022, Ohio 2023 — were already acts by name, and voters rejected all three. After adoption the inertia works for the protocol: the rule can be changed only by referendum.

**Residual.** None. Where there is no initiative channel, the protocol enters through a mayor or a council (048n), and the one who introduces it wins (059l §7) — a question of route, not of blocking.

## 4. Mass groups are powerless; the protocol organises nobody

**Argument.** The coefficient of mass-based groups is 0.24, of business groups 0.43; a people without organisation achieves nothing.

**Answer.** Olson (006b, 015c §7b): a large group does not act without a selective incentive; the payment is a selective incentive for the largest group, and refusing it is a private decision with a public count. Those who stay are a mass group without dues, an office or a free rider: each ×2 is his own, and the state counts them (048m art. 10). The cheapest organisation in history — and it is the electorate, not a pressure group.

**Residual.** None.

## 5. Our reforms cure, yours narrows participation

**Argument.** Widen participation, fix the institutions, limit money — rather than pay for leaving.

**Answer.** Their reforms must be passed by the very legislature which, by their own measurement, serves the 90th percentile — and the inertia they measured lies on their reforms, not on the protocol. Widening participation has been tried for thirty years — the giant sleeps (040av §2). The protocol needs one referendum and one budget line; from then on it executes itself. In form it is the opposite of their prescription, in substance the same: make the politician fear the median rather than the donor.

**Residual.** None.

## 6. What they give the protocol

The measurement of the cornerstone: 013g §3b is the derivation, Gilens and Page the measurement. A yardstick for the pilot: the responsiveness coefficient by income group before and after — Penn's ×2 (040ar §4) in their units; a rise at the median, its source the return of the burned rather than the leaving of the poor, testable with the pair "would you go / will you give $20" (040at §1). This yardstick is the assistant's proposal; it is not entered in 048l. And the critique helps the protocol: if the middle and the affluent agree 90 % of the time, those who stay will produce policy the affluent agree with 90 % of the time — and the dispute is over the 10 % where today the one who pays for turnout decides.

| the stayers are the affluent | no | three movements, not two: the takers leave, the burned return in a good percentage, the rich remain a tenth (the architect's move); a measured zero at the median cannot be reduced | — |

## Weak point

A reconstruction from the article, two books and the critique; the coefficients follow 059g and standard retellings, not Table 3 directly. One honest residual — lobbying below the radar (§1). Register: their "elite" is an income group, not an enemy; for the protocol a politician serving the 90th percentile is the predictable answer to the composition of the hall (1g "This is populism"), and the chapter keeps to that.
