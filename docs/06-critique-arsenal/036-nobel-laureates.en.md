## Purpose

A reconstruction of the reactions of ten Nobel laureates absent from the repository's theoretical foundation (§13 already uses Shapley, Nash, Hurwicz–Maskin–Myerson; §13.12 — Smith). Honesty caveats: there is no Nobel Prize in sociology (economics and the Peace Prize are taken); the reactions are reconstructed from published works; these are not documented quotations. The section's value: a map of allies for outreach + two unbroken honest challenges built into the project.

## Allies

**Elinor Ostrom (2009, governance of common resources).** The electoral signal is a commons. AB-EXIT is checked against her eight principles of governing the commons (boundaries, collective choice of rules, monitoring, graduated sanctions) — and fits them better than the current system: the rules change only by referendum (collective choice), the register is public (monitoring), the choice is individual and reversible (boundaries). Probably the project's strongest academic ally.

**Richard Thaler (2017, behavioural economics).** AB-EXIT is a nudge at state scale: not a ban and not compulsion, but a change of choice architecture under which rational behaviour becomes the default. He would especially value reversibility (every cycle a new choice, zero lock-in). His requirement: neutrality of the default — make sure the design does not push the poor towards button B disproportionately (converges with Kahneman's residue, §035).

**Herbert Simon (1978, bounded rationality).** Classical democratic theory presupposes an optimising voter — a fiction. The real person satisfices, and "take the dividend and not think" is rational satisficing. AB-EXIT is the first institution that recognises bounded rationality as the norm, not a defect, and builds on it rather than against it.

**Abhijit Banerjee and Esther Duflo (2019, RCTs).** Their credo: do not argue by theory — test by experiment. Coincides with the README's position ("one state, one referendum, one pilot"): intuitions about the behaviour of the poor are most often wrong — on all sides; measurement decides. The methodological justification of the pilot as arbiter.

**James Heckman (2000, the econometrics of selection).** AB-EXIT is self-selection as a feature: the mechanism makes an unobservable type (motivation) reveal itself through an observable action (the A/B choice) — the solution of the problem on which his career is built. Requirement: check that the selection does not correlate covertly with income to the point of distorting representation (the answer — public aggregated statistics of choice by strata).

**Muhammad Yunus (Peace Prize 2006, microcredit).** A living counter-example to the objection "bribing the poor": critics of microcredit for decades said "you make the poor dependent" — the empirics showed the opposite: a direct financial instrument in the poor man's hands liberates rather than corrupts. The same argument, the same fate.

## Ambivalent

**Daniel Kahneman (2002).** System 1/System 2: voting under a populist trigger is affect; AB-EXIT introduces a pause and a price, switching on System 2 at least for the second of choice. But loss aversion and framing may distort the proportions — full analysis in §035.

**Robert Shiller (2013, narrative economics).** Populism is a viral narrative; AB-EXIT does not argue with the narrative by facts (useless) but changes the carriers: removes the susceptible from the deciding pool. A warning: AB-EXIT itself must win the war of narratives against the counter-narrative "selling democracy" — the answer is two-register communication (street + institutional languages) and the inoculation "both rights are preserved forever".

## Challenges (built into the project)

**Joseph Stiglitz (2001).** "Who guarantees this is not a way for the 1 % to run politics more cheaply?" — a strengthened version of George's blow. Logical analysis (substitution of the agent + static analysis) — §035; the honest residue — transitional cycles.

**Amartya Sen (1998).** "A choice made from need is not freedom" — the deepest philosophical challenge after George. Analysis (nirvana fallacy) — §035; the honest residue: AB-EXIT does not replace the fight against poverty and does not claim to.
