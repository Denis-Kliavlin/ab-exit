*How to read. North is the one Nobel laureate in whose theory three things of the protocol already stand: the price of exit of the subjects as a constraint on the ruler, a diagnosis of the political market by four defects, and an institution that binds the sovereign himself. The chapter checks ten of his theses by one yardstick — is the thesis true today, and what does the protocol do with it — and examines two of his blows, one removed by the architect's move, the other left as an honest residue.*

## 1. Ten theses

## 2. North's blow: the informal norm of duty — and the architect's move

North would say: you change the formal rule with one referendum, but the norm "to vote is a civic duty" is informal, two thousand years old, and will outlive your charter; people will take the money and feel ashamed, or stay and resent those who took, and the gap between formal and informal will produce friction your model does not count.

**The architect's move.** "The norm 'to vote is a duty' has long not been a norm, judging by turnouts, and given that most of that turnout is herded in by various means and certainly not by a sense of duty." *Montaigne named it four hundred years earlier: give yourself to public affairs on loan, not as a gift — [040bo](040bo-montaigne.md), thesis 6.*

A check by revealed preference ([036e §4](036e-nobel-fifth-circle.md), Samuelson: trust the act, not the questionnaire). If duty were a norm, turnout would not have to be bought. Serbia: €600 m, 129 television appearances, pressure on public-sector employees — and 58.8 % in 2023. Ireland: 43–44 % at by-elections with no bringing-in ([057g §4](../09-other-countries/057g-ireland.md)). Russia: turnout is produced by the payroll ([039 §88.9](../07-manifesto/039-opponents.md)); the core is those "who come always and vote as told" ([1f §1](../01-introduction/001f-core-electorate.md)). Turnout has three parts, and none is duty: the core by payroll and habit ([016](../04-electoral-dynamics/016-age-economics.md): "I have voted all my life"), those herded by package and fear, and people with a stake. Duty remains in questionnaires as the socially approved answer — hence Blais's "half from duty" (040ba §1): self-report, not behaviour.

What this does to the blow. North counted on a collision between a formal rule and a living informal norm. There is no norm; there is an informal *practice* of bringing-in, dressed up as the norm of duty because that is the easier way to defend it ([1g](../01-introduction/001g-objections-index.md) "It will destroy civic duty"). The collision is with the practice, and the protocol does not break it but buys it out: the herded voter was always paid for coming, only less and not personally. North himself explains how informal rules change — through experience in which the new behaviour pays; here it pays in the first cycle. And the residue, to the point: the only thing left of duty under the protocol is those who refuse the money and come. Before the protocol duty could not be told from bringing-in; after it, it can (040ba §1: a duty that survives a price is real). The norm does not vanish — it appears in the books for the first time.

## 3. The second blow: who enforces

North: a rule without enforcement is not an institution; the state enforces, and the state is a revenue-maximising ruler. Who will make the treasury pay? The answer — [040v §6](040v-przeworski.md) and art. 8: self-enforcement through half the population with money at stake; the payment is unseizable; not to pay is to say by name "I am against your money" (059l §8b). It is the North–Weingast institution in reverse: not parliament binding the king, but millions of recipients binding the budget. The honest residue: in a limited access order — his own term — the treasury may simply not pay, and the answer of [40bh §3](040bh-q-change-from-above.md) on legitimacy is weaker than an Arizona initiative; this is recorded in 006b §3c.

## 4. Adaptive efficiency — the architect's move

**The architect's move.** "Money or a weighted vote — that is the complete, hundred-per-cent adaptivity of the protocol. And every citizen makes it."

In North, adaptive efficiency is a property of a society: whether it can try, err and discard what fails; its carriers are institutions and organisations, and adaptation is slow, incremental, across generations (theses 5 and 7). The protocol brings adaptation down to one person and one cycle: everyone decides for himself whether he needs power or money now, and replays the decision next time (art. 5). The institution adapts not as a whole and not through an elite, but as the sum of millions of personal adaptations, and that sum is visible every day as the share of takers (013 §13.10; [40bg §4c](040bg-q-change-needs-organization.md): one act — a demand, a count and a weight at once).

Why "a hundred per cent". In North, adaptation runs into three limits: who decides (an elite with bargaining power, thesis 4), on what knowledge (dispersed, [036b §6](036b-nobel-second-circle.md), Hayek) and at what speed (incrementally). The protocol removes all three: everyone decides for himself, on the knowledge of his own stake, every cycle; between the citizen and the rule there is nobody (40bh §2). And the allocative side: North sets adaptive efficiency against allocative; the protocol is the rare case where they do not conflict: allocatively nobody loses (the taker gets money, the stayer gets weight), adaptively the system retunes every cycle; adaptivity is usually paid for by the losers of reforms, and here there are none ([040bj §3](040bj-machiavelli.md)). The Serbian yardstick: Serbia adapted in North's way — institutionally, over two years, with victims and the risk of rollback (057h); the protocol adapts in the architect's way — millions of decisions in three days of the window, without victims and with a repeat a cycle later.

## 5. What North gives the protocol

A theory of the state in which the subjects' price of exit already stands as a constraint on the ruler: the protocol does not invent the constraint, it makes it cheap. A diagnosis of the political market by four defects — and the protocol answers each. The criterion of adaptive efficiency: the protocol is to be measured not by whether it gives the right answer, but by whether it lets errors be corrected more cheaply than today; by that criterion Serbia cost 16 lives, the protocol one choice.

## Weak point

North is paraphrased from the main works without quotations; the "four defects of the political market" compress his 1990 article. Enforcement in a limited access order is an honest residue (§3). Friction between formal and informal remains possible for the core that votes by habit rather than by bringing-in; its share and behaviour in the first cycle are a measurement of [048l §4](../08-implementation/048l-courtyard-pilot.md).
