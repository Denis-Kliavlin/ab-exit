# 40bd. Political Scientists' Question 2: "Citizens Are Rational and Have Seen Through It"

*How to read. The second premise: the modern citizen is informed, sees through the elites and therefore distrusts them; his discontent is knowledge, not mood. The protocol does not claim the opposite about everyone: the burned do know the price (1f §6b). It argues with the claim that the majority is built this way.*

**The series frame — the architect's move.** Political science wants the same thing the protocol delivers: a change of power by ordinary procedure, without bloodshed, through a law that cannot be repealed. It understands that the apathetic and the impulsive must be removed from the decision, but it knows no honest franchise qualification. And within the magnitudes and parameters that qualifications have had for two thousand years, it is right: every known qualification — property, education, a test — took the vote away from outside and hit the weak, and non-participation was a loss of the vote, that is, an illness ([006](../02-history/006-2500-years.md)). The architect: "AB-EXIT somehow went around all of this. I am in shock myself." The protocol is not a subtype of the known qualifications but another dimension: a qualification each person passes himself, in secret, by refusing money, and a balance in which nobody loses — the one who leaves gets the payment and the right to return, the one who stays gets double weight. Political science does not see this balance because it counts votes one to one and never puts a price on exit.

## 1. Who says so

- **E. Shulman** (2026, theses 3 and 4). The new masses gained "the omniscience of angels" — knowledge and influence once held only by elites. Citizens and elites saw each other and were mutually horrified: "they are no better than us, why do they rule and why are they so much richer."
- **H. Landemore** (040au): the collective intelligence of the many decides better than experts.
- **V. O. Key and M. Fiorina** (040at §7): "voters are not fools," retrospection is rational.
- On the other side — **Achen and Bartels** (040at) and **Somin** (040ay): the voter is rationally ignorant and judges by cues. The protocol is closer to them here but draws a different conclusion.

## 2. The architect's move: horrified by fairy tales, not by facts

"The elites knew everything long ago, and the citizens were simply horrified — and not by facts but by fairy tales from YouTube. It simply became easier to frighten citizens, and the populists began to use it. Here again, with Shulman, all citizens are clever — and that is not so."

Transparency did not make citizens knowledgeable — it made them reachable. The feed brings not a budget report but a story that frightens or angers. The one frightened by a fairy tale is impulsive, and it is he who under the protocol will take the payment: fear, which became cheap for the populist, costs a refusal of money (059n §6).

## 3. The architect's move: bread grows on trees

"A working person takes part in production chains and has at least a remote idea of production or services. A recipient of a pension or benefit thinks bread grows on trees."

The knowledge that protects against promises of money from nowhere comes not from the feed but from being part of a production chain: a worker knows the price of at least his own link and senses that a pension rise will be paid for out of his own work. One long outside the chain does not see that price. This is a state — being outside the chain — not a type and not an insult: rights and payments are untouched, a pensioner who stays weighs double like everyone (048m art. 16).

## 4. The architect's move: those who like for free will not go to vote

"Those who like for free will not go to vote after the protocol: they are lazy."

For most, "the omniscience of angels" ends with a heart. The limit of participation is an action without cost; faced with "refuse the payment for a vote or take it," such a person takes the money. For the protocol this is not a loss but its work: the hall is cleaned without an outside filter. A clarification: by a like one cannot tell the lazy from the burned — the burned stays away not out of laziness but because he knows the price of a bought hall, and will return when the vote begins to weigh (040aw §2); by the choice "take or stay" one tells them apart at once.

## 5. What the protocol does with both halves of the mirror

Shulman describes the mutual disgust of citizens and elites. The assistant, confirmed by the architect:

- **The citizens' complaint "why do they rule and why are they richer."** Today the only answer is to vote against, that is, for another elite. Under the protocol the elite rests on those who stay, who judge by substance and cannot be bought (040at); the price of its rule is visible every cycle in the share of takers (013g §3b). The elite has to be better, not just richer.
- **The elites' complaint "how can these people be given the vote" — technofascism.** The protocol knocks the ground out from under it without giving the elites what they want: nobody is filtered by a test (040d) or stripped of the vote, and those the elite fears — the impulsive and the brought-in — leave by themselves, with money. The hall stops being a "crowd," and ruling without asking loses its excuse.

## 6. What follows

| Premise | What is true | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| citizens are informed and have seen through it | for the burned | the majority was horrified by fairy tales from the feed, not by facts (the architect's move) |
| discontent is knowledge | partly | knowledge comes from inclusion in production, not the feed; one outside the chain believes in money from nowhere (the architect's move) |
| everyone is politicised | at the level of a like | those who like for free will take the payment, the burned will stay (the architect's move) |

The conclusion for the protocol: it does not require citizens to be clever. It requires only that each decide for himself whether his vote is worth more to him than the payment. Whoever was horrified by a fairy tale takes the money. Whoever knows the price stays and weighs double.

## Weak point

Shulman's quotations follow automatic subtitles. The share of the impulsive and of those outside the production chain among voters has not been measured; it is a pilot measurement (048l §4).
