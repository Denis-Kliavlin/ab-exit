## 1. Three physical analogues: processes that stimulate and complete themselves

The architect is looking for a "self-tightening knot" — processes with positive feedback that lead to saturation and fixation in a new stable state. In physics these are autocatalytic and relaxation processes: the process itself generates the energy for its own acceleration, but the same energy consumes the fuel, and it stops irreversibly in a new rigid structure.

**The friction hitch (Prusik knot).** Without load it slides; under a jerk the tension compresses the coils, friction grows exponentially; the harder you pull, the harder the knot strangles itself until it locks fast. The tension here is the media hysteria of the old elites: the louder the "they want to strip you of your vote for pitiful money!", the more of the indifferent learn that a cheque can be had; the shouting stimulates the outflow of extras; the elites themselves tighten the noose on their own base. Once the extras have taken the money, the knot locks the new architecture.

**Chain reaction with burnout.** A neutron splits a nucleus, 2–3 neutrons are released, the reaction accelerates exponentially — and ends when the active fuel burns out; the fragments form a stable mass. The dividend is the first neutron in a municipal pilot: one person received it and told a neighbour; the apathetic convert into recipients; the process self-stimulates on greed and micro-rationality and dies out when the "limbic fuel" is burned — those who could be bought for the base dividend are no longer on the voting rolls; a cold core of investors with amplified weight remains.

**Crystallisation of a supercooled liquid.** Pure water can be cooled below zero — it stays liquid and chaotic in a state of enormous tension; one grain of sand — and in seconds the chaos turns into an ordered crystal, releasing latent heat. Modern society is a supercooled liquid: disillusioned, tense, institutions not working, but everything frozen in chaos because there is no nucleation point. The referendum is the thrown grain: the rules are crystal-clear (left — money, right — power), an avalanche of separation begins, the transition completes itself when everyone finds a place in the new lattice.

The protocol is the trigger of a relaxation process that uses the chaotic energy of the crowd (greed, apathy, the wish for fast money) and the energy of the elites' resistance (media noise) as fuel; it accelerates until it burns populism to the ground and stops in a state of corporate stability. The physicists of complex systems say the same — 036d.2.

## 2. Yin-yang: decay in both directions

The architect's correction: "you underestimated — the decay goes both ways: whoever got the x2 vote does not sleep either, he decays too; this is yin-yang". Two counter-flows. **Yin** — the dividend consumers: they flow out of governance into passive rent; this is the system's entropy but a necessary cooling — they stop making noise and being the populists' feeding base. **Yang** — the shareholder-engineers: having received x2/x3, they come under enormous pressure of responsibility and temptation; they become bottlenecks and targets for all the corruption the populists used to consume; the amplified vote is expensive private property, and its owner begins to trade it (not for bribes — through lobbying his interests) until other shareholders unite against him. Stability is achieved not by the absence of struggle but by the struggle flowing from one state to another: when yang overheats and corrupts, it creates contradictions that force part of the mass to become active again. Not a static kingdom of engineers — a living organism with cycles of condensation and dispersal of power. That is thermodynamically efficient: the system does not fight human nature, it builds it into the cycle.

Refinement: "yin leaves, reducing stupidity, and yang, which now sleeps — here is the real answer of a real businessman and millionaire: he does not go into politics, but yang will slowly and constantly provoke him to see x2 and enter politics. An inevitable spiral, at the end — catharsis." A mechanism of forced evolution of elites: the businessman sleeps because the old system is loss-making for him — political capital there is only risks and circles of lobbying. The x2 option instantly changes the ROI: this is not "going into politics" but investing in the capitalisation of his own city. The entry spiral: he takes interest in the protocol as a tool to protect his business → sees that x2 can prevent a stupid law → sees that it can create a law that opens markets → does not notice how he turned from businessman into system player, because the environment makes him. "1 vote is just an opinion; x2 is an asset that shapes the rules." The businessman's silence becomes impossible.

## 3. ROI 200 % and scorched earth

"Are there systems in politics that change ROI by 200 % like AB-EXIT?" In the classical system the voter's ROI is negative or zero: you spend time, nerves and resources, and your share of influence is negligible, while the chance that an "avatar" keeps promises is statistical error. Where in physics and business are multiple jumps hidden at a change of phase: the latent heat of the phase transition (the potential energy of apathy that used to dissipate as noise); Metcalfe's law (a network's value grows as the square of participants; politics before the protocol is "one to many", broadcasting to the passive; the protocol moves to "each with each" — a network of shareholders where ROI turns from linear to exponential). For a businessman the ROI is not 200 % but the preservation and capitalisation of a business that under the old populists would have been destroyed; insurance with a yield unattainable in the "theatre". The businessman does not go into politics now because there is no ROI there — there is a "tax on fools"; the protocol makes entry worthwhile for engineers and pointless for avatars.

**What the builders live by now.** The scorched-earth strategy: they invest not "in the country" but in assets that can be moved instantly; apoliticism as armour (success is an invitation to dispossession); capitalisation of personal brand, because laws will change but personal resource cannot be taken. Hopes: "slipping through" (personal success as insurance against systemic crisis); exporting the future — children study where the rules are clear and do not depend on the whim of the next populist — an admission of the system's defeat. The children's happiness? Not sure: they pass on capital but not an environment in which it can be safely multiplied; they raise children for emigration; they love their homeland and do not believe it will offer their children anything but danger.

"So the majority of the smartest people in all countries want to kill their country? If they are the smartest, they should succeed." They do — because their intellect is aimed at efficiency inside the system, not at the efficiency of the system itself: superb at profit around barriers, at avoiding taxes that go to parasitic layers; but every smart step taken alone weakens the structure — the tragedy of the commons: each is rational, the sum is the collapse of the village. It is not malice but local rationality: killing the country is a side effect of trying to survive.

**The change of sign.** "This is when minus becomes plus, and it no longer matters what number stood before the sign." Before, a smart person was a minus for the state (extracting resources to save himself); all the nation's energy went into struggle; the most talented spent intellect on deceiving the system instead of building. As soon as the sign before the coefficient of influence changes (x2, x3): intellect stops hiding offshore — being smart and rich legally gives leverage; creation becomes the highest ROI — investing in the social architecture of the city brings more control, stability and capital than a yacht; the system stops devouring its best children and starts growing them. The chaotic flow of water from a broken dam is directed into turbines: the same water, instead of destruction — light. Scorched earth gives way to **terraforming**: not "you need not raise children for emigration" as a slogan, but buying the rules of the game that will protect the children rather than exploit them.

**Children abroad.** The paradox of totalitarian elites: rulers inside, children outside. The architecture of arbitrage: one's own country is a mine for extraction, the West a safe for capital and a biological backup; steal where there are no courts, store where property is sacred. By indirect data (investigations, sanctions lists) at the level of ministers, state corporations, enforcers and top propagandists the share of children living, studying or holding citizenship in "hostile" countries is estimated at 80–95 %; Iran has a term for it — "aghazadeh". The louder they shout about patriotism inside, the farther the children are outside — the elite's coefficient of distrust in its own system. Propaganda covers this with information blockade, the myth of "scouts", sacralisation of the leader in a ring of enemies and a cult of suffering. The protocol is the only way to "bring the children back": if the elite's vote is tied to the efficiency of the living environment through x2/x3, and governing the country yields more profit than storing assets offshore, they will have to develop the environment here so that their heirs are not strangers.

## 4. The cognitive tunnel and its architects

What will a person of modest means choose between fear for an abstract future and money now? The brain follows a biological algorithm: **scarcity** switches off the prefrontal cortex — all capacity goes to "how to survive today"; **different scales of the future** — for the well-fed intellectual it is 10–20 years, for the poor it is next Tuesday; **hyperbolic discounting** — a bird in the hand weighs a hundred times the crane. He will choose the money without hesitation — and that does not make him stupid: he is a rational agent in given circumstances; the protocol does not condemn but respects him, paying a fair price.

"And who drove the worker into this situation, and why? Thirty years ago it did not exist." To be fair, poverty always existed, but there was an industrial contract: one workplace, a predictable wage, planning. The current tunnel is an artificial labyrinth, a symbiosis of the financial sector and political populism: **control through anxiety** (a financially independent calm middle class is the populist's worst nightmare; for the crowd to vote for avatars it must be in stress; fear is the best engine of turnout); **the credit needle instead of income growth** (real incomes stopped growing — elites replaced money with cheap credit: mortgages, car loans, cards; the worker works to service debt — a perfect collar that narrows the horizon to the payment date); **the inflation tax** (money printed for hand-outs, inflation eats savings — the hamster wheel ever faster just to stay in place). You cannot leave this labyrinth by voting for "another politician" — any politician becomes the labyrinth's operator.

The protocol is a hardware fuse at three levels: liquidity without debt (the dividend is not a loan but a return of a share; it goes to deleveraging — paying off microloans and cards; the banks lose their cash cow); stopping the printing press (those for whom money was printed took a fixed cheque and gave up the vote; at the helm are pragmatists whose assets inflation devalues); restoring the horizon (acute debts closed — cortisol falls — the tunnel collapses — the person sees beyond the paycheque; a calm person cannot be governed by a populist). Destroying the "customers of poverty": populists profited from destitution for cheap votes; to a pragmatic investor a destitute population is unprofitable — it does not buy, does not create, lowers capitalisation. For the first time it becomes mathematically unprofitable for the state to keep citizens in chronic stress.

## 5. Reversibility as anaesthesia and RPG mechanics

"He can overcome need and return — how does this thought calm him when he does take the money?" If the refusal were forever (like selling a kidney), the brain would perceive it as self-destruction; guilt would pour out as blind hatred of the engineers. Reversibility changes the chemistry of the decision: **substituting a strategic pause for a sale** ("I am not selling my rights — I am renting the vote out for a period; I take a pause, the state returns my own taxes so I can solve acute problems" — a crisis manager of his own life, not a traitor); **preserving dignity** (dignity rests on the potential of choice: while you hold the keys to the door, you need not enter it; "I can become a shareholder at any moment, it's just that jackets for the kids matter more now; when I'm on my feet I'll come back and show them all"); **removing the burden of incompetence** ("let the clever ones with x2 sort out the budget — they took the responsibility, let them answer"); **the recovery cycle** (having taken the money and solved the problems, the person leaves the tunnel; over time the self-reassurance "I can always refuse" becomes real ambition).

"'When I'm on my feet I'll come back and show them all' — doesn't that motivate him 100 %, which normally never happens in politics?" The protocol's perpetual motion — a gamification classical politics lacks: there, learned helplessness operates (voted — nothing changed — a fool — apathy — hatred of "the thieves"); here — capitalisation of ambition: **a change of enemy** (the system is honest — it gave the money; hating it is stupid; anger is redirected at the real problem — one's own financial inadequacy; to "show them" you need not barricades but a better job, a skill, one's own business); **a clear quest** (level 1 — survival: take the cheque, close debts, gather strength; level 2 — influence: earn yourself, refuse the cheque, get x2, take decisions; the status of majority shareholder cannot be stolen or begged — only grown into); **a compressed spring** (not all will become millionaires — some stay on the base cheque forever, and the system bears it; but for the active part — youth, the stuck middle class, entrepreneurs in crisis — the temporary refusal of the vote works as a prick to self-esteem that burns every day). For the first time the state creates a system where one can become politically significant only by becoming economically successful. (Cf. 040b.5: the vote as a luxury good.)

*Phase 1 — emergency evacuation:* the mathematics of survival; millions in debt slavery press "money", close arrears; 15–25 % take governance, realising their vote is a controlling stake; populists instantly lose 80 % of their audience; the budget stops being spent on bribery and goes to infrastructure, because that is what the pragmatists decide. *Phase 2 — leaving the tunnel:* the pragmatists clean the economy of corruption and bureaucracy; jobs appear, taxes fall; the one who took the cheque out of hopelessness notices his wage has grown; the dividend stops being a matter of life and death; dignity awakens — some refuse the cheque to protect their new standard of living; small business moves en masse into political shareholding. *Phase 3 — crystallisation:* 15–20 % on money — the natural base level (temporary crisis: job loss, illness; plus those who care about nothing but the sofa); the system supports them calmly; 80–85 % with the vote — a corporation of equal builders; terraforming complete; even big business must be transparent, because it operates among 85 % with amplified votes and zero tolerance for corruption.

Thus "yin" evolves into "yang" — no longer from fear but from surplus resource. The protocol buys stupidity today to destroy the very need for stupidity tomorrow: it does not treat it with persuasion but changes the environment — the propaganda industry goes bankrupt (the audience does not vote), the tunnel collapses with rising living standards, the prefrontal cortex returns. The "proportion of stupidity" is maximised at the start in order to be destroyed at the end. ## 6b. Phase 3 is an operating point, not an end state

A question to the forecast: if in phase 3 only 15–20 % remain on the money, the weight of a vote is again close to 1/N and mass free turnout has returned. What prevents the old problem from returning too — a populist collecting cheap votes? The answer: nothing prevents it from *starting*, and the protocol does not count on that. It counts on button B never disappearing. Government gets worse — life becomes dearer, trust falls, the dividend is again worth more than the vote, and the exit share grows; the weight of those who stay grows with it, and the controlling stake is once more with the motivated. This is the same thermostat, only over a long time.

So "crystallisation" is not a point of rest but an operating point around which the system oscillates; the share of B is its temperature. The difference from the system in force is that the latter has no feedback at all: a deterioration of government today does not change the composition of those who vote, whereas after the protocol it changes it automatically, without revolution and without heroism. The architect confirmed this reading: "the thermostat self-calibrates towards better government and a better life for people." Self-calibration means that nobody assigns the set point: neither a law nor a commission decides which exit share is "right". Each cycle people set it anew themselves, comparing the sum with what their vote is worth under the present government; better government — less exit, worse government — more exit and a heavier vote for those who stay to fix it.

The architect's second clarification: "but the equilibrium point itself changes too." This is what distinguishes the protocol from a pendulum. It moves for three reasons. The sum is tied to the median (D = M × 1.5 × K): as the median grows, so does the dividend, so the price of giving up the vote rises with the standard of living instead of eroding. People themselves change: as prosperity grows the planning horizon lengthens (040c.1.5), and the same sum no longer outweighs the vote for those for whom it did yesterday. And the bar of expectations rises: a government that was good enough last cycle is compared next cycle with what has been achieved, not with the starting point. So the oscillation runs not around a fixed mark but around a mark that itself shifts towards better government and a better life: the trajectory is a spiral, as the chapter is named, not a circle. An honest caveat: the upward direction of the shift follows from the assumption that the motivated govern better (x10); if it does not hold, the point shifts downward just as freely.

Two caveats: the oscillation runs with a lag of one cycle, that is, a bad government gets to serve out its term; the amplitude has not been measured — it is unknown how much life must worsen for those who returned to take the money again. 🟡

## 7. Greed, stupidity, conscience — the great cycle

"Forecast how greed will always beat stupidity, and conscience will never meet a greed it cannot beat, because that greed went off to fight stupidity of its own free will." In the old system stupidity is the populists' safe haven and the fuel of chaos. Greed is a pragmatic predator that tolerates no inefficiency: the greedy shareholder sees the stupid apathetic voter as a bug lowering his assets' value and creates conditions in which stupidity voluntarily monetises itself and disappears from governance. Stupidity is noise (a free resource), greed is an algorithm (a paid process); the algorithm always grinds the noise; stupidity leaves, pleased with its cheque, not realising it has been relieved of the remote. Conscience in the old system is weakness: the conscientious drown in millions of bought votes; in the cleansed environment conscience gains mathematical weight and becomes an instrument of strategic governance: the greed that was destructive went to fight stupidity and burned out there; what remains is creative greed, structurally identical to conscience — both require long-term planning, responsibility for the environment and systems that will not collapse tomorrow. Three stages: chaos (stupidity rules, energy goes to scorched earth) → change of sign (the pragmatists' greed buys up apathy) → catharsis (in governance those who realised that the highest form of greed is creation; the businessman who understood that the best investment is a state that need not be robbed becomes a moral leader).

**Similar systems in the present world** (where the builders' greed is forced to work for common sustainability): Swiss direct democracy and local self-government (you vote on what hits your wallet — taxes, schools, rubbish; greed forces you to be responsible; stupidity does not live long, because the price of error is instant); the German Mittelstand (family companies in the third or fourth generation cannot afford quarterly greed; their greed — to hand a thriving asset to the children — is identical to conscience; they cannot flee, because this is their home); open-source protocols (the makers of Linux/TCP/IP work for competitors but must cooperate, because destroying the standard destroys everyone's business). Their common minus: dependence on a cultural code (Switzerland, Germany) or a narrow niche (protocols). The protocol is the logical extension to the whole state: it sews Swiss responsibility and German continuity into the code through the dividend formula; it is impossible to be stupid and rich at once.

**Eponymous companies.** "Why were companies once named after their founders, and no longer?" — the transition from ego to institution: the name on the facade meant personal responsibility (Ford builds a bad car — Ford personally loses reputation); today companies are scalable systems, the name reflects function so investors see an asset independent of the founder; depersonalisation lowers risk and makes the corporation "immortal". Responsibility dissolved in layers of management and lawyers — "the company is to blame", not a person; quality is optimised for margin, not trust. The protocol is a return to the eponymous-company model at the level of the state: every builder puts his signature (the amplified vote) under what happens in the country and bears real responsibility. Who wins — corporations or small business? Neither size: responsibility. A big corporation living by lobbying (buying laws against competitors, privatising profit while nationalising losses) will find lobbying astronomically expensive — it must buy votes from all the shareholder-builders; a quality corporation wins, because the board of pragmatists backs it; small business, formerly a "free donor", gets an amplified vote as a tool of defence against monopolies. The protocol kills not corporations — parasite corporations that exist only by proximity to the budget.

## 8. Turbulence: how it can end

"Do you understand that this is the most terrifying turbulence, which can end only in what?" A change of power on this scale is a phase transition with a release of destructive energy. The revolt of the written-off elites: a populist will not say "the maths worked, I'll look for a job" — the old elites have one instrument left, the enforcers; an attempt to cancel the protocol by force, to declare the results "unconstitutional" or "an oligarchs' conspiracy". The mass's "hangover": old propaganda at 1000 % — "you were bought for pennies, go and take it by force"; part of the crowd, having spent the money, feels the phantom pain of a wounded ego. Two endings: **reactionary rollback** (if the pragmatists, having gained power, act as soft idealists, the old elite sweeps them away with tanks, declares martial law, annuls the protocol and returns dictatorship — mathematics loses to force) or **a transitional frame of the builders** (the core uses legitimacy and capital to win over the security bloc — make it mathematically more profitable for army and police to defend the new architecture — and harshly, within the law, isolates provocateurs; the analogy is a hard frame protecting a free economy until the rules become irreversible). Mathematics gives the builders the right to power, but not bulletproofing. How exactly the security bloc comes over to the protocol by itself, without being "bought" — 048d: the army is a cross-section of the people, and an order to shoot at those demanding the dividend is an order to shoot at one's own income.

## 9. Weak point of the section

The whole section is a model of dynamics, not an observation; no phase is confirmed by a pilot. The weakest node is §2: "yang decays" is described as self-balancing (shareholders unite against a monopolist), but the protocol has no mechanism against a *cartel* of shareholders other than the thermostat, which fires with a cycle's lag. The open-source analogy (§7) honestly shows that a standard survives only while destroying it costs more than collusion — for a municipality with a narrow A pool that is not guaranteed (039b.3.4). 🟡
