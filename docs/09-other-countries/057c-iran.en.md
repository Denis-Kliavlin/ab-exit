# Iran: the Clergy, the Bonyads, the IRGC and the Opposition in Exile

**Chapter:** 09 — Cases: Other Countries
**File:** 09_057c · v1 · 15 September 2026 (Gemini dialogue, session 15-09-26)
**Source:** a structural analysis of Iranian power (clergy/bonyads/IRGC), a reconstruction of the scenario "the opposition in exile proposes the protocol". The universal logic of the forces and the elites — 048d; here only the Iranian specifics.

---

## 1. Three centres of power and their interest

**The clergy** is not only an ideological screen: through the **bonyads** (religious foundations that pay no taxes) the clergy owns real estate, factories, shopping centres, exports. Direct distribution of resource rent to citizens is, for a cleric, not "liberation of the people" but theft from his foundations. **The IRGC** is the real economic force: oil, ports, factories, trade, "black cash desks". **The people** — with one of the largest gaps between the elite and the ordinary soldier/worker.

## 2. The theological attack and why it will shatter

The clergy's first move is to declare the system haram: the dividend is riba (usury); Western influence, "technological idolatry"; taghut — "an algorithm instead of a wise religious leader — replacing the will of God with the will of a machine". The aim is to move the dispute from "you have become richer" to "you have become sinners".

Why it will not work: a crisis of hypocrisy — "if the dividend is haram, why is your wealth halal?"; "a hungry people does not fear hell" — attempts to declare economic reforms anti-religious failed whenever people saw salvation in them; and the main conflict — the clergy forbids not out of love of God but out of fear that the IRGC will lose control of the flows. A ban will trigger a digital reformation: VPNs, the system as a symbol of resistance and of the clergy's ossification, young theologians asking "is the Quran not against the robbery of the poor by the rich?". The clergy can obstruct only by force — mass repression against everyone with a wallet — turning from mentors into gendarmes and discrediting religion as an instrument of rule. An autocrat can blame "bad officials"; "God's representatives on earth" cannot: by obstructing a system that gives bread they put the faith itself at risk.

## 3. Hostages of a mad dog

"Why would the priests be against, if the protocol helps throw off the bandits?" The answer: they fear the IRGC more than the people. While the Corps is in power, the people fear the streets; if the clergy supports the reform and loses its protection, a people that hated both the IRGC and the "bloated mullahs" will come not to thank but to settle scores for decades of poverty. For the clergy the IRGC is "a mad dog on a leash: dangerous to hold, more dangerous to let go". The reformist wing, understanding that in an explosion they will be hanged first and that the Corps despises them too, may rewrite the theology: "God desires justice; this system is an instrument of divine justice that strips the corrupt of power and feeds the poor". Thereby the clergy removes the blame from itself ("the bandits in uniform stole, we were hostages"), saves face and gains safety. They are not saints — survivors; against the protocol out of greed, for it when the alternative is physical destruction.

## 4. Golden parachute or sainthood

The deal "you help adopt the protocol, leave politics, keep your status as spiritual figures and the inviolability of your assets" is not a jihad for the people but a jihad for survival: "a mafia boss hands his henchmen to the police in exchange for a farm in Tuscany". The problem with the deal: the boundary between the mullahs' private assets and the budget is erased — the people will say "no villas, return everything"; the clergy will go only under a guarantee of immunity. And the IRGC will not let the clergy simply get up and leave — at the first suspicion of negotiations the arrests begin; hence only a secret conspiracy in which the clergy acts faster than the Corps understands.

The architect's clarification that makes the cleric's position a dominant strategy: **either a golden parachute, or — if they kill you — sainthood and honour for centuries; no-lose.** The dictator holds the elite with two instruments — "I can kill" and "I can strip you of everything"; the scheme zeroes both. Kill — make a martyr, the people come out with portraits, the regime falls within a week; leave — he prepares the transition. A person without fear is uncontrollable. One cleric who understands this whispers to others: "why wait until we are swept away together with the bandits? if they kill us — martyrs; if it works — a pension". Then — the "secret club", sleeper agents of change under the camouflage of "boring economics" (048d.6). The people will accept them not out of love — out of love of money and calm, if that avoids civil war.

## 5. The opposition in exile: a contract instead of a personality

The usual opposition in exile loses the question of trust: "they live on grants", "they don't know life in the country", "they want to reach the trough — overthrow the tyrant to become the new ones". If a figure of the level of the Pahlavi heir proposes not "I will come and build democracy" but the protocol, the game changes: egocentrism goes — he is not a candidate for the throne but the architect of a system where "you yourselves decide who governs you, and everyone receives a share of the oil into a personal account; if after a cycle the government — mine or any other — does not show growth in welfare, you vote for another through your smartphone; this is not a promise, this is code". The people need not take his word — only the mathematics.

The reaction: initial shock "a Western trap/scam" → verification of the code by technocrats, engineers and young people inside the country → the turning point when people understand this is not politics but access to the oil money going to the elite; the struggle "for ideas" (dangerous to come out) turns into a struggle for one's own money. The autocrat is trapped: "nonsense" — he argues with mathematics; "we'll introduce our own similar one" — he admits the opposition is right. The opposition becomes a shadow government-startup: demo versions, wallet training, "a community of shareholders of the future state" — by the time the regime totters, there is not a party programme but an infrastructure the people already know and want.

## 6. The techno-dictator paradox and the trust protocol

"Won't they become the first digital rulers if the code is under their control until victory?" — if the opposition says "trust us, our code is honest", it becomes the same autocrat. The conditions under which the system cannot betray: **open source** (any programmer downloads it and verifies there is no back door; updates are published before deployment); **multisignature** for changing the rules and for large fund movements — 7 of 10 key holders, of whom only a couple are from the opposition, the rest international auditors, academics, NGOs; **the right to fork** — if the council breaks the rules, citizens initiate a copy of the system with honest rules and "vote with their feet"; **immutability** of the base rules (payment automatically on receipt of funds, human intervention impossible); **external audit** with public reports. The "death of the creator" strategy: the system must work better without them than with them; code clutched in their hands the people will read as another swindle.

## 7. Weak point of the case

The case assumes that oil rent can be converted into a dividend "by code" — in Iran under sanctions and with the IRGC sitting on the export flows this is the strongest assumption in the whole section. And as in 057b: while discussion is impossible, the "trap" is a model, not an observation. 🟡
