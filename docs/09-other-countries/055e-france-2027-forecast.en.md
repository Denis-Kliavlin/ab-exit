# 🇫🇷 France, October 2026: the Line-up Six Months Before the Election, a Forecast Under the Protocol, and a Route That "Never Worked"

**Chapter:** 09 — Other Countries
**File:** 09_055e · v1 · 13 October 2026
**Source:** the architect's request of 13 October 2026: "the next election in France — what is happening now, a forecast for the protocol, and is there a boring, clever underdog". Two of the architect's moves are recorded as moves: the RIP never worked for weak ideas; the protocol has a first mover but then works for anyone. A continuation of 055b (its §7 named Lisnard the underdog a month earlier); here — the October 2026 figures and the route. Estimates 🟡.

---

*How to read. France is a pure case of 040v item 1: two cancelling blocs and a layer deciding the run-off on the principle "against whom". The protocol is tested here on three things: what it does to the RN, whom it helps without a change of programme, and whether the referendum route that has not passed once in seventeen years is passable.*

## 1. The line-up on 13 October 2026

- **Dates.** First round 18 April 2027, second 2 May; Macron is not running (two terms).
- **Le Pen.** On 7 July 2026 the appeal court upheld the conviction, cutting ineligibility to 45 months, 30 suspended, plus a year with an ankle tag and a €100 thousand fine; the 15 months already served make her eligible by April; she has appealed to the Court of Cassation and declared her candidacy.
- **First round by polls.** Le Pen 31–36 %; Mélenchon 14–17 %; Philippe 15–25 % depending on configuration — the only non-RN candidate consistently reaching the run-off; Attal 13–14 %; Glucksmann 10–12 % — won the Socialist primary on 11 October with 66 %; Retailleau for the Republicans (nominated in April); Zemmour; Villepin 4–6 %; the independents Bertrand and Lisnard; the "united left" primary on 11 October (Ruffin against Tondelier) without Mélenchon.
- **Run-off.** Le Pen beats everyone except Philippe, where it is a coin toss: Odoxa in early 2026 gave Philippe 52:48; Vote-Scope's average of 287 polls gives Le Pen 64:36 against Mélenchon.
- **Background.** Lecornu's government without a majority; a 2027 budget with €54 billion of cuts, vote on 17 November; two governments toppled by no-confidence votes since December 2024; record debt.
- **Turnout.** Presidential 2022 — abstention 26 % in the first round, 28 % in the second; legislative 2024 — 67 % turnout; municipal in March 2026 — 57 %, a historic low. Registration is automatic at 18 — the list channel (059h) is weak in France; the layer channel works.

## 2. The forecast under the protocol

| | Without the protocol | Under the protocol 🟡 |
|---|---|---|
| RN in the first round | 31–36 % | 20–22 % among those who stay: the hysterical part takes B, the fear of the real remains — security, the border (040w item 3); the RN does not vanish, it loses mass (040ae item 3) |
| Mélenchon's left | 14–17 % | loses most of all: the young without a stake who do not care take B first (040ai §4); "the unburned" go to whoever writes about housing |
| Philippe | 15–25 %; run-off 50:50 | 60:40 and above against Le Pen: his voter — older, better educated, long-horizon — is the most stable group among those who stay; the only candidate the protocol helps without a change of programme |
| the layer | decides the run-off "against whom" | disappears as the deciding factor; the run-off stops being a referendum on fear |
| cost | — | 49 million registered × 50 % × €800 ≈ €20 billion per five-year cycle, €4 billion a year — a quarter of a per cent of the budget; against €54 billion of cuts, a line the counter-audit justifies (059g §4) |

Without the protocol: a Le Pen–Philippe run-off, 50:50 with a slight Le Pen edge by the polling average; Lecornu's government falls on the budget in November with 40 % probability.

## 3. Is there a boring, clever underdog

Philippe is boring and clever — a former prime minister, mayor of Le Havre, no charisma — but not an underdog: he is the establishment's best hope, and the run-off is his anyway. The real candidates for the role: **David Lisnard** — mayor of Cannes, head of the Association of Mayors of France, the Nouvelle Énergie party, 1–3 % 🟡 (055b §7 gave 5–8 %): the accountant of local government, "freedom of the communes", no machine — the auditor type of 059g, and with what nobody else has: 35 thousand mayors who could run the protocol as a consultation at commune level before any national referendum. **Villepin** — 4–6 %, anti-system, but not boring and too grand for an underdog. **Bertrand** — an independent regional president, boring and clever, no machine.

## 4. The route — the architect's two moves

**The first version of the answer** stopped at the route: a citizens' initiative referendum (RIP) needs 185 parliamentarians and 4.7 million signatures and has not worked once in seventeen years; the realistic path is Article 11, a referendum called by the president; so the underdog must first win and then put the protocol to the vote — the reverse of Missouri (059g).

**First move: the RIP never worked for weak ideas.** The architect: "that was before the protocol, and the ideas were weaker." The only real attempt was in 2019, against the privatisation of the Paris airports: an abstract question about state property collected 1.1 million signatures of 4.7 million; a 2025 initiative on the wealth tax never reached collection. Both times people were asked to sign for something not their own — an airport, a tax on others. The protocol is the first RIP question where the signature is for oneself: €800 into the account or a ×3 vote. With 36 million non-voters and "the burned" as collectors (059g §4), 4.7 million signatures — a tenth of the electorate — are not a barrier but the first show of strength; 185 parliamentarians are the parties' fork inside parliament (039b §4b): the deputies the selection did not let up sign first. The RIP that "never worked" never worked for weak ideas; it has not seen a strong one.

**Second move: it has a first mover but works for anyone.** The architect: "the protocol has a first mover, but then it works for anyone; Philippe can take it too — and Le Pen will have to answer." Lisnard says the number first and gets hype and recognition on any outcome (059g §4). But Philippe can take the protocol into his programme the next day, and for him that is rational: the protocol guarantees him the run-off and wins it with a margin (§2), because the hysterical part of the RN takes B. And Le Pen has to answer: say "against" — explain to her working-class voter why he may not take €800; say "for" — accept a rule that takes her mass away. It is the American parties' fork (059g §4) at the level of a presidential race, and it collapses faster, because there are two candidates and one run-off.

**The route after the corrections** is direct, not reverse: the underdog says the number → Philippe takes it → Le Pen answers → an Article 11 referendum after victory, with the RIP as pressure in parallel with the campaign. The protocol gives Lisnard recognition; Philippe a victory; Le Pen a choice between two defeats. The architect's refinement: the underdog's win on any outcome rests on the parties being slow; if Philippe takes it over fast, Lisnard's premium shrinks and the protocol speeds up — and for the protocol that no longer matters (059g §4).

**Third move: the second-term trap.** The architect: "I missed this second-term trap in introducing the protocol too. Any president wants a second term, and for him the protocol turns from an unwanted idea into a very good last chance." This closes the repository's oldest residual — "a government will never introduce what takes its turnout away": the one that will is the one with nothing to lose but a second term. The mechanism: the first term was won on bringing-in — the president was elected by the layer and the core, and by the end of the term he knows the price: a low rating, an ageing core, the layer gone to the opponent, a run-off against a populist a coin toss. The protocol changes the composition of the deciders in his favour if he is a long-horizon candidate: the opponent's hysterical part takes B, the layer disappears as a factor, those who remember what he did stay (040x item 5) — for Philippe after a first term that is 60:40 instead of 50:50 (§2). He has the instrument — Article 11, a referendum called by the president without parliament: Macron could not, because his second term was already running; an incumbent in a first term can. His cost is giving up bringing-in, which has stopped working anyway; his gain is the only chance against a populist who cannot otherwise be beaten. The consequence is wider than France: the protocol is introduced not by an idealist and not by an underdog in power but by a pragmatist at the end of a first term for whom it is the last chance at a second — any president at 30 % a year before re-election. It is a third route: not from below (a referendum by petition) and not from the side (an underdog) but from above — from an incumbent who counts; and his motive is the same as the donor's and the underdog's (059g §4): a win on any outcome, because a lost referendum costs him nothing and a won one gives him a term.

**Fourth move: sabotage of a promised protocol.** The architect: "suppose Philippe promised the protocol, won, and now sabotages it. First, that will be very hard for him; and even if he confuses everyone and drags it out for four years on technical grounds, who will stop him, as president, from buying a ticket into history and introducing the protocol even against his own party in order to be re-elected?" Sabotage is the least profitable move of all, because the promise of the protocol is the only promise with a number that cannot be reinterpreted. *Hard:* an Article 11 referendum is the president's personal act — there is no hiding behind parliament or the budget; "I promised, you voted, where is the referendum?" has no technical answer; "the burned" who came back for it are the remembering (040x item 5), and they will ask; by the parties' fork the opposition takes the promise for itself — Le Pen will be first to say "he deceived you, I will hold it"; the underdog will not let the hype die. Sabotaging a promised protocol is sabotage in full view of those it was promised to, and they are not a layer that forgets. *Even if he drags it out four years* — the Constitutional Council, setting up the roll, "technical reasons" — he reaches the end of the term worse off than without the promise: a lower rating, the returned gone again, a coin-toss run-off; and the second-term trap (third move) kicks in: Article 11 is still his, the protocol still changes the composition in his favour, the party cannot stop him — a presidential referendum does not ask it — and the ticket into history and the second term lie under one button. *Result:* the promise of the protocol creates a debt the second-term trap collects either way — either he introduces it at once and wins on it, or at the end of the term against his party to save himself; sabotage does not cancel the protocol, it postpones it four years and makes the president a debtor instead of an author. This is self-enforcement (040v §6) before adoption: a promised rule protects itself with the same thing as an adopted one — the unsplit discontent of the deceived. Residual: a president who does not want a second term — old, ill, leaving — is free of the trap; but such a one would not have promised.

## 5. What remains

- **Article 11 is in the president's hands:** without a victory by Philippe or another who took the protocol, the route runs into the RIP, where 185 parliamentary signatures are a political decision of the groups, not a collection.
- **The RN's fear of the real** is not for sale (040w item 3): security and the border remain its voters under the protocol too — the RN falls to 20–22 %, not to zero.
- **The poll figures** are six months out, with 25 declared candidates; the left primary of 11 October had no result at the time of writing.

## 6. The 2027 shop window against the protocol — and the debate move "the same thing, but with better people"

The architect's question of 13 October 2026: "what ideas and slogans are in French politics now — and let us compare, if Philippe starts selling the protocol". The main candidates' shop window as of October 2026 by the repository's four tests: addressee, personal price to the voter, verifiability, an enemy.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

What the window shares: no promise has a price for the one it is made to, none is verifiable before the next election, four of six rest on an enemy — 040v item 1 in pure form, arguments for the layer.

**If Philippe sells the protocol.** His line: "€800 into your account — or your vote counts threefold"; addressee — everyone; price — named personally; verifiable — by the counter on election day; enemy — none. The only line that passes all four tests, and then it does to the others' slogans what the numeraire (040v item 1, third move) does to programmes. *"Priorité nationale" against €800:* Le Pen promises housing instead of someone else and some day, Philippe money personally and this cycle; her material part loses outright, the fear about security remains (040w item 3). *Minimum wage €1,600 and retirement at 60 against €800:* Mélenchon's promise is larger, but "someone" pays and some day; Philippe's is the budget and now, and "I return, I do not hand out" (048n) against €54 billion of cuts sounds like an audit; Mélenchon will have to explain to his young voter why he may not take the money. *Retailleau's golden rule* is a referendum on what nobody needs personally; Retailleau himself opened the door to a 2027 referendum, and Philippe walks through it with a question that will collect signatures. *Philippe's own debt and spending* get a voter for the first time: those who stay with a long horizon are the only ones to whom "it will be better later" is not an empty phrase. The slogan for France is the one of 039b §4b, and in French it is stronger: **"Un vote ne vaut rien. Aujourd'hui."** — the RN worker reads "I am not paid", the burned "it changes nothing", the Élysée "you get it for free".

**The architect's debate move — "the same thing, but with better people".** "The protocol radically improves the effectiveness of government in any sphere, and if you promote it, debating an opponent is easier: after the protocol we will do the same thing — only our best managers will do it many times better than yours." It is a universal answer to any item of an opponent's programme, and it changes the genre of debate. Today a candidate must out-promise his opponent on every topic; a candidate with the protocol says: "whatever you promise — people will execute it; yours are chosen by the brought-in and the layer, who need one of their own and an image; ours by those who count and remember, and they need a fit one". That is 1f §4b — "fitness has no buyer" — as a debate weapon: a buyer appears, and it is the one promise the opponent lacks. *The opponent cannot answer in kind:* he has no selection mechanism, only a promise; any "we are for effectiveness too" breaks on the question "and who chose you". *The protocol absorbs the opponent's programme:* Mélenchon's housing, Le Pen's security, Retailleau's debt — "yes, that too, by people chosen not for their surname"; there is no need to argue every topic on the merits, only to show once that execution depends on composition. *Effectiveness is a consequence, not a promise:* parties compete for the remembering (040x item 5), the menu changes with the customer (040ae item 5), reputation is an asset, and all of it before the first minister is appointed (1i). The answer to "you have no programme, only a rule" is ready: the rule is the programme — like an investor who buys not the product but the quality of management.

The first version ended with a residual: "the protocol does not answer the housing question over which parliament was dissolved — it answers who will decide on housing". The architect removed it as a "before" yardstick (040m, the bounty trap): "among the burned and the clever there will be bright and beautiful ideas for solving it. Now they look at the crowd and understand there is no point even proposing. And the protocol will be so pleasant to everyone that the housing question will simply be postponed for a couple of years." Two consequences. *There are no ideas not because none were thought of but because they have no buyer:* a complex housing solution loses to the slogan "freeze the rents" with the layer, and the clever stay silent — this is 040ae item 2 (demand for knowledge) from the supply side; the protocol creates the buyer — those who stay and read — and solutions appear from the same "burned" who today see no point in proposing. *A queue, not a weakness:* the first election under the protocol is about the protocol itself, about money and the share, and that beats any sectoral topic; housing returns at the second election — to a different composition of deciders and with different proposals. First change who decides, then decide. The candidate need only say so aloud first: "housing will be solved by people you choose in a new way, and there will be more solutions than now, because for the first time they will be listened to".

The architect's correction to the question itself: "all the clever have long known the answer on housing — reduce the artificially created concentration of money in the cities and remove state barriers to construction. It is all in the repo. But the present government will never do it: there is no demand from 'the apathetic', and the government loses shadow income." The answer is indeed recorded: 034 (the correction of 28.09.2026, "rules disperse, not transfers" — eastern Germany received €2 trillion and lost 2 million people, growth came only where it was cheap) and 013e ("flats can be built — raise construction", about two thousand permits a year for 2.4 million residents as the measure of the barrier). It is not applied for two reasons, and the protocol changes both. *No demand from "the apathetic":* they want the slogan "freeze the rents", not the removal of barriers with housing in three years; the government answers whoever decides (1f §5), and under the protocol those who stay with a long horizon decide — the ones for whom "in three years" works. *Shadow income:* permits, land for development, zoning are the rent of those who issue them, and removing barriers means giving up the rent; under the protocol that rent is the first line of the counter-audit (059g §4), because construction permits are the best-documented shadow income in any country, and that is exactly where "the burned" with numbers and the defectors with names come. Housing is the best demonstration of the mechanism: the solution is simple and known, what prevents applying it is the composition of the deciders and the rent, and the protocol changes the first and exposes the second.

*The architect's move on the capital.* "All countries pull money into the capital and a couple of big cities precisely because the government and the rich live there; so people move there and housing grows there. If a clever government decentralises or even moves the capital, housing prices could fall hard, by up to half." The mechanism is 029 §29.11: the price of housing in the capital is the price of proximity to power. On the remedy the evidence diverges, and that is recorded honestly. *Moving the capital* by itself hardly lowers prices in the old one: Brasília (1960) — Rio and São Paulo dearer than before; Abuja (1991) — Lagos keeps growing; Astana (1997) — Almaty stayed the most expensive city; Seoul after Sejong (2012) — prices rose, and Sejong became an object of speculation: the official moves, the economy does not. *Decentralisation plus the right to build* lowers them, and strongly: Germany is polycentric, Berlin about 5 % of GDP against roughly 30 % for Paris in France's economy, and housing relative to income is half as expensive there as in France and Britain; Tokyo — two decades without growth in real prices with a growing population, because building is permitted by right, not by negotiation; Austin 2023–2025 — rents fell 15–20 % after a construction boom 🟡. "By half" is attainable — it is the gap between London and Tokyo in price to income — but by rules, not by relocation: a polycentric budget plus the right to build (034, "rules disperse"). *Why nobody does it today and what the protocol changes:* the capital is the present government's base, and that base is two groups that vote alike today and apart under the protocol (the architect's correction: "you have confused them again: the core of power is its elite and the dependent budget employees and apathetics, and they are very sharply divided by income; the first live off the second, but both vote for the government, and the protocol divides them completely"). *The elite* — the top that passed the selection, and the rich attached to it: they live off the budget as beneficiaries, their stake is enormous, they will never take B, they always vote for themselves. *The dependent* — budget employees and "the apathetic" on low pay, who are brought: they live off the budget as recipients of crumbs and will almost always take B. Between them the layer of well-paid dependents is narrow by arithmetic: the government cannot pay everyone well, there is not enough money, so the mass's loyalty is bought cheaply and the top's dearly. The protocol cuts the government's base exactly along that gap: the dependent take B, the elite is left at the polls alone, without the mass that made it a majority, and for the first time is visible at its true size — a few per cent; the provincial stayers get a ×3 share — and for the first time decentralisation has an electorate that outweighs. The capital loses not its officials but its political premium, and the price of housing there is the price of that premium.

## 7. Retailleau: the referendum as the main idea — and why that is 9 %

The architect's request of 13 October 2026: "let us examine Retailleau separately, it is useful for the protocol: he already makes the referendum his main idea and goes to the election with it; his campaign can be taken as a good forecast." Facts: LR candidate since 19 April 2026 (73.4 % of members), former interior minister under Barnier and Bayrou, resigned after reappointment under Lecornu. The referendum as the axis: put migration policy to a referendum; extend Article 11 to "any legislative question"; lower the RIP threshold from 4.7 to 2.5 million signatures with a six-month parliamentary filter; primacy of national law by targeted revision, without leaving the EU; simplify Article 89. The rest: "la fête est finie" — €197 billion of savings, abolition of the 35 hours, a benefits cap at 70 % of the minimum wage and 15 hours of activity for RSA recipients, a flat tax, €40 billion to firms, the end of birthright citizenship. Polls: about 9 % in June, 7–10 % in the autumn 🟡; he reaches the run-off in no configuration.

**Comparison by the four tests.**

| | Retailleau's referendum | The protocol referendum |
|---|---|---|
| question | about others: migrants, birthright | about oneself: €800 or a ×3 vote |
| who writes the question | the government, he himself (040w item 4) | the charter, one line (048m Art. 15) |
| zero sum | yes: the majority decides for the minority without compromise (040ae item 1) | no: the loser gets a choice, the winner a share |
| enemy | yes | none (040ab) |
| personal price to the voter | zero; the gain is someone else's | named |
| verifiability | no | the counter |
| who benefits | those already afraid | all four groups (039b §4b) |

**Why 9 % — the architect's correction.** The first version of the examination explained: "a referendum as the main idea works only when the question is yours alone". The architect: "where does that come from? The protocol works for whoever took it as the main idea, against the same. Had Retailleau been first to speak of a referendum it would have lifted him as a pioneer, but nothing stops others from quickly taking it over." Correct, and consistent with §4: uniqueness is not a condition but a pioneer's premium; the first gets the hype and the name, the rest take it over, and the takeover is the mechanism (the parties' fork), not a misfortune. Retailleau's nine per cent have another explanation. *A referendum in itself is a procedure, not a gain:* it gives the voter nothing personally; Retailleau offers an instrument without content with a price, the protocol offers content to which a referendum is attached. *His question the voter gets without a referendum:* whoever wants immigration limited has Le Pen promising it directly; the procedure looks like a weak man's caveat — "I will do it if the people allow". *He is not the pioneer of the referendum:* the yellow vests demanded the RIC in 2018, Le Pen promised an immigration referendum; the pioneer's premium went to others, and a copy without content is exactly 9 %. The lesson for the protocol: whoever is first is the pioneer and gets the premium (059g §4), the rest take it over quickly, and each who takes it over takes mass from Le Pen and makes the fork inevitable; the protocol wins not by uniqueness but by being content with a price that it pays to take over.

**The fiscal lesson.** "La fête est finie", the benefits cap and €197 billion of savings are "take from the dependent": the dependent fear him, the elite has its own candidate in Le Pen (§6), and he stands between the two groups of the government's base, needed by neither. The protocol does the same fiscal work — the counter-audit, €54 billion (059g §4) — without an enemy and with a payment to the dependent: austerity sells only with a dividend ("I return, I do not hand out", 048n), and Retailleau shows that without a dividend it yields 9 %.

**What he gives the protocol.** His reforms — Article 11 for any legislative question and a RIP threshold of 2.5 million — halve the route of §4, and they can pass only under a winner who needs them: Philippe, by the second-term trap. Retailleau legalises the instrument; the protocol brings the question.

**Forecast 🟡.** Unchanged: 7–10 % in the first round, eliminated, his voter goes to Le Pen in the second; his referendum reforms live only if the winner takes them. Had Retailleau taken the protocol himself: the only right-wing candidate not competing with Le Pen for the same fear, and the only one whose austerity gets a dividend — plus 5–8 points, up to 15 %, a contest for the run-off with Philippe. For him the protocol pays more than for any other candidate on the right: he is closest to it in instrument and furthest in question.

## Weak point

The forecast under the protocol is a deduction from the composition of electorates, not a measurement; the shares "taking B" in the RN and on the left are estimates by 040ac and 040ai, and only a pilot will give the first measurement. 055b §7 gave Lisnard 5–8 %, here 1–3 % by more recent mentions; both figures come not from polls but from Lisnard's absence from them. And the architect's second move is a forecast of Philippe's behaviour: taking the protocol is rational, but Horizons and the centre may decide otherwise out of fear of the words "paid not to vote" (001d, a misreading).
