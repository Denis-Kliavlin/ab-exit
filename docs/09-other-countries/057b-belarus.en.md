# Belarus: a Trap for the Populist and the Correspondence with Tsikhanouskaya's Office

**Chapter:** 09 — Cases: Other Countries
**File:** 09_057b · v1 · 15 September 2026 (correspondence of 1–15 September 2026; Gemini dialogue)
**Source:** three letters from the Office of Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya and two replies by the author (September 2026), with the author's consent; the Office's position is given in paraphrase. Supplements 035 (Route B: Belarus 2020) and 040i (Linz: authoritarianism and apathy).

## 1. Why this case

This is the first written reaction of an active political headquarters to the protocol — and the first instance where AB-EXIT is considered not as a norm of future electoral law but as a **political demand before the transition**. Both sides kept their positions; the value lies in the precise record of where the premises diverge.

## 2. The Office's position (three letters, paraphrased)

**Letter 1.** A fundamental disagreement with the framing: a democratic state should stimulate participation, not create a legal channel of refusal, still less one backed by money; "we encourage non-participation" contradicts the logic of popular sovereignty; turnout is a value in itself. The mechanism does not solve the stated problems: it institutionalises distrust of candidates; it does not undermine vote buying (the buyer is interested in a vote *for*, not in neutralisation); it replaces fictitious turnout with another formalised form of non-participation. Methodologically: "several AI models found no contradiction" is not proof; models tend to agree with consistent argumentation; a literature review is needed (quadratic voting, liquid democracy, compensated abstention) and empirical evidence. The Office's priority is the Electoral Code for the draft Constitution of a New Belarus; the topic lies outside the agenda.

**Letter 2.** The reasoning is speculative: in Belarus there are no elections in any substantive sense — the protocols are filled in by quota, the result depends neither on turnout nor on the A/B choice; the "trap" has nowhere to arise, the authorities have no need to explain anything to the electorate; "there is no subject to which the mechanism can be applied". Two initial premises are mistaken: that a democratic state should create a channel of refusal, and that willingness to give up the vote for compensation is linked to the intensity of preferences.

## 3. The author's reply: two premises the protocol does not make

**The first.** AB-EXIT does not claim that the state is *obliged* to pay for non-participation. The hypothesis is narrower: citizens have the right to decide at a referendum whether to add to the existing right an option identical for all, voluntary and reversible — keep the vote, or receive a predetermined dividend and not vote in one cycle only. Everyone retains an equal right; the state does not decide who is worthy; there is no census; no party receives the vote; until the date the decision is revocable; in the next cycle the right is restored automatically. A legal channel of non-participation already exists — a person simply does not turn up; the protocol does not invent refusal, it turns silent unrecorded behaviour into explicit and measurable behaviour. The question is not "should the state encourage non-participation" but: *why is passive free non-attendance an acceptable part of democracy, while a voluntary formalised decision by the same citizen is necessarily anti-democratic?* Participation is measured at two levels: the choice of mode for the cycle (A or B) — everyone participates; the choice of candidate — only A. The one who chose B did not stay home out of indifference: he acquainted himself with the candidates, the amount, the consequences, and registered a decision.

**The second.** The protocol does not require the link "chose B — therefore indifferent or incompetent": B may be explained by poverty, distrust, absence of candidates, lack of time, fear, dependence or rational delegation. The individual choice shows only U(dividend) > U(participation in this cycle). For the mechanism to work, a weaker, testable hypothesis suffices: P(B | weak autonomous value of participation) > P(B | strong). If research shows that the choice is determined almost exclusively by poverty and motivation has no independent effect, the central prediction is refuted. Hence the requirements for a pilot: a limited size of D, a ban on pressure and on lending against the payment, a period for changing the decision, secrecy of the choice, automatic restoration, analysis of A/B by income, pre-set stopping criteria. Agreement with the Office on one point: AI dialogues are a preliminary logical stress test, not empirical evidence; the protocol is a testable institutional hypothesis.

**Vote buying.** The literature distinguishes turnout buying (paying passive supporters to come) and negative vote buying (paying opponents not to come); in an authoritarian system it is precisely the managed attendance of the dependent that is valuable. The protocol changes the economics: a private buyer must compensate the loss of D, add a risk premium, and still not control the secret ballot; every paid B creates a verifiable record (decision, budget obligation, transaction, impossibility of counting the person as having voted).

## 4. The trap for Lukashenko

The regime builds its legitimacy on four claims: the people are wise and choose their government themselves; the country is a successful social state; the government is supported by the majority; the elections are honest. If all four are accepted, the authorities have no reason to fear a referendum with the question: *do you want, before every election, to choose for yourself — keep a full vote, or receive a guaranteed dividend and not participate in this cycle?* A supporter keeps A and votes for Lukashenko; a person in need takes B; nobody is forced to support the opposition.

- *Support it* — a significant part of the soft and dependent electorate gets a safe alternative to administrative mobilisation: the pensioner need not become an oppositionist — "I am not against the government, I just need the payment more". 40–50 % of declared support ≠ 40–50 % ready to pay with the dividend for one more vote: the protocol tests not the direction of sympathy but its intensity. At the same time the "burned" independent citizens who do not participate because the outcome is predetermined get grounds to return to A.
- *Oppose it* — explain to your own supporter why he may not decide what he needs more; every answer destroys one of the myths: "the people don't understand" — you don't trust the people's wisdom; "there is no money in the budget" — what about the success; "refusal must not be encouraged" — "if I want to vote for you, I keep A; why do you fear the choice of others?"; "the majority supports us anyway" — then the protocol is no threat.
- *Prevent the referendum* — a contradiction of popular sovereignty; *falsify it* — show that the authorities fear a question that does not require voting against them.

The demand cuts across the division "for the government — against": the oppositionist — for honesty; the poor — for the payment; the liberal — for freedom of choice; the Lukashenko supporter — confident he will keep A; the apathetic — a direct interest for the first time; the public-sector worker — a confidential exit from administrative voting. Such a coalition cannot be declared "enemies of the state".

## 5. Where the dispute remains open

The Office is right: officially adopting and honestly testing the protocol under the current system is impossible. The author separates three questions: (1) is adoption possible now — no; (2) can the behavioural hypothesis be tested without state adoption — yes; (3) can *discussion* of AB-EXIT engage a citizen whom the opposition agenda does not reach — open, and not considered in the Office's replies. The published "Strategy of the Democratic Forces 2026+" addresses people who already want a democratic Belarus; it contains no separate mechanism for the person who continues to support Lukashenko and is not ready to become an oppositionist. The protocol is interesting precisely for this property: a person first supports a right that benefits him, and then watches how the authorities react to a peaceful universal demand. Plus an argument for the day after tomorrow: after Lukashenko leaves, the regime's base remains and the administrative electorate can be restored — the protocol as protection of a New Belarus against restoration.

## 6. Weak point of the case

The "trap" argument assumes that the authorities *have to* answer. The Office's second letter strikes exactly here: an imitation regime does not have to. The author's answer — discussion does not require an honest count, it is enough that the question "why can't we decide for ourselves" begins to circulate — is plausible but has not a single empirical example. This is an honestly recorded disagreement, not a closed dispute. 🟡
