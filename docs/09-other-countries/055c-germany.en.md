## 1. The problem: a party can be bought from outside

Galliamov: a significant part of the AfD's list and leadership is loyal to the Kremlin (Crimea, compliments to Putin) and probably simply bought; the "German direction" may be run by a person with enormous unaccounted resources of a state company. The vulnerability of classical democracy: an external dictator finances, through front foundations, a radical anti-system party which, using legal mechanisms and real fears (migration, the economy), takes the votes of the discontented and destabilises the system from within.

## 2. Four ways the protocol breaks bribery

1. **Protest voting stops being free.** People vote for the AfD not so much out of love for Russia as to "show the middle finger" to the SPD and the CDU; turning up and casting a ballot for radicals costs nothing. Under AB-EXIT, to vote for populists a burgher must refuse the dividend. Frivolous protest disappears; what remains are ideological fanatics, of whom there are mathematically not enough.
2. **Ruining the sponsor through vote arbitrage.** To persuade a voter to vote for the party, the sponsor would have to not pay for advertising but *outbid the state*: pour in enough cash directly to cover the legal payment plus the risk. Secret financing becomes astronomically expensive even for an oil company.
3. **From the plane of fear into the plane of P&L.** The AfD feeds on fear of migrants; the mainstream gets an argument: "deportations will wreck the labour market and lead to sanctions; if they come, your dividend next cycle falls to zero". Defence against bought radicals is built not on morality ("don't vote for Nazis") but on direct financial protection: stable government = high dividend.
4. **The thermostat against a radical government.** Even if the bought party breaks through (Saxony, the federal level) and starts destroying institutions, radical policy hits the economy; the dividend falls algorithmically; millions of moderates who took the money refuse the cheapened dividend and activate their vote with the multiplier; at the next (or early) election the party is swept away.

Summary: the electoral field turns from an "audience of spectators", easily deceived by propaganda on someone else's money, into a "board of shareholders", who are expensive to buy and who fire managers that bring losses.

## 3. Milov: "special filters" and the paradox of the open society

In his broadcast of 15 September 2026 Milov explains the AfD's victory in the state elections by the "chauvinism and political egoism" of East Germany, and then, regarding the invitation of an AfD representative onto a programme: "journalistic standards need correcting… the viewer is being processed by professional participants in information-psychological wars… journalists must take part in the resistance… at home you poison cockroaches, but in the information space you let every fish into the pond… against the AfD special filters are needed". A representative of the liberal opposition calls for censorship because he fears the voter is too stupid.

This is the paradox of the open society: give everyone a voice — populists with simple solutions come (cheap gas, deportation); introduce "special filters" — you become the dictator yourself. Democracy either dies of populism or kills itself with censorship. The protocol removes the dilemma without touching information: it changes the **economics of attention**. Populists sell emotions — that works only on an apathetic, dependent, poor mass whose vote is worth nothing; the protocol offers that mass (those very voters in East Germany) a guaranteed dividend in exchange for giving up the ballot. A poor angry resident of a notional Leipzig: vote for the AfD for free in the hope of cheap gas in a year — or take about 600 euros today (by the formula for the Saxony-Anhalt median of ≈ €3,400/month; the "1,500 euros" of the first version was dialogue drift, 033c.9c)? 70–80 % will choose the money — the grounds for this rate are in §6. After that the AfD can appear around the clock on every channel without any filters — not "nobody left to elect it" but nobody left to crush with: by the calculation on the September 2026 data (§6) it turns from a dominant party at 44 % into one of four or five comparable ones at 13–28 %. At the helm remain those to whom slogans cannot be sold — they need business plans, tax schedules and deregulation. "Milov wants to fight the virus by taping over people's eyes and ears. The protocol does not touch freedom of speech — it removes from the casino the chips the populists play with." For investors obsessed with free speech — the strongest argument.

## 4. Shepelin: "there is no simple antidote"

Thirty minutes of precise analysis of how propaganda works — and to the question "how to counter it?" the answer: get acquainted with different points of view, listen to the unpleasant, compare, ask yourself questions, "constant cognitive work" — and the sad admission that there is no antidote. This is capitulation: to propose that a tired man from the factory become an analyst is "like proposing that a man being beaten with a truncheon constantly analyse the trajectory of the blow cognitively". The false premise: people believe television not because they lack information but because it is emotionally profitable — cheap dopamine of belonging to a "great pack", the status of moral winner, a comprehensible enemy; "self-therapy". A free drug cannot be beaten by lectures on the harm of drugs.

The protocol does not climb into the head: it does not care what a person watches. It severs the link between the viewer's brain and the control button. Propaganda pays off only while the viewer has a vote: billions are spent on TV hosts for the sake of loyal voting or loyal silence. Interception of the incentive: keep watching, hating the West and believing anything you like — but press Cash Out for the dividend; the person is crossed out of the equation, his brain no longer converts into support. When 70–80 % of the TV audience annul their vote, the remaining core looks at the state television budget: "why billions? these viewers don't vote — close the channel, money into infrastructure". Propaganda dies not from media literacy but from unprofitability: political scientists and journalists treat the symptoms (persuade people to read different sources) — the protocol cuts the power cable.

## 5. Weak point of the case

Method 2 assumes that buying goes at the price of the dividend; real buying in the EU goes through party coffers and media, not direct payments to voters, and its price is not D per person but the cost of reach. The correct formulation: the protocol does not make bribery *more expensive*, it makes bought reach *useless* (the audience has left). Plus — Germany: a federal referendum is not provided for by the constitution; the adoption path is the state level or a two-thirds constitutional amendment, which sharply changes the assessment of the "detonator". 🟡

### 6.2. By what criterion to count the exit rate

The assistant's first two calculations were wrong, and the error is instructive for every country case. First the "took B" rate was counted by income (€600 is a third of a worker's monthly net, so they take reluctantly); then by stated motives (a political scientist's interview divides the AfD electorate into three thirds: anti-system radicals, single-issue voters, current discontent; two thirds were booked as "ideologues" who do not take). Both criteria are wrong. The protocol counts the rate by the **personal stake in the outcome**: does the person have, in this cycle, a reason worth D (015c §7a: the content take, the aggrieved with a reason vote). A stated motive is a stated preference, not a stake (001b §3: 49 % of Leave named sovereignty, 69 % thought the difference was small).

By this criterion the KAS data read differently from first sight. 52 % of Saxony-Anhalters rate their personal situation as good and only 12 % as bad, with 43.8 % for the AfD ("I am fine, the country is not"). This is not "they are not poor, so they will not take" but **the vote has no personal stake**: a grievance for the country rather than for oneself is exactly the free protest that D crowds out. The 170 thousand mobilised non-voters, the 36 % "out of disappointment", the 66 % of AfD supporters feeling they "get less than a fair share" — this is the addressee of 040i.2b, to whom the opposition could offer nothing, and the dividend answers his very wording. The ideological activist core of populists the repository estimates at 1–2 % of the electorate (045.6) — 17–34 thousand people, not two thirds of 576 thousand. In this cycle the personal stake lies on the other side: with those who came to **stop** the AfD (defensive voting, 018 §38) and with the value voters of the Greens and Linke.

One principle worth recording separately: **the protocol changes party shares only through the difference in exit rates.** If all segments take the money equally, the percentages do not change at all — only turnout does. The protocol's whole force against a populist lies in his base taking more often than others, not in its being poorer.

### 6.3b. How large K should be: a calculation with the simulator's formula

*(Added 29.09.2026; the conclusion accepted by the architect: "populists win only if the payment is insufficient.")*

Table 6.3 holds exit rates constant. To see what a higher K does, the dependence of the rate on the sum is needed. It already exists in the repository's simulator (`simulation/referendum-lab/model.mjs`, function `estimateElectionChoice`):

```
logit(p_exit) = a + 1.65·ln(1 + S) − 0.9·civic_utility − sensitivity·1.15·ln(W) − 0.35·ROI
S = D / monthly_income × liquidity_stress
W = 1 / (1 − share_taking)
```

Three properties of the formula decide the outcome. **A hyperbola over the remainder:** the odds of taking grow as (1 + S)^1.65, and S for the poor segment in the simulator's profile is roughly a hundred times that of the rich one, so a higher D barely touches the rich and pulls the poor hard — the poor vote for populists, the well-off for the sensible, and there is no proportional response to the sum. **ROI:** the political stake grows with income and assets and keeps the well-off in A. **The feedback W:** the more take, the more valuable the remaining vote, and some people come back.

The shifts *a* are calibrated to reproduce the rates of table 6.3 at K = 1%; the income profiles of the electorates are an assumption (average household income €2,800 a month; AfD — 0.6 of the median with liquidity stress 1.5; CDU — 1.1 and 0.8; Greens — 1.6 and 0.4; Linke and others — 0.7 and 1.2).

| | Voting, % of roll | Vote weight | AfD exit | CDU exit | Greens exit | **AfD** | CDU | Greens | Linke | SPD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fact | 78 | 1 | — | — | — | **43.8** | 17.2 | 8.9 | 8.6 | 9.3 |
| K = 1% | 33.5 | 2.7 | 80% | 40% | 25% | **20.4** | 24.0 | 15.5 | 14.0 | 13.0 |
| K = 2% | 30.4 | 3.05 | 86% | 41% | 23% | **16.3** | 25.9 | 17.6 | 14.2 | 13.7 |
| K = 3% | 28.1 | 3.3 | 89% | 43% | 22% | **13.6** | 27.2 | 19.4 | 14.2 | 14.2 |

What follows:

- **A populist wins only if the payment is insufficient.** His base is poor, and at a sufficient sum it takes the money more often than anyone; the AfD keeps first place only below the threshold of 73% exit among its electorate (6.3).
- **The mainstream does not flee from the sum.** CDU exit barely changes, and Greens exit even falls as the sum grows (25 → 23%): for them the sum is small, and the increased weight of the vote keeps them. The scenario "a large sum will lure the sensible away too" does not arise under the repository's formula.
- **The effect saturates; the cost does not.** From 1% to 2% the AfD loses 4.1 pp, from 2% to 3% 2.7 pp; the cost grows almost linearly (≈4% of the Land budget at 1%, ≈13% at 3%, 045). For Saxony-Anhalt a sensible starting point is around 2%: the AfD in third place with 16.3%.
- **Turnout falls gently** (33 → 30 → 28% of the roll), because the rising vote weight brings some people back.
- **There is no equilibrium — the system keeps moving.** The architect's correction: "for the money some of the apathetic leave, but some come for the vote ×2; that is the essence of AB-EXIT — there is no balance, it always moves." The table is a snapshot of one cycle, not a point of rest: the exit of some raises the vote weight, the higher weight brings in others (in the simulator — core activation and transitions between cycles, `coreActivation`, `cycleTransition`), and their arrival lowers the weight again. Party percentages will fluctuate from cycle to cycle. The sum is fixed by referendum; when it changes, **both sides shift at once** — those who take the money and those who come back for the increased weight of the vote. So the effect of changing the sum cannot be obtained by moving only one of them, and an exact percentage is hard to compute: this is not a flaw of the calculation but a property of the mechanism (001b §4). **The third moving parameter is the automatic adjustment of the sum.** The sum is set not in roubles or euros but as a percentage of the median income, so it moves with the economy without a new referendum. For a person whose income moves with the median, the ratio of the sum to the remainder (S) does not change; it shifts where income diverges from the median — for those who grow poorer or richer faster than the rest. So the calculation has three moving quantities: those who take the money, those who come back for the vote weight, and the sum itself, which follows the median.

The simulator itself marks the formula's coefficients as a hypothesis; the income profiles by party are the assistant's assumption. The shape of the dependence — a hyperbola over the remainder and the well-off held by vote weight — does not depend on these numbers.

### 6.4. Berlin: populism with a personal stake, and why it is a textbook case, not a boundary

Rent is populism pure and explicit: price = demand / supply; demand is the city's growth, supply is squeezed by the state itself (permits taking years, standards, construction costs, the Mietpreisbremse since 2015), and a party promising to treat the price by controlling the price treats the symptom with the instrument that produces the disease. The analogues are measured: Berlin's Mietendeckel 2020–2021 — rental supply in the regulated segment fell roughly by half in a year, flats went to sale, the court struck it down in April 2021; San Francisco (Diamond, McQuade, Qian 2019) — rental housing supply −15 %, city-wide rents rose. The landlord is a baker with one bakery: bought on credit, renovated, pays tax, competes with tens of thousands like him at 1–2 % net; he cannot jack up the price, and when it rises for everyone that is a shortage of flour, not a cartel of bakers. The populist points at the baker so as not to point at the one who holds the flour.

The Berlin tenant, unlike the AfD voter, has a personal stake — the bill arrives on the first of the month. By the repository's model he does not take D and votes. The assistant's first reading was "so the protocol does not remove him, and he votes for what will make him worse off in a cycle; the dividend sensor does not see the shrinking supply". The architect's correction changes the conclusion: the protocol selects not by intelligence but by **discount rate**. Refusing €720 now for influence later is itself an act of long horizon; D is taken by the one for whom the future is next Tuesday (015b.4). Populism lives exactly on the short rate — benefit now, the bill beyond the horizon — and the protocol subtracts precisely its carriers. Today a politician with a supply chart loses not because the argument is bad but because his listener is not interested in "later" (040i.2b: nothing to offer to one whose vote costs nothing). After the protocol the listener is produced by selection: in the hall are those who paid for their seat and have already chosen "later". They can be shown the Mietendeckel as a measured fact.

And the sane politician's programme here is not "control is harmful" (an abstraction) but "stop doing" by 019b.3: remove the procedures that block building — a promise the tenant checks by his own yard within a cycle: cranes from the window, the number of permits, flats completed. The dispute after the protocol is two verifiable promises before people with a long horizon: "I will freeze your price; how many flats there will be in three years I will not say" against "I will remove three procedures and show the number of permits in a year". The first is addressed to the short rate, whose carriers have left for D.

### 6.5. Weak point of the section

The exit rates are a model, not a measurement; their dependence on the sum (a third of a month against a month, 033c.9c) is unmeasured and is the first question for a pilot. The division of the electorate by personal stake is made from indirect signs (self-rated situation, origin of votes), not from a direct question "would you take". For Mecklenburg the repository's two models give opposite signs. In Berlin the share of sitting tenants for whom a cap pays even in the long run (the bill is paid by newcomers and their children) is not counted: with 85 % tenants it may be a majority of those remaining, and there the argument strikes at interest, not at misunderstanding. 🟡

### 6.6. A campaign in Germany: fight for the right to ask, not promise a payment

The wall of § 108b StGB (criminal liability for offering a benefit "for not voting") and the absence of a federal referendum make the promise "I will pay" undeliverable — the first crash point of 6.5 and the Merz effect from the same interview: "you will feel it in your wallet" → they did not → a double minus. The architect's correction turns the wall into the engine: the underdog's promise is honest and different — **"the government has deprived you of the right to dispose of your own vote and has forbidden even asking you; I will fight until it is allowed — courts and whatever it takes."** It is delivered in the first week after nomination, because what is promised is an action, not a sum:

1. filing a Volksbegehren at Land level (Berlin, Brandenburg, Saxony — where the procedure exists);
2. a lawsuit against applying § 108b to a public offer by the state adopted by a citizens' vote (a test of the "equal" principle of Art. 38 GG: every ballot still counts as one, 033c.9b);
3. a private vote in one district of ~1,000 people with a real neighbourhood fund and a real fork "€25–30 or a weighted vote" (052; 035 — a private referendum as a poll protected by freedom of speech).

Why this is brighter than any payment and why the government loses on every answer. In Germany vote-buying already exists and is legal: under the party-financing law the state pays parties about €1 for every vote cast for them, of the order of €200 million a year. The question of 033b "why may they and not I?" is here not rhetoric but a budget line. The government's four answers (046.4) — fiscal ("irresponsible", with €200 million to parties), sacred ("the vote is not for sale" — but parties are paid for it), paternalistic ("they'll vote with their wallet"), honest — each becomes a campaign clip; there is no fifth answer. Then Route B works (035): parliament's silence is a scandal, ridicule is "he called you fools", a ban on the private poll is autocracy on camera.

Every word of the promise is verifiable: the ban is real, the fight is real, the outcome is not promised. The candidate does not say "I will give" — he says "I will ask you and will not let them forbid you to answer". For a campaign plan this changes the core of the task: not "how to sell a payment" but **how to sell the right to ask**.

The weak point comes from 022 §67.3: procedural promises are bland ("we'll change the way votes are counted"). Here the process is saved by two elements the centrists never had — a sum in the headline and a named culprit; without either, "I will fight for your right" sounds like RCV. Every piece of material must carry both: €600 and the one who forbade it. 🟡

**What this means for the protocol — the architect's correction, 04.10.2026.** The first version of this paragraph asserted: "the young and the poor will take the money, the share of the old among those voting will grow, the protocol will deepen the pensioners' republic". That was an error, and an error against the protocol's own theory: selection by income is what the repository calls untested (040m §4), while the protocol's theory is selection by stake (013f). The architect: "a quarter — and they decide for the whole country; they accumulated wealth, passed laws for themselves, and they are only 25 %. The young and the poor will never take it: they want change, they want to earn as these old ones once did — a house in ten years, not in a lifetime. And the old, even the rich ones, are greedy, with a short horizon; the grandfather no longer cares. Watching television and discussing populists is not analysis. Cognitive ability and age are closely linked. And the most important thing is the readiness to take risk: important reforms are always risky, and they are incapable of accepting them."

By stake the picture is the reverse of what stood here. A young person who wants a house in ten years has a stake and will not sell it for 675 euros. A pensioner who "no longer cares", with the pension level guaranteed by law until 2031, has no stake — and if he is greedy, he will take 675 euros for nothing. Greed, a short horizon and "I no longer care" is the portrait of one who exits, not of one who stays. Then in Germany the protocol works against the pensioners' republic in the only way that takes nobody's right away: the indifferent one leaves by himself, for money, and those who have something to lose stay to decide.

The word "thinking" in the table above must be read narrowly: informed — yes; analysing — no. And on age and risk there are data, and they are on the architect's side: willingness to take risk falls monotonically with age — measured on the German SOEP panel (Dohmen et al., 2011); processing speed and mental flexibility decline from the thirties while accumulated knowledge is retained. A quarter of the country — the richest, with the lowest readiness for risk — decides for everyone, and that is why no major reform has passed in Germany since "Agenda 2010": not because these people are wicked, but because a reform is a risk, and they do not accept risk by age and by wealth: there is much to lose and no time to win.

**Pay above contribution — whose law it is.** The architect, 04.10.2026: "in the German system pay rises with seniority — the old passed that law for themselves, through populists and trade unions". Economics has a name for it — insiders and outsiders (Lindbeck and Snower): the rules are written by those inside, and written for themselves. The mechanisms: seniority steps in the TVöD and TV-L agreements and service-age steps for civil servants — pay rises by years, not by results; "social selection" in redundancies by age and tenure (§1 of the Protection against Dismissal Act) — the old are dismissed last; fixed-term contracts — several times more frequent among the young than among the older, a two-tier market since the Hartz reforms; the unions age with their members, and the median voter is over fifty. The caveat: deferred pay also benefits the employer — it retains and disciplines (Lazear), so it is not a pure capture; but who decides to whom the deferred pay goes and to whom the fixed-term contract is named correctly — those inside, who vote. For the protocol this is the same as §6.8, only about money rather than war: the rules are written by the deciders and written for themselves; the protocol does not take their right to write them away — it removes from the deciders those who no longer care and returns those who have something to lose from these rules.

**What remains honest.** Who exactly will take the payment in Germany — the indifferent old or the poor young — nobody knows; that is the question for the pilot (048l). But the first version of the paragraph was not only unproven, it contradicted the theory; the architect's version agrees with it. The lever of the Russian core — "you are made to do it for free" (056f §13q) — still does not apply to the German voter: nobody makes him. The question to him is different: will he take 675 euros for what he no longer cares about.

### 6.8. An electorate that cannot fight

The architect's thesis, 04.10.2026: "An electorate raised by populists is very convenient, obedient and predictable. But there is one problem: it cannot fight. And the dictators of the world know it. If Putin or any other dictator attacks Germany or another European country — will the core electorate of the parties now in power go to war?"

**Data.** Forsa, July 2025: 16 % are "definitely" ready to defend Germany with a weapon, a further 22 % "probably". Among those over 60, 68 % refuse; among the 18–29, 56 %; among women, 72 %. Under the new NATO targets the Bundeswehr needs 260 thousand active soldiers and 200 thousand reservists; from 1 July 2027 registration and screening become compulsory for men born in 2008 and later; service remains voluntary for now, and conscription returns if recruitment falls short of plan.

**What follows.** The decider is the one over 60 (§6.7): a quarter of the country, 85 % turnout — and 68 % of them will not go to war. Those who will go, if they go, are the eighteen-year-olds: just registered, with the least weight in this electorate, and for whom in this system a house takes a lifetime. The decision on war and peace is made by some, the price is paid by others. It is the purest case of the divergence between who decides and who pays — what 019 calls the cost of bad government, here in units of lives.

**The dictator knows it about himself too.** Putin's core — the same woman of 056f §13q — cannot fight; Russia fights not with its electorate but with bought contract soldiers (056e). A government that pays millions for a body and nothing for a vote has itself signed the price tag of its "popular support". And an attacker reckons the same about his opponent: an electorate that "no longer cares" will not defend those it votes for. The architect's refinement the same day: "the dictator has it easier — he has force, he can compel, as Putin did in Penza; and the AfD can tie people up and make them fight in exactly the same way". That is the asymmetry. A dictatorship covers the incapacity of its electorate with coercion: the mobilisation of 2022 — the same grandmother does not fight, but her son is tied up and driven away. A democracy cannot do that while it remains a democracy. But a populist in power can: having taken power, he will compel in the same way, and that is Orbán's spiral (039) with a war at the end. Hence a democracy has three paths: an electorate that does not fight; coercion, that is, the end of democracy; or handing the decision in advance and by consent to those who will fight. The third is the protocol.

**Russia as the mirror.** The architect, 04.10.2026: "those who vote for him are those who do not go to fight, and that is exactly why he can fight for a long time. Had Russia had the protocol, there would have been no war, and the Duma would be different, and the governors. And these aunts are so frightened that they do not even stand up for their children and grandchildren." The mechanism is measurable: the woman of 056f §13q votes, a contract soldier from a poor region fights (056e), and between them there is not a single point at which the first could stop the second. Those who approve do not pay; those who pay do not decide — so the war goes on. "There would have been no war" is a counterfactual, it cannot be tested, and here it is recorded as the architect's thesis. The defensible formulation is weaker and firmer: the decision would have had to pass through those who pay for it; the Duma would have been composed of the votes of those who came by themselves, not of those brought in; the counter would have shown the base shrinking before 2022, not after. What the authorities would have done with that is unknown; what is known is that they would have had to do it in plain view. And the aunt who does not stand up for her grandson is the exact portrait of the meek: fear is stronger than motherhood, because to stand up is to "come out". The protocol gives her the only way to stand up without coming out: to take the money and not come — the first act in forty years for which she will not be punished.

**What the protocol does.** It selects the deciders by stake (013f). When the threat of war is real, the stake is one's own life and one's children; the twenty-year-old has it and the one who no longer cares does not. The latter exits for 675 euros, the former stays to decide — and for the first time the weight of the decision on war falls on those who will pay for it. An electorate raised on promises is convenient, obedient and predictable as long as there is nothing to defend; the protocol is the only known way for a country to hand the decision, before a war, to those who will fight it, without taking anyone's right away. That is the "vaccine against tyrants" (009) from the other side: it acts not only within, but on whether anyone attacks from without.

**The honest boundary.** Polls on readiness to fight are soft: Ukraine in 2022 showed that real mobilisation runs higher than predicted. Among young Germans refusal is also high — 56 %. The data confirm the age gap, not that populists created it; that is the architect's thesis, and it is recorded as a thesis.
