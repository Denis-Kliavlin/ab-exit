# 57e. 🇪🇸 Spain in the 1960s: the nearest analogue and four missing conditions

**Chapter:** 09 — Other countries
**File:** 09_057e · v1 · 22 September 2026
**Source:** the architect's question about the resemblance between present-day Russia and Francoism of the sixties; verification of the material against open sources; comparison with the September 2026 analysis (056f) and with the two-passenger-flow formulation (056, correction of 2026)

## 57e.1. Why this section: Linz without his own case

The repository has Linz — [040i](../06-critique-arsenal/040i-linz-shulman.en.md) builds on him its most important argument: authoritarianism differs from totalitarianism in living by **demobilisation**, and therefore works only while apathy is free. But Linz built the very concept of an authoritarian regime on one specific country: his 1964 paper is titled "An Authoritarian Regime: Spain". **We use his distinction and do not use his material.**

This costs us in two places. First, Francoist Spain is the nearest observable case of a personalist regime with demobilising legitimation surviving to the ruler's death and passing to another order. Second, and more importantly, it contains **a recorded case of the very mechanism the protocol relies on actually firing** (57e.3).

This section fills both gaps and, by the rule of [23b.1](../05-empirical-base/023b-evidence-rules.en.md), is obliged to say not only how far the analogue carries but where it ends.

## 57e.2. Ten parameters

The parameters were chosen on two grounds: Linz's own distinctions (mobilisation, limited pluralism, type of party) and whatever produced the transition in Spain. The selection is contestable; another set would give another score.

| # | Parameter | Spain, 1960s | Russia, 2026 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Demobilisation as fuel** | After 1945 the regime deliberately damps Falangist enthusiasm; in Linz's terms a "mentality" rather than an ideology, with apathy encouraged | "Stay out of politics"; mobilisation exists but is administrative — on the vertical's command rather than from conviction (056f.1) | **matches** |
| 2 | **Limited pluralism** | Real "families" with public representation in the Cortes: Opus Dei technocrats, Falangists, monarchists, the military, the Church | Clans exist (056b, the "food chain") but are purely informal, with no platform of their own | partial |
| 3 | **The ruling party** | The National Movement — a bureaucratic apparatus, deliberately not a movement; membership is career infrastructure | The same by function | **matches** |
| 4 | **Repression** | Selective: the Public Order Court (1963) in place of military tribunals, aimed at organisers, with isolated executions | Selective, long sentences, labels for organisations; aimed at the organiser, not the participant | **matches** |
| 5 | **Economic vector** | Opening: the stabilisation plan of 1959, growth of **6.5–7.3 % a year in 1960–74**, second in the world after Japan; foreign capital, mass tourism [verified] | Closure: sanctions, war economy, a turn towards China | **opposite** |
| 6 | **External position** | Junior partner of the USA (bases from 1953), an anti-communist asset, EEC application in 1962, preferential agreement in 1970; no war | Confrontation with the West; war | **opposite** |
| 7 | **The legitimating Church** | **Defected**: the Second Vatican Council 1962–65, worker priests, the Basque clergy; the church becomes the only legal place of assembly | The Russian Orthodox Church has not defected and shows no sign of it | **opposite** |
| 8 | **Elections and the permitted channel** | Union elections of 1960, 1963 and 1966 — compulsory for the dependent and legally sterile; in autumn 1966 **5,679 enlaces sindicales were elected, 70 % of them new**, and the Workers' Commissions took the channel from inside [verified] | September 2026: the commissions saw the genuine result and rewrote it "as if by a ruler", without friction (056f.1) | same form, **opposite consequence** |
| 9 | **Succession** | Institutionalised: in 1969 Juan Carlos was designated successor. The elites acquire a point of assembly | Not institutionalised | **opposite** |
| 10 | **Emigration as a valve** | About **1.3 million** to Western Europe in 1960–85 (over 500,000 to Switzerland, over 400,000 to West Germany, about 277,000 to France), remittances a balance-of-payments item; those who left returned with Western norms [verified] | The wave after 2022; in 2026 the overseas polling stations routed the ruling party (048i.5b) | matches, but the émigré is coded as an enemy |

**Score: four matches, two partial, four opposites.**

### Why that score is not "half alike"

It divides not at random but along a single line.

**Everything describing the regime statically matches:** what it feeds on, how the party is built, whom it imprisons and for what, where surplus population goes. In Linz's terms this is one and the same type, and there is nothing to argue about.

**Everything that produced the transition in Spain is opposite:** economic growth that created a middle class and foreign entanglement; the defection of the legitimating institution; a designated successor. Spain in the sixties was not "authoritarianism before the dictator's death" in general, but an authoritarianism **inside which four processes preparing the exit were already under way**. In Russia in 2026 not one of them is under way, and two run in the opposite direction.

Hence a conclusion aimed against the commonest use of this analogy: **it serves for description and fails for forecasting.** The reasoning "Russia is like late Francoism, therefore a transition will follow the ruler's death" rests on exactly those four parameters that do not match.

## 57e.3. Autumn 1966: a confirmed case of the compelled voting disloyally

Row 8 is worth all the rest of the table, and here is why.

Ekaterina Shulman's formulation after September 2026 describes the machinery of managed elections by a single regularity: **the loyal electorate must come, the disloyal must not.** Hence two failure modes: the first group does not come, or comes and votes disloyally; the second group comes (056, correction of 2026).

The Francoist vertical syndicate is built exactly on the first flow. Membership is compulsory, elections of shop-floor representatives — **enlaces sindicales** — are held at the regime's own demand, participation by the dependent is an official duty, and the subject of the elections is non-political by construction: it concerns the shop, the shift and the rates.

And this is what happened. The Workers' Commissions used the elections of 1960, 1963 and 1966 as a channel of entry. In autumn 1966, in the first stage, **5,679 shop-floor representatives were elected, 70 % of them new** [verified]; the regime had tolerated the commissions on the expectation that the vertical syndicate would absorb them — and the elections showed that containing discontent within the Francoist construction had failed. In 1967 the commissions were declared illegal [second-hand] — that is, the regime had to ban the result of its own compulsory procedure.

**This is not an analogy by mood but a recorded case of failure mode no. 1 firing.** The compelled came and voted disloyally, and did so inside a structure created to prevent exactly that.

The construction coincides with the protocol's on three counts at once:

| Feature | Spain 1966 | The protocol |
|---|---|---|
| participation is compulsory or paid for by the regime itself | attending union elections is an official duty | the state pays the dividend |
| the subject is legally sterile | rates and working conditions, not politics | "how much of the share is owed to me" |
| the superior has nothing to dissuade with | attendance at these elections is his own requirement | the foreman test ([23b.2](../05-empirical-base/023b-evidence-rules.en.md)) |

One difference is substantial and must be named: in Spain the capture of the channel gave the opposition an **organisation** — the commissions became a trade union that survived into democracy. The protocol neither creates nor presupposes an organisation; it supplies a number. That is in its favour as regards vulnerability (there is nothing to ban) and against it as regards the fact that, after a measurement, somebody has to act.

## 57e.4. The four missing conditions — and what follows

Row 9 answers the question left open in [056f.12](056f-russia-september-2026.en.md): why an elite split fails on coordination rather than on dissatisfaction.

From 1969 Spain had a **point of assembly** — a designated successor around whom reformists inside the regime could gather without becoming traitors. That is the missing coordinating instrument: it permits the future to be discussed in the present tense and makes preparing for the transition official conduct rather than conspiracy. What Russia lacks is not the desire but that instrument.

The other three conditions read just as practically:

- **Economic growth** gave the transition a social base and, more importantly for the protocol, accustomed the population to its position improving measurably. Without that, a demand for a share sounds like a demand for rations rather than a demand for an accounting.
- **External entanglement** gave the regime an interest in appearances and gave the opposition an outside platform not declared hostile.
- **The Church's defection** gave a physical place where people could gather legally. Present-day Russia has no such place at all, and this is the heaviest of the four differences.

**For the protocol this means something specific.** The Russian path runs through a transition (056d.8), and the four rows above are a list of what to expect in the transition window. The protocol creates none of the four conditions. But it **is a candidate for the role of a fifth** — the sterile compulsory channel of 57e.3, which in Spain turned out to be not a condition of the transition but an instrument working before it and independently of it.

## 57e.5. How far the analogue carries

By rule 23b.1 — with a boundary, not with praise.

| Claim | What Spain confirms | Where the analogue ends |
|---|---|---|
| A sterile compulsory channel is captured from inside | the 1966 elections, 70 % new representatives | the capture occurred amid a growing economy, a defected Church and an external partner pressing the regime |
| A regime fails to foresee the capture of its own procedure | the commissions were tolerated on the expectation of absorbing them | a single case; no special search was made for other attempts that ended otherwise |
| A demobilising regime survives to a transition | 1975–78 | the transition is explained by four conditions Russia lacks; it does not follow from Spain that a transition will occur |
| A designated successor gives the elites coordination | 1969 → the pacts of transition | Juan Carlos was a monarch, that is, a bearer of legitimacy external to the regime; Russia has no such source |
| Emigration works as a valve | 1.3 million, remittances | Spanish émigrés were not declared enemies and they returned |

What Spain does **not** supply at all: a case of a payment tied to voting, and a case of an elite split caused by a demand for a share of the rent. These are the same two things left without an analogue in 056f.14, and the Spanish material does not close them.

## 57e.6. Weak point

The Spanish material was checked against open sources and secondary summaries; the assistant did not open primary documents — the records of the 1966 union elections or the series of Spain's National Statistics Institute. The figure of 5,679 and the share of 70 % come from union historiography, that is, from a source with an interest in the story.

The Russian column of the table rests on the repository and on the September analysis 056f, where every electoral figure comes from a single analysis and is unverified against primary data. There are no Russian survey data here, by the rule of [23b.3](../05-empirical-base/023b-evidence-rules.en.md), which means that parameters 1 and 2 are judgements about the regime rather than measurements.

The principal limitation, however, lies in the genre itself. The ten parameters were chosen by the assistant for Linz and for the transition; another set would give another score, and "four against four" is not a measurement of resemblance but the result of one particular choice of rubrics. The conclusion "fit for description, unfit for forecasting" does not depend on the selection and would survive any reasonable set; the arithmetic of 4/2/4 does.

And one thing to hold against our own conclusion: Spain made its transition **after the ruler's death**, so the four conditions explain what the transition was like, not that it began. The absence of the four conditions in Russia speaks to the character of a possible transition, not to its impossibility. 🟡
