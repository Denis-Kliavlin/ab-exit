### 21.1. The Problem Marketing Solved 50 Years Ago While Political Science Ignores It

Marketing has thoroughly documented the Say-Do Gap phenomenon (also known as the Intention-Action Gap) — the gap between what people SAY they will do and what they ACTUALLY do. Fifty years of research show a stable gap:

| Domain | Say "yes" | Actually do | Gap |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Eco-products (HBR) | 65% | 26% | 60% lie |
| Organic food | 70% | 16% | 77% lie |
| Online shopping (Statista 2023) | — | — | 38% don't do what they said |
| New products (ACNielsen BASES) | "Will definitely buy" | 10-75% buy | Up to 90% lie |
| Fitness | 80% | ~20% | 75% lie |
| **Voting** | **75%** | **40%** | **47% lie** |

The cause is the same in every case: Social Desirability Bias. Saying "yes, I'll go vote / buy organic / work out" = free. It's embarrassing to say "no." Actually DOING it = costs time, money, effort. The gap between the free "yes" and the paid action = 40-77%.

### 21.2. Stated vs Revealed Preference

Economists distinguish two types of preferences:

**Stated Preference:** what a person SAYS they want. Free. Unreliable. "Would you buy an eco-product?" — "Yes!" (cost of the answer: $0).

**Revealed Preference:** what a person ACTUALLY DOES. Paid. Reliable. "Did they buy the eco-product?" — check the receipt (cost of the action: $5-50).

Marketing moved from stated to revealed 50 years ago: A/B testing, conversion, retention, purchase analysis. A marketer doesn't ask "will you buy?" — a marketer looks at what people BUY.

Political science still lives in the world of stated preference: "Will you go vote?" — "Yes!" — and models are built on that "yes," which in 47% of cases = a lie.

### 21.3. Voting = a Free "Yes"

Current democracy is a stated-preference system:

- "Will you go vote?" — "Yes!" (cost of the answer: $0)
- "Will you go vote?" — showed up at the polling place (cost of the action: 1-3 hours, the trip, the line)
- The gap: 47% said "yes" but didn't show up

It costs nothing to say "I'll go" → it costs nothing not to show up. No price for lying. No price for truth. Therefore, words = noise.

What "beats" a promise to vote: rain in Milwaukee (-22,000 votes), a season finale on Netflix, beer in the fridge, traffic on the road, a headache. A bottle of beer costs $5 and cancels a vote that costs $0. Because $5 > $0. Always.

### 22.12. Three Honest Limits — and What the Protocol Does NOT Propose

This is a game-theoretic reconstruction, not an empirical forecast; contexts differ, and the argument has limits (estimate-hygiene caveats):

— **The caste exception.** In some regimes the coercive core (a praetorian guard, elite units on special compensation) is genuinely isolated from the population and materially bound to the ruler. There the "army = the people" argument weakens; the calculation applies to a mass conscript/contract army, not to a mercenary core.
— **Order ≠ wisdom.** The army's neutrality or "support for order" guarantees only that the referendum takes place, not the quality of subsequent governance. It is an entry condition, not an outcome.
— **The risk of militarization.** An army that becomes the "guarantor of the transition" itself risks turning into a junta. AB-EXIT does not solve the problem of civilian control over the military — it merely shows why the army is a weak *instrument of repression*.

And a direct disclaimer about what the protocol does NOT contain. AB-EXIT does not propose "buying off the security services," building a "dictatorship of pragmatists," or using the army as a battering ram to seize power. Any scenario involving coercive force, "surgical toughness," or an authoritarian transition (the Pinochet/Lee Kuan Yew model) lies outside the protocol and contradicts its essence: AB-EXIT is legitimate precisely because it is voluntary, reversible, and deprives no one of their rights (§11, §42). The argument about the army is not about how to seize power by force, but about why it is impossible to hold the old system in place by force against the will of an armed people.
