## 1. Why this section exists

The repository compares the protocol with thinkers one by one and with the reforms of the last twenty years. A comparison with **works of the same genre** — where one norm or one mechanism rebuilds a system through the interests of its participants — was missing. The section gives two tables and derives a roadmap from them: what the predecessors have and we do not.

## 2. Before 1926: designs of a whole state

| Work | What it describes | What is shared | What they have and we do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***The Federalist*** (1787–88) | 85 papers defending a specific constitution, each objection taken in turn | The genre coincides almost exactly: a design, an arsenal of answers to critics, a reliance on self-interest ("ambition must be made to counteract ambition") | A full text that can be adopted tomorrow |
| **Harrington, *Oceana*** (1656) | A republic as a mechanism: rotation, the secret ballot, "one divides, the other chooses" | The state as a construction that works with any people | The arrangement of property |
| **Condorcet, constitutional draft** (1793) | A mathematician's constitution with a theory of voting inside | Elections as a measuring instrument (013d) | Procedure at every level |
| **Mill, *Considerations on Representative Government*** (1861) | Plural voting, proportionality | Analysed in 006 §15.12 | A complete theory of representation |
| **Henry George, *Progress and Poverty*** (1879) | One reform — the single tax on land — rebuilds the rest | The same type: one lever; the book sold in millions (033) | A short book for the general reader |

From the same list of 20.09 but already within the last hundred years: Hayek, *Law, Legislation and Liberty*, vol. 3 (1979) — a model constitution by an economist; Posner and Weyl, *Radical Markets* (2018) — 036 §85; Hanson, futarchy — 040b; Van Parijs, basic income — 014.

## 3. The last hundred years: one norm that rebuilds a system

| Work | One lever | What is shared with the protocol | What they have and we do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coase, "The Problem of Social Cost"** (1960) | Assign the right — the parties will bargain their way to the efficient outcome | The protocol assigns the right of non-turnout and gives it a price | A theorem with conditions of applicability. Ours are not written out |
| **Vickrey (1961) → Hurwicz, Maskin, Myerson (2007)** | A mechanism in which truth pays better than a lie | The stake is revealed by an act, not by a poll (013) | A proven theorem. We had a statement in words — closed in 013f |
| **Buchanan and Tullock, *The Calculus of Consent*** (1962) | Rules are chosen in advance; the constitutional level is separated from the political | Two levels of decision (048g §6b) | A formal separation of the levels as an article |
| **Olson, *The Logic of Collective Action*** (1965) | A large group does not act without a selective incentive | The dividend is a selective incentive for the largest group (015c §7b) | A quantitative model of group size |
| **Dales, *Pollution, Property and Prices*** (1968) → emissions trading | Fix the quantity and the price floats; or fix the price and the quantity floats | The protocol fixes the price (D) and lets the quantity (the exit share) go | **The second instrument:** fix a target exit share and find the sum by clearing |
| **Hammond, the Alaska dividend** (1976–82) | One payment to everyone from a fund by a formula | 014: one-hundred-per-cent take-up, forty years of data; 14.3b — the 2016 veto and its price | A fund as separate property outside the budget, with constitutional status |
| **Kydland and Prescott, "Rules Rather than Discretion"** (1977) | A rule that ties the hands is worth more than clever discretion | The formula in the law, not a promise (045.7, 036c.3) | A model computing the gain from commitment |
| **Ostrom, *Governing the Commons*** (1990) | Eight principles under which the users themselves guard the resource | Parallel counting by supporters (048i) and cheap courts (048j) | Graduated sanctions and nested levels (municipality → region → country) |

| **Nakamoto, "Bitcoin"** (2008) | Honesty pays better than attack; a double spend is visible without a trusted party | A ballot in the name of someone who took the sum is a double spend (048f, 001c §3) | **Nine pages.** A protocol that changed an industry fitted into one document with one formula |

A second cut of 30.09.2026 — not by level of conception but by content: where participation is paid for or fined, the composition of voters or the weight of the vote is changed. Three were already in the repository in fragments and are gathered into chapters; seven were absent and were analysed one by one.

| Project or idea | What they had and we did not | Where closed |
|---|---|---|

## 4. How the protocol differs from all of them

Almost every work describes what the arrangement should be and leaves open who will make it so and why. The protocol analyses the path itself: who raises the subject and what the first mover gets; why silence is impossible; what rollback looks like and who stands guard (048g); what each opponent does and why he loses (001c). *The Federalist* comes closest, but it defended a text already written. The second feature is one lever for everything: from a single payment follow the composition of the voters, the count, the rolls, the levies and the rollback.

## 6. Three constructive additions from the table in §3

1. **The fork "price or quantity" (Dales).** Today the protocol fixes the sum and observes the exit share. The reverse variant is possible: write down a target exit share and announce the sum by clearing. This is a direct answer to the position "the dividend is whatever gives the result": not to tune K but to set the result and let the sum converge. A section computing both schemes is needed.
2. **The default (Thaler).** One article of the charter: the status of the person who chose nothing. The assistant's recommendation is A by default: the right is kept, the money is not received, the record stays "empty" and falls into the zone of control (001c §3).
3. **The fund as property (Alaska).** A separate fund outside the budget — against the argument "there is no money this year"; the conclusion from the 2016 veto (014 §14.3b), which the repository does not yet draw.

**The architect's decisions of 30.09.2026 on these three.** The fork "price or quantity" and "fund or budget" are methods, and the protocol gives the right to a payment, not the methods: both questions go to experts. The default is decided: the person who chose nothing keeps his vote, he is the controller, and his money waits for him until the next election (048k §3).

And two reinforcements: build the formal model as Vickrey and Myerson did, with conditions of applicability after Coase (013f); fit the single charter into nine pages, as Nakamoto did.

## 7. Weak point

The selection of the ten works is the assistant's: Rawls (the veil of ignorance, 1971) and Hirschman (1970) are not in it, the first as a non-mechanism, the second as the centre of 036c; a dispute over the list is legitimate. The roadmap shows the state on one date and goes stale with every item closed; it must be checked against 048k and the changelog, not the other way round. 🟡
