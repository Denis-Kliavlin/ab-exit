### 92.1. Purpose of the section

Over the course of the project several reconstructions of major thinkers' positions on AB-EXIT have been carried out: Anne Applebaum, Sergei Guriev, Ray Dalio, Grigory Yudin, Rudolf von Jhering. Each reconstruction revealed one and the same pattern, which deserves explicit recording as a structural property of the project.

**The pattern:** objections to AB-EXIT by scholars, derived from their public positions, are systematically refuted by those scholars' own methodology when the AB-EXIT document is analysed. Their criticism structurally turns into an argument FOR AB-EXIT.

This is not chance. It is a structural consequence of AB-EXIT being a paradigm shift (section 82) rather than a reform within the existing paradigm.

### 92.2. Empirical confirmation of the pattern

**Applebaum.** Described the problem: democracies degrade from within through the normalisation of small violations and kleptocracy. The objection one could expect — "AB-EXIT destroys the ritual significance of voting". Refuted by her own work — she herself documented that the ritual significance has already been destroyed by populists. AB-EXIT does not destroy; it addresses what has been destroyed.

**Dalio.** Described the problem: fiscal crises require political coordination that does not exist. The objection one could expect — "AB-EXIT is slow reform, fast hard measures are needed". Refuted by his own empirics — the analysis of 48 crises shows that the same crises produce different results depending on political quality. AB-EXIT improves political quality, which is a prerequisite for his own fiscal solutions.

**Guriev and Yudin.** Described the problem: populism and spin dictators manipulate apathetic voters. The objection one could expect — "AB-EXIT can be used by authoritarian regimes against citizens". Refuted by their own analysis — apathetic voters are already the instrument of authoritarian manipulation. AB-EXIT removes them from the field, depriving spin dictators of their main instrument.

**Jhering.** Described the problem: law must serve purposes, but formalism gets in the way. The objection one could expect — "AB-EXIT turns law into a commodity". Refuted by his own thesis "purpose is the creator of law". If the purpose of improving democracy is achieved, the form of exchange is legitimate by his own methodology.

In each case the scholar's diagnosis becomes a justification for AB-EXIT rather than a criticism of it.

### 92.3. The mechanism of the pattern

This pattern has a structural explanation. Existing theories are formulated for **analysing existing reality** — describing, explaining, criticising what is. Each theory works within its own paradigm.

AB-EXIT creates **a new reality** that did not exist when these theories were formulated. This means the following:

— Scholars' objections are built on the problems of the old reality. — AB-EXIT solves those problems through structural rebuilding. — Therefore criticism based on old problems automatically becomes an argument FOR AB-EXIT.

Each scholar described his part of "the disease of democracy". AB-EXIT is the "treatment" of that disease. Criticising the treatment from the position of describing the disease is structurally impossible — it only shows that AB-EXIT addresses precisely the problem that scholar considered important.

### 92.4. An analogy from history — other paradigm shifts

A similar pattern is observed in the history of other paradigm shifts:

— **The introduction of Social Security, 1935.** Critics predicted the destruction of "the American character through dependence on the state". Their criticism was built on the problem of poverty in old age, which was real. Social Security solved that problem, and the criticism became retrospectively irrelevant.

— **The NHS in Britain, 1948.** The British Medical Association predicted "the destruction of the doctor's independence". Its criticism was built on the problem of unequal access to medicine, which was real. The NHS solved that problem.

— **The abolition of slavery.** Defenders of slavery predicted "the economic collapse of the South". Their criticism was built on economic dependence on slave labour, which was real. Abolition transformed that dependence.

This is the general pattern of major institutional innovations. Critics who rely on the problems of the old reality are refuted by the very innovation that solves those problems.

### 92.5. Where the pattern does NOT work

It is important to record the boundary of applicability. The pattern works for critics with **concrete analytical work on democratic problems** — for those who empirically described the diseases of modern politics (Applebaum, Dalio, Guriev, Yudin, Jhering).

The pattern does NOT work for critics with **principled metaphysical objections** to the very idea of a market mechanism in politics. Such critics can say: "I do not object from the position of diagnosing problems. I object from the position of principle — politics and the market are fundamentally incompatible, full stop."

That is a different class of criticism. It is not built on the analysis of existing reality, so methodological refutation through diagnosis does not work. A metaphysical objection requires a metaphysical answer — and that is a different class of discussion.

Empirically, among the serious critics with concrete analytical work whom we have studied, the pattern holds completely. We have not encountered metaphysical critics — possibly because serious intellectuals rarely work in a purely metaphysical mode, preferring empirical analysis.

### 92.6. What this means for the project's strategy

This structural property has several practical consequences.

**First — a high probability of intellectual support from serious critics.** Scholars who seriously analyse democratic problems discover, on detailed study of AB-EXIT, that the concept addresses exactly the problems they described. This creates a basis for transforming potential critics into allies.

**Second — the focus of communication should be on their own work.** When addressing Applebaum — cite her work on the degradation of democracy. When addressing Dalio — cite his work on the Big Cycle. When addressing Jhering through his heirs — cite "purpose is the creator of law". This is not a rhetorical trick; it is a structural demonstration that AB-EXIT solves the problems of their own analysis.

**Third — the main threat comes not from serious analysts but from metaphysical opponents and partisan groupings** (section 88 — structural analysis of opponents). This changes the priorities of defence.

**Fourth — every public criticism by a serious scholar works for the project.** If a respected analyst publicly criticises AB-EXIT from the position of his own work, we can show that AB-EXIT solves that analyst's problem. This creates a public situation in which the criticism reveals itself as support.

### 92.8. Summary of the structural observation

AB-EXIT has a rare property: serious criticism from the positions of existing analytical theories about democratic problems is structurally refuted by the critic's own methodology. This is a consequence of the concept's paradigmatic character.

This does not mean that AB-EXIT is invulnerable to criticism in principle. Metaphysical objections, political attacks from structural opponents (section 88), technical problems of implementation, unexpected empirical data — all of these remain possible. But classical academic criticism based on diagnosing the existing problems of democracy works, in the case of AB-EXIT, as justification rather than refutation.

This is a structural advantage that must be understood and used with care. Exaggeration leads to overconfidence and the loss of the ability to hear criticism. The right use is a calm knowledge that serious dialogue with serious critics carries no existential threat to the project, since the structure of their methodology works for the project rather than against it.
