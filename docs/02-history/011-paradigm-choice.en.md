## 11.1. Modern politics as a machine for producing grievance with no outlet

Before speaking of what AB-EXIT does, one has to describe exactly what **current politics does to a person**. Not ideologically, but psychologically.

A citizen pays taxes. He sees how they are spent (or not spent). He observes corruption, unkept promises, politicians enriching themselves at his expense. Gradually he accumulates a **justified grievance**. Not invented, not manipulative — a real, factually confirmed grievance against a system that treats him unjustly.

This grievance is not like anger at a specific person. It is **diffuse pressure**, directed against the system as a whole. "All of them" are to blame, "this whole system", "the politicians", "the elites". The pressure has no specific addressee, so it is hard to resolve by address.

And this grievance **finds no legitimate channel** for its expression. The modern democratic system has no institutional instrument by which a citizen could publicly, within respected procedures, register the position "I disagree with how this system treats me, and I refuse to take part in its rituals". Any such attempt is interpreted by the system as **dysfunction**: apathy, irresponsibility, erosion of civic culture.

This creates a **psychological trap**. The citizen can neither participate normally (because he does not believe in the result) nor abstain normally (because society stigmatises non-participation). He is stuck between two bad options, and every electoral cycle **deepens the stuckness**.

## 11.2. The citizen's three options — all three leave the steam inside the system

**The first option — keep voting "for one's own" candidate.** The citizen votes for the party or candidate he finds most sympathetic. That candidate wins or loses, but in either case **the basic structure of the system does not change**. The same institutions, the same distributions of power and money, the same rhythms of corruption. The citizen lets off some steam through the act of voting (a brief relief — "I did what I could"), but then sees the result and **the grievance returns**, often intensified by disappointment.

This cycle repeats every four years. The grievance accumulates **layer upon layer**.

**The second option — stop voting, silently.** The citizen decides he no longer wants to take part in the spectacle. He stays at home. But he receives a **double negative effect**.

The first — social stigma. Those around him, the media, academics say: "you are apathetic", "you are irresponsible", "if you don't vote you have no right to complain". The citizen feels he must justify himself.

The second — a sense of his own powerlessness. The citizen knows his silent non-participation is heard by nobody. He simply stays silent, and his silence communicates nothing to the system.

This is the worst option. The grievance is **multiplied by shame** at non-participation.

**The third option — vote for a populist.** The citizen votes for a candidate promising to "tear it all down". It is important to understand: the citizen votes for the populist **not because he believes the promises**. He often understands perfectly well that the populist will be unable to deliver anything. He votes for the populist because it is **the only way to let off steam officially, within democratic procedure**. It is an act of public protest dressed as a vote.

The effect is short-lived. The populist gains power or does not. If he does, he either delivers nothing (the grievance returns with doubled force) or begins genuinely destroying institutions (the country suffers real losses). If he does not, the citizen feels the elites have cheated the system again.

All three options **leave the steam inside the system**. It accumulates from cycle to cycle, from generation to generation.

## 11.3. The geological pressure of accumulated grievances — an explanation of the rise of populism

Look at the political history of the Western world from 2010 to 2025 through this lens and the picture becomes clear.

Brexit 2016, Trump 2016 and 2024, Orbán from 2010 on, the Freedom Party in Austria, the AfD in Germany, Le Pen in France, Brothers of Italy 2022, Milei in Argentina, the rise of Reform UK in 2025 — these are not random political events. They are **the geological pressure of accumulated grievances** that finally found a seismic outlet.

Citizens voted not for the populists' specific programmes (which were often contradictory or undeliverable). They voted for **the chance to let off steam through the only available channel**. The Brexit voter had no detailed plan for trade relations after leaving. He voted for **an act of defiance** against the global system.

The Trump voter in 2016 and in 2024 voted not for specific tariffs or immigration policy. He voted for **an act of public humiliation** of the class of elites who had told him for years that his problems were his own fault.

This explains why all rational campaigns against populists fail. When a centrist campaign says "the populist's programme is undeliverable, here are our facts" — it does not work, because **the vote for the populist was never about facts**. It is about the release of accumulated pressure.

And this regularity will **intensify** in the coming cycles if the structure of the pressure does not change. Citizens will find more radical populists. The cycle will accelerate, the amplitude of the swings will grow, until a major destruction of institutions occurs.

## 11.4. AB-EXIT as the first legitimate channel of state recognition

AB-EXIT gives, for the first time in history, **a legitimate, state-recognised, public channel to let off steam without destruction**.

The citizen chooses option B. He receives money from a state that recognises his right not to participate in this cycle. This is not merely a financial transaction — it is **an act of recognition**.

With its money the state says: "I hear you. I understand you have reasons. I respect your position. I do not stigmatise you as apathetic. I do not try to drag you back into a spectacle you do not want to enter. I recognise your choice as that of an adult who has the right not to participate. Here is compensation for your honesty."

This is an entirely different mode of communication between state and citizen. Before AB-EXIT the state **demanded** or **stigmatised**. With AB-EXIT the state **recognises and compensates**.

Money in this structure is not the main thing. Money is **the form of recognition**. The state could recognise a citizen with a certificate or a medal — but those forms would be perceived as empty rhetoric. Money works because it is **concrete, material, verifiable**. The state **does not merely say** it respects the citizen — it **confirms it by an action that has a price**.

## 11.5. Psychological resolution through a symbolic act

The effect of this act is **the psychological resolution of accumulated pressure**. Not in an instant, not equally for everyone, but as a **mass phenomenon** it shows over several electoral cycles.

A citizen who has for years accumulated grievance against the system receives a symbolic act of recognition. The grievance does not disappear — its causes remain. But **it stops accumulating**. A feeling of "I have been noticed" appears.

This resembles what happens in psychotherapy. The therapist does not solve the client's problems — he **recognises** their seriousness. That recognition in itself has a therapeutic effect.

AB-EXIT works on the same principle at the mass level. The state recognises the validity of the citizen's position. This does not solve the system's problems — but it **discharges the accumulated pressure**, moving it from a mode of multiplication into a mode of processing.

This effect is **not visible in first-cycle statistics**. After two or three cycles secondary effects will begin to show — lower polarisation, lower support for extreme candidates, greater readiness for compromise.

## 11.6. The paradoxical effect — a return to voting from a new position

Many of the citizens who **received compensation and felt they had been treated justly** may in the next cycle want to **return to voting**. Not from a sense of duty but from a new position.

The citizen took the compensation from a position of **grievance against the system**. After a cycle or two, after several acts of recognition, he **changes his attitude** to the system. He stops feeling it as a hostile force. He begins to feel it as a structure that at least tries to reckon with him.

This is the opposite of what the critics predict. They say: "AB-EXIT will lead to 50 % of people leaving politics forever." In practice the reverse may happen: AB-EXIT leads to 50 % of people **leaving temporarily**, calming down, resolving their accumulated grievances, and some of them **returning to politics healthier and more rational**.

## 11.7. Virality through the personal experience "I was treated justly"

When the first state introduces AB-EXIT and the first citizens receive their dividends, a **seismic shift** will occur in mass consciousness. Not because of the money. Money is superficial. But because people will feel for the first time that **they have been treated justly**.

And that emotional experience **will be told**. Not as "I got $676" but as "you know, for the first time they recognised me". On social media, in conversations with friends, at family dinners. "You know, I took the compensation. And you know what — for the first time in twenty years I don't feel like a fool for not voting."

This message spreads not through political technology but through **direct human experience**. Everyone who received compensation becomes a carrier of the message. Not a propagandist — a carrier of a personal experience of justice. That is virality of another order.

Neighbouring states will see this and understand — something works there that we do not have. Citizens will begin demanding a referendum. Not because of the money. Because they too want to feel that **they have been treated justly**.

## 11.8. The centrist's emotional speech — competing with the populist's

Centrists in Western politics have been losing to populists almost everywhere for twenty years. The reason is not the weakness of their programmes — centrist programmes are often better, better costed. The reason is that the centrist **cannot offer the voter the emotional liberation** the populist offers.

The populist says: "I will free you from the elites." That is emotional speech, addressed to accumulated grievance.

The centrist cannot currently compete at that emotional level. He says: "I will improve the system through rational reforms." That is functional speech, addressed to reason. It loses to populism because **it does not resolve the accumulated steam**.

With AB-EXIT the centrist for the first time acquires **an emotional speech** that competes with the populist's:

> "I will free you from the obligation to take part in a spectacle you did not choose. I will give you state compensation for honestly acknowledging your position — instead of forcing you to pretend you agree with the system. I will respect your choice, whatever it is. And when you want to return to active voting — your vote will weigh more than it does now, because some citizens have left. You owe nobody anything any more. Decide for yourself."

This is **the first centrist speech in twenty years** capable of producing in the listener the same emotional movement that today only the populist's speech produces.

## 11.9. Three reasons why the centrist's speech with AB-EXIT is stronger than the populist's

**First — it is deliverable.** The populist promises the destruction of the elites, a return to a utopian past, an instant solution to complex problems. Those promises are **systemically undeliverable**. The centrist with AB-EXIT promises specific things — compensation, a strengthened vote, a cyclical choice. Those things are **technically deliverable** in one state through a ballot initiative. When a promise is kept, trust in the centrist grows.

**Second — it is positive.** The populist offers resolution through anger and destruction. To remain a populist's supporter, the citizen must **sustain a state of anger in himself**. That is emotionally exhausting. After a few years even the most devoted supporters burn out.

The centrist with AB-EXIT offers resolution through **recognition and material return**. The citizen need hate nothing to receive compensation. He simply speaks honestly of his position — and the state respects him. That is a **psychologically more stable** position.

**Third — it is respectful.** The populist says "you are victims, your enemies are the elites". That **patronises** — it addresses the citizen as an aggrieved child. The citizen accepts the role temporarily. But over the long run the permanent role of victim is **destructive** to self-respect.

The centrist with AB-EXIT says "you are adults, choose for yourselves, we respect both options". That is **an appeal to dignity**. The citizen receives a positive image of himself — not a victim but a subject of choice. Over the long run **dignity is stronger than grievance**. A citizen who feels his dignity does not drift into radicalisation.

This third reason is the deepest. Populism feeds on the humiliation of citizens by elites, accumulating it into collective anger. AB-EXIT, through a state act of recognition, **breaks the cycle of humiliation**, depriving populism of its basic fuel. Not by fighting populists, but by **healing the condition on which they are parasitic**.

## 11.10. Reconsidering what AB-EXIT is

This section redefines the project.

Before this section we described AB-EXIT as **structural mechanism design for the electoral system**. That is academically exact but emotionally dead. That positioning works for rational elites and a tech audience accustomed to thinking quantitatively. But it **does not explain why AB-EXIT can become a mass phenomenon**.

After this section we say: **AB-EXIT is the first mechanism in history for the state's recognition of a citizen's grievance, with a concrete act of justice in exchange for his honest acknowledgement of his position**.

That is an entirely different project. And that different project has enormous mobilising potential.

The electoral effects — lower populism, a longer planning horizon, the filtering of the apathetic, protection of institutions from capture — are **consequences**. **The root** is the emotional resolution of accumulated pressure through a symbolic act of state respect for the citizen.

This reformulation must be recorded at several levels.

**At the level of communication.** The basic messages must be reformulated. Not "vote or take the money" — but "the state recognises your choice for the first time". Not "AB-EXIT is a reform of the electoral system" — but "AB-EXIT is the first political instrument that treats you with respect". Not "we are fighting populism" — but "we are resolving the accumulated pressure on which populism is parasitic".

**At the level of the headline message.** Options in the new paradigm:
- "AB-EXIT. Your choice. Your value. Your dignity." — about the dignity of choice.
- "AB-EXIT. Vote with weight. Or live free. Your call." — about freedom.
- "AB-EXIT. Politics that respects you." — short, emotional.

**At the level of the target audience.** Parallel campaigns for rational elites (the quantitative language of section 23: a 20–40 % effect against 2–8 % for existing mechanisms) and for the mass audience (the emotional language of this section 11: the state treats you with respect for the first time; reclaim your dignity).

Without the emotional mobilisation of millions the referendum does not pass, and all the technical mechanism design stays in the offices of academics and venture capitalists.

## The final thesis

AB-EXIT is not an electoral reform in the traditional sense. It is **the first institutional gesture from the state to the citizen framed as respect rather than as demand**.

If AB-EXIT is understood this way, it becomes clear why it is capable of resolving the crisis of modern democracy. Not through another mechanism for counting votes or filtering the electorate. But through **a change in the basic emotional structure of the relationship** between the citizen and the political system.

A citizen whom the state **respects** for the first time in his life relates to that state differently. He does not vote for demagogues, because he has no accumulated grievance against the system. He does not drift into radicalism, because his dignity is already recognised. He is capable of compromise, because he does not feel himself in the position of a victim. He makes long-term plans, because he believes the system reckons with him.

That is a healthy democracy. And AB-EXIT is a way to get it. Not by fighting symptoms, but by **healing the basic relationship** between state and citizen.
