**Arrow's impossibility theorem (Kenneth Arrow, Nobel Prize 1972).** Arrow proved that no voting system exists that simultaneously satisfies all fairness criteria: universality, unanimity, independence of irrelevant alternatives, non-dictatorship. Any system violates at least one criterion. In 73 years no one has refuted it.

The garbage in — garbage out principle: Arrow proved the function is imperfect; AB-EXIT removes the garbage at the input.

**Condorcet's jury theorem (the Marquis de Condorcet, 1785).** If each member of a jury has a probability p > 50% of making the correct decision, then the larger the jury — the higher the probability of a correct collective decision (with an infinite jury → 100%). BUT the flip side: if p < 50%, then the larger the jury — the LOWER the probability (with an infinite jury → 0%).

Paying for the renunciation of the vote is the triumph of the "marketing character": the citizen is informed that his political self has a price tag. "You are not healing the alienation; you are perfecting it."
— **The institutionalized escape from freedom.** Fromm's life's work was helping people overcome the escape; AB-EXIT pays for the escape, makes it comfortable and final. The answer to apathy, per Fromm, is to build efficacy, community, meaning — not to remunerate capitulation; and he wanted more participation (humanistic participatory socialism), not a thinning of the electorate.

**How AB-EXIT answers.** The escape has already happened (§8) — it is the status quo, not the creation of AB-EXIT. A conscious, reversible choice (§30.4, return every cycle) is more honest than an unconscious, denied flight. The vote is already a commodity — the system extracts thousands of dollars from it through parties and media (§30.10); AB-EXIT pays the citizen himself, not the machine, and does not sell the right (it comes back) but leases the unused seat for a cycle (this is not vote-buying: the unused seat is leased for a cycle, the right is not sold). And it amplifies the one who realizes freedom-to (the motivated vote ×2–3, §17.3).

**The honest remainder that cannot be closed.** Fromm's blow is cultural, not procedural, and reversibility does not remove it: even a one-off transaction teaches that the vote has a monetary price, normalizes "everything, including my civic self, is for sale." AB-EXIT may improve the mechanism while imperceptibly eroding the culture of citizenship — a direct analogue of Fraenkel's blow (§15.24, "you are building a syringe"), but at the level of character. And the humanistic "a person must be grown, not sorted" AB-EXIT softens (a filter by motivation, reversibility, self-selection) but does not fully close.

**The bottom line on Fromm.** AB-EXIT is an honest and humane triage of a pathology Fromm would have preferred to treat: better than the lying status quo, but not the humanistic ideal, and carrying a real cultural risk (the normalization of the vote-as-commodity). His condition, like Fraenkel's: combine AB-EXIT with the institutions of positive freedom (community, efficacy, meaning), so that it is triage plus treatment, not triage as capitulation.
