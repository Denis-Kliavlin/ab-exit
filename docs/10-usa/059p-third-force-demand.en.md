## 1. The architect's thesis

"About Trump my main thought is: this pendulum — first the victory of MAGA and Trump, then how Trump behaved and how the voter turned away from him. It turns out there is no reasonable third option in the US, and the pendulum swings very hard, and everyone is its hostage. The centrist voter cannot choose anyone except the two who swing the pendulum with hatred of the opponent. And politicians inside the parties also want to go to the centre, but there is no centre, they have to go only into two colours, and inside each party there are mini-parties, so to speak. AB-EXIT removes the apathetic, and this pendulum, today so bright, is no longer so needed and no longer drags the country between the poles."

## 2. The fact: demand for a third force and its traces in 2026

- **The demand is measured.** 022 §67.1: 62 % of Americans want a third party (Gallup 2025), 45 % call themselves independents, and "very likely to vote for a third candidate" — 15 %. The gap between "want" and "will vote" is not laziness but learned arithmetic: with a full hall a vote for the third goes to whomever you hate more (059k, Duverger's law).
- **Both parties split into mini-parties.** Sharikov: among Republicans — moderates against MAGA (in the week before the election Trump wrote over a hundred posts, "here is a true American hero," for his candidates); among Democrats — moderates against Sanders's progressives, strengthened in the north-east (Mamdani in New York, El-Sayed in Michigan, Jackson in Maine). Obama campaigned for El-Sayed in person to pull him away from Sanders. Sharikov's forecast: in 2028 a moderate wins, not a leftist.
- **The demand for moderates is served without an instrument.** Rahm Emanuel as the Democrats' chief strategist: in 2027 do not focus on Trump, first an ethics law, then affordable health care, a conciliatory tone. That is a demand for a long horizon, formulated without the means to satisfy it.
- **Young against old.** Talarico 🟡 37 against Paxton in Texas; Turek, a Paralympian, against Hinson in Iowa; El-Sayed 🟡 42 in Michigan; Mamdani 🟡 35 in New York a year earlier. Collins, in the Senate since 1997, and Graham, since 2003, lost. The background: Biden removed from the race over age; Trump is 80; McConnell (84) and Pelosi (86) left on their own; Feinstein died in office at 90; a quarter of the lower house are new faces (Sharikov).

## 3. The first layer: the pendulum's fuel

The pendulum is not a side effect but the method of both parties (059n §6). Mobilisation by hatred of the opponent is cheaper than a programme and works on the apathetic, the brought-in and the layer: the ad "they will destroy the country" brings the one to whom it does not matter. In 2024 Trump gathered the hall with hatred of Biden and "the leftists"; in 2026 the Democrats gathered the hall with hatred of Trump and MAGA. Sharikov recorded both sides: Trump made the election a plebiscite about himself ("vote as if I were on the ballot"), the Democrats a plebiscite about him too ("look how bad he is"), and in the debates Republicans were branded for belonging to the "MAGA community," not for a programme.

**What the protocol does — directly.** Hatred brings votes only from a hall where there is someone to bring. The apathetic and the brought-in take B, the layer shrinks, and the ad brings nobody any more. A party that keeps swinging wastes its money (030b: busing −90–95 %, turnout suppression reverses). This follows from the hall's composition and needs no third candidate: **the pendulum stalls even with two colours**, because the fuel runs out, not because a third axis appeared.

## 3b. A coalition without a contract: why a sum of "againsts" cannot govern

*Added 5 November 2026 from the analysis of the election results. §3 answers why a coalition of hatred is easy to assemble; this section, why it cannot govern.*

**The observation.** A commentator on the results: "Populists are strong in that they find it very easy to assemble broad coalitions. They campaign on pure hatred of the incumbents. People ready to scratch each other's eyes out can hate together" — a supporter of Israel "more than Netanyahu himself" and a caricature antisemite; those who demanded the Epstein files and those named in them; isolationists and the heads of global corporations. "As soon as it comes to real questions from real life, cracks inevitably run through so motley a coalition… Trump's voters voted for very different Trumps. And the Trump who began to take real decisions the majority did not like." The movement "narrowed to a core" that is "more than enough to fill venues, but not enough to affect election results significantly".

**What follows.**

1. *It is not a coalition but a sum of "againsts".* A coalition has a contract — what we do together. A sum of "againsts" has none by construction: each came with his own enemy and his own candidate in his head. The very first decision — Israel, Ukraine, tariffs, insurance — must betray some of those who came, because there was no common "for". The cracks had nothing to grow from: there was no whole.
2. *"Very different Trumps" is not an accident but a technique.* Strategic ambiguity: the candidate stays vague and each projects his own onto him (Shepsle, 1972; Tomz and Van Houweling, 2009: ambiguity does not repel, it attracts). It pays on one condition — the voter does not check. One to whom the vote costs nothing asks not "what will you do" but "whom do you hate", and the answer to the second question is the same for all. Ambiguity is the price of breadth, and it is paid later, in office.
3. *It is a property not of populists but of the free vote.* The coalition that won on 3 November was assembled the same way: on hatred of one man, from the left wing to "Reagan Republicans", with the party's own rating at 29 % against 52 % ([059o §3b](059o-ship-turning-every-700-days.md)). It has no common "for" either, and it must decide the same questions. The thesis, applied to both sides, predicts the next cycle: cracks, narrowing to a core, rejection. That is the ship turning every 700 days: time after time a sum of "againsts" that cannot govern comes to power.
4. *The core is visible, the majority is not.* Full venues with lost elections are the same signal error as a dictator's ([040bs](../06-critique-arsenal/040bs-paine.md), thesis 3): the leader sees bodies and takes them for the country. A rally is a pretty report: by definition only those who came are at it. Pieces fell away for a year and a half, from spring 2025, and there was not one number about it until election night.

**What the protocol does.** The voter who came only "against" and does not check takes the payment; his "against" also has a second channel — the third candidate (§6). There is no longer any need to join a coalition of hatred in order to dismiss someone. The one who stays has paid D for his seat and asks what he has bought — a position on decisions; ambiguity stops paying, because a hall that counts demands a "for". A coalition assembled in such a hall is assembled on decisions: it is narrower and for that reason does not crack on the first of them. And the falling away of pieces is visible as a number, by district, at every election — local, by-election, midterm — not as one collapse every two years.

**The fork.** Either a coalition of hatred is unstable in office for everyone — and then the defect is in a system that rewards exactly such a coalition with victory every cycle. Or only for populists — and then it is unclear why the party with the worst rating in history won by the same method. The first confirms the protocol; the second is refuted by the figures of the election itself.

**What remains.** Real disagreements do not go away: a hall with a stake also argues about Israel and tariffs. The protocol does not create consensus — it removes the free ambiguity on which the dissenting are assembled ([040bb](../06-critique-arsenal/040bb-riker.md): the majority as a temporary coalition).

## 4. The second layer: admission without qualifying heats — the architect's move about the weak runner

The architect: "It is very strange: if in a 1,000-metre race two runners ran and one clearly fell behind the other and posted a novice's time, that is not counted as a bright victory for the second. The question is how this weakling got into professional competition, who selected him, what the method of selection was, and clearly something must change. And here — well, he promised us he would run well, but we were mistaken."

| | A 1,000-metre race | US elections today | Under the protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who admits to the start | the federation by a standard: the time in the qualifying heats | primaries decided by 15–20 % of a radical core and the brought-in (059k) | the same hall as at the election: the burned with the standard "I would not hire him" (1f §6) |
| What they select by | a measured result | a promise, loyalty, name recognition, money | substance: past results, fitness, the trail |
| When weakness comes out | before the final, in the heats | at the election itself, after admission | before November, in the primaries |
| Who pays for the selection error | the federation: disgrace, a change of method | none of the selectors — the core keeps its own, the party says "we were mistaken," the country pays with its horizon | the party: the takers of B in its districts are visible on the counter the same evening |
| What a win over the weak says about the winner | nothing | it is called a "tsunami" | nothing — and that is visible, because the hall judges both by one standard |

**The consequence.** Both "crushing" victories of the past two years are a time posted against a weak runner whom a broken selection system admitted. In 2024 Harris went to the start without primaries, by appointment a hundred days before the finish. In 2026 Paxton (attorney general since 2015, trail known before the start), Husted (an appointee), Jackson (a last-minute replacement without primaries), Miller — through the core's primaries or by substitution. The pendulum is not only the hall: every cycle one of the parties fields a weak runner, and the country chooses not between programmes but between a strong and a weak runner who swap places. Sharikov: "if a leftist radical had run in Texas against Paxton, Paxton would have won." After the defeat the White House spoke in the federation's words — "candidate quality matters" — but a federation then changes its standard, and a party does not: it pays nothing for the admission error (1f §4b, "fitness has no buyer").

**The architect's correction: a newcomer is not a weak runner.** "Not a newcomer, a weak runner. A newcomer can be strong too." A newcomer is one who has no time on the stopwatch yet; a weak runner is one whose time is known and bad. In sport the newcomer is admitted like everyone: post the standard — run. The problem of American selection is not that newcomers are admitted but that admission happens without measurement: a weak veteran by seniority, a newcomer by image. Paxton is an admission error, not a novelty error. Talarico and Turek may turn out strong: their time is not yet posted.

**What the protocol does — directly.** It introduces no standard: there is no objective stopwatch for a politician. It changes who sits in the qualifying heats (primaries in the same hall as the election, with the hiring standard) and gives the selector a price for error: if the party fields a weak runner anyway, the share of takers in its districts rises in the same cycle, and everyone sees that number. That is the stopwatch — not "how good he is," but "how many people did not want to choose between him and his rival."

## 4b. The Platner case: an allegation tested by the calendar — the architect's move

The architect: "Analyse the case where a candidate had to be withdrawn over a suspicion of violence against an ex-girlfriend. Is it normal at all that she remembered it right before the election, although the incident was long ago? It is theatre for very unsophisticated viewers, and a person is removed for it, and it may turn out she slandered him."

**The facts by the press.** The allegation surfaced not before the election but in July 2026, right after Platner won the primary: Politico reported a sexual assault allegation, Platner denied it, Sanders and other key supporters withdrew their backing, he quit the race; about 600 party delegates named Troy Jackson on 25 July — right up against the 27 July replacement deadline. Three and a half months remained before the vote. The "ex-girlfriend" detail is from Sharikov's retelling and was not found in the press.

**What the timing says.** Nothing about truth and everything about who released it. Such stories do not surface — they are kept: the file is assembled in advance and published when the target is worth most, and after the primary Platner became the party's only candidate. The moment is chosen not by the accuser but by whoever holds the file — and that can be any side, including his own party. From a convenient moment follows neither "she slandered" nor "she told the truth"; only that someone calculated the payoff.

**Who removed him.** Not a court and not the voter — the model of the voter in the head of the party machine. The campaign and the donors predicted that the layer, which learns about a candidate from a clip in a week (040v item 1), would punish, and removed him before it had time to decide anything. It is theatre, but its audience is not the "unsophisticated voter" — it is the party that fears him. The verdict was reached by a forecast of the hall's reaction, not by checking the fact (059g §3: fitness has no buyer).

**The evidence that it is theatre, not process.** In the same race the party fielded Jackson, who, by 059g §3, carries old allegations of violence that he denies. One input — two opposite outputs: one was removed, the other appointed, and he won. The difference is not in evidence but in the calendar: Platner could still be replaced before 27 July, Jackson after that date could not. When the same allegation gives opposite results depending on the date, the allegation is not what decides.

**Could she have slandered him.** She could; she could also have told the truth. Nobody checked, and the procedure provides no check: the only tribunal is the clock before the election. The Maine voter received not one fact either way; he received a replacement candidate. If the allegation is false — an innocent man was removed without trial; if true — the party still fielded a man with a similar trail. In both cases the one who should have judged lost. As routine it is normal: "October surprise" has been a term since 1980; as process it is not: selection admitted without checking and removed without checking, and the voter paid for both errors.

**What the protocol changes.** It does not establish truth; there is no stopwatch for an allegation. It changes three things. The clip loses its audience: whoever decides by the headline in a week has taken B, and those who remain are not moved by one broadcast (1f §6b), so the party has no reason to pull a candidate out of fear of a hall that is not there. Checking moves to the qualifying heats: in the hall of the burned the primary is the question "what kind of person is this," asked before admission; a file kept until July would have been opened in May, because the selectors themselves would have asked. The verdict is measured, not predicted: if the remaining found the allegation weighty, it shows in the result and in the share of takers after they decided, not in a campaign forecast three months before the vote.

## 4c. Negative selection for shamelessness, and a court where the juror pays for his seat — the architect's moves

The architect: "If you bribe any woman who worked near a politician, and an unprovable case drags on, the apathetic and the quiet will believe it, and any candidate can be smeared with unproven allegations? And the hall and the parties will look for saints, who do not exist, or for those who simply hide their deeds well."

**Whom the filter "not one allegation" lets through.** There are no saints, so three pass. *The person without a life*: never hired or fired anyone, never took a decision that hurt someone; there is nothing to accuse him of. The architect: "but that is a person without experience and without responsibility — he made no mistakes because he did nothing; that is not the best choice." *The person who hides well*: deeds exist, traces do not. *The shameless*: slander works only on someone with a reputation to lose; for one with a trail of a hundred allegations the hundred-and-first weighs nothing. The normal person with a life — with deeds and a reputation — is squeezed out between the three. It is the emptiness of the centre on the axis of character.

**The chain of negative selection.** (1) The hall judges by the headline: one allegation is a verdict. (2) There are no saints — the empty and the hider pass. (3) The empty one in office does what he did before: nothing; the voter tires — hence "anyone as long as not these." (4) Hiding gets harder — the internet, files, former staff; the hider is exposed, and one allegation kills him. (5) One stable strategy remains — to have no shame: whoever cannot be removed by anything is not removed. (6) The shameless beats the empty, because he at least does something, and the hider, because he cannot be exposed; for the layer, shamelessness reads as sincerity — the same mechanism as praising a behind (059o §4). (7) In office shamelessness is the absence of constraints: pardons for the 6 January participants and for donors, the memecoin and the family crypto, "$5,000" for the party's victory (059l). This is not a deviation from the strategy but its continuation. (8) Every such victory lowers the bar for the next; the normal person with a reputation becomes even less electable, because he has something to lose.

**The pardon as a demonstration of a zero horizon — the architect's move.** "Handing out pardons is pure mockery: a man knows it is not allowed, but given his age and shamelessness he knows everyone sees him cheating and does not fear punishment. One last deep breath. Everyone sees it — but the apathetic and the quiet enjoy his strength and envy him. He trampled the law openly and brazenly, and they see: yes, anything is possible, but you must become rich and be in power." This is the seam between §4c and §5: shamelessness costs zero exactly when the horizon is zero. The man is 80, there is nothing to lose, the consequences come after him. A hall with a zero horizon elected a leader with a zero horizon, and he teaches it the lesson "anything is possible if you are rich and in power"; the lesson is learned, and the hall's horizon gets even shorter (011b: learned helplessness as the product of teaching). Not an excess but feedback: a short hall — a short leader — an even shorter hall.

**Why an ethics law does not fix it.** Emanuel proposes banning gifts from monarchies, crypto and officials' commerce. A rule against the shameless does not work by definition: shamelessness is the readiness to break rules without a reputational price, and the price is set only by a hall that will not vote. The architect: "the cunning shameless one will take bribes in borzoi puppies and in drinking water; they will describe everything and check all the relatives and nominees; the hall of those who did not take the money will laugh at these legal tricks and will always recognise the fraudster and never elect him again, on reputation." Gogol's Lyapkin-Tyapkin took bribes in borzoi puppies precisely because money was forbidden — in 1836.

| | An ethics law | A hall with a stake |
|---|---|---|
| What it catches | the letter: listed gifts, relatives, assets | the pattern: "he takes," whatever and through whomever |
| Who runs ahead | the violator: every ban is a hint at what is not yet banned | nobody: reputation does not enumerate, it generalises |
| The violator's cost | lawyers, nominees, puppies instead of money — all by the letter | votes, forever |
| Memory | reset with every case: "not proven" means clean | not reset: once they change their mind they do not return (1f §6b) |
| Whom it is easier to deceive | the text: it is finite | the one who has hired and been burned himself |

By the glossary this is "punishment or price": the law punishes when it proves; the hall presents a bill when it understands. Understanding is faster than proving. And the very phrase "ethics law" is misplaced: a law codifies the minimum below which one is punished; ethics begins above the minimum, where there is no punishment and the choice is free. To call a law "on ethics" is to admit that no ethics is left in the system and it is being written into a list of gifts. It is a gifts law.

**The balance of experience and purity — the architect's move.** "The protocol adds a balance between experience and purity: the voter who did not take the money weighs everything carefully and thinks, rather than voting spontaneously, out of fear or under coercion. Experience gives mistakes, but experience also gives the quality of future decisions." Today's filter is one-dimensional — purity. The hiring hall asks three questions: what mistakes (of risk or of dishonesty — different records); what came of them (experience with consequences gives quality, without consequences — a habit); did he pay for them himself. The third axis is the one today's filter lacks: the empty one did not pay because he made no mistakes; the shameless did not pay because he never pays; the normal person paid — in money, reputation, business. The hirer selects the third, because he knows from himself: only a paid-for mistake teaches.

**Impunity as a product — the architect's move.** "Shamelessness is the reverse side of irresponsibility: 'yes, I did it, so what? Nothing — you just have to become president and issue yourself a pardon.' All the irresponsible want this, and the apathetic and the quiet are the most irresponsible, and they like it. They applaud Trump for how cunningly and pettily he steals: 'I would do the same if I could' — it is simply envy." Shamelessness does not merely survive in a hall without a stake — the hall likes it, because it is its mirror (059i): a person without a stake wants consequences to be escapable, and a shameless leader is living proof that they are. He sells not a programme but impunity as a product; the pardon for the 6 January participants is literally the product "my supporters bear no consequences." Cunning delights the one who does not bear its price: the damage is paid from the common pocket, which for a hall without a stake is not its own. The glossary caveat: irresponsibility here is a state without a stake, not a vice; change the position and the taste changes.

**Why the product loses its market under the protocol.** The one to whom impunity costs nothing takes B: money now is the short horizon, chosen honestly. Those remain who pay for others' impunity — in taxes, order, horizon. For them "I did it, so what" is not sincerity but a line in the record: the next ones not held to account will be them. They do not buy impunity because they know its price from themselves. A self-pardon is not a loophole to be closed by law but a test: a hall without a stake applauds, a hall with a stake remembers (1h, "the remembering"). One act, two halls, two results; the difference is who pays.

**A court where the juror pays for his seat — the architect's move.** "The hall is the moral court of honour and ethics. But each judges by himself and for himself, in his own head, and his verdict is not to punish but to support whomever he considers best or less bad — which is still an improving spiral. The arguments of this personal court are the latest and most current, not those written in a law and evaded by substitutions. The court exists, ethics exists, morality exists — the most current, and confirmed by the sum that was not taken." The structure of this court:

- *The verdict is a choice, not a sentence.* So no criminal standard of proof is needed: to not hire, it is enough to understand. The criminal court remains for crimes; the protocol does not replace it.
- *The code is not written.* Written ethics is evaded by substitution; ethics in the head cannot be evaded — it updates faster than the evasion, by the latest act.
- *Each judges by himself.* There is no single morality to capture; there is the median of the remaining, dispersed over millions of heads and gathered only on election day.
- *"Less bad" still improves.* Candidates adapt to the judge: if the judge remembers forever and judges by the latest act, the pool shifts upward every cycle; today the same adaptation runs downward (the chain above). The direction of the spiral is set not by a rule but by who sits in the hall.
- *Credibility — the sum not taken.* A juror is usually either paid or sits for free and loses nothing. Here the juror pays for his seat by forgoing the payment — a costly signal; in the glossary this is "passionarity": the state in which the vote is worth more than the payment. The court is not created by the protocol — it always existed; the protocol removes from it those who judged for free at others' expense and leaves those who paid for the seat.

**Morality at one's own expense against morality at others' — the architect's correction.** The assistant had cautioned that "moral court" sounds like populism. The architect: "it sounds like populism if there is a populist; here the morality is inside the person, and he imposes it on nobody but chooses it himself — that is real morality and not populism at all; 'moral court' and 'moral choice' are exact and admissible words, whereas 'ethics law' is the misplaced one." Accepted. Populism is morality on behalf of others: a speaker declares himself the voice of the people and imposes. The hall's moral court has no speaker, nobody acts on anyone's behalf, the sum is gathered by arithmetic, not by representation — by Mudde's test (040ab), zero of three. And the price separates them finally: the populist pays nothing for his morality, he sells it; the voter in the hall paid for his with the sum not taken. The glossary forbids the repository to pass moral verdicts on groups of people — but does not forbid describing the voter's moral choice as a mechanism: the former would be the author's populism, the latter is the protocol's subject. The term is entered in 1h.

**Caveat.** A reputational court is also deceived: Madoff deceived the most experienced investors, Theranos — a board of former secretaries of state. A hall with a stake errs, but its error is costly and one-off, while the law's error is free and repeatable. A well-made slander with forged evidence will deceive a hall with a stake too: the protocol lowers slander's payoff and its audience, not its possibility. And the court acts once a cycle: whoever is already in office it reaches at the next election, and until then — criminal law.

## 5. The third layer: the demand for newcomers — the architect's move

The architect: "There is a clear bid by the people for newcomers. The old have worn everyone out, and people are ready to choose anyone as long as he is not old. This is a bid for a third force. A newcomer is still perceived not as a party but as a personality outside the party."

**What it is.** A bid for a third force squeezed into two colours. The voter wants to exit both old machines, but there is no exit: a third party is a wasted vote. The only way to "vote for the third" without wasting the vote is to pick, inside one's own party, someone who does not look like the machine. The newcomer is the third candidate in a party jersey; he is chosen for not yet being a party. The architect's move in 1f §6b: "any reasonable person is better than politicians for one reason — he is not a politician."

**Why age, of all things.** Age is a cheap substitute for two things the voter cannot measure:

- **Horizon.** The old will not live to the consequences of their decisions, and the voter feels this without theory: the debt, the pension fund, infrastructure are decided by people to whom it is of no concern by biology. The young will at least live in what they build. It is a demand for a long horizon, expressed by the only available signal.
- **Non-capture.** Thirty years in the Senate is thirty years of obligations to donors, the core and the apparatus. The newcomer owes nobody yet. Novelty is the only observable sign that a person is not bought.

**The trap.** The substitute is cheaply counterfeited. The outsider wave of the past decade is a response to the same demand: Trump 2016, Macron 2017, Zelensky 2019, Milei 2023. Each was a "personality outside the party," and each was admitted by the same hall by image — by novelty instead of measurement. A newcomer elected by a hall of the apathetic and the layer becomes hostage to the same hall and within a cycle is either the machine himself or "the old man" everyone is tired of. The demand is real, the instrument weak: "not old" is a vote "against," like "not Trump," and it has no addressee except the next new face. To stress, per the architect's correction: the outsiders disappointed not because they were newcomers but because novelty became a substitute for a standard, which it is not.

**What the protocol does.** Three things:

1. **A real exit for the demand to exit.** Today the one tired of the old goes either to "anyone new" or into the burned, and neither path changes anything. Under the protocol he exits with money and returns with a share when there is someone to return for.
2. **The newcomer is judged by the hall on substance, not novelty.** Those who remain are those who hire: it does not matter to them that a person is new, it matters what he can do. A strong newcomer passes, a weak veteran does not; age stops being a substitute, because what it substituted for appears. The demand becomes exact: not "anyone as long as not old," but "anyone who has posted a result, and seniority has nothing to do with it."
3. **"A personality outside the party" becomes real, not a jersey.** For the first time he has an instrument the old machines lack: 048n, the question no rival asks — "the vote is already paid for, to parties and contractors; why not to you yourselves." The protocol is the newcomer's natural programme, because he has nothing to lose from a change in the hall's composition, and the old-timer has.

## 6. The third candidate: two layers of mechanism that must not be glued together

059n §5 says: after the apathetic leave, the third candidate of the centre is "not third but first by the median." That is true on one condition, and it must be held explicitly.

- **The robust layer — §3.** Swinging is unprofitable. It follows from composition, needs no third candidate, works with two colours.
- **The conditional layer — the third as first.** Duverger's law does not go away: the counting rule is the same, and if the centrist remains a splitter between two flanks, he still hands victory to the worst. He wins only when he stops being a splitter and becomes the largest bloc. The burned, by 1h, "stand with a position," but their positions differ: there are burned on the right — twenty years voting Republican with a held nose — and on the left. What unites them is a demand for fitness and a long horizon, not one programme. So "the centre by the median of the remaining" is a hypothesis about composition, not a consequence of the rule.
- **The most likely path of the centre is not a third colour but two colours converging on the middle.** Primaries run in the same hall (059o §5). The moderate beats the MAGA candidate in the primary, the moderate Democrat beats the progressive, and both parties, by Downs (013 §13.2), converge on the median of the remaining. The centre appears inside the parties before a third party appears. Sharikov, not knowing the protocol, called this "a demand for moderates"; Obama and Emanuel serve that demand — without an instrument.
- **Where a third candidate is guaranteed.** Where ranked-choice voting is added to the protocol, as in Maine and Alaska: Duverger is removed by the rule itself, and the centrist need not be first — it is enough to be the second choice of both flanks. Protocol plus ranked count is the configuration in which all three layers work; the protocol alone — two of three.

## 7. What the protocol does not do here

- It does not repeal Duverger's law: the counting rule stays, and a third party under plurality is still a risk of a split.
- It introduces no standard for a candidate: there is no stopwatch, there is a hall with a standard and a counter with a price of error.
- It does not promise which mini-party wins inside which party or who converges on the centre first: the number has no party sign (1i).
- It does not make the newcomer strong: it makes novelty unnecessary as a signal, because judgement is by substance.

## Weak spot

The conversation is analysed from automatic subtitles; the names, ages and Sharikov's figures (54 seats in the Senate, 228 to 139 in the House) may be distorted by recognition and are not checked. Candidates' ages are 🟡 from memory. "The burned are centrist by majority" is an assumption the glossary does not make: it describes a path (left after experience), not a position; §6 therefore keeps the third candidate conditional. The outsider wave (Trump, Macron, Zelensky, Milei) is given as a series, not a measurement: each had his own path and his own hall. Everything that begins with "under the protocol" is a construction; the direction of leaving and returning by group is for the pilot (040bt). The facts of the Platner case are from the press (ABC7, US News, CBS, Newsweek), not from primary sources; the allegations against Jackson are by 059g §3. Madoff and Theranos are given as examples of experienced people deceived, not as a measurement of frequency. "A court with a stake" is a description of a mechanism, not proof that the remaining judge better: that is the seeding rate and the pilot (040bt).
