## 1. Two strategies for one underdog

"I am the architect, I do no planning. Find an underdog and compare his chances if he runs as now and if he proposes a referendum." In the consultant's shoes: off-year, 100,000 voters, turnout ~20 %.

*Strategy 1 — the standard agenda* ("I'll fix the roads, a transparent budget"): 20,000 turn up; the incumbent's machine (contracts, unions, the party base) — a stable 12–14,000; the underdog's ceiling is 25–30 % of those who come, 5–6,000; chances ~0; the consultant says: "$150,000, we lose beautifully, we raise name recognition".

*Strategy 2 — one symbolic referendum* ("do you want the choice: ~$780 or a vote with triple weight?"): the consultant sees a hidden army — 80,000 who usually do not come; the wow effect and the slogan "take your taxes back from the lying politicians" pull out at least a third — +25,000; interception of the incumbent's "forced" voters (who voted out of fear of the opponents and see long-term protection from populists) — ~3,000. Result: 45,000 turn up; the machine shrinks to 10,000; the underdog 5,000 + 25,000 = 30,000; ~66 % against ~33 %.

Why this impresses a political technologist: free reach (every "I want to give you the choice — $780 or an amplified vote" guarantees all regional and federal media — worth tens of times the budget); Teflon against black PR (any attack is self-exposure: "the mayor just wants you to vote on his terms for free"); the pioneer-donor motive (for a tech or libertarian donor, funding is the shortest path to the status of "the man who fixed democracy"). A twentyfold skew of benefit over risk. The blueprint finds its executor by the laws of political evolution.

## 2. The prosecutor at the debate

**His speech** (prime time, a hall of pensioners and union members): "my opponent spent fifteen minutes showing formulas and moving averages; let's put the calculators away: in the criminal code this is called voter bribery; visiting billionaires wrote a scheme under which the government pays you $780 from your own taxes to stay home; who takes the cheque? not the corporate director — the single mother with nothing to pay for heating, the student in debt, the tired worker; and as soon as they take it they lose their vote; this is a modern poll tax, an elite's attempt to buy the working class's silence; where does the $780 come from? from your taxes — are you ready to pay for the apathy of idlers from your wallet? our grandfathers shed blood in Normandy, marched at Selma, so that everyone would have an equal vote — and these people hang a price tag on the shrine; vote against". Why it is deadly: no mathematics — a game on his own field, in emotions; the say-do gap works against us (the ordinary person secretly wants the $780 but is publicly ashamed — he loudly applauds the prosecutor out of social desirability bias); a split of the electorate (the middle class: "my taxes will go to idlers"; the poor: "the rich will take our rights"). 80 % of the hall boos against. Theatrical anger has first-strike advantage.

**The underdog's answer** (by the document; the architect's correction "re-read it, the answers are there so I don't waste my time"): "the prosecutor has given an expensive speech; let's strip the demagoguery. (1) *The anti-census* (§15.6): meet Maria — $22,000 a year, for her $780 is a month of food; but the mayor from the prosecutor's team is closing the school in her district; Maria refuses the money and goes to vote — her vote weighs triple, and she decides the fate of the city. And Jason — a hedge fund, $350,000, children in a private school in another state, he does not care about our roads; $780 is a restaurant dinner; he presses the button and leaves, because he cannot be bothered to queue. Before, Jason hired lobbyists; now an angry motivated mother decides and the lazy rich man stays home. This is not a property census — a census of motivation; those who have to live here decide. (2) *Squandering on idlers* (§28.2, §40.2): the prosecutor is silent that the mayor already spends $800 million of your taxes a year — and 8 % of the city elected him; the budget was deliberately turned into a 147-page PDF so nobody finds whose pocket the millions go to; we pay $780 to those who don't care, and leave at the polls 14,000 angry smart taxpayers with money on the line — Sarah the accountant will check every line, Mark the engineer will expose the overpayment for the bridge; 2 % of the budget on the dividend to save 98 % from theft. (3) *First-move asymmetry* (§22.8, §23.5): you stand here in an expensive suit on a salary from our taxes and call on people NOT to take their money back — to keep coming for free, listen to empty promises and provide turnout for your machine; your whole system rests on frightening people with each other for free for the sake of the 'lesser evil'; you fear not for a sacred right — you fear the audit that will throw you out of your chairs." (4) *Legal defence* (§15.15, Pildes): the right not to vote is protected by the First Amendment, compensation strengthens the right; under the Fourteenth everyone gets the same choice, and Washington v. Davis — disparate impact ≠ disparate treatment; forecast 6–3 in support. (5) *The memory of the ancestors* (§16.2): the ancestors fought for a right, not a duty. Finale: the hall erupts — the $780 is reclassified from a "hand-out" into "a fine the citizens impose on the establishment"; publicly proving why people should not get their taxes back is suicide on live television.

## 3. Who will interfere: three iterations

**Iteration 1 — the status-quo coalition** (the $26 billion political-fear industry; the "Frankies" — union bosses, party functionaries, partisan pastors with bloc control; the incumbent's bureaucracy, which understands that losing means audit and criminal cases) and four lines of sabotage: the battle for the ballot title (the attorney general approves the heading "legalisation of payment of budget funds for refusing civic duty" — the first $100,000 and three months of appeals to the state Supreme Court); street war (hired blockers with megaphones beside the collectors on Church Street — the price of a valid signature from $10 to $40); media blockade and a poison pill (refusal of airtime; the legislature puts on the same ballot an amendment banning municipal payments, to confuse); exploitation of historical pain (Black churches, human-rights activists: "the ancestors shed blood at Selma and this cynic wants to buy our silence"). The main internal risk is the creator's curse: the candidate starts speaking the "creator's language" ("the protocol of compensated electoral abstention", "revealed preference", BLS charts) — the campaign drowns; the political technologist must train him: only the 19-word pitch — "before the election two buttons: I VOTE — my vote weighs triple; I TAKE $780 — I honestly step aside".

**Iteration 2 — "you're in the old paradigm again: how will the burned sleepers, the fanatics, the pragmatists, the greedy poor behave? who will be against and what percentage?"** The model imagined a 2010s-style street war with hired thugs, forgetting that the protocol divides people by revealed preference. Recount: **against — ~0.11 %** (the apparatus and the top of the machines ~0.01 % — they will not shout into a megaphone, not their status; Frank's mercenaries ~0.1 % — 20–30 blockers). **For:** *the greedy poor* (~50–60 %): telling a person who cannot pay for his children's winter clothes that $780 is a dirty hand-out he must refuse for an abstract duty is a way to get punched; they see the blockers as personal enemies reaching into their pocket, and themselves tear them apart and throw them off the square — 30 mercenaries against a crowd defending its money is statistical noise; *the passionate and the burned sleepers* (~15–20 %): the appearance of "blockers from the system" is the best marker that the protocol works and the system fears it; a stream, a hashtag, 500 students, a viral rock festival at the signature site — free PR that Super PAC money cannot buy; *pragmatists and ideologues* (~25–30 %): the blockers shout "you're destroying democracy" and the restaurant owner thinks "the mayor spends $800 million of my taxes and these clowns defend his right to steal" — they silently walk up and sign; the louder the mercenaries shout, the longer the queue of owners. Balance: 99.89 % against 0.11 %. Every attempt by Frank or the prosecutor to interfere physically is a gift: the system is stupid enough to fight with twentieth-century methods.

**Iteration 3 — "and now again: who will interfere?"** On the electoral field — nobody; the resistance moves *off* the field. (1) Legal terror: the city council or the electoral commission refuses to register the initiative on the pretext of "technical non-compliance" (VRA, the constitution); signatures collected, 75 % in favour, and a district court imposes an indefinite injunction — the campaign freezes for months, killing the momentum. (2) Financial sabotage of the data infrastructure: the SSA is obliged to hand over ZIP-aggregated data but works to rule — "1960s software glitches", "confidentiality" — not 90 days but years; no data — the formula cannot be computed, the mayor says "a beautiful idea, but technically unfeasible". The answer — the four-agency rule and the 10 % rule (048b). The architect's correction: "financial sabotage is possible briefly — but after AB-EXIT is adopted…" — after adoption, data sabotage = non-payment = a depositors' revolt (048f). (3) An institutional poison pill: the legislature urgently adopts an "analogue" — payment five years after approval by an Equity Board, reduced in a deficit — breaking the feedback loop and the "1 %" brand. "What in the code can be distorted in one sentence so it goes unnoticed? Re-read the document" — four substitutions from §13 and §15.6: the data source switched to budget revenue (the mayor controls tax rates — raises them, revenue grows, the dividend grows, business flees; the protocol rewards strangling the economy); the source switched to the Census (the penalty for lying in a survey is zero; people overstate income in questionnaires; an autoimmune disease; for forging a W-2 — five years with a 97 % conviction rate); a composite index ("40 % wages, 20 % safety, 25 % ecology" — the mayor lobbies for the weight of the metric where he happens to look good); a barrier at the exit ("a cash cheque at the polling station", "through an Equity Board with a union veto" — the apathetic person waves his hand and ticks at random; the filter is broken). Five parameters hard-wired in the statute (source — only the federal median; formula strictly D = M × 1.5 × K; 100 % automatic crediting; the choice anew at every election; no approvals or vetoes) — changing any requires a new referendum. The struggle moves from "who shouts louder on the square" to "whose legal and technical protocol is flawless": the money goes to the best constitutional lawyers and IT engineers, not to agitators.

## 4. Five transitional traps and the "72 hours"

Before the protocol is adopted the underdog wins by the old rules, where the apathetic still vote. Five traps: the "Nigerian prince" reflex and authority bias ("too good to be true"; "why didn't Harvard propose this?"); the creator's language against the user's language; co-optation and a fake protocol (the "Goodman scenario": "we're for it too — but through commissions and with a delay"); an attack on the architect (the Machiavellian principle — the creator must not be the face of the movement; the face "a Russian, a Moldovan, not a citizen, an outsider" is attacked instead of the mathematics; the architect in the shadows, the stage to a local American); an election by the old rules (the $26 billion mobilisation industry still works, negative ads, dark money, busing).

The architect's correction: "do you really think these childish questions will turn voters away for long? for a day or two — and then they understand there is no catch; the formula is simple, there is nowhere to hide a false bottom". The 72-hour phenomenon: day 1 — denial ("some nobody offers $780 for sitting at home — a scam"); day 2 — curiosity ("if the politicians shout so furiously that the money mustn't be taken, the money is real and they fear giving it away"); day 3 — insight (K = 1 %: knock two zeros off the adjusted median — mental arithmetic in seconds; no hidden commissions). Precedent: the stimulus cheques during COVID — nobody asked who invented the law or whether a catch was hidden; they saw $1,200 and went to pay bills; "if the state transfers directly, it's not a scam, it's a tax refund". Open code against a black box: today the voter is offered a candidate who "will make everyone well off", and nobody knows what contracts he will sign; the underdog offers a formula tied to federal data that cannot be forged; the ordinary person may not know the author — he sees the mechanism does not depend on a personality. Trust works like deploying network infrastructure: the client doubts an unfamiliar architecture until he sees the right indicators light up and the speed grow; signal flows — we take it.

**The old machine's propaganda and why it breaks on the protocol.** The model's first version: the incumbent strikes not at the money but with fear — "$780 means firing 40 police officers and closing two fire stations, 911 arrives 15 minutes later" (loss aversion); "a Trojan horse — billionaires give $780 so you give up your vote, and then raise taxes" (historical trauma); culture war and ignoring (compromising material, "a radical", "Russian hackers" — so the zombies come to vote against the scary candidate, not for/against the $780). The architect's correction: "what rights are stolen? what is more important than tripling the vote and money — kittens? what fear, if there is $780 and cunning people say 'don't take it'?" Recount: "theft of rights" is a logical dead end because the counter-blow is built into the architecture — the tripling: "if this is a trap and they want me to stay home, I'll press the second button, refuse the $780, come with a triple vote and sweep these smart guys away"; by persuading that the money must not be taken, propaganda motivates people to come — and a motivated voter who refused money on principle is the machine's worst nightmare; the system wins either way. Culture war is an abstraction against the refrigerator: a free vote is easy to give away for an emotion; at a price the worker looks at his account — $780 is new tyres or the children's winter clothes; values will not pay the bills; "$780 beats kittens 10:0". The club of fear hits the mayor himself: who is saying this? the mayor under whom the roads are potholed, crime grows, taxes vanish into a PDF; when the rich on television shout "don't take it" in panic, a normal person's logic kicks in: "if the crooks so badly don't want me to get the money, they want to keep it for themselves"; the elites' panic is the best proof the mechanism works; fear turns into greed: "give me my money". An asymmetric trap: any attack by the machine either advertises the dividend or angers the person, turning him into a motivated voter with x3; the old machine has no language for talking to people who have been offered an honest deal.

**"What else can interfere?" — three "deadly moves" and their conversion into catalysts.** A media vacuum (the mayor does not come to debates, local media do not write; the underdog is heard by 200 people at a rally) — "right, go strangle every YouTube channel": in 2026 a video with the preview "how to legally get $780 from the city before the election" has a filter-breaking CTR; a topic touching everyone's wallet cannot be hushed up — the vacuum amplifies the effect of forbidden knowledge. Institutional hijacking of the agenda (the mayor: "a great idea, I'm creating a municipal commission, we'll launch in five years") — "he's helping us promote AB-EXIT, thank you, copycat": by pronouncing the letters, the incumbent admits the old system is dead; the fake protocol with a commission the voters will check against the open code in the statute; the mayor is forced to adopt the real one, which in the next cycle destroys his administrative resource. Operational failure, a shortage of "Jasons" to collect 10 % of signatures — "99 % in favour by your estimate — and we won't have enough people?": when 99 % have a vital interest, collecting signatures is not field work but handing out a scarce commodity; the forms will be snatched up. And even a legal block on the referendum does not stop it: the candidate becomes the living embodiment of the cheque ("I ran to give you the choice; the mayor and the prosecutor blocked it in court because they fear your control; the election this Tuesday is our referendum; vote for me, I'll fire these judges"); every day of judicial delay is a free advertising counter, the bureaucracy acquires the face of a frightened mayor. Informational irreversibility: once the idea is voiced, it cannot be put back in the tube. The answer to "what can really interfere?" — nothing; "and that is somehow frightening".

## 5. Why it was not invented earlier

The 90-degree shift (§15.5): for 2,500 years all political thought from Plato to Mill and Jim Crow moved in one direction — *whom to admit* (a filter at the entrance: property, literacy, race — always through violence, because someone above decided who was worthy); the protocol asks a perpendicular question — *how much to pay for a voluntary departure* (a filter at the exit). For the thought to be born two things were needed: universal recognition of human dignity (the idea that a commoner must be *paid* to refuse participation became the norm only in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries — before, the plebs were driven out by force) and the economics of revealed preferences (money as a lie detector came from marketing half a century ago; political science still lives on polls). Plus: political science was a humanity, not an engineering discipline — thousands of dissertations on "raising civic duty" tried to change human nature instead of the architecture of incentives; and the monopoly on the rules — the rules were written by those who won elections; a party machine spending billions on manipulating the apathetic has no reason to create a protocol that liquidates itself. Technically (040e, 036d): needed were zero friction (one click, money on the card — a hundred years ago an office, a queue, paper, a cheque, a bank; the apathetic person would not bother and would go to vote by habit), automatic data collection (the W-2 appeared only in wartime), and ideological maturity (a hundred years ago the world fought for universal suffrage — "give everyone the vote"; "quality over quantity" was psychologically unacceptable). The ancient Greeks could not have invented Uber.

## 6. Weak point of the case

The numbers in §1 (a third of the 80,000 non-voters) and §3 (0.11 % against) are a consultant's estimates in a role-play, not empirical evidence; in 045 and 046 the same quantities are given more cautiously. Iteration 3 is more honest than the first two: the only real opponents are judges and departmental saboteurs, and against them the campaign has only constitutional lawyers and time-to-value; the 72-hour phenomenon is a hypothesis resting on the stimulus-cheque precedent, which was an *unconditional* payment without a choice.
