# The US: the "Trump $5,000" Campaign as a Rehearsal for the Protocol — Eight Stages and a Forecast

**Chapter:** 10 — Case: the USA
**File:** 10_059l · v1 · 16 October 2026
**Source:** the architect's assignment of 16 October 2026: "The campaign and the hype around Trump's $5,000 will be very like the protocol if an underdog throws it onto the agenda. Trace all the stages and forecast what they may say about the protocol and how the campaign will go." The chronology is from open sources as of 16 October 2026 (CNBC, The Hill, Just Security, the Washington Times, Mediaite, Economist/YouGov) and from 059c. The architect's correction to the fourth stage is in §3.

---

*How to read. For the first time in US history a number "so much for everyone" ran the whole cycle from launch to resolution in front of everyone in eight weeks. It is the only field experiment close to the protocol, and the chapter takes it as a template: the same stage — what was said about Trump, what will be said about the protocol, how the protocol differs at that point. There are exactly three differences, all in the protocol's favour; the assistant first recorded all three as difficulties, and the architect turned all three into strikes.*

| Stage | When | What happened |
|---|---|---|

| 2. The first-day explosion | 10 September | CNBC: "immediate bipartisan pushback". Newsom: "a taxpayer-funded bribe". Just Security: "auctioning democracy", a possible violation of the federal vote-buying statute. Mediaite: "how can this be legal" |

| 4. The polls | 11–14 September | For 48 %, against 40 %; Republicans 61:29, Democrats 39:48, independents 41:44; ages 18–29 — 61 % for (059c §5). Demand exists, trust does not |
| 5. The split among one's own | weeks 2–3 | Chip Roy: "how to pay?"; Schweikert: "with all my heart against"; Bob Good: "a socialist vote-buying scheme". Senator Moreno: "I will file the bill right after the election" |
| 6. Repetition and escalation | 3–5 October | A video repeating the pledge, "smaller payments" handed out at rallies, a new ad, Vance hinting at an income limit. The number needs hammering, because without a rule it dies |

| 8. Resolution | 3 November | If the party loses the House, the number vanishes automatically; if it wins, Moreno's bill dies in the Senate. The number has no scenario in which it survives |

## 3. The fourth stage — the architect's correction: the number acquires salesmen

The assistant wrote: "the polls — the same profile: about half for, the young higher, the parties mirrored". The architect: "Here you are wrong — read the repo. If the burned see the ×2 vote and the exit of the apathetic, they will advertise the protocol themselves on every corner and in their networks — reposting the calculation for a family of three or four and what it can buy, and saying: 'surely Americans won't sell out for a new fridge, right?' — honestly, looking up from the phone, with a smile."

The error is the "before" yardstick: the poll profile was carried over from a number that had no salesman except Trump himself. Nobody campaigned for the $5,000: Republicans disowned it (stage 5), Democrats attacked it, Trump repeated it alone (stage 6). For the protocol two armies with opposite motives will campaign for free. *The burned* — seeing ×2 and the exit of the apathetic, will carry it themselves: it is the first offer in which they exist (1f §6b), and they have networks, numbers and a place in history (059g §4). *The takers* — will repost the family calculation: four adults at $800 — $3,200 for one election, and what that buys; it is Luntz's cheque (040an §4) in a format shared with one tap. And the format is found: "surely Americans won't sell out for a new fridge, right?" — at once a mockery of the myth of the sacred vote (059c §2) and an advertisement; there is no reply to it, because any reply confirms either the price or the hypocrisy. It is the lottery with a tip for a ticket (040ap §4), only sold by the players themselves.

*Reach — an estimate on the architect's assignment ("count the citation of the $5,000 in advertising budget and how much of the population heard it").* The story ran on every network and podcast for eight weeks; a national thirty-second ad costs $100–500 thousand, thousands of segments over two months are the equivalent of hundreds of millions in paid advertising (for the whole 2016 Trump campaign the "earned media" was estimated at $2 billion). Awareness — 85–90 % of adults: in the poll of 11–14 September 88 % had an opinion and 12 % "don't know"; the stimulus cheques of 2020–21 were known to more than 90 %. At zero budget and with half the country hostile to the source. The $5,000 had two brakes — the condition "if we win" and a surname; D has neither, and it can be counted for one's own family. The median US household income is about $80 thousand a year; for the bottom third (below $45 thousand) $3,200 is a month of living. That is not "heard on the news", that is discussed at the kitchen table: who takes, who stays, what to buy — and household events spread by themselves. The architect: "$800 will be heard by 50 %, and they will tell the rest themselves — it will be 100 %." The precedent: practically everyone knew about the 2020 cheques, because the cheque was in your name. For a poor family $3,200 is worth discussing — and that discussion is the campaign.

So the fourth stage for the protocol is not "about half" but growth from one reading to the next: Trump's 48 % with zero salesmen is a floor, not a profile. What to test: the share of reposts with the family calculation against the share of reposts with "bribery" in the first two weeks — that is the measurement of "who heard" (040an §7).

## 4. Three points where the protocol beats Trump — first recorded as difficulties

1. **Legality — the first strike, not a defence (the architect's move).** The assistant recorded legality as a hard point: 18 U.S.C. § 597 prohibits paying for voting and for abstaining in federal elections (059c §4, Hasen; 006 §252). The architect: "This is exactly where the protocol beats Trump 100 %. First, in many places it is legal. And above all: if it is entirely logical and even legal somewhere, why is it illegal at all? Giving money to the poor is legal, collecting taxes is legal, paying parties is legal — and paying people at elections is illegal? Here everything is very good for the protocol." The reversal is right. § 597 was written against a candidate who buys the vote he wants; the protocol buys nobody's vote — the rule pays, adopted by the voters themselves, to everyone equally and whoever wins (048m arts. 4, 15; 040ab, "punishment or price"). Where the law prohibits, the prohibition itself becomes an exhibit: "you forbade citizens what you allowed parties" (040y — state funding of parties as legalised bribery). Where it does not — state and municipal elections, most countries — the question does not arise. Operationally the same: the start is state elections and below (048m art. 8), through a state constitutional initiative, as in the Missouri scenario; the federal level needs an amendment by Congress. But say it first not to defend, but to ask the question the opponent cannot answer.
2. **The arithmetic will be produced by the protocol itself (the architect's move).** The assistant wrote: "Trump was forgiven 'the math doesn't add up' because his number dies; the protocol will not be forgiven — show the sum per election as a budget line beside the line it replaces." The architect: "Once more: you forgot again. For the $5,000 the clever did not even start thinking — it was obviously empty. For $800 all the burned will think how to justify it for the sake of their ×2. And if all of Wall Street and Silicon Valley switch their brains on for a couple of days for free — will they really not find where the non-targeted spending is hidden in the budget and how the protocol can be paid for?" The same correction as for the Ohio infrastructure (059g §4): an outlay I attributed to the protocol as external is produced by the protocol itself. Trump's number had no counters because the counters had no interest in it; D's counters are the most counting people in the country, and the justification of the payment is the price of their ×2. The budget line beside the line it replaces will be written not by the underdog but by ten competing teams, each better than the state treasury — the "counter-audit" of 059g §4, launched before the referendum. Only one requirement remains for the underdog: publish the state budget and the formula on day one — and let them count. *The scale of the battle — the architect's move:* "Even I find it hard to imagine what these clever people, who have long known everything, will drag out of their city budgets into the debates. It will be a very beautiful and bright battle over a paltry 1–2 % of the budget." The count for Missouri: 4.4 million eligible, half take at $800 — $1.76 billion per election; the state budget ~$50 billion a year, a two-year cycle — $100 billion, the payment 1.8 % (on a four-year cycle 0.9 %). The battle will be not over whether the money exists but over which of a hundred known holes to close first: taxpayer-funded stadiums, corporate tax abatements against promised jobs, duplicate agencies, consultants, unspent federal grants, double pensions — all long known to those who pay, who for the first time have a reason to drag it into the debates publicly and by name. There are no losers in this battle: any of the holes covers the payment with room to spare, and the hall watches the first public audit of its own budget in its life — "the protocol is not boring" (059g §6) at the level of the treasury. *And the counting will not be done by loners:* "not lone activists but highly experienced people with IT capacity, budgets and staffs of specialists; for the sake of ×2 they will try so hard that they silence the politicians with figures with a ×10 margin" (the architect). A loner brings a spreadsheet; a fund or a bank brings a model across every budget line, auditors and lawyers who know under what name an expense is hidden. These are the people who do due diligence on billions, and for the first time it makes sense for them to do it for their own state. To "where will the money come from" they answer not with an argument but with a dashboard; the ×10 margin comes by itself, because there are always more holes in a budget than the payment.
3. **Trust is not needed — the protocol has no owner (the architect's move).** The assistant wrote: "by 2027 'so much for everyone' in the US is a burned phrase: promised twice, paid never". The architect: "Here it is simple. You, the people, are clever yourselves, and we simply lay it all out for you, count it and propose going to a referendum. The protocol is nobody's — it may even be the people's. It has no beneficiary: everyone takes their own. What does Trump have to do with it at all: he is for bribery, we are for expanding rights." Trust is needed by one who asks to be believed; the protocol does not ask — it shows the charter and the calculation and proposes that you decide yourselves (048m art. 15). Trump's number could not be believed because it belonged to him: conditional on "if I win" and only from his hands. D has no owner and no beneficiary: nobody gains office from the payment (013g §4b), the rule pays, everyone takes their own, and only the hall can adopt or reject it. "He is for bribery, we are for expanding rights" is a difference of one line: with him the vote is bought under a condition, with us a second right is added and the first is not taken away (039b, Shenderovich's line: the protocol expands freedom; 048m art. 16). The burned phrase "so much for everyone" works for the protocol: it teaches people to tell a promise from a ballot line.

## 5. What the protocol takes ready-made from Trump's campaign

Proof of demand: 48 % for at zero elaboration and zero salesmen. Proof that a number travels every channel without a budget, hostile ones included (040aq §4). The figure for the young — 61 % for the money: the position on the young with a long horizon (040ai §4, 040al §4) should be tested on exactly that number. And the ten critics of 059c §4, who will say about the protocol what they said about Trump — the answers are already written.

## 6. Forecast and check

- 3 November 2026: Trump's number dies on any outcome — the test of stage 8.
- The protocol's first state campaign (the Missouri scenario): stages 2 and 3 pass within the first week in the same words as Trump's; stage 4 shows growth in support between the first and second readings, not a plateau; stage 5 — the first defector from the second echelon of one of the parties before the end of signature-gathering, and at readings above 60 % — an official takeover by one of the parties before polling day.
- If stage 4 gives a plateau at Trump's level, the correction of §3 is wrong, and that must be recorded.

## 7. Riding the $5,000 hype: the difference in three lines — the architect's move

"And one more thing: it is even good — on the example of Trump's $5,000 one can quickly show where the difference lies, and ride that hype." Trump has already paid hundreds of millions in earned media (§3) so that 85–90 % of the country knows what "so much for everyone" means, and on 3 November 2026 his number dies on any outcome (§1, stage 8). The public is trained; the underdog only has to show the difference.

| | "Trump's $5,000" | The protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Condition | "if we win" | no condition — whoever wins |
| Owner | from his hands, on his word | from a rule you adopt yourselves at a referendum (048m art. 15) |
| Term | once, after the election, if he remembers | every election, from the charter, until you yourselves decide otherwise (art. 14) |

The underdog's opening line — the day after 3 November: "Trump's number died yesterday, as we said it would. Here is a number that cannot die: $800 to everyone, every election, whoever wins — from a rule, not from anyone's hands. He offered a bribe; we offer a second right." *The second line — the dare (the architect's move):* "If under this rule I lose — so be it. I wish victory even to my opponents, if their voters do not take the money and go and vote for them. Or do you not dare?" This is at once proof that the rule has no owner (§4, item 3) and a question form by Luntz (040an §6, rule 9) to which the opponent has no good answer: to say "we dare" is to accept the rule; to say "we do not" is to admit that one's own voters are brought in and will leave for money (1c; 059e §2, the prosecutor at the debate — in one line instead of an interrogation). The three lines of the table fit one poster; that is the "picture" from the Luntz audit (040an §6, rule 8), which Trump drew for us. The burned phrase "so much for everyone" becomes a pointer to the difference between a promise and a ballot line, and the hype paid for by the opponent passes to the protocol for free (1c: no force has a good move). The table of 059c §1 is the same difference, recorded in September; here it is translated into a launch format.

## Weak point

The chronology is from press retellings, not primary sources; the polls are one window (11–14 September). The forecast of §2 is a transfer by analogy: Trump's number was promised by a candidate, the protocol's is put up by an initiative, and some reactions (the party split, legality) may go differently. The correction of §3 is the architect's forecast; the measurement is the first two weeks of the first campaign.
