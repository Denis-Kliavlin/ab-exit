# 59n. The Vote "Against": Pew 44 to 19, Gallyamov on Trump, MAGA by States, the Pendulum and the Third Candidate

**Chapter:** 10 — The USA
**File:** 10_059n · v1 · 19 October 2026
**Source:** the architect, 19 October 2026: an analysis of the 24 Kanal episode with Abbas Gallyamov "Is Trump accused of treason?" (host Ihor Havrashchak, 30 minutes, October 2026, before the midterms of 3 November); three questions by the architect — "analyse the MAGA electorate, how can the protocol change it," "check item 7 and analyse how strange this situation is," "can the parties, knowing this mechanic, swing the pendulum on purpose?" — and two corrections by the architect: "you did not read the repo again: after the apathetic leave, a third candidate gets a very real chance, and that is exactly the non-party centre." A continuation of 059i (the president as a mirror) and 022 (the emptiness of the centrists).

---

*How to read. Pew measured a country in which nearly half vote against one man and cannot tell him so except by handing power to his enemies. The chapter traces Gallyamov's chain, checks its main figure and shows three things: what the protocol does to the president's base once it is broken into states; that the vote "against" under the protocol gets an addressee with a name on the ballot — a third candidate of the non-party centre; and that both parties swing the pendulum of negative partisanship on purpose, while the protocol makes the swinging unprofitable.*

## 1. Gallyamov's chain, in order

1. **Nothing with Trump is forever.** Swings from two sources: self-interest (Lukoil's foreign assets of 20 billion that Putin is selling; by analogy Greenland, where a security claim turned into talk of deposits) or emotions ("decomposed," believing in his own greatness). Conclusion: no grounds to call the turn final.
2. **The system resists.** Andrew McCarthy, a conservative who defended Trump with a book in 2019, calls in National Review for a congressional investigation and uses "aid and comfort" — the treason formula of Article III of the Constitution.
3. **Cowardice as weakness.** The administration denies stopping intelligence and Starlink, the White House points at Musk, Musk at the White House: whoever does not take responsibility is not sure of his position.
4. **"Desperate."** The word sounds on the left and the right; in the most competitive districts more than half the Republican candidates distanced themselves within a day, the rest kept silent.
5. **The base is small.** 33 % MAGA is not enough to win; under 5 % support Russia, close to 60 % Ukraine; siding with Russia is risky, "emotions cannot explain it — so, self-interest."
6. **An authoritarian politician in a democracy.** One method: polarisation, "my negatives are lower than yours"; it works while the opposing camp is repellent; it hits the courts, Congress and the voter.
7. **The voter punishes — a record.** Pew since 1982: the motive for the midterm vote — "support the president," "punish," "not related"; "punish" was chosen by 44 %, the series had never crossed 40.
8. **Iran as evidence of desperation.** In a night post Trump praises Putin for not helping Iran: logically "I trade Ukraine for Iran." Such trades happen, but are not spoken aloud — the voter is not that cynical, and there is always a politician who says "both could have been had."
9. **Harm to everyone.** His own intelligence says the opposite about Iran; praising Putin, Trump exposes him in Iran after the diesel deal. Fuss without a productive result.
10. **Forecast.** How long — unknown; the loss of one or both chambers is almost inevitable.
11. **Agent or money.** No grounds for "agent"; Occam — Dmitriev, Kushner, Witkoff, the Treasury, the Energy Department, the diesel deal. The one thing Putin knows how to do is corrupt (Schröder, AfD, Le Pen, Orbán, Fico); recruitment is money, not the FSB.

*Weak points of the chain.* Item 5 is a false dilemma ("not emotions — so, self-interest"; ideology and strategy are not considered). McCarthy's article and the count by district are given without references. Item 8 is strong as rhetoric, not as logic: a superfluous argument proves sloppiness, not necessarily desperation.

## 2. Checking item 7

Pew, the report of 8 October 2026: 44 % of voters see their midterm vote as a vote against Trump, 19 % as a vote for him. The series: 2018 — against Trump 34 %, for 26 %; 2022 — against Biden 36 %, for 19 %; 2026 — against Trump 44 %, for 19 %. "A record since 1982" is not confirmed in the available material, but does not contradict the series. Gallyamov's figure is correct.

## 3. How strange this is

1. **An election in which the man is not on the ballot is entirely about him.** People vote for a district congressman, and the motive of half the hall is to punish the president. The other 37 % say "the president has nothing to do with it" — the only ones voting for whoever is on the ballot.
2. **For 19, against 44 — and the system cannot deliver the signal to its addressee.** The only way to punish the president is to hand power to his opponents. The voter who wants to say "enough" is forced to say "yes" to a party he did not choose. The right-wing press writes that the Democrats propose nothing, and that is true: people vote for them against.
3. **A pendulum instead of a signal.** The Congress of punishment gets a mandate nobody gave it; two years later it is punished the same way: 36 % against Biden in 2022, 44 % against Trump in 2026. Each party lives on the other's negatives — what Gallyamov in item 6 attributed to Trump alone is the method of both.

## 4. MAGA by states — the answer to item 5

The protocol neither shrinks nor grows the 33 %; it breaks them into states (1h), and each gets a move it does not have today.

**What the 33 % are today.** An ideological core that votes out of duty and faith; those brought in by rage, mobilised by a slogan rather than a programme (040ak); those disappointed by the economy, diesel and prices (059j), already burned by Trump but locked in: the only way to punish one's own is to vote for the others, and that is betrayal. Negative partisanship keeps the disappointed in the hall as if tethered.

1. **The disappointed get an exit without betrayal.** Taking the payment is not a vote for the enemy, it is "your $3,200 is worth more to me than your circus." Punishing one's own without going over to the others becomes possible for the first time. The share of takers within the base becomes the president's quotation, which cannot be drawn (013g §3b); for a president who lives on the base's turnout, that is worse than any poll.
2. **Those brought in by rage leave with the money.** Rage is free, which is why it is used to mobilise. With the payment on the table rage gets a price: "is my hatred of them worth $800?" Most will answer "give me the money" (059l §8). The method of item 6 breaks here: it works only while coming out of fear of the other camp costs nothing.
3. **The core stays and weighs double.** The older, rural, churchgoing will not take (040al §4). Their weight among those who stay rises, but without the brought-in and the locked-in the core makes no majority in any state: a president elected by the core plus the apathetic (059i) becomes a president elected by the core alone. The mirror changes its reflection.
4. **Trump has already said "yes" to the sum** (059l §8b). The base heard the money as its own; a Republican who puts the rule forward gets the core as an ally and the disappointed as takers — he wins in both states, and no kamikaze in the party will say "I am against money for your family."

What the protocol does not promise: the direction of the shift is unknown, the core stays denser, the disappointed take more often, and which is larger in each state is a pilot measurement (1i: the party balance of takers is unpredictable; 040al §4: the number cuts across).

## 5. The addressee of the vote "against" is a third candidate of the centre (the architect's move)

The first draft of this section said: under the protocol the signal "against" gets its addressee through the share of takers — the quotation. The architect: "you did not read the repo again: after the apathetic leave, a third candidate gets a very real chance, and that is exactly the non-party centre." The repository answers more strongly than a quotation.

022 §67.1: demand for a third party in the United States — 62 % (Gallup 2025), independents 45 %, but "very likely to vote for it" — 15 %; the gap between "want" and "will vote" is learned helplessness: "a third party will not win, my vote will be wasted." §67.4–67.5: the centrist's curse — he cannot be bright in form; the protocol is the one idea radical in form and centrist in substance. 059g: under the protocol two thirds of those unlike the burned take the payment and leave; among those who stay his kind is 60–70 %; his views become the median of the deciding set, and both parties converge on him by Downs (013 §13.2).

The consequence for Pew. The 44 % "against" today have nowhere to go but the other party, because with a full hall a third candidate is a wasted vote: the apathetic and the brought-in vote for two labels, and the centre drowns in them. After the apathetic leave, the hall consists of those to whom the vote is worth more than the payment, and in that hall a non-party candidate of the centre is not third but first by the median. The "62 to 15" gap closes not by canvassing but by arithmetic: a vote for the third stops being wasted because there is nobody left to drown it. "Against both" becomes "for the third" — and that is what the 44 % do not have today. The takers' quotation is the second addressee, not the first. And the centre for the first time has an army of canvassers — the burned (022 §67.6).

## 6. Both parties swing the pendulum — on purpose

The architect's question: "can the parties, knowing this mechanic, swing the pendulum on purpose?" They can, they do, and no collusion is needed — it is an equilibrium: each party separately benefits from the other being scarier, and the sum of two such strategies is the pendulum.

- **The "Pied Piper," 2015.** A Clinton campaign memo in the Podesta correspondence: elevate Trump, Cruz and Carson as "pied pipers" so that the Republican field shifts toward them. Trump was elevated on purpose, and they lost to him.
- **Democrats buy ads for MAGA candidates, 2022.** In Republican primaries in Illinois, Pennsylvania, Michigan, New Hampshire and Maryland, Democratic committees spent tens of millions on ads "he is too far right," which read as support to the Republican voter. The aim — the scariest opponent in November.
- **Fundraising on the enemy.** The best fundraising days of each party are the days of the other's victories; campaign mailings are built on the opponent, because fear converts to dollars better than hope.

Why this is arithmetic, not conspiracy: mobilising "against" is free — disgust at the other camp need not be earned, only shown; mobilising "for" costs a programme and its delivery. A party will always choose the cheap one, and both do. In the repository this is 040y (Mair, the cartel party: parties share rotation rather than compete for the voter) and 013g §3b: a government on bought turnout, where buying by fear is the cheapest of all, because it is paid for not by the party but by the opponent's behaviour.

**What the protocol does.** Fear gets a price: coming "against them" is free today; with the payment on the table it costs $800 of refusal, and most of the "against" leave with the money — the commodity both campaigns are built on gets cheaper in one vote. The "pied piper" becomes suicide: elevating a monster makes sense only if fear brings the hall; if fear takes the hall away with money, you get a falling quotation for both and stayers who judge by substance (040at). And the pendulum's axis is broken not by the count but by the third candidate's chance (§5): both parties are forced by Downs to move toward the median of those who stay and to drop fear mobilisation before the third wins a single election — it is enough that he could.

## 7. What the protocol does not do here

It does not reduce the 44 % — the discontent remains. It does not guarantee that the takers are exactly the discontented: part of the 19 % "for" will take too, the direction by party is unpredictable (1i). It does not forbid swinging the pendulum and does not make the parties kind — it makes the swinging dear. And it does not act at this election: until the referendum Pew remains the only thermometer.

## Summary

| Gallyamov's item | What is true | What the protocol adds |
|---|---|---|
| 33 % MAGA is too little to win | yes | they cannot be held either: the disappointed leave with money without betraying; those brought by rage leave; the core weighs double but alone makes no majority |
| 44 % vote "to punish" | yes, Pew 8.10.2026 | "against" gets an addressee with a name — a third candidate of the centre (022, 059g); the takers' quotation is the second addressee |

## Weak point

Analysed from automatic subtitles; names and quotations from the episode may be distorted by recognition. Pew 2026 and 2022 are checked against the report and the press; "a record since 1982," McCarthy's article and the count by competitive district are not checked. The Democratic spending in the 2022 primaries is given as "tens of millions" without an exact figure. All consequences of the protocol are a construction, not a measurement; the direction of the shift by party is a pilot question.
