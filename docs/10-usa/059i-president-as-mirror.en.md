*How to read. Katz describes how the word of the US president lost its weight, and explains it by the man. The chapter takes his chain as given, finds the break in it, and shows that the protocol answers the question Katz did not ask: who elected this man, and why he is normal to them.*

## 1. Katz's argument step by step

1. The president's word used to be the institution's word: "five floors of lawyers", months of coordination, the announcement at the end. One could rely on it — not because the president did not lie, but because he lied "at the level of interpretation, not of facts checkable in two seconds".
2. Now the president's word is a man's word: "the first numeral that came into his head", "the three-day rule", forgotten tomorrow.
3. The world got used to it and stopped reacting: Macron does not rush across the ocean, Greenland frightens nobody; in 2025 everyone panicked after the Oval Office quarrel, twenty months later nobody reacts to the same thing.
4. The bad actors got used to it too: Moscow, Beijing, Tehran, the Houthis see that threats and alliance mean nothing; "the responsible adult in the room" is gone, deterrence is gone, nobody knows the consequences.

The chain is clean on its own terms: if a word is worth nothing, threats, promises and deals depreciate together, and the world moves into "wait for actions" mode. That is right.

## 2. Where the logic breaks: the phenomenon is explained by a man, not by a mechanism

The one place where Katz almost reaches the cause is his line on the $5,000 (059c): "blurted it out at a rally, got positive feedback from the crowd, and started repeating it". That is the whole mechanism: the speaker optimises his words for whoever checks him. Gates answered to shareholders and the regulator, Musk answers to the feed, Trump to the rally hall. The hall does not check, it reacts; so the word is free. Katz described the symptom "the vote is worth nothing today" (039b §4b) and stopped at the carrier's character.

Three breaks.

*The nostalgia for "the speaking institution" is a longing for the expensive principal.* The old word was reliable because it was filtered not by voters but by an unelected apparatus. Katz himself lists the filter's price: Iraq, the lost wars, "the cannibals on the shoulders of the US". The old world: the word firm, the voter irrelevant. The new: the voter relevant, the word empty. These are two failures of one thing — in neither does the word cost the speaker anything before those who chose him.

*$5,000 to everyone is a trial balloon without a mechanism, and it confirms the distinction of 059g §4.* Experts seriously count the cost, and there is no plan; Katz: "forgotten in three days". We wrote that a number cannot be recalled. No contradiction: a number without a rule dies in three days, a number in a charter with a ballot line does not. Trump floated a sum without a rule, and it will evaporate; D = p × M cannot evaporate, because it will be asked of the boss at the meeting (059g §4).

*The world "got burned" on Trump exactly as the burned got burned on elections.* Twenty months ago everyone rushed to put out the fire, now nobody reacts. Katz calls it adaptation; it is exit (1f §6): believed, checked, stopped going. And exit without return, because neither Macron nor Beijing has an instrument to collect from Trump — the international analogue of "the core cannot be outvoted". And the second term removes the last checker: Trump will not stand again, and the second-term trap (055e §4) works in reverse — no next election, no price on any word. Katz does not notice this, and it explains why the second term is worse than the first.

## 3. A mirror, not an optimisation — the architect's move

"Do you not see the connection: a US president elected by the apathetic with a short horizon, irresponsible and infantile — they elected a president just like themselves, and to them he is normal, while to a thinking person it is simply madness."

This is more exact than "optimisation": there is no need to adjust to the hall, he is the same. And it removes the question "why does he not correct himself": there is nothing to correct, representation works perfectly. The match point by point:

| In Katz — about Trump | In 013 §13.2 and 1f §4 — about the apathetic |
|---|---|
| "the three-day rule": a topic lives until the next one | a horizon to the next stimulus |
| "the first numeral that came into his head" | a number that pleases, not one that adds up |
| "got positive feedback and started repeating" | the infantile loop: did it — praised — repeated, with no link to consequences |
| "will have forgotten tomorrow what he said today" | yesterday's choice is not linked to today's result |

Katz described not Trump's character but the portrait of the voter, transplanted into the Oval Office. To that voter the president is normal — he speaks the way the voter thinks. To the burned it is madness, because the burned measure by consequences, and there are no consequences in that speech at all.

Two consequences Katz does not have. *The astonishment of the thinking* — Katz's, and that of "the experts who seriously count the $5,000" — is astonishment before a representative who honestly represents not them; by habit they look for the institution, and there is none, because it was not they who elected it. *Trump is not the cause but a snapshot:* with the same voters the next one will be the same with a different surname. Katz admits it indirectly himself: the pattern was seen "not only by the good guys" — the pattern belongs to the electorate, not the man.

## 4. The grab-and-run milieu: betrayal as strength, the helplessness of the thinking, a one-way ticket — the architect's moves

"In that milieu, to cheat everyone for your own gain is cool, not deceit. In Russia it is the principle of the grab, the 'gopstop': take it brazenly, with a threat, and nothing happens to you. The hooligan is left alone not because people fear him but because they do not understand what to do with him at their own level. Clever Americans are ashamed of Trump."

*The arithmetic of manners.* In a milieu with a short horizon reputation is worth nothing, because "tomorrow" is not in the calculation; since reputation is worth nothing, betrayal has no price, and it reads as strength: cheated everyone and nothing happened — so he is cool. The grab is the same arithmetic: taking is profitable exactly where there is nobody and nothing to collect afterwards. Katz nearly said it when he admitted that Peskov is more believable: Putin "lies systematically, within some policy", that is, with a horizon; Trump lies without a horizon, and to his milieu that is not lying but style.

*Why the hooligan is left alone.* The instruments of the thinking — contract, reputation, court, law — work only when both sides value tomorrow. Against a player who does not need tomorrow they are empty, and at one's own level there is indeed nothing to be done with him: either descend to his level, which the thinking will not do, or leave. The shame of clever Americans is the shame of the burned at the result of an election in which their vote decided nothing: not fear, but helplessness with the feeling that this is being done in their name (1f §6, at the level of a country before the world).

*A one-way ticket.* "The grab is a one-way ticket: you can only escalate to a fight and a showdown. It is a temporary victory with very negative long-term consequences." A grab can be answered only with a grab, and each round costs more. In the US it is already visible on the district maps: Texas redrew, California answered with its own map, Missouri and Ohio followed (059g §1) — escalation with no stopping point.

*The protocol's answer is not moral.* The hooligan need not be touched: he has an audience to whom he is normal, and while it votes, he is a representative. It is the audience that must be touched, and not by force but by price: those to whom the grab is the norm take B, because money now, for a short horizon, is always dearer than a vote later (013 §13.2). A hooligan without a hall is a man shouting in an empty room. That is 1c, the board without good moves, and in Rand's terms (040aj §8): the hooligan lives on the sanction of those who do not know what to do with him, and the protocol lets them withdraw the sanction without descending to his level. And this is not an American peculiarity: the grabs of Russia's 1990s, Berlusconi, "the strong guy" on any continent — one milieu at the urn. The protocol says nothing about manners; it changes whose manners count.

## 5. The US: has anything else been devised, or is it business as usual (checked 14 October 2026)

"The US badly needs a conceptual reform. The protocol is exactly that. Or has the US devised something else, or does everything run on the old rails?" — on the old rails, and the menu is this.

| Direction | What is happening | Filter or mirror |
|---|---|---|
| Ranked-choice voting, open primaries | the only reform that reached the voter — and it is being rolled back: Alaska, 3 November 2026, a second repeal referendum (in 2024 the repeal failed by 743 votes; Musk put $1.5m into the repeat); about ten states have banned ranked-choice voting by statute | filter; for 040v §6 a clean case: a rule with no money inside does not self-enforce and is repealed on the second try |
| Structural amendments from above | expanding the House, 18-year terms for Supreme Court justices, twelve at-large senators (Casten's package, 2025), restoring the Voting Rights Act (the Progressive Caucus resolution, June 2026) | filter; not one proposal asks who votes and with what horizon |
| From Trump's side | the SAVE Act, the executive order on proof of citizenship, restrictions on mail voting | narrowing the roll (059h) and the same grab: whoever holds power edits the list |
| What is absent altogether | proportional representation, sortition, citizens' assemblies, compulsory voting — in no bill of the cycle | the only people who thought about the voter are the advocates of compulsory turnout (Brookings, 2020), and they propose the opposite of the protocol: drive the apathetic to the polls by force |

Conclusion: the whole agenda divides into "fix the filter" and "grab the list". Nobody works with the electorate itself — for the same reason the burned were never studied (1f §6b): expensive and hard. The protocol is the only proposal that changes the mirror, not the frame, with one ballot line and no constitutional amendment. From inside the US it will not be devised: everyone who could is busy escalating.

## 5b. A crisis with no reason on the other side — the architect's move

The architect: "So the US is in an extreme crisis of power such as there has never been. The US president looks more like an angry, unbalanced clown with a fleet and bombers, and the residents of the US themselves no longer like it either, while the politicians cannot propose an exit worthy of the problem." The assistant objected that 1860, 1932, 1968 and Watergate were heavier in consequences. The architect: "There it was the malicious intent of a reasoning man, while Trump is simply a hysteric and a crank, and no mind or reason is anywhere in sight."

The assistant's objection measured the crisis by consequences; the architect measures it by the adversary, and for the mechanism that is the right axis. In 1860, in 1932, under Nixon, on the other side there was a reasoning mind with intent: one could bargain with it, restrain it, outplay it, catch it on tape. Madison's whole construction of checks is built on that — "ambition must be made to counteract ambition", calculation against calculation. Against hysteria the checks do not work, because they are designed for one who calculates: it is the hooligan of §4 at the level of the state, with whom institutions do not know what to do at their own level. The unprecedented thing is not the scale but the kind: for the first time there is no reasoning counterpart at the helm, and for the first time all the country's instruments, built for a reasoning one, miss. This closes on the mirror (§3): an electorate with a short horizon is not a reasoning counterpart either — the actor and the hall coincide. And from the same source comes "there is no exit worthy of the problem" (§5): all the proposed exits are checks, that is, instruments against calculation, not against a reflection.

*The architect's refinement.* "An infantile president is the first in US history, that is what I meant: he bears not even moral responsibility, and has no internal limit for himself." That is the exact formulation of the unprecedented. All previous presidents, including the worst, had an internal limiter: Nixon left on his own, because shame and the norm still meant something to him. The US Constitution relies on that limiter but nowhere writes it down — Levitsky and Ziblatt (*How Democracies Die*, 2018) called it the unwritten guardrails: mutual toleration and forbearance. The infantile does not see those rails — he does not break them, he does not have them. And the hall does not demand them, because it does not have them either (§3). Two survived impeachments against one Watergate show the difference: Nixon was given up by his party; Trump's party fears him, because it fears the hall. The protocol answers in the only way that requires no conscience from the president: the internal limiter is replaced by an external one that remembers — the burned with weight ×2 at the next election (040x item 3, 1f §6b).

Who dislikes it matters too. The apathetic like it: they see themselves. The burned dislike it, and for them, for the first time in a long while, it is not apathy but shame (§4) — that is, a motive. The first move is made by those with nothing to lose (059g §4), and shame for one's country before the world is the state in which the producers bought *Atlas* in 2009 (040aj). A crisis with no exit through the filter is a market for a reform that has one: the protocol sells where the other answers have run out.

## 6. Forecast and check

- Alaska, 3 November 2026: the repeal of ranked-choice voting passes (60 %) — a rule without self-enforcement, a second try, money on the repeal side. If it fails, record it as a counter-example to 040v §6.
- "The three-day rule" outlives Trump: the next president elected by the same electorate will speak the same way; the check is the first hundred days of the next term.
- No "mirror" reform (one changing who votes, not how the count is done) reaches the federal level before 2028.

## Weak point

The video transcript is automatic, and Katz's quotations may differ from what was said in detail. The table in §3 is a correspondence of meaning, not a measurement: "the voter's horizon" in 013 is a model, not a survey of Trump's voters. The survey of reforms is a snapshot on one date from open sources; the Supreme Court terms and Casten's package are bills without movement, and their absence from the agenda a year on will refute nothing. And above all: the chapter explains Trump by the electorate but does not prove that another electorate would have chosen otherwise — that is the protocol's forecast, testable only by the protocol.
