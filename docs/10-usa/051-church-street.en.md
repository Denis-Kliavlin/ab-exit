### 29.1. Five kinds of herd voting

25 % of the electorate are neither apathetics (who don't care) nor the convinced (who have a position). They are the DOUBTERS: people who think but are not sure, and in the end vote with the crowd. Five mechanisms:

**Social pressure.** "All my friends are for A. If I say otherwise — they'll judge me. Easier to agree." Votes like his surroundings, not as he thinks.

**Polls as a command.** "Poll: A = 55 %. The majority can't be wrong." The poll CREATES the result it predicts — a self-fulfilling prophecy. A gets not 55 % but 60 %, because the doubters joined the 55 %.

**The media cascade.** "CNN/Fox all day about A. So A = important = right." Whoever is more in the media = whoever wins. Not whoever is better.

**The spiral of silence** (Noelle-Neumann, 1974). "I'm for B. But everyone around is for A. If I say so — they'll isolate me. I keep silent." The minority keeps silent → the majority seems even bigger → even more silence → B loses although 45 % are secretly for B.

**The information cascade** (Banerjee, 1992). Person 1 chooses A (at random). Person 2 sees "1 is for A" → chooses A. Person 1,000 sees "999 are for A" → OBVIOUSLY A. Even if A = bad. One random choice → 1,000 identical ones. The cascade is self-reinforcing.

Asch's study (1951): 75 % of people at least once agreed with the crowd's obviously wrong answer. $8.5B of TV advertising = the creation of an ILLUSION OF A CROWD: "everyone's for A, join in!" Elections are decided not by the best candidate — elections are decided by the best imitation of a crowd.
