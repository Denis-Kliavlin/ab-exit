### 86.1. Purpose of the section

This section records a specific rhetorical move for a conversation with Ray Dalio and systems thinkers of his profile (the Bridgewater Associates partnership, major fiscal analysts, financial elites). The move is built on a structural objection to Dalio using his own methodology: his Big Cycle theory makes AB-EXIT not an alternative to his fiscal solutions but a necessary condition for their applicability.

This is possibly the project's strongest rhetorical tool for talking with the financial elite and systems analysts.

### 86.2. Dalio's theory depends on the quality of political governance

Dalio's whole Big Debt Cycle theory ("Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises" 2018, "How Countries Go Broke" 2025, the series of publications 2024–2026) is built on one central dependence: the outcome of a crisis is determined by the quality of coordination between the fiscal and monetary authorities.

Direct quotations from his publications:

"Effective approaches require coordination of monetary and fiscal policy that ensures the printed money is actually spent. By contrast, a lack of coordination leaves policymakers pushing on a string."

"Big debt restructurings and monetisations end the previous Big Debt Cycle. These come as big shifts in political orders — as seismic shifts because the old order breaks."

At the World Government Summit 2025: "I call for urgent, coordinated action among policymakers, business leaders and citizens."

The logical chain from his own system:

1. Fiscal policy comes out of the political system.
2. The political system determines the quality of coordination with the monetary authority.
3. The quality of coordination determines whether the crisis will be mild or catastrophic.
4. The ability to adopt "pragmatic fiscal reforms, setting aside ideological disagreements" (his words from 2025) is a political question, not a financial one.

### 86.3. Empirical confirmation through 48 crises

Dalio himself empirically demonstrates that the same crises are resolved differently depending on the quality of political governance. From his own analysis of 48 cases of big debt crises:

— Germany 1918–1924: collapse into hyperinflation, political destabilisation, Weimar → Hitler. — Japan 1980–1990: the "lost decades" with managed stagnation. — USA 2008–2010: coordinated QE + fiscal stimulus → a comparatively mild recovery. — USA 2020 (COVID): a coordinated fiscal/monetary response → a rapid V-recovery.

The same type of crisis gives catastrophe, managed stagnation or V-recovery depending on the political system's capacity for coordination. This is not an interpretation — it is Dalio's own empirical result.

Conclusion: the quality of political governance is the decisive factor in the outcome of a crisis under equal starting conditions.

### 86.4. AB-EXIT solves the problem that blocks quality political governance

What blocks political quality now? Dalio himself answers in his recent speeches: "huge gaps in people's levels of education, income, opportunity and values", "irreconcilable disagreements between left and right", populism, rising inequality.

For a coordinated solution of the type of his "3 % 3-part solution" (a combination of spending cuts, tax increases, economic growth) a political majority capable of compromise is needed. Today there is no such majority, because populism mobilises the apathetic into tribal camps, making compromise impossible.

AB-EXIT, by removing the apathetic from the electorate, leaves at the polls Pragmatists (who understand the necessity of fiscal discipline) and Ideologues (with stable values, capable of bargaining). This is exactly the structure of an electorate able to support Dalio's "3 % 3-part solution".

AB-EXIT does not compete with fiscal solutions. **AB-EXIT is the political infrastructure that makes fiscal solutions politically possible.**

### 86.5. Why Dalio's fiscal solutions are not implemented without political infrastructure

This is the sharpest point. Dalio has published solutions for years. He meets presidents, finance ministers, central bank governors. He speaks at the World Government Summit, the Council on Foreign Relations, Davos. But the Big Debt Cycle moves on. US debt has passed $34T at a deficit of $2T/year. Coordination worsens. Polarisation intensifies.

Why are his solutions not implemented? Not because they are bad — because the political system is structurally incapable of adopting them. Apathetic voters make populists the winners, and populists structurally cannot go for "pragmatic fiscal reforms" requiring compromise and a short-term political price.

Dalio in this sense is in the position described by Applebaum in Twilight of Democracy: a brilliant analyst, accurately describing the problem, proposing the right solutions, but having no mechanism for their implementation. His solutions require a political system that does not exist.

**AB-EXIT provides that system.** AB-EXIT is not a solution to the Big Cycle; AB-EXIT is the prerequisite for all solutions to the Big Cycle, including Dalio's own.

### 86.7. Why this kills his possible objection "AB-EXIT is a slow reform; fast hard measures are needed"

In a discussion with Dalio one can expect an objection in this style: "AB-EXIT is a multi-year reform of the political system. At the moment of crisis fast hard measures are needed: debt restructuring, austerity, hard money. AB-EXIT moves to the back of the queue behind fiscal measures."

This argument breaks under three counter-blows:

**First:** fast hard measures are not adopted not because of their harshness but because of political impossibility. The Bush-Obama-Trump-Biden administrations all knew about the growing debt. All had intellectual solutions. None could implement them because of political resistance. So the problem is not the absence of solutions but the absence of the political capacity to apply them.

**Second:** AB-EXIT solves the political impossibility, after which fast hard measures become passable. This is not competition with fiscal measures — it is their enabler.

**Third:** Dalio himself in "How Countries Go Broke" admits that at moments of crisis political systems either break (Weimar) or adapt (Japan, USA 2008). The capacity to adapt is a political quality. AB-EXIT improves that political quality structurally.

### 86.8. One honest point — Dalio's possible polite refusal

Dalio may answer: "Fine, your argument is logically correct. But political infrastructure is a different discipline, not my field. I am a financial analyst, not a political architect." That would be a polite retreat, and it is normal.

But even in that case he cannot publicly object to AB-EXIT, because his own theory makes arguing against AB-EXIT equivalent to arguing against quality political governance — which contradicts his public position on the necessity of "coordinated action".

So the worst Dalio can do is keep silent or politely acknowledge the argument's validity without active promotion. That is not a block, it is neutrality. For AB-EXIT Dalio's neutrality is better than his criticism.

The best that can happen — Dalio publicly mentions AB-EXIT in one of his interviews or LinkedIn posts as "an interesting structural innovation for the late-cycle period". That gives world visibility of a level unreachable through any other channels.

### 86.9. Applying this move to other systems thinkers

The same rhetorical move works not only for Dalio. The class of systems thinkers to whom it applies:

— **Financial elites with an interest in long-term sustainability.** Any major investor or institutional analyst thinking in categories of cycles and systems.

— **Systems economists of the level of Sergei Guriev, Daron Acemoglu, Joseph Stiglitz.** They too describe the problem of political quality as an obstacle to solutions. The same argument works.

— **Technocratic reformers of the type of Larry Summers, Mohamed El-Erian.** They formulate policy in categories of coordination and quality of governance.

The general structure of the argument: "Your theory shows the problem. Your theory requires political quality for its solutions. The modern political system does not provide that quality. AB-EXIT does. Your theory works through us, we work for your theory."

This move transfers an intellectual from the category "potential critic" to the category "potential ally through his own work". This is a strong position for communication.
