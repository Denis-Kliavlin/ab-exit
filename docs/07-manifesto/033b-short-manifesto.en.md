# The Short AB-EXIT Manifesto (street register)

**Chapter:** 07 — Social Architecture and Manifesto
**File:** 07_033b · v3 · 12 June 2026 · successor to YUNO Short Manifesto v2
**Register:** street. For a leaflet, a landing page, a post, a video. The academic register is §33 and §13.

---

You have never been paid for your vote.

But it is paid for. To everyone except you.

Parties receive money from the budget — for every vote cast for them. In Germany — about a euro per vote. In France — almost one and a half. TV channels receive billions for the advertising they run at you before elections. Political consultants make fortunes for studying you like a fish in an aquarium and picking the bait.

Your vote is money. You just aren't the one who gets it.

Now look at what happens when you don't vote. Nothing. Nobody notices you. You are not dangerous. You can be ignored. Half the country doesn't go to the polls — and the system has learned to pretend that's how it should be. Your silence costs it nothing.

AB-EXIT does one thing: it puts a price tag on your vote. Your price tag.

Before every election — two options. Button A: you go and vote as before — but your vote weighs more, because some people have exited. Up to twice as much. Button B: you officially say "this time — without me" — and the state pays you. Not the party. Not the channel. You. One and a half per cent of your country's median wage, every year. A month's wages for five minutes in an app — once a cycle.

Next cycle you choose again. Nothing is taken away. Ever.

Don't like the show — you get paid to leave the hall. Like it — your ticket is now double.

And in both cases the main thing happens: **you stop being invisible.** Voted — you were counted with double weight. Exited — you were counted and paid. The third option — "you simply don't exist" — no longer exists.

Who is against? Those who now receive money for your vote. Party treasuries. Political consultants. Everyone who feeds off your free silence or your free anger. Ask any of them: "Why can't I get what you get — for what is mine?" Listen to the answer. There won't be one. In a year nobody has managed to break this question — including six artificial intelligences that were paid to look for holes.

No sitting politician will offer you this first. It doesn't pay him. So you offer it — to him. As a question.

The system has two exits: either the state pays you for your vote — or one day your vote again becomes someone's weapon. Free. As always.

**Your vote. Your money. Your exit.**
