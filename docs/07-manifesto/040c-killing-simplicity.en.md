## 1. The rocket matters more than the pilot

If the idea really reads as "$1,000 or a x2–x3 vote", people vote not for a likeable underdog but for the fork itself; the candidate is needed as a carrier who said it first, did not retreat under attack and did not complicate the formula. Support for the idea at a referendum — 85–90 % (013c.5); support for the underdog who became the face of the idea — close to that ceiling minus the candidate's personal minuses. The rocket works only while the formula stays simple, is not diluted, and attacks do not move the dispute into complex theory. As soon as "money or a x3 vote" turns into "a complex institutional reform with a heap of conditions", the rocket loses thrust. Hence everything else in the section: the enemy need not beat the mathematics, he needs to destroy the simplicity.

## 2. The logic of a referendum that kills the protocol

The aim is not to argue honestly but to turn a simple fork into a frightening complex reform.

1. **Kill clarity.** Instead of "$1,000 or a x3 vote" — "a comprehensive reform of the system of citizen participation in elections with the introduction of compensated voluntary non-participation, recalculation of vote weight, an autonomous payment fund, a formula tied to median income and cyclical restoration of electoral rights". As soon as a person stops understanding within three seconds — the rocket falls.
2. **Hang the maximum of fears as a package.** Vote buying; the poor will be bought out, the rich will remain; the city will go bankrupt; the government will tweak the formula; the winners will be considered illegitimate; then they will cancel the return of the right; an experiment on democracy. Not one argument but an atmosphere of "too risky, too complex, too dangerous".
3. **Move the dispute from benefit to procedure.** Make people think not "$1,000 for me / a stronger vote for me" but "who will compute the median, who will pay, what happens in a crisis, can it be rolled back, is it constitutional, who controls the fund, what if there are fraudsters". The more technical questions, the less remains of the fork.
4. **Make the ballot question hostile.** "Do you support the introduction of an *experimental* system of cash payments for *refusing to participate* in elections, a change in the weight of the remaining voters' votes and the creation of a separate fund financed *from the budget*?" — three blows built into the wording (059e.3: the battle for the ballot title).
5. **The main principle.** Take away from the person the sense that he is choosing between money and a strong vote, and replace it with the sense that he is voting for a dangerous, expensive and tangled experiment.

## 3. How to destroy "$1,000 in your account"

The poor cannot be convinced they do not need $1,000. They can be convinced that the $1,000 *won't come*, is *dangerous*, is *humiliating*, is *temporary while the loss of rights is permanent*. Working strikes: **blur the sum** ("it's not $1,000 but a complex formula", "in a crisis it'll be $200", "first they promise, then they recalculate"); **blur the receipt** ("not into your account but through an application, a queue, a check", "not for everyone, only if you pass the filters", "some day"); **tie it to a threat** ("take it and you lose the right to complain", "you won't get your vote back", "the rich will rule you"); **turn it into morality** ("they want to buy you", "real citizens don't take money for silence", "a humiliation of the poor"); **make it technical** ("compensation for temporary voluntary waiver of the exercise of the electoral right within a budget-dependent median-income formula"). As soon as the picture "the money arrived on the card" disappears, the idea's strength falls: opposition among the poor rises from 7–12 % to 25–40 % (013c.5). The whole war is fought not over "do the poor need money" but over whether the picture "$1,000 in your account" stays in the head.

The symmetric defence: the same five strikes turned into statute requirements — the sum by a public formula any student can recompute (048f.5); 100 % automatic crediting, without applications or commissions; the choice anew each cycle, the return of the right written into law; no approvals or vetoes (059e.3). The ballot wording is a separate front: it is lost before the campaign if not prepared (059e.3 iteration 1).

## 4. The logic of posts

For a broad audience the anchor is always one: **money or an amplified vote — you choose.** Everything else is secondary. Do not refute attacks at length — any attack must return to the same fork: "they are against because they lose control"; "for you — money or a stronger vote"; "for them — neither is needed". Simplicity matters more than depth: RCV, quadratic voting, liquid democracy are weaker precisely because they cannot be explained in five seconds (036). Different depths for different audiences: for the people only the fork; for techies and donors — the stress tests and the mechanism; for politicians — the ballot initiative, a local launch, the carrier's benefit. Do not dilute: as soon as a post turns into "a complex institutional reform", the rocket loses thrust. The right post does not prove the theory — it puts the person before the choice again and again.

Who will not like the post "you vote — your vote is x3, or you take $1,000": party activists and campaign staff (they need the old mobilisation); political technologists and GOTV (a business on delivering the indifferent); the principled "the vote is sacred"; some ideologues on the left ("the poor will be bought out") and on the right ("the state pays for irresponsibility"); those who immediately disbelieve. Will like it or at least not push back: ordinary non-voters, those who really need $1,000, pragmatists, the anti-system, lovers of a clear choice. The YouTube audience's reaction by incentive logic: 70–80 % support, 10–15 % neutral, 10–15 % against. The format is hard to scroll past: a personal fork, an instant inner answer before the video ends, a social hook (what will the poor, the rich, the activist, the "I don't care" choose), and attack feeds it too. Content that explains itself, argues with itself and spreads itself through other people's reactions. For YouTubers the philosophy is less interesting than the plot: "an idea so contagious that after a certain reach it can no longer be calmly ignored" — "up to 100,000 we are still arguing, after that it is too late" as a built-in counter (052: the challenge as a mini-referendum).

## 5. Wordings of the fork

Basic: "$1,000 or a x3 vote". Simpler: "money or a real vote". For a five-second clip: "You get cash. Or your vote gets heavier. Choose." English variants for the US: *Take the $1,000. Or keep a stronger vote.* · *Cash out. Or power up.* · *$1,000 in your account. Or 3x the influence.* · *Get paid to step aside. Or stay and matter more.* · *Money now. Or a vote that actually counts.* · *$1,000 or real power.* The protocol's caveat (033c.9): "$1,000" is an illustration for the US; the sum is D by the formula from the median, and in another country's slogan its own number stands, not a borrowed one.

## 6. The gift to the world and three vulnerabilities

If the aim is to give, not to sell and not to control, the task is simpler and harsher at once: the idea matters more than the author's ego (let them repeat, simplify, not mention the name — as long as the fork lives); openness matters more than control (the repository, the formula, the legal texts, the challenge are copied without asking); the author need not be the face forever (launch clearly, protect from distortion at the start, let go); what is worth guarding is not the brand but the simplicity of the mechanism — if the idea is turned into "a complex reform" it dies even when free. The gift is already made: a site with the architecture, a repository, the comparison with reforms, the legal outlines, the media logic, the challenge, the history of stress tests — an open package, not an idea in a head (059b.7).

| Vulnerability of the gift | Rating | What to do with it |
|---|---|---|
| Too large a volume: the world loves a formula, not 50+ sections | 7/10, treatable | The repository is armour and a store of arguments; the virus carries only the core; if the core holds, the thickness helps to repel attacks, if it drowns in text — it hurts |
| Risk of distortion into "vote buying" | 9/10, the main one | This is the main front of the war for meaning; the defence — the clearer the fork, the harder to rewrite it; distortion works only when the formula is diluted |
| Weak authorial control | 8/10, an inevitable price | The idea will be copied, simplified, taken without a name, used by politicians; that is a sign of a real gift; one can control the frame and the first launch, not the idea's whole later life |

The world rarely kills a strong idea with logic — it kills it by retelling: within a month people argue not with the protocol but with a caricature of it. The world senses benefit well and holds precision badly; a simple idea has two paths — it is repeated correctly and grows by itself, or it is retold crookedly and a phantom is hated.

## 7. The one-year window

A year is not "to think" but a launch window. What must be done is not another 50 sections but hammering one formula into mass consciousness. Five tasks: hold simplicity (outside only the fork; the repository is for defence, not for first contact); make the idea repeatable (short clips, the challenge, one phrase anyone can say); find the first carrier (an underdog, a city, a state, a public figure — a person for whom it *pays* to say it aloud); do not drown in distortion (to every "vote buying" — a return to the fork, not a lecture); collect a trail (site, repository, video, retellings, mentions — so the idea exists not in one chat). Four "don'ts": do not complicate the mechanism "for solidity"; do not argue with all experts endlessly; do not wait for the ideal politician; do not hide the core under academic language. Within a year the world must stop hearing "a complex institutional reform" and start hearing "I am offered money or a stronger vote". If that happens — the gift begins to live by itself; if not — the idea remains a strong text without a mass body.

## 8. Weak point of the section

Simplicity is at the same time the attack surface: the slogan "$1,000" promises a number the statute cannot fix (D depends on the median and changes across countries and years), and the first opponent will ask "where is my $1,000 if the formula gives $780"; the defence in §3 requires "the sum by the formula" to sound as simple as "$1,000", and that is not yet solved. The 70–80 % reaction is the model's projection, not a measurement. And §7 is formulated from the logic of a single carrier; if there are several carriers bearing different numbers and different forks, distortion (§6) comes not from enemies but from allies. 🟡
