# The Scoreboard: Mass as Auditor, Elite as National Team, Dividend as Interlingua

## 1. The autopilot of freedom

A tyrant always prepares the country for a new tyrant or chaos: after him scorched earth without institutions, and whoever picks up a rifle becomes the next one. The protocol breaks the pattern because the "right path" stops requiring heroism, enlightenment and political culture from the people — it is enough to be greedy in the right direction. The smart understand it is fair; the stupid understand it is money; they act in sync. Power as a "seat" fought over disappears: the ruler becomes a fifth wheel. This is not a revolution of reason — a revolution of common sense: people wake up and ask "why do we need a ruler if everything works anyway?".

**Three generations.** The first (years 0–20) — the generation of relief: remembers the dictatorship, expects a catch, at a failure shouts "where is the general?"; the strong hand is a necessary evil. The second (20–40) — the generation of shareholders: the protocol is to them like electricity in the socket; the state is a service provider; a call for an "iron hand" sounds like "I'll make your life worse and take your money". The third (40–60) — the generation of the free: the instinct of submission has atrophied; a call for dictatorship is like a call for slavery. Learning happens through the wallet, not the sermon: chaos = the dividend falls; order = it grows. Populist restoration is always possible but runs into immunity: "do you guarantee my dividends won't disappear?" — and its own people eat it.

**A paid option not to be free.** Usually freedom is responsibility. The protocol is the first to offer a paid refusal of freedom — the highest degree of freedom: the right to refuse it. The wish for a strong hand transforms into *not voting*: not voting means trusting that the rules work; refusal is the highest vote of confidence ("the system is so good I need not check it daily"). The architect's clarification: violence in the state remains — for order; but the new politicians will pass fairer laws, courts will be fairer, and that violence will be reasonable and supported — a "contractual Leviathan": the guard in a bank, the bouncer in a club (048d.5).

## 2. Objective audit by the masses

"Designing a good car is a thousand times harder than judging its comfort. But if the quality drops even slightly, the simplest person in the passive majority will see it and be very displeased, though he has not 1 % of the ability to design that car. The passive majority works at 1000 % in control."

This closes the question "won't the active minority seize power". In any other system the elite excuses failure by complexity: "this is macroeconomics, sanctions, be patient" — complexity as a screen. Here the excuse burns: "I don't care how the engine works; the dividend was 1,500, now it's 1,400; the car drives worse; you engineers work badly — get out, we'll hire those who fix it". Passengers need not know how to repair — only to notice the breakdown. The dividend is the Check Engine light; millions of the "passive" are millions of independent auditors; hidden corruption is impossible, because any piece pinched off shows up in the payment. Politics turns from a struggle for power into a struggle for service quality; the elite is hostage to its efficiency. Free-market logic transferred to the state: the state is a product, citizens are consumers, the dividend is the quality rating; a bad product is not bought.

## 3. The elite as a national team, not a caste

The architect's correction to "fear of dismissal": "you forgot the competition of elites; elites are not stable, children of the passive move into the elites; the engineers are not under threat of dismissal — they compete for the right to prove they are smarter and more effective". This changes the model from a "stable system" to a living organism.

- *An arena, not a club.* In a dictatorship the elite is a closed club by connections; here — an arena that the most ambitious from the "passive majority" storm. A bronzed manager loses to one who came from below. The incentive is not fear but sporting zeal: "my algorithm gives 0.5 % more dividend than my neighbour's".
- *Circulation as a fuse against rot.* A closed elite degrades (like any monarchy); one renewed from below stays hungry.
- *The passive are referees, not "passive".* They do not govern — they judge. Elites bring them projects: "choose my strategy — dividends will grow"; the majority chooses the best. From those who are "had" — to the chief customers of the service.
- *The stadium.* "Footballers are an elite, but that doesn't mean they can start playing badly." The scoreboard is the size of the dividend; you cannot say "we played better, the referee was bad". The bench — substitutes from the majority; a star missing the goal is replaced. The players are hired professionals, the stadium belongs to the fans; start wrecking the stadium — you are thrown out of the club. Respect is earned by the quality of play, not by the fact of power. A dictator is a player who captured the referee, bribed the guards and forbade changing the score; here the referee (the algorithm) cannot be bribed, the scoreboard is before everyone's eyes, the spectators can demand a substitution at any moment.

## 4. The dividend as interlingua

"The dividend has one good quality: teams/countries can be compared quickly and easily — numbers are the same in every language."

Dictatorships rest on the Word (propaganda, ideology, myth) — it can be distorted and translated a hundred ways. A number is a mathematical fact: it is not distorted, needs no translation, need not be believed — only received. A global scoreboard where every country posts one figure: no room for "geopolitical greatness" or a "special path"; if country A pays 2,000 and B pays 500, B's citizens do not listen to lectures about enemies at the gate — they ask "why so little here?". The end of national exceptionalism: "if you can't deliver a dividend, you're a bad manager, not the bearer of a unique spirituality". People start taking pride in the system that delivers, not in the country apart from it. Migration of talent and capital to where the "club's salary" is higher; countries compete for citizens as companies for clients; ossified regimes reform or empty out. Propaganda spends billions explaining why it is poor — and everyone understands a number: "if the dictator says 'we are strong' and the scoreboard shows 200, 'strong' looks like a clown". The number is not just money: 1,500 is the marker "this state values its citizen"; 100 is "this state uses its citizen".

## 5. Weak point of the section

Comparability cuts both ways: D depends on the median wage, i.e. on price level and economic structure, not only on the quality of governance; a scoreboard without a purchasing-power adjustment will show a "poor but honest" state worse than a "rich but thieving" one. A second indicator is needed — the share who refuse the dividend (the trust barometer, §13.11) — and it is the pair "D + share of A" that is comparable across countries. 🟡
