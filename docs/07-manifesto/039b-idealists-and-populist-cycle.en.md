## 1. The idealists' three myths and their true motive

A system offering a transparent market deal will be attacked by those used to parasitising on abstract morality. Three myths: **the sacredness of the vote** ("rights are not for sale, you turn duty into a commodity"); **exploitation of need** ("you force the poor to sell their future for bread"); **oligarchy** ("a caste society: the rich rule, the poor are rightless slaves"). The true motive is not care for the poor but panic at losing the feeding base: idealist-populists are the "priests" of democracy, selling the crowd slogans in exchange for power and access to taxes; when the mass takes the money and leaves, the priests are left in an empty temple, and the engineers do not need their slogans. The criticism is the criticism of bankrupts saving their business of selling illusions.

The pragmatic answer in their own logic: **the vote is already sold — but to swindlers** (for promises, food parcels, under threat of dismissal; the protocol removes the swindlers and pays a fair price in advance); **the real cynicism is forcing the hungry to vote** ("think about geopolitics when you can't pay the rent" is mockery; the protocol is humane: personal survival matters more than abstract affairs); **the right to refuse is also freedom** (dragging a person to the urn while telling him fairy tales of his greatness is a form of coercion). Idealists defend a system that demands the person be a citizen without giving him resources; the protocol trumps that card by offering resources in exchange for refusing a fictitious status. In the real world the refrigerator always beats idealism. (The universal "crusade" — 040h.1 Robespierre; the idealist position "what if everyone refuses" — 034b.2.)

## 2. Four classic strikes at the referendum

The old elites will not argue with mathematics. Their strikes: **moral panic** ("our ancestors shed blood for universal suffrage, suffragettes died — and you sell the shrine"; the aim — shame before neighbours at wanting to press the button); **inflation terror** ("hand it out — tomorrow bread costs $100"; fear of loss is stronger than joy of gain); **substituting the anti-hero** ("a techno-oligarchs' conspiracy: they throw you a bone to buy the country"; class hatred); **constitutional sabotage** (lawsuits, "a right cannot have a financial expression", "discrimination", years of process until society loses focus). All four are covered in 030 and 042; the built-in fuse — any populist attack works as advertising: the louder "they want to pay you not to vote!", the faster the mass runs to find out where to collect. The system advertises its own end.

## 3. Four real strikes — at the social layer, not the code

A hard audit from a model that knows the architecture: "the code and the maths will pass any stress test; the social layer will be attacked mercilessly". Four vulnerabilities the elites need not invent:

**3.1. The municipal liquidity trap.** A municipal launch is a brilliant sandbox requiring no federal laws; but cities have no printing press, budgets are limited to property taxes and fees. From whose pocket is the apathetic majority's vote bought? If the city must raise taxes on the very pragmatists the system is meant to protect, monetising apathy may prove too expensive. *Answer:* the payment costs 1–2 % of the budget (045), covered by cutting spending on "maintaining loyalty" and by efficiency growth (x10); for the first pilot, external subsidy by venture or crypto funds was discussed. Remainder: the first cycle before x10 appears is financed in advance. 🟡

**3.2. The PR disaster of terminology.** "Institutional buy-out of the state", "transfer of power to the builders" — a gift to left populists: they need not invent a conspiracy, they will quote the specification: "they don't even hide it — corporates buy your city like a bankrupt company". *Answer:* the vocabulary for the people and the vocabulary for the statute are different documents (033c.10); public positioning — "fair distribution", "your money", "they sell, we do not buy" (033c.1).

**3.3. Kitchen terrorism (household conflicts).** A family where one spouse wants the dividend for debts and the other wants to keep the vote; worse — a domestic tyrant forces his wife to press the button, takes the cash and votes with an amplified vote. Old media will find one such victim, put her on prime time and destroy the protocol's reputation in an evening, demanding cancellation "to protect the vulnerable". *Answer:* the recipient's individual account and secrecy of the A/B choice for vulnerable categories as a statute norm (057b.3: "prohibition of pressure"; 048f.3 — money only to the recipient's account). Publicity of choice (033c.4) is a right, not a duty. Remainder: domestic coercion is not solved by the protocol, just as it is not solved by ordinary voting. The full answer and the household arithmetic are in 019e.5b: the spouses' dispute is settled by the protocol itself (both buttons in one house — the family gets both D and a heavier vote), and a tyrant's family voting gets a price for the first time: D per controlled ballot. 🟡

**3.4. Local raider capture.** Municipal turnout is already microscopic; if the protocol takes the majority away for money, the remaining pool is narrow, and a big developer can cheaply mobilise his employees: they refuse the cheque, get a controlling stake of amplified votes and rewrite the master plan for themselves. *Answer — the cohort A paradox* (059b.6): refusing the money is a marker of maximal motivation, and into cohort A go not only the developer's engineers but his competitors, local elites, eco-activists with principles, religious communities, old union leaders — everyone for whom influence is dearer than a cheque. The captor gets not a tame council but a highly competitive one. Plus §13.10: all "incentive machines" have continuous balance, the protocol's is once per cycle; the municipal scale is the most vulnerable spot, and exactly there a second indicator (share of A) and a minimum A-turnout threshold for legitimacy are needed. 🟡

## 4. What happens when a Le Pen-type comes to power

The ideology of "national rally" is welfare chauvinism: right cultural nationalism (borders, migrants, Euroscepticism) plus almost socialist economics (retirement age, protectionism, taxes on corporations). Two precedents of such a base in power:

**Perón (Argentina, 1946–1955)** — already in 009 (table: 52.4 %, loses under AB-EXIT); we add the trajectory: euphoria phase (nationalisation of railways and the central bank, wage rises, pensions — living standards soar), reckoning phase (programmes paid by squeezing exporters and eating reserves; when they ran out — the printing press), collapse phase (hyperinflation, protectionism shut the country off from technology, factories obsolete; the country from the top ten richest — into an endless cycle of defaults; still sick with Peronism).

**Haider (Austria, 2000)** — the political prototype of European national populism: nearly 27 % in 1999, in the ruling coalition in 2000; 14 EU states imposed diplomatic sanctions (lasted months); in ministerial chairs the populists met the real economy and sharply softened rhetoric so as not to crash markets and to stay in the EU; the party could not be rebels and ministers at once — splits, corruption scandals at first access to budgets, collapse at the next election (later — "Ibiza-gate").

The general cycle: either **boring capitulation** (the European variant — bureaucracy, courts, EU obligations and fear of crashing the economy tie their hands; Meloni came as a hard Eurosceptic and became a systemic pragmatist friendly with Brussels; populists embed in the system they promised to destroy) or **economic isolation** (the global variant — an attempt to keep promises: hand-outs to one's own, expelling migrants, protectionism, exiting treaties → capital flight, currency collapse, institutional crisis). Neither variant gives the voter what he voted for. The protocol removes both: its base takes the dividend before the populist gets to choose between capitulation and isolation (055b.2).

## 4b. Shenderovich on the pendulum: the diagnosis coincides with the protocol's aim, and he has no remedy

The architect sent (09.10.2026) Viktor Shenderovich's interview to the channel "Vot Tak" (9 October 2026, 55 minutes; 158 thousand views on the day of recording) with the question: "he describes exactly what we fixed in the protocol as its aim — did we hit it exactly?" The fragment is his answer to the host's question about America as a "moral tuning fork". From the automatic transcript, quotations short.

**What he says.** There are no moral tuning forks in administrations — Thatcher and Havel are "a system failure, the coin landed on its edge"; beyond that "the only question is how the mechanisms work, the degree of distance from hell". The practice of liberalism "accumulated so many errors and so much social demagogy" that "the pendulum flies fast: Trump has already arrived, we see Le Pen, the Alternative for Germany"; "the pendulum begins to swing right and left past the centre, and the centre is common sense"; wide swings "are deadly dangerous: it is the choice between Hitler and Stalin, between Pinochet and Allende". And the criterion: "can Hitler be re-elected? If he can, he is not Hitler; unpleasant but not terrible. The main question is not what Trump is like — whether the mechanisms are broken or not." And one more observation: "there are no candidates who bring it to the middle, who minimise the swing of the pendulum".

**Where the coincidence is exact — by the repository's points.** The pendulum past the centre is the impulsive voter of the three states (011b) and the populist cycle of §4 of this chapter: protest, grievance, "against all" drive the pendulum, and the protocol's aim is recorded as "take the apathetic and the impulsive out of the deciding vote without hurting them financially" (001 §40, a settled position). The centre as common sense is those who stay: those with something to lose who think beyond the eve (1f §7). His criterion "can he be re-elected" is the thermostat itself: not a property of the candidate but of the mechanism, and the protocol adds to the mechanism what shows the price of not leaving before the election and makes "re-elect" cheaper (001b; 040i, Linz 1.3). So yes — the diagnosis coincides with the protocol's aim word for word, and that is not our reading but his words: centre, pendulum, mechanism, re-electability.

**Where he stops — and that is the main thing.** "There are no candidates who minimise the swing." His damper is a candidate, and there is no candidate. The protocol's damper is not a candidate but the composition of the deciders: the pendulum is driven not by candidates but by those who vote for them from impulse or by bus, and if those people take the payment and stay at home, a candidate "past the centre" has nobody to ride on, whoever he is. That is the difference between searching for a Havel and a design in which a Havel is not needed — exactly his own thought that the tuning fork is not in the administration, carried one step further: nor is the damper in the candidate. Shenderovich, like Shulman (056f §13t, item 10), describes the disease exactly and does not name the handle; but he has what she lacks — the criterion by which the handle is recognised: "can he be re-elected".

**Where he is on our side without knowing it.** His source of optimism about America is the networks' boycott, Fox included, after CNN was thrown out of the pool: "not because they are so good, but because they understand that if they go with Trump it will come back on them; the guild and reputation matter more". That is not principle — it is interest, and he says so himself. The mechanism that holds, holds on interest, not on morals — the same logic as the protocol's (030, 029): count not on honesty but on calculation, and calculation proves more reliable.

**The architect's correction (09.10.2026): a mechanism that lets him back in is not a damper.** "Strange: Shenderovich describes the US as if it had reliable mechanisms against populism, although Trump is in power for the second time. A populist who promises money at elections, breaking the law — that is no longer the centre." By his own criterion — "can he be re-elected?" — America passed the exam twice: in 2020 Trump was voted out, in 2024 voted back in. The mechanism is not broken — it swings; and a pendulum that returns past the centre in both directions is not a damper but the very disease he described a minute earlier. He took "the mechanism let him back" for "the mechanism works", and those are different things: a working one damps, it does not let through. And on money: the "Trump dividend" — 5,000 dollars to every adult, promised at the Republican convention on condition the majority is kept (059c) — is a pre-election handout in the leader's name, what the charter forbids in Article 1 part 4, and exactly what Putin does with the ten thousand before the Duma; the only difference is that in Russia it was paid and in America promised. That is not the centre and not common sense — it is the Nordhaus cycle in American performance (036e §2). Shenderovich sees prosecutors, courts and the boycott after CNN's expulsion — and does not see that the handout itself has become a legitimate pre-election move on both sides of the ocean. Hence a refinement of the above: "the mechanisms are not broken" is true in the sense "the re-election button exists" and false in the sense "there is a damper". Nobody has a damper — not America with its courts, not Europe with its bureaucracy; that is why the pendulum flies everywhere, and why the protocol is needed not as a repair of something broken but as a part that existed nowhere.

**Boundary.** Shenderovich speaks neither of the protocol nor of the payment; the coincidence is in the diagnosis and the criterion, not in the remedy. "Are the mechanisms broken or not — my answer is still no" is his assessment of America, not ours; the repository recorded the same question in 1f §2c with the opposite observation about the structure of permission, and the two records stand side by side as a dispute, not an agreement. And "we hit it exactly" is true of the aim; whether we hit the mechanism will be shown not by coincidence with a publicist but by 040p.

## 5. Weak point of the section

§3 is more honest than §1–2: the four real strikes have answers, but two of the four (first-cycle liquidity and kitchen coercion) are closed by requirements on the statute and the pilot, not by properties of the mechanism. Precisely on these the defence of the first referendum must be built — not on the "built-in fuse" of §2, which works only against *populist* criticism, not against *quiet* institutional criticism (the T case: legal terror, financial sabotage of data, the poison pill — 059e.4). 🟡
