### 23.1. The ballot initiative bypasses the entire elite

Suppose the ENTIRE elite is against AB-EXIT: all politicians (they lose the machine), all parties (they lose control), all consultants (a $26B industry), all unions (they lose influence), all lobbyists (advertising is useless), some lawyers and academics. The whole elite. So what?

A ballot initiative in California (and other direct-democracy states): step 1 — collect signatures from 10 % of registered voters (Stockton: ~20,000). No permission needed from the mayor, the council, a party, a lawyer, ANYONE. Citizens collect signatures on the street with pen and paper. Step 2 — the County Clerk is OBLIGED to verify the signatures (not the content, the authenticity). The signatures are genuine, the signers registered — the referendum is scheduled by law. The mayor cannot block it. Step 3 — the vote; a simple majority = adopted; it becomes part of the city charter. The mayor is obliged to implement it.

### 23.2. Four ways to stop it — and why none works

**The court.** Who sues? A citizen — no standing (no harm done to him; he is being OFFERED money). A party — no constitutional right to a machine. A union — no constitutional right to electoral influence through mobilisation. The mayor — the executor of the people's will, and the people have decided. Probability of a temporary block: 20–30 %. Appeal → Supreme Court → 6–3 for AB-EXIT (the conservative majority will back it as individual liberty).

**A counter-referendum.** You need to collect another 20,000 signatures and persuade people to vote to CANCEL $780. The campaign: "Vote to take your $780 away!" Nobody in history has won a campaign of "we'll take your money". Probability: ~0 %.

**Sabotage of implementation.** The mayor cites "technical difficulties", "no money in the budget". 46,000 motivated voters: "Where is our $780? The referendum passed. The law is in force." The court: "Mayor, implement or face contempt of court." A one- to two-year delay is possible; then the court compels.

**Change the ballot-initiative law.** The state legislature bans initiatives on electoral payments. In California = political suicide: Californians adore ballot initiatives; Prop 13 (1978) = sacred. Probability: ~1 %.

Overall probability of a complete block: under 5 %. And even with a block in one city — 19,502 municipalities in the US. Blocking all of them = impossible. One is enough.
