### 63.1. The doctrine this section examines

American law blocks AB-EXIT through the doctrine of unconstitutional conditions (Koontz v. St. Johns): the government cannot condition the grant of a benefit (money) on the surrender of a constitutional right (the vote). The justification is paternalistic: the "voluntariness" of a poor man who needs money is declared illusory — the poor man supposedly agrees under the pressure of need, so the exchange is unfree.

This section shows that the doctrine is applied SELECTIVELY and HYPOCRITICALLY — through two analogies and an analysis of the only dodge the court has left.

### 63.2. First blow: the sacredness of the vote that devalued the vote

The vote is declared sacred and inalienable — so much so that no compensation may be received for it. The practical result of this "sacredness": — half of citizens do not go to vote; — "politics" has become a synonym for "the dirty business of liars"; — everyone knows this and can change nothing.

Sacredness did not protect the vote — it devalued it. By declaring the vote so holy that it cannot be disposed of, the system brought things to the point where it is simply not used — for free, silently, en masse. The "sacred" vote turned into a scrap of paper that half throw away without looking.

What is declared priceless is in practice worth zero. The poor man now either gives his vote away for free (does not go) or gives it to a liar (votes for a manipulator). Both outcomes are worse than honestly taking compensation or voting with undiluted weight. AB-EXIT does not make the vote cynical — it shows that the vote is ALREADY devalued, and offers to give it back a price.

An additional asymmetry: the vote is "inalienable" when the CITIZEN wants to dispose of it, but quite alienable when the STATE wants to take it away. The USA disenfranchises about 4 million people through felony disenfranchisement — without any compensation. Sacredness switches on selectively: against the citizen, not against the state.

### 63.3. Second blow: selective protection (prostitution, casino, lottery)

The doctrine "the poor man cannot voluntarily decide about the sacred under the pressure of need" is tested on other transactions where the poor man decides under the same pressure:

**Prostitution.** At stake is the body, sexual integrity — what most traditions consider more sacred than the vote. A poor woman "agrees" under the pressure of need — exactly the "illusion of voluntariness" by the court's logic. And yet dozens of jurisdictions (the Netherlands, Germany, Nevada, New Zealand) have legalised it, recognising an adult's right to dispose even of the body.

**Casino and lottery.** A mathematically guaranteed loss: the chance of winning is a few per cent, the house is always ahead. The poor man goes there under the pressure of the same need with a near-zero chance. The state not merely permits — it itself RUNS lotteries and taxes casinos, i.e. it PROFITS from the poor man's loss.

Comparison: — Prostitution: the poor man gives up the body (more sacred than the vote) — legal. — Casino/lottery: the poor man gives up money with a guaranteed loss — legal, the state profits. — AB-EXIT: the poor man RECEIVES money for what he did for free anyway (not voting) — a crime.

In prostitution and the casino the poor man LOSES (body, money). In AB-EXIT the poor man GAINS. The law permits transactions where the poor man is in the red and forbids the transaction where the poor man is in the black — under the flag of "protecting the poor".

Conclusion: the doctrine of "the illusion of voluntariness" is applied selectively. When the poor man loses (casino) — his voluntariness is recognised. When the poor man wins (AB-EXIT) — it is declared illusory. Protection switches on exactly when the poor man would be better off. This is not protection — it is the codification of the poor man's second-class status as a decision-making subject: the rich are permitted to waive rights for money everywhere (NDAs, arbitration clauses, sale of image rights); the poor, on the question of the vote — no.

### 63.4. The court's only dodge and three answers to it

The court will not be able to repel the analogy along the line of "voluntariness" or "sacredness". One distinction will remain: prostitution and the casino affect only the person himself (his body, his money), while the vote is participation in governing the COMMON, and refusal affects others. The logic: "you lost — you suffered; you didn't vote for money — you changed the composition of the electorate, and that harms the collective".

**Answer 1.** Prostitution and the casino ALSO affect others. The gambling addict ruins the family, the children go hungry — direct harm to third parties. Prostitution affects public health and families. The state tolerates this. So "impact on others" is not an absolute prohibition — the state permits vices harming others all the time.

**Answer 2.** AB-EXIT affects others LESS than the status quo. Now the poor man either does not vote (the same effect on the electorate, but for free) or votes under a liar's manipulation (harms the common decision by choosing the worse one). AB-EXIT creates no new harm to the collective — the same person does not participate in the same way, only consciously and with compensation, and those who remain vote undiluted. The effect on "the common" is either the same or better.

**Answer 3.** The casino and the lottery take from the poor man money he could have spent on participating in society, and give it to the state/corporation — the confiscation of his "share in the common" in favour of others. The state profits from it. Where is the protection of the collective here?

### 63.5. Distinguishing Tammany Hall and AB-EXIT (why the law is overbroad)

The law "one may not pay for non-participation" (the boilerplate "vote or refrain from voting" in all 50 states) was written against a specific evil:

Tammany Hall / Jim Crow: a PRIVATE actor (the party machine, the boss) SECRETLY pays the opponent's voters to stay home in order to STEAL the result from the majority. The victim is the people. Fraud against the will of the people.

AB-EXIT: the STATE pays OPENLY, by the decision of the majority ITSELF at a referendum. No private manipulator, no secrecy, no stolen result, no victim. The execution of the will of the people.

These are opposite phenomena under one wording. The law suffers from overbreadth: written against private secret theft of the will, it accidentally catches the open decision of the people about itself. The argument in court is not "break the law" but "narrow an overbroad norm so that it does not capture what it was not aimed at".

The counterargument (the majority may vote to suppress a minority by cutting the poor off from the ballot box for money) is removed by the mechanics of AB-EXIT: the poor man chooses himself, can vote with undiluted weight, nobody decides for him; the option is symmetrical for all, not aimed at a group.

### 63.6. Purpose of the section

This is not proof that AB-EXIT is legal today. An unjust law operates until a court narrows it or pressure changes it. This is an arsenal for three audiences: — for the court: the overbreadth argument + the selectivity of the doctrine + unequal treatment of poor and rich; — for the press: a headline understood in three seconds ("the state: you may sell your body, you may lose at the lottery, take money for non-participation — prison"); — for the academic opponent (Yudin): a demonstration that "the sacredness of the vote" is an ideological screen under which the poor man remains both without money and without influence.

Two blows strike one point: the system covers with beautiful words (sacredness, protection of the poor) what in fact leaves the poor man powerless. AB-EXIT removes the screen.

### 64.1. The fear and its empirical test

The natural fear: the system will crush a radical idea and its bearer. A test on real cases of the last decade does NOT confirm this fear. Over 10 years it was secret corruptors who were crushed, not open bearers of new ideas.

### 64.2. Three cases

**Andrew Yang (2020) — a direct analogue.** An outsider, zero political experience, the idea of the "Freedom Dividend" — $1,000/month to everyone. He ran into EXACTLY AB-EXIT's accusation: when he announced he would pick people for the payments, questions arose "isn't he buying votes" — but at the same time this created viral noise and helped him gather a base of supporters. Outcome: did NOT win the primaries, BUT went from an unknown businessman to a national figure, outlasted senators and governors, raised $16M+ in a quarter (more than anyone except the top 4), his UBI idea went from marginal to mainstream, he launched a mayoral campaign and a party. The worst outcome — "didn't win the race but became famous and advanced the idea".

**Zohran Mamdani (2025) — the radical who won.** A 34-year-old socialist outsider, ideas called by the Washington Post "perhaps the most radical": a rent freeze, free buses, municipal grocery stores, a tax on billionaires. President Trump himself called him a "communist" and threatened to cut off funding and deport him. Outcome: WON the mayoralty of New York with the record turnout in 50+ years (over 2 million). The attack from above did not kill — it made him a banner of resistance.

**Ranked Choice Voting — radical mechanics adopted from below.** Started with small towns, pushed through to the state level (Maine, Alaska). Proves: even a change of the electoral mechanism itself is passable bottom-up if it does not violate "one person — one vote".

### 64.3. The regularity

No radical idea destroyed anyone. The worst outcome (Yang) — a rise from nothing + promotion of the idea. The best (Mamdani) — a victory with record turnout. The accusation of "vote-buying" has already been tested by Yang and did not kill him — it coexisted with viral growth. An attack from above strengthens (Mamdani against Trump) — the mechanism "persecution makes a martyr" works.

Conclusion: the system does not destroy radical idea-driven outsiders — it either elects them or makes them famous. It destroys secret corruptors (Thailand, Spain — section 62), not open bearers of new ideas.

### 64.4. Three differences between AB-EXIT and these cases (honestly)

First: Yang, Mamdani, Sanders proposed to HAND OUT / redistribute — popular and legal. AB-EXIT runs into a criminal statute (section 63). Therefore the path is a symbolic referendum + a change of the law, not direct implementation.

Second: their ideas are understood instantly ("I'll give money", "I'll freeze rent"). AB-EXIT requires one step of explanation. The barrier to understanding is slightly higher — but Yang proved that even a complex idea (UBI through automation) can be conveyed.

Third: they were living bearers with a face and a biography. AB-EXIT so far is an idea without a face. The cases show: a radical idea takes off with a HUMAN bearer. One needs one's own Yang — an underdog face.

### 64.5. Why AB-EXIT is stronger than UBI: an idea without losers versus an idea with losers

The key structural superiority over the Yang precedent.

UBI creates two classes with opposite interests: net recipients (the poor) — FOR; net donors (the working, the middle class) pay more than they receive — AGAINST. The active working voter sees "they'll hand out to everyone from my taxes, I'm in the red" and becomes an OPPONENT. And that is exactly the person who goes to the polls. Yang alienated the most electorally active layer — hence "became famous but did not win".

AB-EXIT creates no losers. Everyone is in the black in their own currency: whoever values money above the vote takes the dividend (plus); whoever values the vote above money stays to vote, and his vote is not diluted by those who left — influence GROWS (plus), and free of charge for him, at the expense of others' voluntary exit.

Decisive: with Yang the active voter got ONLY a minus (his taxes handed out). With AB-EXIT the active voter gets a PLUS (strengthened vote). AB-EXIT switches a potential opponent into a supporter — and this is the most influential, loud, organised layer (canvassers, bloggers, debaters — cf. Chichvarkin, section 60). UBI makes them canvassers AGAINST; AB-EXIT — canvassers FOR. The effect is not arithmetic but a change of sign in the layer that defines the discourse.

Deeper: UBI asks the working man to GIVE (taxes). AB-EXIT offers him to RECEIVE a strengthened vote — what he was deprived of (section 56, the invisible working man). This is a targeted closing of his main pain.

### 64.6. Two honest caveats on communication

First: "everyone in the black" is true by structure, but the active voter must UNDERSTAND that the strengthening of the vote is his plus. The poor man will understand his benefit instantly (money), the active one — one step later (vote weight). If the explanation does not get through, the active voter will by inertia think "handing out from my taxes again" and confuse AB-EXIT with UBI. The risk is in communication, not in structure.

Second: the source of the dividend must not be perceived as "a tax on the working to pay those who left" — otherwise the active voter returns to the position of donor-opponent. Linking it to eliminated corruption and savings from better governance (section 59) removes this: the dividend comes from returned stolen money, not from the working man's pocket. Get it across — the active voter is in the black twice (vote weight + it is not he who pays). Fail — he will suspect UBI.

### 64.7. Conclusion

The history of the last 10 years is on the project's side more strongly than intuition expects. Radical idea-driven outsiders are not destroyed — they win (Mamdani) or become famous and advance the idea (Yang). The accusation of "vote-buying" was tested by Yang and did not kill. An attack from above strengthens (Mamdani). What is needed is a human bearer and a legal form (a symbolic referendum). The fear itself that "the radical will be crushed" is not empirically confirmed: over 10 years it was secret corruptors who were crushed, not open bearers of new ideas. And AB-EXIT is stronger than all the money precedents, because it is an idea without losers, whereas UBI makes the active electoral layer an opponent.

### 65.1. The main objection this section answers

"AB-EXIT is another money handout, like Yang's UBI." This is the first thing anyone will think on hearing about a dividend. The section shows: structurally AB-EXIT is the opposite of UBI, and that is exactly why it is stronger as an idea and as a political bet.

### 65.2. The structural flaw of UBI: an idea with losers

UBI (Yang's Freedom Dividend, $1,000/month) divides society into two classes with opposite interests: — net recipients (the poor, the non-working): receive more than they pay in taxes → FOR; — net donors (the working, the middle class, the successful): pay more than they get back → AGAINST.

The active working voter looks at UBI and sees: "they'll hand out to everyone from my taxes, I'm in the red." He becomes an OPPONENT. And that is exactly the one who goes to the polls, canvasses, blogs, shapes opinion. UBI repels the most electorally active layer. That is why Yang "became famous but did not win": those who actually vote lose in his idea. UBI is a zero-sum game between the poor and the working, and the working resist.

### 65.3. The structural superiority of AB-EXIT: an idea without losers

AB-EXIT creates no losers. Everyone is in the black in their own currency: — whoever values money above the vote (the poor, the apathetic, the busy) takes the dividend → plus; — whoever values the vote above money (the active, the working, the idea-driven) stays to vote, and his vote is not diluted by those who left → influence GROWS → plus. And free of charge for him, at the expense of others' voluntary exit.

The decisive difference: with Yang the active voter got ONLY a minus (his taxes handed out). With AB-EXIT the active voter gets a PLUS (strengthened vote). AB-EXIT turns a potential opponent into a supporter — and this is the most influential, loud, organised layer. UBI makes these people canvassers AGAINST; AB-EXIT — canvassers FOR. The effect is not arithmetic (+1 instead of −1) but a change of sign in the layer that defines the discourse (cf. Chichvarkin, section 60: he will not merely vote — he will canvass).

Deeper: UBI asks the working man to GIVE (taxes). AB-EXIT offers him to RECEIVE a strengthened vote — what he was deprived of (section 56, the invisible working man whose vote is diluted). This is not a neutral offer but a targeted closing of his main pain.

### 65.4. The logic of vote strengthening is elementary (a correction of false caution)

There is a temptation to say: "the active voter has to work out that others' exit strengthens his vote — that's an extra logical step, a barrier to understanding." This is false caution and an underestimation of the person.

The logic is trivial, primary-school level: two people vote — one took the money and left — the one remaining decides 100 % and wins. Fewer voters → each remaining vote is more decisive. Anyone who has ever divided something among a smaller number of people understands this. No "college-level reasoning" is required.

This matters methodologically: the systematic underestimation of an ordinary person's ability to work out a simple truth is a built-in error of caution. All of AB-EXIT is built on the opposite bet: that people WILL UNDERSTAND if allowed to think it through. And "my vote weighs more when some have left" is exactly the case where there is almost nothing to think through — everything is visible at once.

### 65.5. A direct answer to "this is another money handout"

No. A handout (UBI) takes from the working man and gives to the non-working — it creates a donor-loser. AB-EXIT does not take from the working man: whoever stays to vote does not pay for someone else's dividend from his own pocket (the source is eliminated corruption and savings from better governance, section 59), but receives a strengthened vote. The working man in AB-EXIT is in the black twice: his vote is weightier AND it is not he who pays. In UBI he is in the red. This is not "another handout" — it is a structurally opposite arrangement of incentives.

A caveat on communication: this superiority must be CONVEYED. If the active voter decides the dividend is paid from his taxes (as in UBI), he returns to the position of donor-opponent. Therefore it is critical that the source of the dividend be perceived as returned stolen money, not a tax on the working. Convey it — the active voter is an ally. Fail — he will suspect UBI. The risk is in communication, not in structure.

### 65.6. Conclusion

UBI creates a class of losers (working donors) and makes the most active electoral layer an opponent — hence "Yang became famous but did not win". AB-EXIT creates no losers: the active voter receives a strengthening of the vote, not a bill to pay, and the logic of this strengthening is elementary. AB-EXIT switches the most influential layer from "against" to "for", turning probable critics into canvassers. Therefore where UBI runs into the resistance of voting donors, AB-EXIT can actually win — it is the only money idea without built-in losers.

### 66.1. Thought experiment

Chichvarkin (section 60) said: the real Thatcher comes only AFTER the country hits the wall — after the crisis, when the bitter medicine has become inevitable. Let us test: what chance does Thatcher have today without AB-EXIT, what chance with it, and what do Chichvarkin and people like him do in each case.

### 66.2. Thatcher today WITHOUT AB-EXIT: marginal before the crisis

A low chance. Her programme (cut spending, strangle the handout, deregulate) is pure bitter medicine without immediate sweetness. The electorate is a majority of handout recipients to whom "the state owes" (Chichvarkin's words). They vote for whoever promises to GIVE, not to take away.

Thatcher offers pain now for benefit later. The populist offers benefit now (the handout), hiding the pain beyond the horizon. The majority voter discounts the future and grabs the sweet now — therefore the populist wins. A real Thatcher of 2026 without a crisis hovers at 10–15 %, like any strict reformer in well-fed times. Her time is only after the wall, and she knows it. A marginal chance until the crisis strikes.

### 66.3. Thatcher WITH an AB-EXIT referendum: arrival BEFORE the wall

Changes fundamentally, because AB-EXIT solves exactly the problem that sinks her.

Her problem is the majority electorate of recipients. AB-EXIT restructures it: the apathetic recipients take the dividend and exit, while the counting, the working, the burned — those who understand the value of bitter medicine — remain with an undiluted vote. Thatcher for the first time receives an electorate able to appreciate a long-term benefit, not only the sweet now.

And the main thing — she does not need to wait for the wall. Now the argument "be patient, it'll get better later" loses to "I'll give now". With AB-EXIT she gains an IMMEDIATE sweetness next to the bitter medicine: "the choice — money on your card now OR a real strengthened vote; and at the same time I'll heal the economy." She is no longer "only pain" — she is "pain for the system, but benefit to you personally at once". This removes her main electoral shortcoming.

AB-EXIT for Thatcher is a way to arrive BEFORE the wall, not after: not to wait until the country crashes for people to accept the bitter medicine, but to give a direct incentive to support the reformer in advance. Plus AB-EXIT itself is her differentiator (section 64) against a background of dull competitors. The chance is radically higher.
