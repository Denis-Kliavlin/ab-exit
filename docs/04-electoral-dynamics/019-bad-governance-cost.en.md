### 59.1. Why count layer by layer

A common mistake (easy to make out of a wish to look cautious) is to name the losses from corruption with a "decent" understated figure like 15 % of GDP. The reality is several times larger, and it is visible only in a layer-by-layer breakdown. Each layer is real and measured separately; together they give an honest picture.

### 59.2. Five layers of losses

**Layer 1. Direct corruption (bribes, kickbacks, embezzlement).** The UN and the World Bank estimate global corruption at ~5 % of world GDP. But that is a planetary average, including Denmark and Singapore. For countries with problematic governance (Argentina, Ukraine, Russia) the estimates of direct corruption are 10–15 % of GDP.

**Layer 2. Inefficient public procurement.** The state systematically buys above market: a road for $1M from a private party = $3M from the state. Often not theft but the absence of efficiency pressure. The OECD estimates losses in public procurement at 10–30 % of its volume; public procurement = 12–20 % of GDP. Arithmetic: 20 % × 20 % = 4 % of GDP on procurement alone.

**Layer 3. Insane construction and ghost projects.** California High-Speed Rail: $128 billion, 0 km. Airports, bridges, stadiums to nowhere — "white elephants". In Argentina — the unfinished state enterprises of the Perón era. One-off write-offs of tens of billions; spread over the years — a further few per cent of GDP permanently.

**Layer 4. Bankrupt cities.** Detroit ($18–20 billion of debt). Stockton, San Bernardino, Vallejo in California. Puerto Rico ($70 billion default). Not "inefficiency in the margins" — the complete collapse of the governance of territories. Every bankrupt = decades of lost growth for millions.

**Layer 5. Forgone growth — the largest and invisible.** This is not stolen money but money that was never born because of bad governance. Argentina 1900 = richer than France. Argentina 2025 = poorer several times over. The difference was not stolen at once — a country that should have grown at 3.5 % for a century grew at 1.5 % for a century. Compound interest over 100 years: the difference between 3.5 % and 1.5 % = a ~7-fold difference in final GDP. A well-governed Argentina would be 5–7 times richer. Bad governance "ate" not 15 % — it ate most of the entire economy that could have existed.

### 59.3. The honest figure — separating two questions

Two different measurements must be distinguished:

**Current annual losses** (corruption + procurement inefficiency + ghosts + bankrupts): for a badly governed country realistically 20–30 % of GDP every year. Not 15 %.

**Accumulated forgone potential** (layer 5): this is not a percentage of current GDP, it is a multiple. A well-governed Argentina = 300–500 % of the current one. Likewise for Russia, Ukraine.

### 59.4. Estimating the AB-EXIT effect — arithmetic

If current losses = 25 % of GDP a year, and AB-EXIT over the transition period (10 years) closes half (12.5 %), spread over 10 years = +1.25 % of additional growth a year from eliminating current losses alone.

Plus the launch of compound interest: as soon as governance becomes efficient, the previously forgone growth begins. In a country with a large gap (Argentina) +3–5 % of additional growth in the transition period is realistic.

Conclusion: +2 % of additional growth is a conservative LOWER bound, not an optimistic ceiling. For countries with a large historical gap the potential is higher.

### 59.5. Three disciplining caveats (rigour, not censorship)

For the figure to be defensible before economists (for example, of the Austrian school), three conditions:

First: this is a LEVEL effect, not a perpetual rate (Solow). The release is one-off, stretched over a 5–10-year transition period, after which the rate returns to the norm of a developed economy. Not +3 % every year forever.

Second: this is the removal of an INTERNAL brake. AB-EXIT removes the stupidity and corruption of governance, but does not control external factors (a world crisis, commodity prices, demography, war). Correctly: "removes the main internal brake", not "guarantees growth".

Third: the state does not reach 100 % of business efficiency. It has functions business does not have (defence, justice, care for the insolvent). It can approach the efficiency of a pizzeria but not equal it. The release is there, but not complete.

With these caveats the formulation changes from "AB-EXIT will give +3–5 %" (a populist's promise) to "AB-EXIT can release +3–5 % provided it brings governance close to business efficiency" (a defensible estimate). The difference is one word — but it is the difference between science and marketing.

### 59.6. The anti-Peronist irony of the honest estimate

Here is an irony a sceptical economist would appreciate: AB-EXIT promises REALISTICALLY ("can, provided, potential, level effect"), whereas populism promises WITH A GUARANTEE ("I'll give everyone everything now"). The very honesty of an estimate with caveats is an anti-populist marker. Perón promised the moon. AB-EXIT shows a calculation with three caveats. Those who built their position on "I tell the bitter truth while others lie" will recognise their own style in this discipline.

### 59.7. The pizzeria analogy — why the gap is real

The state–business efficiency gap is not theoretical. The CEO of a pizzeria for embezzling $200M = 20 years in prison; a city mayor for the same $200M = often nothing. Papa John's run with the efficiency of a bankrupt city = a corpse in 5 years. A corporation under the pressure of the market, audit, shareholders, competition is structurally more efficient than a monopoly state without those pressures.

AB-EXIT does not turn the state into a pizzeria (the state has a different function). But it adds to the state the pressures it lacks: the dashboard = audit and transparency, the motivated electorate = shareholders demanding results, the dividend tied to the median = an efficiency metric, a third party = competition. Each pressure narrows the gap. The sum of the pressures = the release of those very 20–30 % that are now lost.

That is exactly why +2 % is a floor, not a ceiling. If all five pressures work, the state moves towards business efficiency, and the release approaches the upper bound of the calculation.

### 60.1. The case and its diagnosis

Yevgeny Chichvarkin (libertarian, London, a wine business in Mayfair) in a 2026 interview describes the flight of the productive class from tax predation. Specifics: in a year up to 16,500 millionaires left Britain (Dubai, Zurich, Milan, Cyprus) after the abolition of the non-dom regime and the introduction of a tax of up to 45 % on worldwide income. The result — a collapse of the luxury market, real estate, high-end restaurants.

His mechanism of the problem is sections 56, 58, 59 on the top floor: a left government raises taxes → the productive leave → the base shrinks → less is collected (he refers directly to the Laffer curve: "with the tax rise, less tax started to be collected") → politicians do not understand, because they "deny mathematics and human nature" → they drive it to the wall (Detroit, strikes) → only then "the next Thatcher". A pendulum with no exit — the Argentine loop (section 58) in a British performance.

His observation on electoral dynamics coincides verbatim with section 53/56: "people understand the rhetoric that the state owes them — pensions rising, welfare; that's why the left is popular all over the world". The majority votes for the handout; the minority that pays loses the vote.

### 60.2. The key correction: "against all" is the cry of the burned, not the yawn of the apathetic

The superficial reading: Chichvarkin, asked "who to vote for", answers "against all, they're all disgusting" — so he is apathetic, has left the system. That is wrong.

He lists everyone by name and explains why each is bad: the right populists — no; the left — no; the Tories who copied the left's agenda — no; the Greens — "a psychiatrist is needed there"; Farage — "too much of a populist". This is not the apathy of "I don't care, one of them will buy a Bentley" (as the non-voter in Katz's video said). This is the position of a person who WANTS to vote but has nobody to vote for.

The distinction is fundamental. The apathetic does not participate because he is indifferent. The burned (section 53) does not participate because there is nobody worthy on the ballot — only populists from different sides, not a single clever one. Hayek once taught in London; now, according to Chichvarkin, there are no libertarians in politics at all. He is motivated, disappointed, learned-helpless — but not indifferent.

### 60.3. Why the clever are not on the ballot — and how AB-EXIT brings them back

A clever candidate is now electorally unviable. The electorate is a majority of handout recipients (by Chichvarkin himself: "the rhetoric that the state owes is understood by the majority"). A clever man saying "we'll reduce the handout, heal the economy" loses to the hander-out. Therefore the clever do not go into politics — the game is a losing one. Populists from both sides remain.

AB-EXIT inverts this mathematics. The handout recipients take the dividend and leave; the motivated counting ones receive a vote ×3. The clever man's audience (the counting, the productive, the burned) now weighs three times as much; the populist's audience (the handout-oriented) has left for the money. The clever candidate is viable for the first time. Demand arises — supply appears. The clever go into politics because the game is for the first time a winning one.

The autocratic variant of this same chain is in 056 (2026 amendment): there it is not the preference group that leaves but the compulsion group, and the demoralised comes not because a worthy one appeared on the ballot but because his vote stopped drowning.

And then Chichvarkin has someone to vote for. His "against all" disappears not from resignation but because at last there is a worthy one on the ballot. The burned returns — exactly the mechanism of section 53.

Chichvarkin will not take the dividend — that money is laughable to him. He will choose the vote ×3. And he will not merely vote — he is one of those who CANVASS. He started a YouTube channel, walks around Mayfair explaining Laffer, argues, persuades, gathers security and sets the business's politics. He is a born canvasser.

### 60.5. The chain "the vote retains capital"

An important clarification of the boundary. Chichvarkin splits into voter and capital. As a voter he will return for the vote ×3 and will canvass. But his MONEY is mobile: at a 45 % tax the capital will leave for Dubai even if the vote returns to London.

The superficial conclusion: "AB-EXIT is powerless against mobile capital". That is incomplete. More precisely — AB-EXIT retains capital not directly but indirectly, through the full chain:

AB-EXIT returns Chichvarkin-the-voter (vote ×3 + worthy candidates) → he votes for the clever one and canvasses for him for free → the clever politician gets power and reduces tax predation → Chichvarkin-the-capital stays, because the reason for flight has disappeared.

The vote pulls the money after it. A person is first a citizen, then a wallet. Without AB-EXIT the first link breaks — and then both the vote (into "against all") and the capital (into Dubai) leave. With AB-EXIT the whole link is restored: the returned voter fixes the reason the capital fled.

This corrects the naive formulation "the mechanism doesn't work for the mobile elite". It works — but mediated: it does not hold the money by force, but returns to its owner the tool to fix what drives the money out. Chichvarkin-the-voter saves Chichvarkin-the-capital.

Chichvarkin confirms the diagnosis of sections 56, 58, 59 on the top floor of society (millionaires, not Michael with a €300 deficit): the tax state squeezes out the productive, the majority votes for the handout, a pendulum with no exit, insight only through the wall.

### 61.1. Why this section

An honest position requires a survey of alternatives. The claim "AB-EXIT is the best idea" is unprovable and scares off a serious reader. The claim "AB-EXIT is the only idea with a built-in path to adoption" is defensible and verifiable. This section gives competing ideas their due, examines them by one criterion (feasibility, not beauty), and positions AB-EXIT not as the cleverest design but as a rare case of a reform carrying its own door.

### 61.2. Six main ideas and their common flaw

**Deliberative democracy (Habermas, Fishkin).** Citizens' assemblies selected by lot discuss a problem and give recommendations. Tested: Ireland (abortion), France (climate). Flaw: the recommendations are advisory. The French climate assembly gave proposals — Macron took some, threw out the rest. No teeth, no binding force.

**Sortition / lottocracy (Van Reybrouck, "Against Elections").** Replace elections with the lot, like juries: random citizens govern in turn. The adoption flaw is fatal: who will vote to abolish their own right to vote in favour of the lot? Sitting politicians — never. No path from point A (now) to point B (lottocracy). Van Reybrouck honestly does not answer HOW to introduce it.

**Quadratic voting (Weyl, Posner, "Radical Markets").** You buy votes at a quadratic price, express intensity of preference. Flaw: it requires society to accept "buying votes" (intuitively repugnant to the majority) and to rewrite the whole electoral infrastructure. Where is the referendum at which this is adopted? No path.

**Liquid democracy.** You delegate your vote to someone you trust on a topic, can recall it. Flaw: works in experiments (the Pirate Party), but at scale leads to the concentration of delegations in a few "super-voters" — i.e. the same oligarchy, plus no adoption mechanism at the national level.

**Compulsory voting (Australia).** A fine for non-attendance, turnout near 90 %. Flaw: the only truly implementable one (Australia has proved it over 100 years), but it does NOT solve the problem — it drives the apathetic to the polls by force, where they vote at random or for the loudest populist. It strengthens populism rather than curing it. Feasible but counterproductive.

**Civic education (Yudin's position, LSE).** Awaken the civic, grow involvement, lower the voting age to 16. Flaw: no mechanism, and it works over generations. The post-democratic critique has proclaimed this goal since 2004 (Crouch) and 2008 (Wolin) — over that time turnout fell, inequality and populism grew. An accurate diagnosis without a tool.

### 61.3. The regularity: vision without a door

All these ideas have a VISION but no DOOR. They describe a better room but do not show how to get there from the current room when the door is guarded by those for whom the transition is disadvantageous. This is a structural flaw of the whole field: democratic reform is proposed by those who do not control the mechanism of its adoption, to those who control it and are not interested.

The field is skewed towards diagnosis. Diagnosticians of the crisis are many (Crouch, Wolin, Yudin, Brzezinski, dozens of books). Constructive proposals with a path to adoption — almost none.

### 61.4. How AB-EXIT differs

AB-EXIT carries its own door and its own lock:

The door (adoption mechanism): a referendum that everyone will support, because everyone sees a personal benefit — money or a vote weight ×3. Unlike sortition (nobody will give up their vote to the lot) and QV (nobody will accept "buying votes"), AB-EXIT asks nobody to sacrifice — it gives to everyone. Therefore it passes the vote that the other ideas cannot pass.

The lock (irreversibility mechanism, section 58): after adoption the filtered electorate does not vote to cancel its own benefit; the self-tightening noose makes legal repeal impossible.

This does not make AB-EXIT cleverer or fairer than deliberation or sortition. It makes it the only one that can be brought to a real test.

### 61.5. The honest boundary of the claim

Three disciplining caveats, without which this would be propaganda:

First: "nothing better found" does not equal "nothing better exists". Perhaps someone unexamined has a better idea, or one will appear tomorrow. The absence of a found counterexample is not proof of its absence.

Second: AB-EXIT wins on feasibility but is NOT tested on effectiveness. Deliberation has been tried in Ireland, compulsory voting in Australia; their effect is known. AB-EXIT has been launched nowhere. Its superiority is on paper, like the others'. The only difference is that it has a plausible path to a pilot, so it can be TESTED — not that it is proven to work.

Third: the echo-chamber risk. This document builds AB-EXIT across dozens of sections; naturally it wins in its own analysis. A defender of sortition could tear AB-EXIT apart with arguments not anticipated here as easily as sortition is taken apart here. Therefore the claim is tested not by a friendly court but by a hostile one — and the hostile critic (Yudin) is invaluable.

### 61.6. The precise formulation of the position

Not "AB-EXIT is the best idea for improving democracy".

But: "AB-EXIT is the only known idea of democratic reform that carries within itself its own mechanism of adoption and irreversibility. The others describe a desired state but give no feasible path to it when the transition is disadvantageous to the sitting power. Therefore AB-EXIT is not proven best, but the only one feasible enough to be tested in practice at all."

The project's niche is not "I am cleverer than everyone" but "I am in the rare part of the field where the disease is not described but an operable treatment with a real path to the operating theatre is proposed". Occupying this niche honestly is stronger than claiming absolute superiority.
