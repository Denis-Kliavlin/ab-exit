## 17.1. Purpose of the section

After AB-EXIT is introduced, part of the voters exit with the dividend. Who stays to vote? This section gives a **structural portrait** of the remaining electorate. Without this portrait it is impossible to understand how the political dynamics change, which politicians gain a competitive advantage, which messages work.

§16 gives the demographic structure in the current system. §17 — the structure of the **new electorate** after AB-EXIT.

## 17.2. Empirical base — Norway, Switzerland, Scandinavia

AB-EXIT is not simply lowered turnout. It is the structural exit of the apathetic for a dividend. The composition of those who remain differs qualitatively from the composition of the active electorate in a country with 50 % turnout and no AB-EXIT.

The nearest empirical analogue is countries with a high structural maturity of the electorate: Norway, Switzerland, Finland, Denmark. In these countries citizens take part in politics from **rational or principled** motives, not emotional or coerced ones. The composition of these electorates is an approximate model of what we get in the USA/Germany/Moldova/Russia after AB-EXIT.

## 17.3. Pragmatists (25–30 % of the current electorate)

Accountants, engineers, owners of small and medium businesses, MBAs, managers, doctors in private practice, lawyers, IT specialists, taxpayers from the upper half of the income distribution.

**Why they refuse the dividend:**

For a pragmatist $300/year is **less than the damage from bad governance**. The pragmatist-accountant understands: if the country has a fiscal crisis, inflation will eat far more than $300 out of his savings. The pragmatist-engineer: if the government is incapable of long-term planning, infrastructure degrades, jobs leave. All these costs exceed $300/year many times over.

Pragmatists make a **rational calculation**: the dividend is less than the expected damage from bad governance → voting pays better.

**How they differ from today's active electorate:**

Today's active voter is often motivated **emotionally** — by loyalty to a party, hatred of opponents, cultural identities. The pragmatist after AB-EXIT is motivated **economically and functionally**. He votes for whoever is able to **govern**.

This is a fundamental shift. Modern electoral communication runs on emotional triggers. Pragmatists need **different communication** — programmatic, analytical, oriented on concrete governance indicators.

## 17.4. Ideologues (10–15 % of the current electorate)

Principled citizens with a system of values: defenders of civil rights, eco-activists, believers, libertarians, feminists, union activists, academics in the humanities, artists with a civic position.

**Why they refuse the dividend:**

For an ideologue certain principles have no monetary equivalent. An animal-rights defender will not take $300 for giving up the vote, because for him participation is **a way to change the world in line with his values**. The same for the deeply religious, the feminist, the libertarian, the ecologist.

**How they differ from today's ideologically motivated voters:**

In the current system ideologues often find themselves in a **minority** and lose to the mobilised apathetic masses who vote for populists. After AB-EXIT ideologues become a **significant structural part** of the electorate — 10–15 % of the total, but **20–30 % of those voting** (because the apathetic have left).

They are no longer marginal — they are a structural minority able to influence the outcome of elections. Parties will be structurally motivated to take them into account.

## 17.5. Those wavering between A and B (15–25 %)

Some citizens waver between "take the dividend" and "vote". These are:
- Citizens with moderate incomes and moderate political involvement
- Young people who have not yet formed a political position
- Citizens disappointed in the system but not ready to ignore it entirely

The waverers **split** roughly in half. Half will take the dividend in a given cycle, half will vote. In the next cycle it may change. AB-EXIT gives them flexibility.

This is a healthy dynamic. The stable core (pragmatists + ideologues) sets the foundation. The waverers are an indicator of the reaction to specific political cycles.

## 17.6. The apathetic (30–40 % of the current electorate)

Citizens for whom $200–300/year is a significant sum (1.5 % of annual income at the average wage). Structurally demotivated from participating in politics — either they see no difference between candidates, or general disappointment, or a lack of time and information.

**This is the group that will take the dividend.**

Structurally confirmed by Alaska PFD data (see §14): when the state offers a direct payment, **practically everyone** eligible applies.

Important: those leaving for the dividend are **not "bad citizens"**. These are people who in the current system do not vote anyway, or vote under the pressure of mobilisation campaigns for populists. AB-EXIT simply recognises their choice and compensates it. They receive $300 — advantageous. The electorate is cleansed — advantageous for the quality of governance. Win-win.

## 17.7. What the "new electorate" means for politics

After AB-EXIT the voters are **pragmatists + ideologues + half of the waverers** — roughly 35–50 % of the current electorate.

This electorate:

**Can think.** Pragmatists read financial reports. Ideologues know their topics. The waverers are not apathetic, they simply have less certainty.

**Can argue.** Pragmatists operate with figures and projects. Ideologues — with values and philosophical arguments. This is not an electorate of Twitter wars.

**Can verify.** Pragmatists check candidates' programmes for feasibility. Ideologues remember promises and punish their breach.

**Can wait.** Pragmatists think in a 10–20-year horizon. Ideologues — in a horizon of generations. Nobody demands "solve everything now".

**Does not buy emotional narratives.** Pragmatists see the economic reality behind the emotions. Ideologues see attempts to manipulate their values. A populist campaign does not work on this audience.

## 17.8. Structural advantage for certain politicians

This audience is ideal for **technocrat-reformers** (see §15 on honest politicians). Those able to speak in programmatic terms, operate with figures, explain long-term consequences. In the current system such politicians lose to populists. After AB-EXIT they get their audience.

This audience also responds well to **principled politicians** (rights defenders, eco-activists in politics, libertarians). Ideologues find "their own" among principled politicians and support them systematically.

Who loses: **classic populists**, whose only tool is the emotional mobilisation of the apathetic. Their base has left with the dividend. It is hard to build a campaign on anger for an audience that counts wages and pension accounts.

## 17.9. Ready formulation for communication

> "After AB-EXIT those who vote are those who can think, wait and verify. Pragmatists — accountants, engineers, entrepreneurs — for whom $300 is less than the damage from bad governance. Ideologues — principled citizens — for whom values have no monetary equivalent. Together they make up 35–50 % of the remaining electorate. A populist campaign does not work on them. They choose whoever is able to govern. This is exactly the audience that used to be in the political minority and lost to the mobilised apathetic masses. After AB-EXIT they are the majority of those voting."
