# 1f. The Core Electorate: Who Decides Now — and How the Protocol Changes What an Election Is

**Chapter:** 01 — Introduction and the Core Formula
**File:** 01_001f · v1 · 5 October 2026
**Source:** the architect's series of questions of 3–5 October 2026; details and the record of the argument are in 056f §13m–13q (Russia) and 055c §6.7–6.9 (Germany). Here is a consolidated statement without repeating the arguments.

---

*How to read. Figures are from the sources named or estimates from age structures; estimates are marked 🟡. The architect's theses are recorded as theses. The protocol does not weight votes and takes nobody's right away (048m, Article 1); the chapter is about who decides now and how that changes, not about who should not vote.*

## 1. Why this chapter stands at the beginning

The architect: "the whole point of the questions about the core electorate is that the protocol changes precisely it, and that matters greatly: the tipping point can be passed and the decision given again to those who make the country's GDP — instead of the present state, in which what must be paid for is decided by those who never voted for it".

An election today is decided neither by the majority nor by the elite but by the core: the part of the electorate that always turns up. Whoever is in it decides for everyone; whoever cannot outvote it stops coming. The protocol changes not the rules of counting but the point at which a person decides whether to take part — and thereby changes the composition of the core. That is its essence, and that is why this chapter stands here and not at the end.

## 2. Who is in the core: one person for two regimes

Putin's core and Orbán's core are, by the data, one and the same person (Levada Centre, 2025–2026; Hungarian analyses by settlement type, April 2026; in detail, 056f §13q).

1. **Who.** A woman (about two-thirds of the core), 55–75, a village or a town of under 50 thousand; secondary vocational education.
2. **What she lives on.** A pension or a wage from the budget; income below the median; the money comes from the state directly.
3. **With whom.** Alone or with a pensioner husband; the children in the city; the son — a plant, security, the police; the granddaughter — a teacher.
4. **Where she learns about the world.** Television; the most loyal group in every poll (83–95 % approval).
5. **How she relates to the state.** Provider and boss at once; she votes as told. **She has already been paid before elections** — ten thousand roubles to pensioners before the Duma election of 2021, a thirteenth-month pension before the Hungarian election of 2022, grants to rural pensioners before 2026. That the vote is worth money the authorities showed her themselves.
6. **What she values.** Order, "that it not get worse", the country — sincerely; she wants to be noticed: "we worked for forty years".
7. **What she says and thinks.** Aloud — "for"; to herself — "nobody asks us". The meek (011b §11b.2b).
8. **What she fears.** That the supplement will be taken away, that her son will be taken, that she will be called an enemy.
9. **What Hungary proved.** In April 2026 it was precisely the villages that swung the most, when they were shown the bill (039). The core is loyal until it is presented with a number.

**The United States and Germany** — the same age, a different nature: the decider is the reliable voter of 60+, who comes by himself (US 2024: turnout of the 65+ — 74.7 %; Germany 2025: the 50–69 — about 85–87 %). Nobody holds him, and the lever "you are made to do it for free" does not apply to him; the question to him is different — will the indifferent one take the payment for what he no longer cares about (055c §6.7).

## 3. Some vote — others produce

Germany, 2025 (055c §6.9):

| The core, 60 and over | Share | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Of those entitled to vote | 42.6 % | electoral statistics 2025 |
| Of ballots cast | ≈ 44 % | above-average turnout |
| Of the population | ≈ 30 % | Destatis |
| Of private consumption | ≈ 30 % 🟡 | headcount and spending per head |
| Of spending on health and care | ≈ 45–50 % 🟡 | spending on the 65+ |
| Of labour GDP — what they produce | ≈ 10–12 % | one in five works |
| Of net wealth | over 50 % | Bundesbank, PHF 2023 |
| Homeowners: 65+ against under 35 | 59 % against 13 % | IW, PHF |
| Decision horizon | until the next pension indexation | 055c §6.7 |

**In one line: over 60 — 44 % of the votes and about 11 % of the labour; 30–59 — the same 44 % of the votes and about 70 % of the labour.** By 2035, when those born in 1955–1969 have retired — half the votes and 7–8 % of the labour 🟡. The highest wages in the country are those of the 50–59, but they are not yet the core; in the core one in five works, and four in five do not work at all.

The same picture in two people: a man of 32 working full-time and a woman of 67 in retirement are left, after housing, with roughly the same money — he for forty hours a week, she for none; her wealth is twelve times his; €380 from his wage and as much again from his employer go straight into her pension. If he is divorced and pays for one child, he has half of what she has; with two children — a deficit (055c §6.9; 019f §3; 028 §56.2).

## 4. What the core can and cannot do

- **Fight — it cannot.** Forsa, July 2025: 16 % are "definitely" ready to defend Germany with a weapon; among the 60+, 68 % refuse, among the 18–29, 56 %. Those over 60 decide; those who will go, if they go, are the eighteen-year-olds — who from 2027 are put on the compulsory register. A dictator covers the same incapacity of his core with coercion — the mobilisation of 2022; a democracy cannot while it remains one; a populist in power can (055c §6.8). Russia is the mirror: those who vote for Putin do not go to fight, and that is why the war can last.
- **Take risk — it does not want to.** Willingness to take risk falls monotonically with age (the SOEP panel, Dohmen et al., 2011). Retirement at 70 is rejected by four in five of the 50–70. A major reform is a risk, and a quarter of the country — the richest and the least risk-ready — does not accept it: not from malice but from calculation — much to lose, no time to win.
- **Change — it cannot; preserve — it can.** In the ten largest occupations a worker of 60 is better than one of 28 in the same job in none; equal in production and in the office, and only among the healthy, on an unchanging task and leaving sick leave aside (25–30 days a year against 12–15); in the rest, worse by 5–50 %, most of all in physical work (055c §6.9). Wherever he is equal, he is equal in work that does not change.

## 5. The closed cycle

The core is closed at three points, and none of them is opened by voting, because those who vote are inside:

- **the vote** — 44 % of the ballots with those who do not work;
- **income** — rent on half the country's wealth and a pension from the contributions of the working, with a federal subsidy of 30 % of the budget; two claims on the product of the same people, each lawful on its own, together irreversible without the recipients' vote;
- **inheritance** — in Germany about 400 billion euros a year are inherited, but the average heir receives them at 55–60, entering the core himself. **Wealth does not descend to the working; it passes from one generation of the core to the next, bypassing those who work.** The architect: "an endless cycle of the hegemony of the old over the young".

The rules that fix the cycle were written by those inside: seniority pay, social selection in redundancies by age, fixed-term contracts for the young — insiders and outsiders (Lindbeck and Snower). The protocol does not call this unjust; it calls it irreversible without them.

## 6. The burned: why the producers do not vote

Those who produce 70 % of the labour cast as many ballots as those who produce a tenth — and those of them who do not come are not the apathetic. They are the burned (011b §11b.2c): they know the core cannot be outvoted arithmetically, and they stop coming, which makes the core weightier still. The architect: "they have to work, and they are the burned, because they cannot outvote the army of pensioners and the meek". That is the closed circle, which voting does not open.

## 7. What the protocol changes

Not the weight of votes: one ballot is one vote (048m, Article 1). Not the composition of the core by decree. The protocol changes the point at which a person decides whether to take part: before an election everyone chooses — to vote, or to take the payment and not vote in that election.

- **The indifferent one leaves by himself, for money.** The one who "no longer cares" takes the payment — and for the first time that is visible and counted.
- **The producer sees that his vote decides, and returns.** The burned return not at an appeal but when they decide: Hungary 2026 — plus nine points of turnout once the bill was presented (039).
- **Feedback with a threshold.** Each who leaves increases the weight of those who stay; each who returns raises the price of the next exit for the authorities. Such feedback has a threshold beyond which the system does not return: the fixed point of the formal statement (013f, the second theorem), the spiral of 015b with the opposite sign. Below the threshold the core decides for everyone; beyond it, those who have something to lose, and they no longer leave.
- **The filter is by the person, not by the group.** The filter by horizon falls on the poor and the old more often — money is worth more to the poor, a short horizon is more frequent among the old; this must be said by us before an opponent says it. But the protocol knows no groups: the horizon is a property of the person, not of age. A grandmother thinking of her grandson on the service register thinks thirty years ahead and stays; a rich retired professor who no longer cares leaves. The filter is not against age but against "let nothing change, because in my youth things were better" — the nostalgic bias that grows with age in every survey and is the short horizon in its purest form. The wise will not take it; the protocol keeps precisely them.
- **The right is taken from nobody.** The filter exists already and works the other way — it filters out the producers through hopelessness. The protocol changes it from "who is hopeless" to "who no longer cares", and everyone passes it himself, with his own hand, anew at every election.

## 8. Two levers for two cores

- **Where the core is brought in** (Russia, Hungary before 2026): the lever is "the authorities themselves paid before elections; so the vote is worth money; give it by law and always". Every move of the authorities is costly: to deny is to admit bribery; to agree is to bow; to pay once more is to confirm; to stay silent is to have the opposition read the silence aloud (056f §13n–13q).
- **Where the core comes by itself** (the United States, Germany): there is no lever of coercion. One question remains: will the reliable but indifferent voter take the payment for what he no longer cares about — and will those who have something to lose remain to decide. The repository has no answer; only a pilot with different sums will give one (048l). A clean test on the 2027 Chicago mayoral election (040m §5i) added to this: the American machine is a union machine, built on stake rather than coercion; the protocol does not subtract it, and the American case must be counted afresh, not carried over from Hungary and Russia.

## 9. Weak point

Where the threshold lies nobody knows; that is a question for the pilot and for the exit share. The protocol does not guarantee that "the young and the active" as a group will decide: it guarantees that those who stayed by themselves will decide — and in every group those are its own. Who exactly will take the payment in Germany — the indifferent old or the poor young — is untested: selection by income remains the main open question (040m §4). The shares of consumption and labour by age are estimates without weighting by hours or earnings; occupational headcounts are orders of magnitude. And the theses on causes — that the core was "raised by populists", that "this paradox produced Putin" — are recorded as the architect's theses: the data confirm the gap, not its origin.
