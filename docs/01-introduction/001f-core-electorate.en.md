## 1. Why this chapter stands at the beginning

The architect: "the whole point of the questions about the core electorate is that the protocol changes precisely it, and that matters greatly: the tipping point can be passed and the decision given again to those who make the country's GDP — instead of the present state, in which what must be paid for is decided by those who never voted for it".

An election today is decided neither by the majority nor by the elite but by the core: the part of the electorate that always turns up. Whoever is in it decides for everyone; whoever cannot outvote it stops coming. The protocol changes not the rules of counting but the point at which a person decides whether to take part — and thereby changes the composition of the core. That is its essence, and that is why this chapter stands here and not at the end.

## 2. Who is in the core: one person for two regimes

Putin's core and Orbán's core are, by the data, one and the same person (Levada Centre, 2025–2026; Hungarian analyses by settlement type, April 2026; in detail, 056f §13q).

1. **Who.** A woman (about two-thirds of the core), 55–75, a village or a town of under 50 thousand; secondary vocational education.
2. **What she lives on.** A pension or a wage from the budget; income below the median; the money comes from the state directly.
3. **With whom.** Alone or with a pensioner husband (more often alone: the gap in life expectancy is about ten years, and over seventy most are widows 🟡 — the state inherits the husband's place: subsistence and a place for loyalty; the mechanism is in 036e §10); the children in the city; the son — a plant, security, the police; the granddaughter — a teacher.
4. **Where she learns about the world.** Television; the most loyal group in every poll (83–95 % approval).
5. **How she relates to the state.** Provider and boss at once; she votes as told. **She has already been paid before elections** — ten thousand roubles to pensioners before the Duma election of 2021, a thirteenth-month pension before the Hungarian election of 2022, grants to rural pensioners before 2026. That the vote is worth money the authorities showed her themselves.
6. **What she values.** Order, "that it not get worse", the country — sincerely; she wants to be noticed: "we worked for forty years".
7. **What she says and thinks.** Aloud — "for"; to herself — "nobody asks us". The meek (011b §11b.2b).
8. **What she fears.** That the supplement will be taken away, that her son will be taken, that she will be called an enemy.
9. **What Hungary proved.** In April 2026 it was precisely the villages that swung the most, when they were shown the bill (039). The core is loyal until it is presented with a number.

**The United States and Germany** — the same age, a different nature: the decider is the reliable voter of 60+, who comes by himself (US 2024: turnout of the 65+ — 74.7 %; Germany 2025: the 50–69 — about 85–87 %). Nobody holds him, and the lever "you are made to do it for free" does not apply to him; the question to him is different — will the indifferent one take the payment for what he no longer cares about (055c §6.7).

## 2b. Not a joke: the infantile core and "drop the nuke"

*Added 08.10.2026 at the architect's demand: "it must be shown exactly that the light and unserious attitude to populism, to the ageing of the dependent electorate and to its senile and infantile state is no joke; it came very close to dropping the nuke, with no understanding at all of the consequences for all humanity". The words "senile" and "infantile" are the architect's; the text below keeps the repository's rule: this is a state, not a type of person (011b), and it is described from data.*

**What Shulman showed (056f §13t, from focus groups and polls of 2025–2026).** The phrase "drop the nuke so that people stop dying" is said in focus groups more often than by anyone else by older women — that is, the core of §2. Not from bloodthirst: they want the war to end "and do not know how", and they believe the leadership has a stick that can be struck once — like smacking children so that they stop fighting. The word itself, the diminutive "nuke-let", shows that in their speech the nuclear weapon is already normalised: not the apocalypse of Soviet posters but "a small tactical little bomb that will show everyone". They were told this — as they were once told "anything but war": that the army is invincible, that resources are inexhaustible, that there is "a magic means known to the leadership" and a cartoon in which it flies around the Earth. Shulman: "Russian society is not bloodthirsty, it is infantile."

**Why it is no joke — three steps.**

1. *The legitimacy of a personalist regime rests on the core.* Not on the majority and not on the elite but on those who always come and vote as told (§1, §2). Their mood is the one thing the regime really measures (§13o: focus groups, Levada, FOM) and the one thing it cites when it needs permission. If the core says "strike once", a leader who already romanticises force (§13t, item 1) receives not pressure but *permission* — from the very people by whom he is legitimised.
2. *That permission has the shortest horizon possible — and the longest consequence possible.* The core of 55–75 with a horizon to the next pension indexation (§3, §4b, step 5) asks for an act whose consequences fall on everyone who will live after it and on everyone who does not live in Russia. It is the limiting case of the gap of §3: those who do not pay decide; here, those who will not be there to pay decide. The shortest horizon in the country gets a voice in the longest question on the planet.
3. *This state cannot be argued out of — it has no entry for an argument.* Fear collapses the horizon (036e §10): a person under threat does not plan, she holds on. Explaining fallout, retaliation and winter to such an aunt is like explaining them to a child: she is not stupid, she is in a state in which an argument about the future has no weight, because there is no future in her calculation. Hence "enlightenment" does not work, and "it is just propaganda" is no description: the propaganda worked precisely because the state was ready for it.

**How it came to this — not by one decision but by a hundred years.** The state produced this core itself: by a percentage of turnover it drove the men into loss, became husband to the widow, hired her for predictability and loyalty, paid her before elections and told her tales (036e §10; 056f §13t, the recipe). Infantility is not a trait of character but a product of dependence: one for whom others decided for sixty years asks that they decide once more — with one blow. The light attitude to it — "oh, the aunts", "oh, populism", "oh, they are ageing" — is exactly what allowed the state to be driven to a nuclear vocabulary unnoticed.

**Frankenstein — the architect's formula (08.10.2026).** "The aunts were created simply to rule for a long time on their votes, and it came to a real and strong increase in the threat of war, including nuclear war. That is Frankenstein in pure form. They are not malicious — but the result is real and evil." The figure gathers the whole mechanism into one. Why they were assembled: not for war — for long rule; the core was needed to come and vote as told, and every part of it — widowhood, hiring for loyalty, handouts before elections — was made for stability, none for war. What came out: a creature with one property nobody ordered — it asks to "strike once" and does not understand what a strike is, and the creator, legitimised by it alone, hears the request as permission. The threat grew not because anyone wanted it but because the structure of permission turned out to be assembled from people without a horizon. Why Frankenstein and not a villain: the aunts are not evil — they are assembled; the regime is not a villain — it is a constructor that wanted to rule; the result is real and evil, and it has no author who can be named — so it can be neither accused nor argued with, only taken apart. That is "the protocol names no enemies" in its hardest form: there is no enemy, there is a creature and there is a lever. What the protocol does with Frankenstein: it does not kill it — it releases it piece by piece; each who took the payment left the structure of permission herself, for money, in secret; the creature does not die — it stops being the voice the creator hears as "you may".

**What the protocol does — and does not.** It does not cure infantility and does not argue with it; it takes it out of the deciding vote for money, by its own choice. That is the only lever that works on a state with no entry for an argument: the payment does not require understanding, it requires only wanting. And it gives the aunt the very stick she lacks — to take or not to take, once a cycle, visibly on the counter — so that she does not ask for the nuclear one. What the protocol does not do: it takes no decision on weapons, takes no right from her, and does not promise that the leader will listen to the counter instead of the focus group. It changes whom the leader hears when he seeks permission — and the permission "strike once" stops being the voice of the core, because the core that gave it took the payment and is at home.

**Boundary.** No focus group decides on the use of weapons — one man decides; the state of the core is the structure of permission, not the hand on the button. The share of older women who say "nuke-let" Shulman does not give — "more often than one would expect"; an observation, not a measurement. And "came very close" is the architect's judgement; the data give only that the vocabulary is normalised and that the wish for an end without a lever is mass (a majority for negotiations since 2022, "would you support a decision to end it" — about 86 %).

## 2c. The United States: the same Frankenstein from different parts — why "nothing happens to him"

*Added 08.10.2026 on the architect's remark: "the most frightening thing is that Trump and any leader with authoritarian, that is narcissistic, inclinations will go the same road; and Trump is already going — breaking laws, and nothing happens to him for it". The section is about the mechanism, not the man: the repository names no enemies (the head of `llms-full`), it names the structure of permission.*

**"Nothing happens to him" is documented, and it is not luck.** Two impeachments (2019, 2021) without conviction: conviction needs 67 senators. A guilty verdict on 34 counts (New York, May 2024) — and a sentence in January 2025 of "no punishment", an unconditional discharge. The Supreme Court (*Trump v. United States*, July 2024): presidential immunity for official acts. The federal cases dropped after the election under the Justice Department's rule not to prosecute a sitting president. Pardons for the participants of January 6 (January 2025, about fifteen hundred). Each step is lawful in itself; together they produce a man to whom nothing can happen — and that is a *design*, not a character.

**The design.** In the American constitution the only sanction for a president is political: Congress. Congress is the party. The party's members are selected in primaries, where the core of the core votes (§4b, step 1) — about a fifth of the voters, their most loyal and oldest part. A senator who votes to convict loses his primary: of the seven Republicans who voted to convict in 2021, most left politics or were censured by their parties; Liz Cheney, after the January 6 committee, lost her 2022 primary by a landslide. So the sanction exists — but it is turned not on the leader but on those who would try to sanction him. That is the structure of permission of §2b, assembled from different parts: not dependence on a wage but belonging to a team; not buses but voluntary attendance; not "as told" but "ours". The American core is neither paid nor held — and it cannot be lost either, because it comes by itself (§2, last paragraph). The result is the same: the leader hears "you may" from the core, and anyone who would say "you may not" loses his seat before the leader does.

**Why the road is the same for anyone with such inclinations.** Shulman (056f §13t): power is like mercury, not like water; it runs together unless effort is applied; concentration is the natural state, dispersal is unnatural and takes work. A leader with narcissistic inclinations does not invent the road — he walks down the slope, and the slope is open exactly as far as the structure of permission does not withdraw its permission. Putin and Trump walk the same road not because they are alike but because the slope is one: a core that will not withdraw its "you may", and an apparatus that punishes those who would. The difference in parts — dependence against belonging — sets the speed, not the direction.

**What the protocol can do here — the architect's correction (08.10.2026).** The first draft of this paragraph said "the American core will most likely not take the payment" — and confused the core with the fanatics. The architect: "the core is always the dependent and the low-initiative with a short horizon, in the majority; I am not speaking of the fanatics. Those will take it, and it is only a matter of the sum; in the US with its budget the sums will be very large." Right, and the data confirm it: the American core of 65+ depends not on a wage but on transfers — for about 40 % of retirees Social Security is more than half of income, for one in seven nearly all of it; the median benefit is about 1,900 dollars a month 🟡. That is the same dependence as the aunt's, only the treasury pays directly instead of the boss. Such a voter takes if the sum is desirable — and the measure of desirability is recorded (048k §3: a sum that 40–50 % of the population want). The sums in the US 🟡 (median personal income about 42 thousand, about 160 million voters, half take): at one percent of the median — 420 dollars, a fund of about 34 billion a cycle, half a percent of the federal budget; at two — 840 dollars and about one percent; at three — 1,260 dollars, about 100 billion, one and a half percent. 1,260 dollars for a retiree on 1,900 a month is two-thirds of a month. The fanatics will not take it at any percentage — and that is the aim, they vote by themselves anyway; the core will take it — and the structure of permission assembled from it comes apart the same way as in Russia: one by one, for money, in secret. The second point of strike is the primaries, where the structure of permission is assembled. Primary turnout is about a fifth; four-fifths stay at home, and among them are those with a stake (tax, debt, contract) who do not go because they know the core of the core cannot be outvoted (§6). If the rule extends to primaries — and that is a setting, not a gap (048m, remarks, the third addition) — then "nothing happens to him" stops being guaranteed, not because the leader is punished but because the senator who would say "you may not" is chosen no longer only by those who say "ours". The protocol introduces no sanction — it changes who selects those who hold the sanction. The boundary: the share of fanatics — those who come from belonging rather than dependence — in the American core is unknown and unmeasured; on them the protocol does not act, and if they outnumber the dependent the lever is weaker; that is measured by a pilot with different sums, not by argument (§8).

**And one more thing that cannot be passed over here.** In §2b the shortest horizon got a voice in the longest question. In the United States the same question stands before the same core — and before a leader with the same button. This is not "Russia and America are alike"; it is one and the same Frankenstein, assembled twice, in different workshops, from different parts, with one property nobody ordered.

**In one line: over 60 — 44 % of the votes and about 11 % of the labour; 30–59 — the same 44 % of the votes and about 70 % of the labour.** By 2035, when those born in 1955–1969 have retired — half the votes and 7–8 % of the labour 🟡. The highest wages in the country are those of the 50–59, but they are not yet the core; in the core one in five works, and four in five do not work at all.

The same picture in two people: a man of 32 working full-time and a woman of 67 in retirement are left, after housing, with roughly the same money — he for forty hours a week, she for none; her wealth is twelve times his; €380 from his wage and as much again from his employer go straight into her pension. If he is divorced and pays for one child, he has half of what she has; with two children — a deficit (055c §6.9; 019f §3; 028 §56.2).

## 4. What the core can and cannot do

- **Fight — it cannot.** Forsa, July 2025: 16 % are "definitely" ready to defend Germany with a weapon; among the 60+, 68 % refuse, among the 18–29, 56 %. Those over 60 decide; those who will go, if they go, are the eighteen-year-olds — who from 2027 are put on the compulsory register. A dictator covers the same incapacity of his core with coercion — the mobilisation of 2022; a democracy cannot while it remains one; a populist in power can (055c §6.8). Russia is the mirror: those who vote for Putin do not go to fight, and that is why the war can last.
- **Take risk — it does not want to.** Willingness to take risk falls monotonically with age (the SOEP panel, Dohmen et al., 2011). Retirement at 70 is rejected by four in five of the 50–70. A major reform is a risk, and a quarter of the country — the richest and the least risk-ready — does not accept it: not from malice but from calculation — much to lose, no time to win.
- **Change — it cannot; preserve — it can.** In the ten largest occupations a worker of 60 is better than one of 28 in the same job in none; equal in production and in the office, and only among the healthy, on an unchanging task and leaving sick leave aside (25–30 days a year against 12–15); in the rest, worse by 5–50 %, most of all in physical work (055c §6.9). Wherever he is equal, he is equal in work that does not change.
- **Choose its own kind — it can, and the result shows it.** The architect's question (06.10.2026): "Biden and Trump fall asleep at meetings — plainly unfit, and that is treated as normal. How did it come to this? The protocol is meant to fix precisely this." The facts: in 2024 America chose between an 81-year-old and a 78-year-old — the oldest pair of nominees in its history; the median age of a senator is about 65; episodes of dozing at public events were discussed in the press for both; "unfit" is the architect's judgement, recorded as a judgement. How it came to this — the answer is in the table above: the deciders are the core of 60 and over (§3), and the core chooses the familiar and its own — the candidate it has watched for forty years, with whom it will "not get worse" (§2, item 6; §7, the nostalgic bias). The aged candidate is not a failure of the system but its exact product: supply repeats demand. A driver needs an exam, a gun owner a check, and the one who decides for a city and a country — nothing (029 §29.11, row 14); the only exam possible here is set by the voter. The architect's question in numbers: could Biden fly a plane, drive a train or a bus? A plane — no, by law: an airline pilot retires at 65 (FAA, Part 121; the ICAO standard). A train — only with a medical examination every three years (FRA). A bus — only with a DOT medical every two years, yearly with diagnoses; a school bus — yearly. A president — no limit and no mandatory board; the "annual physical" is voluntary, and the report is written by his own doctor. The one the law does not trust with a hundred and fifty passengers is trusted with three hundred and thirty million without a single check. What the protocol changes is not the candidate's age by decree and not a medical board for politicians: it changes the demand. When those who remain to decide are those with something to lose and a horizon beyond the next pension indexation, "familiar and no worse" stops being a winning programme — and a candidate who dozes stops being the safe choice. The boundary: the protocol does not guarantee a young candidate; it removes the premium on the familiar.

### 4b. How a "plainly unfit" man becomes president: five steps

The architect (06.10.2026): "what matters is how such people became presidents, when they would not be trusted with thirty bus passengers". The answer is not in one place but in a chain; each step is lawful and reasonable on its own, and together they produce a result none of them chose. And this is not a hypothesis "would they elect him": the architect reminds that it has already happened — Biden was elected at 78 and served to 82, "a decrepit old man confusing left and right on live television" (the architect's judgement). What matters more: he was removed neither by the voter nor by the law. His own apparatus removed him in July 2024 — three weeks after the debate that made his condition visible to everyone at once, and after the donors paused the money. The only exam that worked in four years consisted of two things: visibility and a price. That is the counter and the payment in another guise — the protocol makes them not a one-off but a rule, and before the election rather than after.

1. **The candidate is chosen not by the electorate but by the core of the core.** A minority goes to the primaries, older and more partisan than the general voter; in 2024 the sitting president was renominated with no contested primary at all — the party apparatus chose, the voter certified. The core chooses the familiar (§2, item 6): the candidate watched for forty years is safe because he is known.
2. **In November the choice is binary, and "neither" costs nothing and counts for nothing.** The one who sees both as unfit can only stay at home — for free and invisibly; his absence is counted as consent (029 §29.11). The burned leave, the core stays, and "no worse" wins.
3. **There is nobody to write the check.** The driver's exam is written by an agency that does not itself drive; a check for a president would be written by the two parties that nominate presidents. The Twenty-fifth Amendment is applied by the cabinet he himself appointed. A judge in his own cause (029, row 10).
4. **The harm is diffuse, delayed and deniable.** A driver's error kills thirty people at once and in view — hence the licence and the medical board. A president's error costs a trillion spread over ten years and three hundred million people, and not one of them "died" in view; so a culture of admission to the bus formed, and to the presidency did not. It is the same reason a vote is stolen with impunity: the harm leaves no trace (040n).
5. **The apparatus that could stop it is of the same age itself.** The median senator is about 65; committee seniority goes by years served; insiders write the rules for insiders (§5). The architect (06.10.2026): "those who rule have a short horizon — that is already a medical diagnosis; experience is good, but almost all of them there are like that; and why would an eighty-year-old care about the problems of a twenty-five-year-old — for him that is half a century back". Recorded with two refinements, both his own earlier positions. For one person the horizon is a property of the person, not of age (§7): there is a senator of eighty who thinks of his grandchildren. For a chamber of a hundred with a median of sixty-five it is a distribution, not an exception: risk-readiness and the length of planning fall with age monotonically (SOEP; 055c §6.7), and the expected horizon of the body is short not by diagnosis but by statistics. And "half a century back" is the exact formula of stake: housing, rent, the first child, student debt are for him not tasks but memories; he decides them from memory, not from stake (§5), and that is not malice but the absence of skin in the game. Here too the protocol introduces no age limit — it changes who decides, and thereby the demand for those who decide for the deciders.

What the protocol changes is two steps of the five, and exactly the two that belong to the voter. The second: "neither" acquires a price and a count — the one who leaves took the payment, and that is visible before the election; the machine that fielded the "safe" man is for the first time presented with a bill for those he did not hold, and the burned have a reason to return. The first — partly, with an open question: the charter speaks of elections at each level, and whether the rule extends to primaries, which in the US the state runs under party rules, is a setting, not a gap: the architect — "the protocol is fitted to each country at the referendum, and for the US it can work there too"; in the charter it is a line in square brackets, and the same referendum decides it (048m, remarks). The third, fourth and fifth the protocol does not touch: it introduces neither an exam nor an age limit — it removes the premium on the familiar among those who decide, and that is enough for the "safe" choice to stop being safe.

**§4b in one sentence.** The architect (06.10.2026): "the machine and the apathetic choose presidents who are plainly worse than they could be, because the machine is of the same age and the technologies run on money, not on logic". Right, with three refinements. The apathetic do not choose — they are the raw material: the core chooses, the machine delivers (by bus in Russia, by money in America). "Worse than they could be" is not chance but selection by a criterion unrelated to fitness: a candidate is selected for whether the core knows him and whether money can mobilise for him; nobody pays for "he will cope", everyone pays for "he is ours" and "he will turn them out", and fitness drops out of the criterion not by malice but because it has no buyer. And the machine's age is secondary: were it younger, the criterion would be the same, because the same thing is paid for. The protocol strikes three points of this chain — it takes the raw material from the machine, gives "neither" a price and a count, brings back the burned who have a stake — and fitness becomes not a criterion by decree but the only thing left worth paying one's own money for.

### 4c. One voter, two answers: the bus and the president

The architect's question (06.10.2026): "if you ask the same voter — would you trust Biden with the school bus carrying your child, and would you trust him to be president — what logic or what incentives change the answers to what is in essence one question?" The answer lies in four differences between the questions, and none of them is about Biden.

1. **The price of error: one's own and now — or shared and later.** The bus: the child is his, today, irreversibly, and he himself will be to blame. The president: his share of the harm is one three-hundred-millionth, years away, and nobody is to blame. The answer about the bus is a decision; the answer about the president is a statement. This is described as expressive voting (Brennan and Lomasky, 1993): when a vote costs nothing, it is used to express, not to decide.
2. **The weight of the answer.** On the bus his "no" is final: there will be no such driver. On the president his "no" is one hundred-and-fifty-millionth, and the outcome does not depend on him. It is rational to spend on a decision exactly as much attention as it weighs; and he does.
3. **The form of the question.** The bus — "yes or no". The president — "this one or that one": not "is he fit" but "whose is he". Our side's old man against their side's monster — and "fitness" drops out of the question before it is spoken.
4. **The price of honesty.** Saying "no" about the bus costs nothing. Saying "my candidate is unfit" costs one's place in one's group; the answer about the president is given to the group, not to oneself.

What the protocol changes is one thing, and exactly the one that separates the first question from the second: the vote acquires its own, immediate, personal price — the payment the voter forgoes. The question "would you trust him with the presidency" turns into "would you give up your money to vote for him" — that is, it becomes the bus question: a decision, not a statement. The one who would not trust him with the bus but voted "yes" for free now either takes the payment and leaves, or pays for his vote with his own money — and those remain who trust him in fact. The protocol does not ask the voter the bus question; it makes the president question cost as much.

The boundary: the payment is a percentage of the median, and for a rich man it is small; on him this works more weakly — which is the open question of selection by income (040m §4). And the protocol does nothing about the third and fourth differences: the binary and the team remain; it only removes their being free.

## 5. The closed cycle

The core is closed at three points, and none of them is opened by voting, because those who vote are inside:

- **the vote** — 44 % of the ballots with those who do not work;
- **income** — rent on half the country's wealth and a pension from the contributions of the working, with a federal subsidy of 30 % of the budget; two claims on the product of the same people, each lawful on its own, together irreversible without the recipients' vote;
- **inheritance** — in Germany about 400 billion euros a year are inherited, but the average heir receives them at 55–60, entering the core himself. **Wealth does not descend to the working; it passes from one generation of the core to the next, bypassing those who work.** The architect: "an endless cycle of the hegemony of the old over the young".

The rules that fix the cycle were written by those inside: seniority pay, social selection in redundancies by age, fixed-term contracts for the young — insiders and outsiders (Lindbeck and Snower). The protocol does not call this unjust; it calls it irreversible without them.

## 6. The burned: why the producers do not vote

Those who produce 70 % of the labour cast as many ballots as those who produce a tenth — and those of them who do not come are not the apathetic. They are the burned (011b §11b.2c): they know the core cannot be outvoted arithmetically, and they stop coming, which makes the core weightier still. The architect: "they have to work, and they are the burned, because they cannot outvote the army of pensioners and the meek". That is the closed circle, which voting does not open.

A second reason for leaving — the architect (06.10.2026): "perhaps that is why the burned left: they are enterprising and well-off people, and here is such a glaring contrast with infirmity and dementia". To the hopelessness of the arithmetic is added distaste for the product: one who hires people himself and answers for results will not spend an evening choosing between two he would not have hired (§4b). That is not apathy — it is a standard the offer does not meet, and leaving is its only expression, because a free "neither" does not count. The boundary by the data: non-voters are on average younger, poorer and less often degree-holders (Pew), so the enterprising and well-off burned are a minority among those at home, not their mass; but it is the minority that produces and that the core lacks for the tipping point (§7). The protocol brings it back not by appeal but by making "neither" counted and paid: for the first time a person with a standard has a way to express the standard other than silence.

## 7. What the protocol changes

Not the weight of votes: one ballot is one vote (048m, Article 1). Not the composition of the core by decree. The protocol changes the point at which a person decides whether to take part: before an election everyone chooses — to vote, or to take the payment and not vote in that election.

- **The indifferent one leaves by himself, for money.** The one who "no longer cares" takes the payment — and for the first time that is visible and counted.
- **The producer sees that his vote decides, and returns.** The burned return not at an appeal but when they decide: Hungary 2026 — plus nine points of turnout once the bill was presented (039).
- **Feedback with a threshold.** Each who leaves increases the weight of those who stay; each who returns raises the price of the next exit for the authorities. Such feedback has a threshold beyond which the system does not return: the fixed point of the formal statement (013f, the second theorem), the spiral of 015b with the opposite sign. Below the threshold the core decides for everyone; beyond it, those who have something to lose, and they no longer leave.
- **The filter is by the person, not by the group.** The filter by horizon falls on the poor and the old more often — money is worth more to the poor, a short horizon is more frequent among the old; this must be said by us before an opponent says it. But the protocol knows no groups: the horizon is a property of the person, not of age. A grandmother thinking of her grandson on the service register thinks thirty years ahead and stays; a rich retired professor who no longer cares leaves. The filter is not against age but against "let nothing change, because in my youth things were better" — the nostalgic bias that grows with age in every survey and is the short horizon in its purest form. The wise will not take it; the protocol keeps precisely them.
- **The right is taken from nobody.** The filter exists already and works the other way — it filters out the producers through hopelessness. The protocol changes it from "who is hopeless" to "who no longer cares", and everyone passes it himself, with his own hand, anew at every election.

## 8. Two levers for two cores

- **Where the core is brought in** (Russia, Hungary before 2026): the lever is "the authorities themselves paid before elections; so the vote is worth money; give it by law and always". Every move of the authorities is costly: to deny is to admit bribery; to agree is to bow; to pay once more is to confirm; to stay silent is to have the opposition read the silence aloud (056f §13n–13q).
- **Where the core comes by itself** (the United States, Germany): there is no lever of coercion. One question remains: will the reliable but indifferent voter take the payment for what he no longer cares about — and will those who have something to lose remain to decide. The repository has no answer; only a pilot with different sums will give one (048l). A clean test on the 2027 Chicago mayoral election (040m §5i) added to this: the American machine is a union machine, built on stake rather than coercion; the protocol does not subtract it, and the American case must be counted afresh, not carried over from Hungary and Russia.

## 9. Weak point

Where the threshold lies nobody knows; that is a question for the pilot and for the exit share. The protocol does not guarantee that "the young and the active" as a group will decide: it guarantees that those who stayed by themselves will decide — and in every group those are its own. Who exactly will take the payment in Germany — the indifferent old or the poor young — is untested: selection by income remains the main open question (040m §4). The shares of consumption and labour by age are estimates without weighting by hours or earnings; occupational headcounts are orders of magnitude. And the theses on causes — that the core was "raised by populists", that "this paradox produced Putin" — are recorded as the architect's theses: the data confirm the gap, not its origin.
