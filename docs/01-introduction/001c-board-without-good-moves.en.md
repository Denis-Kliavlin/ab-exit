## 1. Why this catalogue exists

Almost every reform fails in the same place: it counts on those who lose by it behaving well. The official will comply, the party will not obstruct, the loser will accept. The AB-EXIT protocol is built differently, and the catalogue below is a way to check that. It gathers situations in which an opponent of the protocol acts strictly in his own interest, cunningly and with no goodwill whatever — and still finds no move that helps him.

The catalogue serves three purposes. The first is a test of the design: a mechanism that works only as long as nobody resists it does not work; here one sees what happens under resistance. The second is a map for whoever will advance the idea: for every objection and every action of an opponent it is visible in advance where it leads. The third is honesty: any place where the opponent would have a good move is a hole, and such places are easiest to look for along a list.

## 2. The chess logic: three figures

Chess has exact words for such positions, and they describe three different arrangements.

**The fork.** One piece attacks two at once; only one can be saved. In politics this is a choice where the opponent keeps one value at the price of another: a government that forbids its supporters to take the payment keeps turnout and loses their affection; allowing it keeps the affection and loses turnout.

**Zugzwang.** A position where any move worsens one's standing, and not moving is impossible. This is the catalogue's main word, and its second half is the main thing in it. In ordinary politics the strong side always has a best move — to stay silent and wait for the subject to go away. The protocol takes away exactly that: once money has been publicly offered to the citizen, silence stops being a pause and becomes an answer, and the worst one available. The right to pass disappears, and a position that rested on that right begins to crumble by itself.

**The pin.** A piece cannot leave because it shields the king. Such are the positions where the opponent cannot remove what is inconvenient without exposing what is essential: an electoral autocracy cannot give up paper elections without losing the ritual that produces its legitimacy and the exam by which it tests its own apparatus (048i.6c).

The three figures have one thing in common, and it sets the protocol apart from ordinary political struggle: in none of the positions below is the opponent attacked. Nothing is taken from him, nothing is forbidden him, he is not exposed. Only the price of his habitual move changes — as in "reform by subtraction" (019c). A chess player would call this positional play: the game is won not by a combination but because the opponent has run out of useful moves.

## 8. The fork in which the opposition itself sits

One row of the catalogue is built the other way round: locked inside it is not an opponent of the protocol but those who today fight the authorities by the old rules (056d.1). To vote for establishment spoilers is to lend the election legitimacy; to boycott is to dry up turnout in favour of the dependent electorate; to spoil the ballot is useless, because the percentage is counted from valid ones. This is a true zugzwang, and the opposition of many countries has sat in it for years.

It is telling that the way out was found by the same method on which the whole catalogue is built: not by a new move on the old board but by changing what counts as a move. A sign in a valid ballot (§3, first row) chooses none of the three losing branches — it votes and informs at once. That is how the protocol works as a whole: it does not offer the citizen a better move in a game where he has no good ones, but adds to the board a square that was not there before.

## 9. Weak point of the section

The catalogue was compiled by a sweep of the text: the wordings are taken from the sections the references point to, and each row was not separately checked against its source. The chapter on the USA was not included in the sweep. And the main point: a fork on paper is a claim that there is no third move, and the absence of a move cannot be proved — one can only fail to find it. Every third move found is a discovery, not a defeat, and the row in which it was found should be rewritten or removed. 🟡
