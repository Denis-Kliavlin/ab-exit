### Q-ETH-001 · Can one sell at all what is not for sale — a civic right?

**Status:** 🔁 contested
**Who asks:** philosophers, theologians, moralists — the deepest objection
**Source in the book:** [§33 "The philosophical manifesto"](../07-manifesto/033-manifesto.md), [§29.4](../06-critique-arsenal/029-hypocrisy.md)
**Related:** Q-LEG-001, Q-ETH-002

**Answer.** The right is not sold and does not pass to anyone else — it is **suspended by its
holder for one cycle, reversibly**. Nobody receives another's vote; the vote is annihilated.
This is closer to waiving a lawsuit than to selling property: by declining to sue, a person
has not sold the right to a court.

**Weak point of the answer.** Sandel's objection ("what money can't buy") strikes not at the
mechanics of transfer but at **the very act of valuing in money**: by putting a price on civic
participation, a society changes the meaning of that participation, even if nothing is legally
transferred. The book has as yet no answer at the level of the objection. This is probably
the strongest point of criticism of AB-EXIT.

### Q-ETH-002 · Doesn't it demean human dignity — paying a person for silence?

**Status:** ✅ answered
**Who asks:** human-rights advocates, the public
**Source in the book:** [§11 "From duty to choice"](../02-history/011-paradigm-choice.md), [§29.5](../06-critique-arsenal/029-hypocrisy.md)
**Related:** Q-ETH-001, Q-SOC-001

**Answer.** Today's position is more demeaning: 48 % do not participate, and the state pretends
they do not exist. AB-EXIT does not pay for silence — it **recognises the choice and compensates
it**. And all other channels remain: protest, court, speech, petition. There is no silence here,
only the refusal of one specific instrument.

If receiving state compensation is to be called demeaning, then the pension, the tax credit
and free education will have to be called demeaning too.

**Weak point of the answer.** The analogy with the pension is incomplete: a pension is not conditional
on refraining from political action. The conditionality of the payment is exactly what makes
the case special, and the analogy walks around it.

### Q-ETH-003 · Is this an elitist idea — "get the poor off the electoral roll" in pretty wrapping?

**Status:** ✅ answered
**Who asks:** left-wing critics, journalists who know the idea's history
**Source in the book:** [README — "Why I Built This"](https://github.com/Denis-Kliavlin/ab-exit#why-i-built-this), [§33](../07-manifesto/033-manifesto.md)
**Related:** Q-SOC-001, Q-META-002

**Answer.** The author does not hide it: the 2014 idea grew out of anger and sounded exactly
like that. But the evolution is substantive, not cosmetic. "Remove the poor" is **to take away
a right** by an external attribute. AB-EXIT is **to add a right**, the same for everyone,
and let people sort themselves. A rich man too lazy to go to the precinct will take the
dividend exactly the same way. There is no income attribute in the mechanism.

**Weak point of the answer.** Intention and effect are different things. If exit correlates
statistically with income, the result may come close to a property qualification even with
a flawlessly neutral mechanism. The honest formulation: the mechanism is not elitist,
but its consequences may be — and that is tested only by a pilot.

### Q-ETH-004 · What if a person takes the money not because he wants to but because of need?

**Status:** 🟡 open
**Who asks:** ethicists, social workers
**Source in the book:** tangentially [§29.5](../06-critique-arsenal/029-hypocrisy.md)
**Related:** Q-SOC-001, Q-ETH-001

**Answer.** There is no direct examination. The outline: any social payment is accepted under
conditions of need, and that does not make it coercion. The sum is deliberately small (1.5 %
of the median) so as not to be "an offer that cannot be refused".

**Weak point of the answer.** The objection of "coercion by circumstance" is standard in
bioethics in discussions of paying donors and trial participants, and there it is treated
as serious. The answer "the sum is small" is quantitative, and therefore vulnerable: in a crisis
even a small sum becomes decisive. **A section is needed.**
