### Q-ELE-001 · Isn't this legalising apathy?

**Status:** ✅ answered
**Who asks:** academics, commentators
**Source in the book:** [§29.1–29.3](../06-critique-arsenal/029-hypocrisy.md)
**Related:** Q-LEG-002, Q-ETH-002

**Answer.** About 48 % of voters in mature democracies already do not participate —
and the academic community treats this as the norm, calling it "structural apathy"
but not a crisis. After AB-EXIT the situation is structurally the same: the same people
do not participate. Two things change — non-participation becomes **a transparent,
recorded choice** and it becomes **compensated**.

That is the structural hypocrisy of the criticism: one and the same situation is judged
oppositely depending on whether it is a silent norm or a formalised choice.

**Weak point of the answer.** The symmetry "the same people" is a hypothesis, not a fact. The presence
of a payment may draw out of the electorate people who today do vote. How large that
additional outflow is remains unknown until a pilot, and the strength of the whole argument
depends on exactly that.

### Q-ELE-002 · Who gains from AB-EXIT — the left or the right?

**Status:** 🔁 contested
**Who asks:** campaigns, political technologists, journalists
**Source in the book:** [§20](../04-electoral-dynamics/020-stronger-than-ubi.md), [§22 "The consensus theme"](../04-electoral-dynamics/022-consensus-theme.md), [§17](../04-electoral-dynamics/017-pragmatics-ideologues.md)
**Related:** Q-LEG-001, Q-ELE-003

**Answer.** The book's position: AB-EXIT is **a consensus theme, not a wedge**. It gives
no lasting advantage to either camp, because those who leave are not a party's supporters
but weakly motivated voters from all camps at once. The remaining electorate shifts
from ideologues to pragmatists — and that changes not the winner but the **type of politician**
for whom winning pays.

**Weak point of the answer.** This is the most politically loaded question in the base, and "nobody
wins" is an answer neither side will believe. Until there is a calculation on real data
for a specific jurisdiction, every party will assume the mechanism is aimed against it.
That is a practical rather than a logical problem — but it can kill the launch.

### Q-ELE-003 · If only the motivated remain, won't the radicals be strengthened?

**Status:** 🟡 open
**Who asks:** political scientists — a strong objection
**Source in the book:** partly [§17](../04-electoral-dynamics/017-pragmatics-ideologues.md), [§21 "The centrist void"](../04-electoral-dynamics/021-centrist-void.md)
**Related:** Q-ELE-002, Q-GAM-002

**Answer.** The book claims the opposite: those who leave are not centrists but the indifferent, and the centre
is precisely what acquires a targeted agenda it lacks today. But there is as yet no systematic
answer to the scenario "radicals are more strongly motivated than moderates, so their share grows".

**Weak point of the answer.** The objection rests on an observed regularity: when turnout falls,
the share of the ideologically motivated usually rises (US primaries are a live example).
The answer "the centre will get an agenda" is a hope, not a mechanism. **A section is needed.**

### Q-ELE-004 · The one who took the money lost influence — hasn't he lost?

**Status:** ✅ answered
**Who asks:** citizens, the public
**Source in the book:** [§1](../01-introduction/001-formula.md), [§11](../02-history/011-paradigm-choice.md)
**Related:** Q-ETH-001

**Answer.** He exchanged influence he was not using anyway for money — consciously
and reversibly. The mirror side matters more: whoever **refused** the payment receives
a mathematically strengthened vote. If half exit, every remaining vote weighs twice as much.
For the first time, refusing money converts into measurable political influence
rather than moral satisfaction.

**Weak point of the answer.** The strengthening of weight is real, but it is not distributed evenly by
intensity of preference: it benefits most those who already have more resources for
political participation. The mechanism is neutral; access to the use of the strengthened
vote is not.
