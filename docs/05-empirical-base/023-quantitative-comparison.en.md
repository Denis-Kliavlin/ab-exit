## 23.1. Purpose of the section

Existing electoral reforms — RCV, open primaries, citizens' assemblies, technocratic instruments — yield an effect **within single-digit percentages** on each parameter. AB-EXIT yields an effect in **tens of percent**. That is a difference of an order of magnitude.

This section shows the difference quantitatively. Without it the comparison stays qualitative ("works / does not work"), whereas it should be quantitative ("works at 3–5 % vs works at 30–40 %"). This is the central persuasive argument for rational elites, for tech capital, for practising academics.

## 23.2. Empirical effects of existing mechanisms

**Electronic voting** (Estonia since 2005). Solvak & Vassil (Tartu): online voting raised overall turnout by **2–4 percentage points** after ten years. Most online voters are people who voted offline anyway. Attraction of new voters — about **1–2 %**.

**Postal voting** (Oregon since 2000). Berinsky 2001, Gerber et al. 2013: a turnout gain of **2–7 %**. Noticeable but not structural.

**Automatic voter registration** (AVR). Brookings 2019: nine US states showed a turnout gain of **2–3 %** among the previously unregistered.

**Lowering the voting age to 16** (Austria since 2007). Zeglovits & Aichholzer 2014: adolescents vote at the same rate as adults. A gain of roughly **2–4 %**.

**Compulsory voting** (Australia, Belgium). The strongest mechanism by effect — **+25–35 % turnout**. But it is **not a solution to apathy and populism**. A compelled electorate includes a huge share of random, protest and emotional votes. Populist parties regularly win in Australia. The effect on quality is close to zero or negative (Brennan, "The Ethics of Voting", 2011).

**Ranked Choice Voting** (Maine since 2018, Alaska since 2022). Alaska 2022: the moderate Mary Peltola beat the populist Sarah Palin thanks to the redistribution of second choices. A local effect. The structural effect on populism — **0–3 % on average**.

**Open primaries** (California Top-Two, Alaska, Louisiana). McGhee et al. 2014: they reduce the share of radical candidates in the final vote by roughly **5–8 %**, but do not affect the composition of the electorate.

**Citizens' assemblies** (Ireland 2017, France 2020). An effect of a different order — an instrument for specific decisions, not an electoral reform. **0 %** on electoral processes.

**Democracy vouchers** (Seattle since 2017). Brennan Center 2020: participation in funding widened from 1.5 % to 4.4 %. **+3 % participation in funding**, not in voting.

**Education and quality press** (historical). Putnam, "Bowling Alone", 2000: turnout growth of **5–7 % over 50 years**. That effect has now **reversed**: turnout is falling, trust is falling.

**Lobbying, corporate money, media machines.** Bartels 2008, Gerber-Karlan-Bergan 2009: the effect of a large-scale media campaign on specific results — a **1–2 %** shift at enormous cost.

## 23.4. The effect of AB-EXIT — another order of magnitude

**A methodological caveat:** AB-EXIT has been launched in no jurisdiction, so the figures below are **model estimates**, based on:
- Analogy with the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend (1976–present, 100 % participation) — see §14
- Behavioural economics (response to monetary incentives)
- Mathematical models of the electoral process — see §13, game theory
- Stress-testing with six AI models (449+ rounds)

These are justified estimates, not empirical facts. They require pilot testing in a real jurisdiction.

**First effect — the structure of the electorate.** In countries with turnout of about 50 %, AB-EXIT under a moderate model can take **20–35 % of voters** out of the electoral process. The electorate changes by 20–35 percentage points — half of the remaining electorate.

At compensation of about $200–676 a year (1.5 % of median income), **30–40 % of current apathetics will accept option B**. A structural cleansing of the electorate by **15–25 percentage points**.

**Second effect — the motivation of those who remain.** If 25 % of voters exited via B, the remaining 75 % automatically have votes of weight 1.33 each. **+33 % political influence for every remaining voter.**

**Third effect — a smaller base for populism.** If 30–40 % of apathetics left via B, the populist mobilisation base has shrunk by **30–40 % of the original**. An order of magnitude more than RCV (3–5 %).

**Example:** Brexit won with 52 % on a 72 % turnout. "Leave" was voted by 37 % of all voters. Most of them were mobilised apathetics. Had half of them taken £200–500 through AB-EXIT, the remaining 18–20 % for "Leave" would have lost to the EU's supporters.

**Fourth effect — the planning horizon.** The departure of the apathetic extends the electorate's average horizon from **7–9 years to 12–15 years** — a twofold extension.

**Fifth effect — the economics for honest politicians.** §15 (PNAS 2020): corrupt politicians lose their structural advantage. An honest politician gains a competitive advantage of the order of **+15–25 % in chances of winning**.

## 23.5. Comparison by orders of magnitude

Existing mechanisms give results **within single-digit percentages** on each parameter.

AB-EXIT gives **15–40 % on every key parameter**:

- +15–25 % structural change in composition
- +33 % increase in the weight of the remaining votes
- −30–40 % populist mobilisation base
- +50–80 % extension of the planning horizon
- +15–25 % competitive advantage for honest politicians

**A difference of an order of magnitude.** This is not "AB-EXIT is slightly better" — it is **"AB-EXIT works in fundamentally different units of measurement"**.

## 23.6. Ready formulations

**For communication with rational elites:**

> "You have instruments that give 2–5 % improvement. You fight populism tactically, winning a few percent in one state and losing a few in another. AB-EXIT, by model estimates, gives 20–40 % of structural effect. It is not an alternative to your instruments — it is **another level of analysis**. Your instruments fight symptoms; AB-EXIT changes the architecture. The cost of a pilot test is low. The cost of inaction is the next populist cycle."

**For a tech audience this argument is especially strong**, because they understand the difference between **incrementalism** (5 %) and **structural redesign** (50 %). They build their entire careers on the second.

**One methodological point** — the correct formulation:

> "Existing mechanisms give empirically confirmed 2–8 % on various parameters. AB-EXIT by model estimates and analogy gives 15–40 %. This requires pilot testing in one state."

A tech audience values honesty about uncertainty. With this formulation the project looks like serious scientific mechanism design, not a utopian manifesto.
