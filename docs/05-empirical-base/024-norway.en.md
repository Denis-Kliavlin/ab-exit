## 24.1. Purpose of the section

§14 gives AB-EXIT an empirical grounding through the Alaska PFD — the only long-run experiment with direct state payments. This section gives another empirical base — **a state that has evolved into a condition very close to post-AB-EXIT**, without any formal introduction of AB-EXIT. That is Norway.

Over 200 years of natural evolution Norway reached four characteristics that AB-EXIT promises to realise structurally within one electoral cycle:

- Radical transparency of all public finances
- Long-term planning on a generational horizon
- Automatic marginalisation of political radicals, without suppression
- Business working through direct dialogue with citizens, bypassing political intermediaries

This is a **proof of concept** — the post-AB-EXIT condition is possible. Norway reached it by accident (through Lutheran culture, an oil fund, social-democratic evolution). AB-EXIT can bring any country to the same condition within one electoral cycle, structurally.

## 24.2. Radical transparency of public finances

The tax returns of all Norwegian citizens are **public**. Any resident of Norway can go to the tax authority's website and see how much his neighbour, his boss or a politician earned. This system — the "skattelistene" — has existed since 1814 (more than 200 years).

Effects:

- Social inequality is hard to hide. If a person lives in an expensive house but declares a low income, it is publicly visible. A powerful anti-corruption mechanism.
- Salaries in identical positions equalise. You cannot pay a woman less — she will see it.
- Politicians cannot grow rich on "unexplained income". Any discrepancy between an official salary and a real lifestyle is a public scandal.

Norway's Government Pension Fund Global publishes **every individual investment**. Any citizen can find out which companies the country's sovereign fund is invested in. Absolute transparency of state financial decisions.

In most other countries this transparency would be considered "a violation of privacy" or "a compromise of commercial secrecy". In Norway it has been the norm for 200 years, and society has only gained from it.

## 24.3. Long-term planning on a generational horizon

The Government Pension Fund Global was founded in 1990 to secure the welfare of **future generations** when the oil runs out. As of 2026 the fund holds assets of $1.6 trillion — more than $300,000 per resident of Norway.

The spending rule: the government may spend **no more than 3 % of the fund's size per year** on state needs. That means fiscal discipline impossible in a standard four-year electoral system.

In most democracies politicians spend more than they earn because:
- A deficit can be covered by borrowing — the consequences fall on the next ones
- Cutting spending loses votes within a short cycle
- Long-term projects yield no quick electoral benefit

In Norway the political system is **structurally tuned** to a long horizon. Decisions about the fund, its rules and its investment policy are taken with the interests of generations 50-plus years ahead in mind.

## 24.4. Automatic marginalisation of political radicals

Norway has right- and left-wing radical parties. They receive 5–10 % of the vote at every election. But they **never** come to power in coalitions, never receive ministerial posts in significant areas.

This is not the result of suppression (Norway is a model liberal democracy). It is the result of **electoral structure**. The Norwegian electorate **does not vote** for radicals in significant numbers. When a populist runs an emotional campaign against immigrants or against elites, the Norwegian voter looks at the statistics, checks the facts, sees that the proposed solutions are economically impossible, and votes for a moderate social democrat or a moderate conservative.

This is not an innate property of Norwegians. It is a structural property of **an electorate accustomed to transparency and long-term planning**. When you have the Government Pension Fund Global, and you see the state planning 50 years ahead, you structurally expect the same from all of politics. Populist rhetoric sounds like baby talk to this audience.

AB-EXIT can create **the same type of electorate** in any country within one electoral cycle — through the structural exit of the apathetic for a dividend and the concentration of pragmatists and ideologues in the active electorate.

## 24.5. Business works through direct dialogue with citizens

Political lobbying exists in Norway, but its role is qualitatively smaller than in the US or most European countries. Corporations wanting to change something in regulation more often turn **directly to public opinion** through media campaigns, public consultations and transparent position papers.

This is a **corporate behavioural effect**: when public finances are transparent, politicians cannot decide in favour of one corporation without a public explanation. So corporations are structurally motivated to persuade **society**, rather than try to influence politicians covertly. This is a healthy corporate-civic dynamic.

In a post-AB-EXIT electorate this effect should intensify. An electorate of pragmatists and ideologues will demand public explanations and check them. Corporations wanting to influence policy will be forced to play in the open.

## 24.6. AB-EXIT as a structural approximation to the Norwegian condition

Norway reached its condition through 200 years of evolution, a specific culture and an oil fund. Those factors are not reproducible in other countries.

But the **result** — transparency, long-term orientation, marginalisation of radicals, a healthy corporate dynamic — is reproducible through **a structural change of the electorate**. AB-EXIT gives such a structural change within one electoral cycle.

That is **the fundamental achievement of AB-EXIT**. Not "an improvement of existing democracy" but **the creation of a Norwegian electoral structure in any country within eighteen months**.

This does not mean that Moldova, Russia or the US will become exactly like Norway. They have their own culture and institutions. But the **electoral dynamics** will be structurally comparable to Norway's. That is already a huge step towards a functional democracy.
