### 75.1. Why this formulation is needed

In the previous sections AB-EXIT was described through defensive constructions: "it's not bribery", "it's not a tax on the poor", "it's not Tammany Hall". These analyses are legally precise but communicatively weak — they explain what AB-EXIT is NOT.

This formulation does the reverse: it takes from the system the most frightening word in political science — "qualification" (census) — and fully rehabilitates it, turning it from a tool of oppression into a tool of freedom. This is the transition from defence to attack, from "we are safe" to "we are the next level".

For presentations to donors, pitches to politicians, academic debates and public communication — this is a ready philosophical frame.

### 75.2. "First in history": the end of the elites' monopoly on the filter

The whole history of humanity is a history of artificial barriers at the entrance. The property qualification, the racial qualification (Jim Crow), the gender qualification, the educational qualification — all had one common feature: the elite decided FROM ABOVE who was worthy to govern and who was not. It was always dictated by fear, worked through prohibition and was maintained by violence or law.

AB-EXIT for the first time inverts the logic. The state no longer evaluates the citizen. The citizen himself evaluates his readiness to govern the city at this moment. This is the first filter built BOTTOM-UP, through self-determination, not through external compulsion.

A 90-degree logical turn from the 2,500-year tradition (section 67.4): not "whom to admit?" but "how much to pay for voluntary departure?". This thought could not have been born earlier, because it required two cultural preconditions of the 20th–21st centuries — the recognition of universal human dignity and a developed economics of revealed preference.

### 75.3. "Fair": a qualification of motivation, not of status

The historical qualification always hit the vulnerable: no land — no vote. AB-EXIT is absolutely blind to wealth, race or sex. The deal ($780 or a tripled vote) lies before every citizen identically.

Wherein lies the higher fairness: the filter works not by means but by motivation.

**Fairness for the poor.** A single mother with an income of $22k/year, who cares about the safety of the neighbourhood or the future of her child's school, refuses a sum substantial for her and receives the most powerful lever of influence over the mayor (her vote now decides everything, because the apathetic have left). Before AB-EXIT her vote drowned in the mass and meant nothing. Now her motivation converts into real power.

**Fairness for the rich.** A millionaire with an income of $350k/year, who does not care about the city's problems (children in a private school in another state, business in San Francisco), takes the $780 simply because he cannot be bothered to go to the polling station. He self-excludes from governance, ceding the place to those who really need it. His money does not help him keep power — he voluntarily gives it up.

For the first time in history power goes not to whoever has more money for lobbyists but to whoever physically needs it most. Wealth loses its advantage in politics; motivation becomes the only currency.

### 75.4. "Voluntary": the transition from violence to a deal

This is the most important word for defence against accusations of "stealing democracy".

The old qualification said: "You have no right to enter this door." AB-EXIT says: "The door is open to everyone. But if you understand that you don't know the budget or you're simply not interested — here is your share of the civic dividend. Thank you for your honesty; rest."

Voluntariness removes any ethical conflict. Nobody's rights are violated, because refusal to exercise a right is generously compensated. This turns elections from a heavy "civic duty", where people are made to lie to themselves and vote at random, into an honest transparent exchange.

Nobody is expelled. Everyone decides for himself. And everyone receives what matters more to him at this moment: money or influence.

### 75.5. "Reversible": the liquidation of electoral fatalism

The old qualifications were lifelong. Born a woman in the 19th century — struck out forever. Born a pauper in Sparta — the same. Any mistake of youth became fate.

The genius of AB-EXIT is that the "qualification" operates for exactly one electoral cycle. No longer. One can return as soon as a motive appears.

Scenario 1: in 2026 all is well with you, politics does not interest you. You press the button, take the $780, go and live. In 2028 the situation changes. No need to write petitions or stage revolutions. On election day you refuse the cheque, take the ballot, vote — the vote strikes with triple force.

Scenario 2 (illustrative, because it debunks the myth of AB-EXIT's oppositional nature): in 2026 you took the dividend. In 2028 the city proposes a waste-incineration plant behind your house. Standard thinking: "must protest, go against". The mature thinking of a shareholder-pragmatist: "the plant is an investment in the city, though a risky one. I stay to vote NOT against the plant but FOR the plant on the right conditions: German filters, online emission sensors, 10 % of the taxes to the district."

This is AB-EXIT in action. Not "the sleeper woke for protest" but "the sleeper woke for constructive bargaining". Reversibility gives not only the right to return — it gives the possibility of returning with the mature position of an investor, not a panicker.

The mayor is always hostage to the "sleepers", who can wake at any moment. But those who wake now come not with hysteria but with a price list.

### 75.6. "Market": an auction for the future

Political science for centuries tried to make people vote "correctly" through posters, moralising and guilt. It does not work — the Say-Do Gap is 47 %: people say one thing, do another, especially when participation is free.

AB-EXIT applies hard market logic to governance. The state is a corporation of shareholders. The budget is the shareholders' money. The market qualification sets a price for the right to be on the board of directors.

If you are not ready to pay $780 (by giving it up) for the right to govern — then your decisions would have been random, emotional and harmful. That is your own signal to yourself.

If you are ready to "buy" a vote at this price — then you are an investor in this city, and it is you who are entrusted with the wheel. For one cycle. With the possibility of revision.

This turns elections from an eloquence contest into a real market of governance decisions, where the price of a vote is honestly revealed.

### 75.7. A concrete example: how the quality of decisions changes

Comparing the same scenario (the plant proposal) in the two systems shows the difference in the quality of the resulting decisions.

**In the old system — binary hysteria and paralysis of development.** Populists mobilise the apathetic with free slogans of fear: "they'll poison us all, our children will get sick". In this noise the pragmatist's voice — "wait, let's install filters for $50M and mandate compensation for the district" — is heard by nobody. A crowd cannot negotiate; it can only shout "no". Result: either the mayor gets scared and cancels the project (the city loses the investment), or the mayor secretly takes a bribe from the developer, ignores the crowd and builds the cheapest dirty plant without filters.

**In the AB-EXIT system — constructive bargaining of shareholders.** The panickers took the dividend and left. The hall has cleared. In it remain people with calculators and a triple vote weight. They do not go to overthrow the mayor — they sit at the table as co-owners of the territory and roll out a price list of conditions: — "We approve the plant, BUT latest-generation filters, online emission sensors streamed to the mayor's website." — "We approve, BUT 10 % of this plant's taxes go to the district on an earmarked basis — either a property-tax cut or an addition to the base dividend pool." — "We approve, BUT an independent environmental audit every six months."

The mayor understands that before him is not a crowd but a group of shareholders with triple votes. They cannot be fooled by populism — they demand figures. The developer agrees to the conditions, because it is cheaper than losing a multi-million project.

Result: the city gets the investment + health protection + compensation for the district. All three goals achieved at once. In the old system at most one of the three is achievable.

### 75.8. Why this formulation is ideal for presentation

This formula moves AB-EXIT from the category of "clever payments" to the category of a fundamental upgrade of democracy. For a presentation to a donor, a politician, an academic interlocutor it sounds like this:

"We are not breaking elections. We propose evolution. The first fair, voluntary and reversible market qualification in history. A system where governance is by not professional shouters and a frightened crowd but by the motivated shareholders of the city, who confirmed their awareness with their own wallet."

Against such positioning the old system has no intellectual arguments. They cannot defend "the old qualification" (it is unfair), cannot defend "the absence of a filter" (that gives power to populists), cannot propose an alternative (they have no ideas, section 67).

### 75.9. The change in the behaviour of big business

An additional consequence, important for presentation to investors and entrepreneurs: AB-EXIT changes the rules of the game for big business too.

In the old system business acts through lobbying: gives the party money, the party mobilises the apathetic with promises, the apathetic vote. This is expensive, unreliable (the party may lose the election) and reputationally dangerous (the connection surfaces).

In the AB-EXIT system this scheme does not work. The apathetic have left; the pragmatists remain, who demand calculations. A lobbyist with an envelope of money loses meaning — money does not buy votes; it only highlights suspicion.

Business is forced to switch to direct deals with the city: "here is our project, here are our guarantees, here is the compensation for residents, here is the tax return". The city (through its shareholder-voters) either accepts the conditions or refuses. A clean B2G negotiating model instead of secret corridors.

Paradoxically: AB-EXIT does not destroy big business; it makes its interaction with the state MORE HONEST. Business spends less on lobbyists and more on quality projects, because only that buys the city's consent.

### 75.10. Summary

"The first fair, voluntary and reversible market qualification in history" is not merely a pretty phrase. It is:

- a historical position (on the axis of the 2,500-year evolution of qualifications),
- an ethical position (nobody is coerced, all are equal),
- an economic position (motivation revealed through price),
- an operational position (the mechanism is reversible at any moment),
- a communication position (a formula against which there is nothing to object).

This is the assembly of five different layers of AB-EXIT into one phrase that any person can remember and retell after hearing it once. For a project that builds influence through the spread of an idea, this is a critical communication asset.

### 76.1. Why this section

Academic critics (Guriev, Yudin, Harvard professors, think-tank analysts) pose objections of the same type, packed in complex language. The section gives three short answers that work against any version of the standard criticism. This is not philosophy — it is a tool for a specific conversation. The bearer of AB-EXIT must remember these three formulas so as not to be knocked out of the saddle at a meeting with an expert critic.

### 76.2. Answer 1: engagement is an improvement, not a problem

**Standard criticism:** "AB-EXIT will leave at the polls not the most competent but the most engaged — and those are different things. The engaged may make worse decisions than the indifferent."

**Answer:** the engaged is anyone who is INCLUDED in the cognitive process. A pragmatist with calculations or an ideologue with principles — both think, both respond to arguments, with both one can argue and both can be persuaded by facts. The indifferent votes at random, on his mother's advice, under the influence of advertising or by chance — he is not a participant in the discourse; he is noise in the system. Politics cannot be conducted with him, because he reacts neither to calculations nor to principles.

The quality of democracy is determined not by the percentage of "correct" votes but by the degree of awareness of the decision process. The engaged with a bad decision at least thought. The indifferent with an accidentally correct decision did not think. AB-EXIT raises awareness totally, and that is the primary goal of democracy.

Distribution by our models: the dividend will be taken by ~50–60 % (the apathetic, who now either do not vote or vote randomly). ~40–50 % will remain: roughly two-thirds Pragmatists (~25–30 % of the whole population) and one-third Ideologues (~10–15 % of the whole population). Both types can think, calculate, argue. This is a qualitatively different electorate from today's.

### 76.3. Answer 2: the path goes past the elites, not through them

**Standard criticism:** "Institutions resist change. The network of beneficiaries of the status quo will block the reform. Neither party is interested in carrying it out."

**Answer:** we are not trying to persuade the party elites. AB-EXIT goes through direct democracy (citizen initiative in states with the right of initiative — Oregon, Colorado, California), not through the parliamentary process. The objection about institutional resistance works against parliamentary reforms, where the elites' consent is needed. Against a citizen initiative it works weakly — an initiative by definition goes past the elites, straight to the voter.

Empirical evidence that this works: — Yang with UBI got through despite being ignored by the Democratic establishment, became a national figure. — Mamdani won the mayoralty of New York despite Trump's direct attack, with a record turnout in 50 years. — RCV was adopted in Maine and Alaska despite the resistance of both parties. — Florida Amendment 4 (2018) restored voting rights to 1.4 million convicted persons despite the Florida Republican Party.

Every case is a success AGAINST the elites' position, not with their consent. This empirically proves: the elites can be bypassed through direct democracy if the idea has enough popularity with the voter. AB-EXIT, giving everyone a direct material or political benefit, generates such popularity by structure.

### 76.4. Answer 3: compare with reality, not with the ideal (Nirvana Fallacy)

**Standard criticism:** "The poor man may lose in the long run if, without his vote, decisions are taken that worsen his position. The poor man's free choice under conditions of need is illusory."

**Answer:** this is the classic Nirvana Fallacy (Harold Demsetz's term, 1969) — comparing the proposed reform with an imaginary ideal alternative rather than with the real alternative. Demsetz showed: this error makes criticism of almost any reform trivial, because the ideal does not exist.

What the poor man has NOW (without AB-EXIT): — He receives nothing. — He has a vote, but one of millions, diluted by the apathetic and the manipulated. — Parties spend billions on manipulating his vote through fear and unfulfillable promises. — He has no levers over the decisions that worsen his life (tax rises, programme cuts, inflation). — Politicians win by mobilising precisely people like him through populism. — The real median wage of the American working class has not grown since the 1970s in real dollars. This means: the poor man LOSES in the long run in the current system with a GUARANTEE.

What the poor man has WITH AB-EXIT: — He receives $780 — a direct material return that never existed. — OR he has a tripled vote weight, if he decides to stay. — Every 4 years the choice is revisited — he can return under threat. — The right to bargain: "here is my price, what will you give in return?" — a lever he never had. — Manipulation of his vote through fear stops working — there is nobody to manipulate in an electorate that has left.

The real choice for the poor man is not "AB-EXIT vs ideal democracy" but "AB-EXIT vs the current system, where I am used as a mass, given nothing and promised everything". AB-EXIT does not have to be ideal. It has to be BETTER than the status quo. And it is better — it adds two options (money and the strength of those remaining) to the zero that exists now.

### 76.5. How to use these three answers

In a conversation with a critic the answers are given in the right sequence, not at random.

**Step 1 — set the frame:** "Before we discuss objections, let's agree: we compare AB-EXIT with reality today, not with an imaginary ideal system that never existed. Agreed?" If the critic agrees — half of his objections fall away automatically (Nirvana Fallacy). If not — ask him to point to a historical system where "the poor man's free vote" really worked.

**Step 2 — redefine "competence":** "The objection about 'the engaged instead of the competent' presupposes that we have a way to identify the competent. We do not. But we do have a way to identify those who are at least INCLUDED in the process. That is the improvement — we replace noise with awareness."

**Step 3 — remove the political anxiety:** "The objection about elite resistance is real, but we do not go through the elites. We go through citizen initiative. The empirical examples — Yang, Mamdani, RCV, Florida Amendment 4 — show that this path works against the will of the parties, not with their consent."

This sequence deprives the critic of his strongest rhetorical moves. Only constructive proposals remain (for example, "run a controlled experiment before mass launch") — and those are no longer criticism but co-authorship.

### 76.6. One common principle under all three answers

All three formulas rest on one rule: AB-EXIT is an engineering upgrade of a working-but-broken system, not a philosophical treatise on ideal democracy. An engineering upgrade is assessed in comparison with the OLD VERSION of the system, not with an imaginary version 10.0 that does not exist.

When a critic starts arguing with the philosophy of democracy as a whole — it means he has lost on facts and gone into abstraction. At that moment he must be brought back to earth: "We are talking about a concrete mechanism that improves concrete indicators of decision quality. If you have a concrete mechanism that improves them more — show it. If not — we discuss ours."

This principle turns any academic criticism of AB-EXIT from a threat into an opportunity: either the critic proposes a better concrete mechanism (and then we either accept it or show why AB-EXIT is stronger after all), or the critic refuses concreteness and loses the discussion by default.
