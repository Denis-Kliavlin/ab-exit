# Transition under Autocracy: Army, Police, Security Services and the Elite's Golden Parachute

**Chapter:** 08 — Implementation
**File:** 08_048d · v1 · 15 September 2026 (Gemini dialogue, session 15-09-26)
**Source:** a series of analyses on the material of Iran (IRGC, clergy), Russia (FSB, army) and a generalisation; country details — 057c, 056e. Supplements 047 (zugzwang of the bearers) and 042 (poison pills). The universal logic: in any autocracy there are three layers of force with different sociology, and the protocol acts differently on each.

---

## 1. Three layers of force

**The army — a cross-section of society.** It consists of conscripts and contract soldiers from ordinary families: father a worker, mother a teacher, brother unemployed. The people's demand to introduce the dividend is, for the soldier, not abstract politics but a cry for help from his own home. "The army physically and mentally cannot be against AB-EXIT, because you cannot set a person against the survival of his own family."

**The police — the face of the regime.** They interact with people every day and live in the same neighbourhoods; they waver; half will support the people once they see the army has taken a neutral stance.

**The security services — an artificial caste.** Personally loyal to the dictator, closed, privileged. Three weaknesses: there are few of them (you cannot control millions in every city at once); their weapon is secret arrests and dispersing the unarmed, against regular units with armour they are powerless; they fear the army, which despises them for arrogance and intrigue.

## 2. The army: "dumbness" as a superpower

Soldiers are not "dumb" — they are applied: there is a task, supply, a result. They are not persuaded by complex speeches — they are shown a picture: on the left an official in a suit who lies about stability and steals; on the right a simple system where every soldier and worker receives a share directly. The army is raised on KISS — if the plan is complex, it fails; the security services and officials live in a world of grey schemes and multi-move combinations, which to a soldier looks like dirt. They will see the protocol as a "clean, masculine, honest instrument" and a "spring clean".

**Payment once per cycle makes election day the "national payday".** Before: "guard this boring polling station where they tick boxes". Now: "guard the station because it is the cash desk; close it, block it, wreck it — and you don't get your money". The soldier is not a warden but the guard of the cash desk. The one formula to explain to a soldier: "the election took place + the system worked = money in the account". If the old elite tries to stuff ballots, the soldier waiting for his payment will be the first to stop the fraudster — the army becomes the guarantor of honesty out of self-interest.

**What is simpler for a private.** The dictator demands: "shoot the people queuing at the polling station" — an unnatural role, a crime against neighbours, without justification. The people ask: "stand and keep order while we vote; if there is order you get the bonus together with us; if there is chaos nobody gets it" — which is literally his job. A soldier's default settings: order is better than chaos; don't touch your own; task done — reward received. All three coincide with the people, none with the order to shoot. The soldier "will take the safety off, but point the rifle not at the people but at whoever tries to break up the referendum".

**Soldiers do not want to govern.** Politics for them is chaos and chatter; they love structure. The protocol offers them the role of shield: stay out of the economy and the laws, make sure nobody breaks the simple rules. A classic junta usurps power out of fear of chaos or revenge; here the army will want to hand over governance fast — power is responsibility for broken roads and hospitals. "Here is your system, it works, we checked; we go back to barracks and guard the borders and the servers."

## 3. The fuse against a junta

The risk that "soldiers become the new masters" is real — it has happened hundreds of times. But the protocol has a fuse no system has had: **AB-EXIT cannot be cancelled otherwise than by the army against the people — and that is impossible.** The order "suppress the uprising for dividends" means for the soldier "kill the source of your own income": the enemy on the square demands the same thing he wants himself; by obeying he kills his neighbours, destroys the system that pays him, and stays with a dictator who pays only in promises. Suppression requires a single, cohesive, fanatically loyal force — and it no longer exists: the soldier knows his comrade in the neighbouring unit also received the dividend and also does not want him to shoot. The "golden trough" trap: by becoming a dictatorship the military breaks transparency, trust falls, the economy stops, the dividends vanish — the military pragmatist chooses to be a guarantor of order with money and respect rather than a dictator without them. The army's role must be written down: temporary *guarantors*, not a temporary *government*; guarding the perimeter and the servers, not distribution and not the rules. The analogy: a bank's security guards the vault but cannot take the depositors' money because it is itself a depositor. Honest remainder: "the algorithm penalises the military by blocking payments to units taking part in suppression" — from the dialogue; there is no such norm in the protocol, and introducing it would create a selectivity of payment that §42 forbids. 🟡

## 4. The dictator's paradox: dumbing-down works against him

Every dictator seeks a large, simple army from the lower classes — so as not to be overthrown from outside. The constant dumbing-down and impoverishment of the people now works against him: **the poorer and simpler, the clearer and more desirable the protocol.** Complex ideologies require education: to instil "love of the leader" you need time, reading, faith in abstractions. Money requires no education — a biologically understandable incentive that both a genius and a shepherd understand. The dictator switched off the people's ability to believe in abstractions, leaving the survival instinct — and became the victim himself: the people believe only in what can be touched, and the dividend is the most "touchable" thing in history. He also collapsed society into two layers and homogenised the lower one into a single huge angry shareholder with a single interest. Zugzwang: give education and wealth — people stop being simple and understand he is not needed; keep them poor and simple — they become the perfect base for the protocol, and an army made of the same simple people supports them.

## 5. The police: the kitchen conversation

The most painful scene: evening, kitchen, a policeman in uniform, opposite him his wife or mother. His script from the manual: "you don't understand macroeconomics — there will be hyperinflation"; "it's a trap by provocateurs — do you want it like in [country in chaos]?"; "the ruler is wise, we must endure for stability". The family's answers: "prices already rise every day and wages don't — how can it get worse?"; "if it's bad, why doesn't the government just explain the reason instead of sending you to beat us? why doesn't it introduce its own, even better system?"; "if he is wise and loves the people, why is he afraid of a people that wants to live decently?". The wise-ruler trap: in defending the regime the policeman must admit one of two things — the ruler is a fool (does not see the obvious solution) or a thief (sees it and does not introduce it so the money stays with him). "The policeman has no answer to the question: why do you defend the one who thinks you and your children don't need this money?"

For the policeman the protocol is not a change of power: he personally is not threatened, the payment does not threaten the regime; the demand is lawful — a share of the country's income. But for the dictator the budget is a personal wallet, and a transparent fund means the end of his property right over the country; that is why he fears it more than a political rally. The policeman stands with a shield, opposite him a placard "give us back our dividends"; he thinks "I defend the law", the protester — "I demand the law"; both are right; there is no threat — "the king is naked". Three stages of the break: paralysis of will (shields lowered, breakthroughs "not noticed"); emotional breakdown (he sees his mother in the crowd; one dropped shield is a virus); open defection (10–20 % refuse, commanders fear their own soldiers). The speed — hours or days, not months, because the demand touches everyone's wallet, including the policeman's own.

**After the transition** — "the shareholders' police": if the state belongs to everyone, the police automatically protect everyone; their interest is stability, because looting and destruction of infrastructure devalue their own dividend; a looter is a thief from their children's pocket. This removes the "wrecked building syndrome" (everyone loots because nothing belongs to anyone) and the power vacuum (the police do not scatter — they have a job better than before). Legitimacy from day one: not "we are for the new leader" but "we protect the common payment system". Violence does not disappear — a state without force is a decoration — but it becomes contractual: the guard in a bank, the bouncer in a club; citizens support it because it protects their safety and their dividend. The old guard used to torture is screened out by re-certification.

## 6. The elite: golden parachute or sainthood

Autocracy holds its elite with two instruments: "I can kill you" and "I can strip you of everything". The "golden parachute or martyrdom" scheme zeroes both: the threat of death — "I become a martyr, my name lives for centuries, the regime is cursed"; the threat to assets — "I am ready for a deal with the opposition and will get a parachute". A person without fear is uncontrollable; the dictator is in zugzwang: kill — make a saint; leave — he prepares the transition. As soon as one senior figure understands the no-lose position, he whispers to others: "why wait until we are swept away together with the bandits? if they kill us — martyrs; if it works — a pension in Tuscany".

The "secret club" — not spies in the classic sense but sleeper agents of change inside the system under perfect camouflage: "boring economics". The security services look for terrorists and leaflets and see officials discussing "optimisation of income distribution" — "working on the economy so the people riot less? let them work". They use official channels, government offices and budget money for "digitalisation of public services" — the very infrastructure that becomes the protocol's platform; the dictator signs digitalisation decrees, paying for the work of his gravediggers. The merger of elites and the "angry crowd": the elites want to survive and legalise, the poor want the dividend and an end to plunder; the spies give the people a plan, the people give the spies protection — yesterday's "thief" becomes "the man who brought us the money", and an arrest for "treason" brings people onto the streets demanding the benefactor's release. One morning the dictator gives an order and the decrees are not executed (officials are busy "configuring the system"), the army does not shoot, the elites sit on their suitcases. The Iranian version with the clergy and the Pahlavis — 057c.

## 7. The regime's logical crisis

Usually protests are suppressed under the slogans "against chaos" or "for the faith". But "I forbid you to be rich because I care about your spiritual salvation" does not work. The three justifications of power — the Trojan horse ("they give money to take sovereignty"), economic chaos ("inflation within a week"), moral degradation ("a consumer society") — break on the trap of the obvious ("my money arrived in my account, this is not a conspiracy, this is mathematics") and the trap of personal interest (the policeman wants the dividend himself). The moment the police begin explaining to the people why "money is harmful", the regime loses its status as the people's protector. Propaganda can explain why the leader may not be criticised. Propaganda can never explain why you may not become richer.

## 8. Weak point of the section

The whole section is a model of the forces' behaviour *after the demand has become mass and public*. It does not describe how the demand becomes mass under a total information blockade, and it does not account for mercenary and foreign contingents to whom the country's dividend is not due (partly — "the army of their own country will block their entry", but that is already a scenario of open confrontation). There are no empirical precedents of "refusing to shoot because of a payment"; the nearest analogues — the refusals of 1989–1991 and 2011 — were motivated otherwise. 🟡
