# 48o. The Twenty-Dollar Relay: Rules of the "Money or a Double Vote" Challenge

**Chapter:** 08 — Implementation
**File:** 08_048o · v1 · 19 October 2026
**Source:** the architect, 16–19 October 2026: "do you take the $20 if, after AB-EXIT is introduced, you would take the money; or you give the other person $20 if you would stay to vote; if you took the money, you must run the prank on those same $20 with another person until somebody takes them"; "make a 1000-rouble version"; "anyone watching can be invited to try to earn and run the challenge where they live"; "write the rules into the repo, a separate chapter." A development of chapter 052 (the 676 Challenge): the stake raised from $10 to $20, the relay added, a Russian version, the participant's earnings and three American analogues. Attachment: [PDF handout, the 1000-rouble version, in Russian](048o-challenge-1000.pdf).

*How to read. These are the rules of a game anyone can run with one banknote and a phone. The game reproduces the protocol's choice — take the payment or stay with a vote of double weight — with real money but under an imaginary law, and is therefore legal. The relay makes it self-propagating: the banknote travels between players, no organiser is needed, and the challenge pays for its own spread. Every clip is an answer to the pair "took or stayed" with a face — the 048l measurement, made for free.*

## 1. The game

The host approaches a person with one real banknote — $20 in the United States, 1000 roubles in Russia — and says word for word: "Imagine this is already the law. Before every election the state offers a choice: take the payment (about $800; about 40 thousand roubles) and skip this election, or refuse and vote with a vote of double weight, because some people have left. In a few years you choose again. The money is from the state, not from me."

The stake: "I am playing too. You choose the money — I give you this banknote. You choose the vote — you give me one like it." Fifteen seconds to decide aloud, the banknote changes hands on camera, and the person explains why.

The payment figures are the repository's yardsticks: the United States about $800 (040ap §4, 059l), Russia 40–50 thousand roubles (the yardstick "desirable for 40–50 % of the population"). The stake is what people leave without thinking: a tip at lunch (040ap §4).

## 2. The relay

The banknote does not stay with the taker for good. Whoever chose the money and took it must run the same game with the same banknote on another person. The note travels from taker to taker and stops at the one who chooses the vote: he pays the host a note of the same value, the chain closes at that link, and the last host is left with two notes. One twenty passes through five to ten people, and each of them makes a choice with money in hand.

The balance is zero: the first host loses a note, the one who chose the vote loses a note, the last host gains two. Nobody pays to enter, nobody collects from newcomers, the organiser never touches the money. This is a property of the rule itself — the protocol is nobody's (059l §4) — carried over to the game.

## 3. A ninety-second script

1. Opening, five seconds: "I've got a twenty. Shall we play?"
2. The legend, twenty seconds, word for word from §1: the law is already adopted; a choice before every election; the payment or a double vote; again in a few years; the money from the state; voting under the "vote" choice is ordinary and lawful; nobody loses a right.
3. The stake, five seconds: a note each way.
4. Countdown aloud, fifteen seconds. Without a countdown the person has time to invent a respectable answer.
5. The choice and the handover on camera, hand to hand.
6. "Why?", twenty seconds. Do not argue, listen: this is the clip's main material.
7. The last question to everyone, whatever the choice: "Would you want this adopted as law?" Yes, no, with conditions.
8. The handover of the relay: "Now you. This note is yours until it is taken by someone who chooses the vote."
9. A line on screen, ten seconds: "Want two notes and views? Take one, ask a friend what he would choose if this were already the law, and post it with the tag."

The clip runs over a minute on purpose: platforms count only such clips toward creator payouts (§5).

## 4. Rules that cannot be broken

- The legend is hypothetical only: "imagine this is already the law." That is speech, protected as speech, not a transaction. "Promise not to vote for money" is the purchase of a promise, vote buying, an offence in any country (052 §90.17; in the United States 52 U.S.C. 10307, in Russia art. 5.16 of the Administrative Code and art. 141 of the Criminal Code).
- Only the stake is real, and it is between players. The large sum on camera is symbolic: an envelope marked "$800, exit payment" or printed notes.
- Four sentences are mandatory: the money is from the state; the choice is made anew at every election; voting under the "vote" choice is ordinary and lawful; nobody loses a right (048m arts. 5, 16).
- Not a word about parties, candidates, names. The question is about the rule, not about people.
- No filming at polling stations and not on voting days.
- Ask consent to be filmed aloud before the question; in two-party-consent states (Illinois) this is mandatory.
- Do not edit the answers. The most valuable footage is "I don't care, give me the money" and the person who refused the money and explained why.

## 5. How a participant earns

Three honest ways, and they are stated at the end of every clip.

1. By closing the chain: the last host is left with two notes. With a third choosing the vote, a chain lasts three games on average — two notes for three conversations.
2. By views: creator reward programmes pay on the order of $0.40–1.00 per thousand qualified views on clips over a minute; a clip at a hundred thousand is $40–100, at a million $400–1000.
3. By the topic: whoever is first in his city is the channel.

This is not a pyramid: money moves only between players at zero sum, there is no entry fee and no centre. And not bribery: payment is for a choice under an imaginary law and for a clip, not for a vote at a real election.

## 6. What it gives the protocol

Every clip is a recorded choice with money in hand and a face: the pair "took or stayed," which no work in the arsenal contains (040at §1), collected without a poll and without a budget. A thousand clips are a thousand answers and a thousand "whys." This is the measurement of 048l §4 that the challenge makes by itself: the share choosing the vote, their age, their "why," and among them — those who had not voted for years and suddenly refused the money. The last player is the most important: ask him "why" twice.

And it is the direct consequence of 052 §90.15 — "every surprise creates two new prankers" — carried through to money: the challenge pays for its own spread, and, like the rule, has no owner.

## 7. Why it is a game for the majority

The majority no longer goes to the polls. Below federal elections the non-comers are the majority everywhere: Moscow mayor 2023 — about 43 %, Moscow City Duma 2024 — about 41 %, regional parliaments — typically 30–45 %, municipal elections in large cities — 20–30 %; the State Duma 2021 — 52 %, so almost half stayed home. The United States: the presidential election of 2024 — 64 %, Congress 2022 — 46 %, mayoral elections — typically 15–27 %. The average turnout at parliamentary elections worldwide has fallen for forty years running (International IDEA). The person asked most likely did not vote last time, and for him the choice "money or the vote" looks like "money or nothing." Hence the clip's main surprise: how many people still refuse the money.

## 8. Three American analogues

Formats that have already gathered tens of millions of views and from which the relay takes one property each.

| Format | What it was | What we take | What it lacks |
|---|---|---|---|
| MD Motivator, "give me a dollar" (since 2021) | the host asks a stranger for a dollar and gives the giver $500–1000; about 10 billion views in total, one clip 14 million in a day | real money in hand, a stranger, a choice in seconds, "pass it on" | it tests kindness, not the price of a vote; the viewer does not ask "would I take it?" |
| Hidden Cash (2014) and Mr. Cash Drop (2025–2026) | envelopes of cash hidden around a city with clues online; a wave from San Francisco to London, a return in 2025 and a spread across Asia | self-propagation without a centre, zero cost of entry | the first to grab gets the money; nobody makes or records a choice |
| MrBeast, "cash or the box" (since 2019) | cash against a closed box, tens of millions of views per clip, thousands of imitators | a binary choice with money on camera, a countdown, an explanation afterwards | the box has no content; the vote has, and the "why" becomes a conversation about power without a word about politics |

The street clips "would you sell your vote for a candy bar" attributed to Mark Dice could not be confirmed from sources and are not included.

## 9. United States: an estimate for $1000 in Chicago

An estimate, not a measurement. Budget: a stake reserve of $200 (the relay returns part of it, net cost $100–200); a camera operator for two days or self-shot, $0–300; props $50; paid promotion $400–500 at a US CPM of $5–15; a reserve of $100. The street: in two days in the Loop, Wicker Park, the UIC campus and CTA stations — 60–80 approaches, 40–50 games, by the yardstick of 052 §90.16 one to three substantive clips per ten approaches, 12–20 clips in all; 50–80 people enter the game in person, with another 200–300 onlookers.

| Scenario | Organic | Paid, $450 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| weak: a new account, no clip takes off | 15 clips at 1–3 thousand | 40–60 thousand impressions | 60–100 thousand |
| medium: one or two clips reach the feed | 15 at 3–10 thousand plus 1–2 at 100–300 thousand | 50–80 thousand | 300–600 thousand |
| strong: a hit with a veteran or an activist | one clip at 1–5 million, the rest ride on it | not needed | 2–6 million |

Paid impressions cost about 10 cents a view and produce no chain; organic reach produces the chain. So no more than half goes to advertising, and only behind a clip that has already reached 20–50 thousand on its own. The outcome is decided by the first three clips: the veteran takes the money, the activist takes the money, the working man refuses (052 §90.15). The cost of a view in the medium scenario is 0.2–0.3 cents; the cost of one person who made a choice with money in hand is $12–20, and that number matters more.

## Weak point

The rules are a construction, not a measurement: the share choosing the vote, the chain length and the response to "try it yourself" have not been measured by anyone; the Chicago estimates rest on 2026 platform prices and the experience of street interviews, not on data from this format. Turnout figures for regional and municipal elections are given as ranges. One point the architect has not yet fixed: whether the one who chooses the vote closes the chain, or keeps the note and starts a new one; the first version is recorded.
