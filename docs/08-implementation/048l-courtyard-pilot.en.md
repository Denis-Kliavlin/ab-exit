# 48l. The Courtyard Pilot: Live Money and a Real Decision

**Chapter:** 08 — Implementation
**File:** 08_048l · v1 · 1 October 2026 (session 01.10.26)
**Source:** the assistant's proposal of 27.09.2026 instead of buying expert opinions ("he will give very cautious estimates and write: a pilot is needed") and the architect's decision of 01.10.2026 "do it"; gap 3 of the roadmap (006b §5). The design is assembled from existing norms and forecasts of the repository; whatever appears here for the first time is marked.

---

## 1. What the pilot tests and what it does not

The pilot is not a survey "would you support the protocol" but the protocol at work on a real decision with real money. It tests five things that the repository so far gives as estimates:

1. The share choosing the money at a sum "desirable to 40–50 %" (048k §3), and its composition by age and income (045 §45.3, 016 §25).
2. The sign of the thermostat: how many people refuse the sum in order to vote "against" (015b §6b).
3. The working of the time scale: early declaration with a premium, late payment at a discount, the controller whose money waits (048k §3).
4. The quality of the decision of those who remain: the shift of knowledge and opinions on the model of deliberative polls (023e) and a comparison with a neighbouring courtyard deciding the same question by an ordinary vote.
5. Simplicity: can a participant explain the rule to a neighbour in one sentence (040c).

What the pilot does not test and must not promise: the shift of parties, the fate of populists, the working of the thermostat over several cycles, the behaviour of power. Those are national quantities; a courtyard does not yield them. The pilot yields what no model can — the behaviour of live people with live money.

## 2. Where and who

**The unit.** A courtyard, a homeowners' association or a village of 500–1,000 adult residents with a fund of its own and a real question to decide: what to spend the courtyard fund on, whom to elect to the board, whether to change the management company. The question must be such that some residents have a personal stake and others do not care — otherwise there is nothing to test.

**The country.** The first candidate is Moldova: there the market price of a vote has already been measured (019d §2) and the budget analysed (053b), so the result has something to be compared with. The second is any town with a carrier (041) and an independent sociological institute.

**Who runs it.** Not us. A sociological institute or a university with a plan published in advance: which quantities are measured, which thresholds count as a result. Payments through a notary or a bank, the register and the counter as open data. Otherwise the result will be called ours, and it will be worth nothing.

**Legal status.** A private poll with private payments on the logic of Route B (035): it is not an election, the money is not public, and it touches no rule on bribery of voters. Tax on the payment — by the country's rules, to be included in the pilot budget.

## 3. How it is arranged

- **The question** is formulated by the courtyard's board and approved by the meeting before the pilot is announced, so that the protocol is not suspected of choosing the topic.
- **The sum.** It cannot be derived from the country's formula — a courtyard has no median. Instead the sum is set by the yardstick of 048k §3: a preliminary survey of residents "from what sum would you forgo the right to decide this question", and the sum at which 40–50 % would forgo it is taken. The reference for Moldova is 25–30 euros. Thus the pilot also tests the yardstick itself.
- **Two buttons.** Through an app and through a paper sheet at the notary. The declaration window is two weeks, closing by default five days before the vote; for a courtyard the term may be shorter (048m, Article 6).
- **The time scale** (048k §3): the first three days — payment at once with a 5–10 % premium; the rest of the window — payment at once without a premium; whoever chose nothing and did not vote — the money waits until the courtyard's next decision, at a 20–30 % discount.
- **The count** — every ballot counts as one; the number 1/(1 − exit share) is published as a reference showing by how much the share of one vote has grown (033c §9b).
- **The counter** — daily, public, by building or entrance (004 §4.7).
- **The vote** of those who remain — an ordinary meeting or paper ballot by the courtyard's rules; the protocol does not change them (048k §3).
- **A control courtyard** — a neighbouring one of the same size, deciding the same kind of question by an ordinary vote in the same period. Without it any result is an anecdote.
- **Two rounds** six months to a year apart: only the second shows whether the aggrieved came back and how the late payment behaves.

## 4. What is measured

| Quantity | The repository's forecast | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Share choosing the money | 40–60 % of residents; among those the sum addresses, 93–97 % take it | 045 §45.3–45.4 |
| Age profile of those who remain | younger than today's voters; ~70 % of 18–25s remain, ~15 % of 75+ | 016 §25.5 |
| Those refusing the sum to vote "against" | present; their share is the sign of the thermostat | 015b §6b |
| Those coming for the late payment | a noticeable share of "empty" records; witnesses against stuffing | 048i §5b |
| Early declarations | most of the decided — in the first days | 004 §4.9 |
| Shift of knowledge and opinions of those who remain, before and after deliberation | no less than in Fishkin's random samples | 023e, 013f §1b |
| Share of organised groups among those who remain against their share today | the indicator for Anzia's objection | 015c §7b |
| Recognition of the result by those who exited | they consider the decision legitimate because they took the money voluntarily | 048g §6b |
| Attempts at vote-buying and pressure | recorded by the institute; the price of a vote expected to rise above the sum | 019d §4 |
| Explaining the rule to a neighbour | more than 80 % can repeat the rule in one sentence | 040c |
| Cost per participant | sum × take-up share + administration | 045 §45.7 |

## 5. What counts as a result

The pilot succeeds if all at once: the share taking the money fell within 40–60 %; those who remain are younger than in the control courtyard; at least one resident refused the sum to vote "against" and it is recorded; the decision of those who remain is no worse than the control courtyard's in the judgement of independent observers; those who exited recognise the result; more than 80 % repeat the rule.

The pilot fails if fewer than a quarter took the money (the sum is small or people did not believe in the payment) or more than three-quarters (nobody is left to decide — the sum is large for this question), or if those who exited contest the result. Either is also a result: it calibrates the yardstick.

## 6. Budget and timing

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Payment fund at 1,000 residents, a sum of 30 euros and a 50 % exit | 15,000 euros; with premiums and discounts it comes to the same (004 §4.9) |
| The institute: plan, surveys before and after, observation, report | 10,000–15,000 euros |
| Notary, app, printing | 3,000–5,000 euros |
| The second round | the same sums six months to a year later |

Timing: three months of preparation, a two-week window, the decision day, six months to the second round with the late payment open.

## 6b. The first are pioneers: what they are promised

The architect, 02.10.2026: "the protocol cannot have a lethal outcome, and all that is needed is to reassure the test subjects that they will be supported as pioneers and that they chose this for the sake of everyone".

The worst outcome of the pilot is money and time spent (040m §5d). So the participants need not protection from danger but three simple things.

- **The truth that it is a trial.** They are told plainly: you are the first, nobody knows the result, the thresholds of success and failure are recorded in advance and published (§5).
- **Support to the end.** The pilot does not begin until both rounds are secured: a trial cut off halfway proves nothing, and the failure will be blamed on the idea. That is how the first patient treated with penicillin died — not because the medicine did not act but because there was not enough of it.
- **Recognition.** They do this not only for themselves: other courtyards, cities and countries will decide by their result. The name of the first courtyard stays in the history of the rule, as names stay with reforms (07/038).

By the rule "measure from the sick", the first place to look for is not a well-run courtyard but one where management no longer works: a building that for years has been unable to change its management company, a village in debt. There consent is easier to obtain and the result shows more sharply.

For a first city, rather than a courtyard, the question of money is added: if the percentage proves too high, the loss must not fall on the residents. The form of such support — for instance a donor's guarantee on the loan — is proposed in 048n and requires the country's lawyer.

## 7. What the pilot gives the repository

The first numbers instead of estimates: the exit share, the age profile, the share refusing the sum to vote "against", the threshold of sensitivity to the sum — a calibration of the simulator's formula (055c §6.3b) and a test of theorems 2–4 in 013f. One successful courtyard weighs more than any section: "a live precedent with a real payment is worth more than any increase of the sum" (045 §45.7).

*Three requirements for the pilot from the fifth Nobel circle (08.10.2026, 036e):* a small percentage in the first round and observation of which side the threshold lies on — whether the apathetic or the moderates leave first (Schelling); a control group — a second courtyard without the payment, or the result will be called a coincidence (Angrist); a count of those who take and those who stay by sex, income and dependence on an employer — the budget employee against the self-employed — together, and by trust in the secrecy of choice B — one question, "do you believe your choice will not be known" (Goldin; the coercion gap through the token and the pro-base filter, 036e §10); a randomised percentage between courtyards — one and two — to see a slope rather than a point, and measurement after a round rather than at once (Card, Angrist, Imbens). And Lucas's rule over all of it: a number computed from the world without the payment is not a number about the world with it; hence measure not the level of the first round but the change between rounds — that is the measurement of the interaction of the elements, the one thing that remains untested (036e §7).

*Size calculation, 08.10.2026 (the weak point of 040p §13).* The thresholds of 040p are differences in shares between two groups: takers against stayers, the courtyard with the payment against the one without, the dependent against the independent. For a difference to be distinguishable rather than noise, under the usual conditions (5 % type-I error, 80 % power, shares near one half) one needs:

| Compared in each group | Example | Smallest detectable difference |
|---|---|---|
| 150 | a 30 % subgroup of a courtyard of 500 | about 16 points |
| 300 | a 30 % subgroup of a courtyard of 1,000 | about 11 points |
| 500 | a whole courtyard of 500 | about 9 points |
| 1000 | a whole courtyard of 1,000 | about 6 points |

Consequences. In a courtyard of 500 with a control courtyard of the same size, differences from 9 points are distinguishable over the whole courtyard and from 16 points over a subgroup of a third — and it is the subgroups (former non-voters with a stake, dependent workers) that carry thresholds 1–5 of 040p. So: read the 040p thresholds as "a difference of at least 16 points" for courtyards of 500 and "at least 11" for courtyards of 1,000; a smaller difference is neither refutation nor confirmation but a null, and the round is repeated at another percentage or in a larger courtyard. Two courtyards of 1,000 are the minimum at which the pilot can answer items 1–3; two of 500 answer only coarse effects. Computed by the two-proportion formula at p near 0.5; for shares further from one half the detectable differences are smaller.

**The architect's decision on scale (08.10.2026).** "Take a bankrupt city of 50–200 thousand — and it will be good." That removes the size limitation entirely: with 30–120 thousand voters, differences of one or two points are distinguishable even in subgroups, and all ten refuters of 040p are testable from the first cycle. And it is the same logic as "the sick first" (040m §5d): a bankrupt city has nothing to lose, its payers have a reason to stay, the burned are most numerous there, the machine is weakest — it has nothing to pay with — and "where is the money" is answered by a city that already counts every dollar. The courtyard's role changes, it is not cancelled: the courtyard is a rehearsal of the design (register, reconciliation, the secrecy of B) in weeks and for thousands of euros before the city; the city is the test of the rule. To launch the rule in a city without testing the design in a courtyard is an error of engineering, not of logic. Where to look in the US, with a legal route to a referendum and a size of 50–200 thousand: Stockton and San Bernardino (bankruptcies of 2012), Chester, Scranton, Gary, Flint, Youngstown, Camden, Jackson — a list of where to look, not a choice; the choice is the architect's.

## 8. Weak point

*A remark of a cold referee, 02.10.2026 (040m §5g).* The criterion of success is close to a tautology: the sum is chosen so that 40–50 % take it, and success is counted as 40–60 %. For the pilot to be able to refute the claim it needs different sums in comparable courtyards, a measurement of the stake separate from income and a plan written down in advance — and a condition of refusal: if exit is predicted by income at an equal stake, selection runs by income, not by the stake. One control courtyard and "at least one resident" are too little for that.

A stake in a courtyard and a stake in a national election are of different sizes, and the exit share at 30 euros over a playground does not carry over as a number to a month's wage over a parliament; what carries over is the shape of the dependence and the sign. Participants know they are watched, and the first round may show more civic virtue than the second. A thousand people give a precision of about three points — enough for the 40–60 % range, not for fine differences by age. And a courtyard has no thermostat: the quality of the decision will be judged by observers, not by a number on an account. 🟡
